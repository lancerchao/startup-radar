# B2C Apps With Investor Money / Breakout Traction — Last 12 Months (US, Europe, East Asia)

*Scan 2026-08-05: 5 parallel research agents, 53 companies. Goal: find ideas worth adapting/beating to market, especially geographic-arbitrage gaps (proven abroad, absent in the US). Funding figures flagged when unverified. This is a breadth scan, not adversarial verification — treat numbers as reported, not confirmed.*

## The 6 clearest steal/arbitrage candidates (scored against Lance's B2C rules)

### 1. Short-drama apps — the biggest revenue gap on the list
**ReelShort** (~$400M rev 2024, 69-90% FROM THE US) and **DramaBox** ($323M rev, 57% US) — Chinese-operated vertical short-drama apps (1-2 min cliffhanger episodes, coin-based unlocks). Americans are paying hundreds of millions to Chinese operators; no serious US-native competitor exists, Hollywood hasn't responded. **Fit**: content-production business, not pure software — heavy ops (studios, actors, writers) puts it outside solo/duo scope at full scale, but an AI-generated variant (AI video is nearly at short-drama quality) could collapse the production cost. Rule 4 strong (real production pipeline moat), Rule 2 strong (proven whale monetization). Biggest risk: content taste + the Chinese incumbents' data advantage on what hooks convert.

### 2. Meet5 (Germany) — IRL group meetups for 40+/55+
2.5M users, 40K events/month, €8M Series A, explicitly beginning US expansion. **US equivalent: none** — Meetup is generic and aging; all US IRL-social startups (Pie $11.5M, Hangout, Sweatpals, Clyx, Timeleft, 222) chase 20-somethings. **This is the standout fit for Lance specifically**: the 40+/55+ demographic is exactly the audience he already acquires on Meta profitably, group-first mechanics dodge the dating-app liquidity trap (Rule 5's exclusion is two-sided marketplaces; a group-event app has gentler cold-start since one host + N attendees ≠ matched pairs), LTV is subscription-shaped, and the loneliness-economy thesis was already validated in his own senior research. Race risk: Meet5 itself entering the US — but with no US brand, no US ad account experience, and a German-language org.

### 3. Jagat (Indonesia/Singapore) — Gen Z friend-map with real-world quests
22M+ users, Zenly successor with "Coin Hunt" (location treasure hunts, cash prizes driving users outdoors). **US gap is real**: Zenly died un-replaced (Snap Map is a feature, Life360 is family-safety-coded), and the outdoor-quest mechanic has no US copy at all. **Fit problems**: young demographic (not Lance's lane), social cold-start (Rule 5 exclusion territory), and location-social carries teen-safety/privacy landmines. Real gap, wrong founder fit.

### 4. Voice-first dating (Voicetep, Japan) — no photos, no texting, voice only
Patented mechanic, ~$1.3M raised. **No mainstream US voice-first dating app exists** (audio features on Hinge/Bumble were bolt-ons; standalone attempts died — the graveyard is a caution). Interesting collision with Lance's existing senior-dating research: voice-first + 50+/senior dating (where his research already showed voice preference 2x and typing friction) is a more differentiated combined thesis than either alone. Same Meta dating-authorization gate as before.

### 5. Licensed-persona fan AI chat (OSHIAI, Japan) — "official" celebrity/creator AI companions
Licensed AI personas of real artists/VTubers/streamers, sold to fandoms. **US gap: genuine** — Meta's celebrity personas flopped, Character.AI is under legal pressure for unlicensed personas; nobody has built the licensed version for US creator fandoms. Rule 7 caution: adjacent to the parasocial/companion territory ruled out earlier — but the licensed, creator-sanctioned version with the creator as business partner is structurally the "family test"-passing variant (the creator endorses and profits). Needs creator-economy BD muscle, which Lance doesn't have.

### 6. Yuka-for-X (France) — scan-and-score consumer transparency
Yuka: 80M users, US is its biggest market, zero VC, funded by premium subscriptions — proof the scan → independent score → cleaner alternatives mechanic monetizes at scale. The generic food version is taken (by Yuka itself), but **vertical spins are open**: supplements, kids' products, pet food, cosmetics-for-seniors. Rule 4 risk: thin-wrapper danger unless the scoring database becomes proprietary accumulated data. Rule 1/6 fit: good (niche-targetable interest audiences).

## Category heat-map (what VCs funded most, last 12 months)

1. **AI dating/matchmaking (US, very hot, very crowded)** — Sitch ($7M, a16z Speedrun), Ditto ($9.2M, Peak XV), Gigi ($3M Khosla), Cerca, Iris, Amata. The "swipe fatigue → paid AI curation" thesis has 6+ funded entrants and no winner. Entering now = 7th player.
2. **IRL social / loneliness economy (US+EU, hot)** — Pie, Clyx ($14M), Posh ($22M), Timeleft (€18M ARR on $2M raised — the efficiency standout), Sweatpals, Hangout, Meet5, Joiner, 222. Crowded for 20s-30s; **empty for 40+** (the Meet5 gap).
3. **Private/anti-algorithm social (US+EU)** — Yope ($20M total, 15M users), Airbuds, Locket. Anti-Meta thesis: small-group sharing, no feeds, no ads.
4. **AI companions/interactive fiction (East Asia ahead of US)** — Wrtn/Crack ($73.5M Series B, Korea), Zeta (profitable, overtook ChatGPT usage among Korean Gen Z), OSHIAI. Korean/Japanese execution and monetization are ahead of US equivalents.
5. **Consumer safety/anti-scam (US, nascent)** — Savi ($7M), Tea (viral then legally radioactive: defamation suits + data breach). Demand proven; execution bar is trust and data security. Overlaps Lance's existing scam-shield research.
6. **AI utility one-shots ("point camera, get answer")** — Cal AI (bootstrapped to $30M/yr, acquired by MyFitnessPal), Phia ($43.5M), Doji ($14M), Yuka. The proven repeatable pattern: one input, one magic output, subscription. Also the most wrapper-prone category (Rule 4).
7. **Short drama / bite-size entertainment (China → US money flow)** — ReelShort, DramaBox. See candidate #1.

## Cross-cutting patterns worth stealing (mechanics, not companies)

- **"One input → one magic output" utilities keep winning** (Cal AI photo→calories; Yuka scan→score; Asleep's mic-only sleep staging). Cheapest probe shape that repeatedly reaches $10M+ revenue.
- **Revenue-funded beats VC-funded in IRL social**: Timeleft (€18M ARR, $2M raised) and Yuka (zero VC, 80M users) are the two efficiency outliers of the whole scan — both charge consumers directly from day one.
- **East Asia is 12-18 months ahead on AI-companion monetization** (Zeta profitable; Wrtn at ~$100M+ raised) — whatever mechanics work there (UGC characters, gifting, per-episode coins) tend to arrive in the US later.
- **Character/companion framing beats feature lists in utility apps** — Focus Friend (a knitting bean, #1 on the App Store over ChatGPT) vs. the crowded Forest/Opal category it beat.
- **Widget/ambient surfaces are an underused distribution wedge** — Airbuds, Locket, Yope all ride the home-screen widget as their retention mechanic.

## Bottom line for Lance

Two candidates clear his rules meaningfully better than the rest: **Meet5-for-the-US (40+/55+ IRL group meetups)** — right audience, right acquisition channel, proven model, incumbent race risk but a real head start on the demographic; and **Yuka-for-a-vertical** — proven mechanic, niche-targetable, cheapest to probe, needs a data-moat plan to survive Rule 4. The short-drama gap is the biggest prize on the board but the wrong size for the current team; the AI-dating and 20s-IRL-social categories are the two most obvious "hot = already lost" traps in this scan.
