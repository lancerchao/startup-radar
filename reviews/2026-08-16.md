## 1. AI/SaaS vendor shutdown-risk monitor for small operators

### Claims

| Claim | Source checked | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 9.3% of tracked AI tools (254/2,718) "shut down or been acquired" (ToolDirectory.ai census) | tooldirectory.ai (egress-blocked; search-indexed excerpts of the same census) | **CORRECTED — the number conflates death with acquisition.** The 254 includes acquisitions where the product keeps shipping (Topaz→Adobe, Modular→Qualcomm, Common Room→Zoom, Warmly→HubSpot). The census's own framing concedes "an acquired tool that keeps shipping is a materially different event." True shutdown rate is meaningfully below 9.3%, and the census is a directory's self-published count, not audited |
| 7–26 day notice windows: Clockwise 8d, Hiro 7d, Agency 26d; Relay.app a "fourth case" | daily.dev/byteiota (Clockwise), TechCrunch + intelligentliving (Hiro), Relay.app announcement coverage | **CORRECTED — mixed, and the newest cases cut both ways.** Clockwise verified: announced Mar 19, dead Mar 27, 8 days (with prorated refunds). Hiro: app stopped 7 days after announcement, but data export ran to May 13 (~30 days). Relay.app actually gave 30 days (free) / 60 days (paid) with prorated refunds, announced Jul 16 — it weakens the short-window claim rather than reinforcing it. New case found this pass: **Productiv — itself a SaaS management platform — announced Aug 2, sunset Aug 6, 4 days, no reason given**: the shortest window yet, from the exact product category that would ship this idea as a feature |
| No direct proactive competitor exists | Whistic, Vanta, Craft.co, Codekeeper, vendorwatch.app, stack.watch, DIY guides (Xact IT) | **VERIFIED narrowly, but every adjacent tier is occupied.** Nothing personalized-and-proactive found — confirmed. But: Whistic shipped native continuous vendor monitoring Mar 2026 and Vanta ships always-on vendor monitoring (enterprise); Craft.co sells financial-health supplier alerts (enterprise); Codekeeper sells the continuity *outcome* at $99–549/mo flat, including a dedicated "AI Escrow" tier, to 3,500+ orgs; vendorwatch.app owns outage alerting; and the free end is a documented DIY pattern — "10 vendors in 20 minutes, $0" via Google Alerts on vendor name + "layoffs/acquisition/shutdown" |
| (Implicit, load-bearing) Public risk signals precede shutdowns, so pre-shutdown alerting is deliverable | The idea's own motivating cases | **REFUTED.** Clockwise, Hiro, and Agency were acquihires — secret until the acquirer announced. Agency had just raised $32M from Sequoia/Menlo/Felicis; any signal-based model (funding, hiring, changelog cadence) would have scored it *healthy*. Productiv announced with no stated reason. Even platform-owned products (OpenAI's Sora app, Atlas browser) died without public warning. Disclosure lag can also run the other direction — coverage of the OpenAI/NextSlide acquihire describes customers learning months after the team left (beri.net, egress-blocked, search-indexed) |

### Strongest argument against

The product's sellable promise is "know before it happens." In the failure mode that defines the 2026 wave — the acquihire — there is no public pre-announcement signal to monitor: the deal is confidential until the day it's announced, and the healthiest-looking vendors (fresh $32M round) are the likeliest targets, since acquirers buy teams, not distressed assets. So the monitor's first possible alert fires at announcement time — exactly when a $0 Google Alert fires. What's actually deliverable is (a) fast relay of public announcements, a free commodity, or (b) continuity insurance, which Codekeeper already sells at $99–549/mo and enterprise TPRM sells above it. The remaining kernel — "nag me to keep data exports current" — is a feature of a backup product, not a company.

### Verdict: KILL

Single decisive reason: the core promise (alert *before* the shutdown) is structurally undeliverable for precisely the acquihire failure mode that motivates the idea — no public signal precedes a confidential deal — and the deliverable remainder (post-announcement alerting) is free via Google Alerts. The already-flagged distribution mismatch (content/SEO to founder communities vs. Lance's Meta-ads strength) and the census's inflated headline rate (acquired-but-still-shipping counted as "dead") close any residual case. The daily agent's instinct was right that no one ships the personalized proactive version; the reason no one ships it is that it can't work.
