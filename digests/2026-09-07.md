## Ideas

Two candidates deep-dived this cycle, both rejected — the thirty-fourth straight cycle with zero survivors. Both had genuinely fresh, dated triggers (a national policy rollout entering its active implementation window; ordinary but real annual state-law churn), and both died the same way this ledger keeps finding: a well-funded response — one philanthropic/federal, one commercial — already exists at the exact price point (free, or a $29/yr direct clone) the target buyer would actually pay.

### 1. Medicaid/SNAP work-requirement navigation & documentation assistant for beneficiaries — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A consumer app/subscription helping Medicaid and SNAP recipients track qualifying work/volunteer/school hours, understand exemptions, assemble documentation, and get reminders before reporting deadlines — positioned to prevent the kind of erroneous coverage terminations seen in past state pilots, as 44 states roll out federal work requirements through January 1, 2027.
- **Risks:** The buyer is, by program definition, a low-income individual — the segment least able to pay for a subscription tool, and the segment Meta's ad policy most explicitly restricts precise targeting of.
- **Consumer pain (quantified):** Severe and proven, not hypothetical. Arkansas's 2018 pilot (the only prior real-world test) stripped 18,164 people of coverage in six months — and a state audit found 97% of those removed were actually compliant or exempt, meaning the failure was reporting/documentation friction, not actual non-work. Nationally, CBO projects 2.9–5.3 million people lose Medicaid by 2034 from the new requirement, CBPP puts 9.9–14.9 million "at risk," and CBPP separately warns that if Arkansas-level attrition repeats nationally, ~7 million could lose coverage from paperwork failure alone, independent of actual eligibility.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Genuinely fresh and actively unfolding — Nebraska went live May 2026, Montana July 2026, Iowa goes live this December, and the federally mandated enrollee outreach-notice deadline (Aug 31, 2026) passed one week before this digest.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do, at scale, for free, and specifically funded to do so. Congress appropriated $200M to CMS plus another $200M split across states in FY26 implementation funding. CMS built its own "Emmy" (Eligibility Made Easy) consent-based income-verification tool. Code for America — a nonprofit, not a startup — published open-source service blueprints and an implementation toolkit specifically for SNAP/Medicaid work-requirement reporting, working directly with states. Several states already run purpose-built reporting portals (Wisconsin's MyACCESS; the multi-state "OSCER" case-management tool with a configurable exemption-screening rules engine).
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it is, at the systemic level:** The tooling gap Arkansas exposed in 2018 (confusing portals, no plain-language exemption logic) is the exact gap this cycle's federal money, CMS's own tool, and Code for America's toolkit are all being built to close before the January 2027 deadline — not an opening for a third party, but a problem already being resourced by the parties with the data and the mandate.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** More states go live and inherit the same federal tools/funding; the CBPP-warned failure mode (compliant people losing coverage on paperwork technicalities) may still occur at scale despite this investment, but the response to it will be political/legal (lawsuits, waiver fights — as already happened in Arkansas, where the requirement was enjoined by a federal court in 2019) rather than a commercial product gap opening.
- **Competitors:** CMS "Emmy" tool, Code for America (SNAP/Medicaid implementation toolkit, service blueprints), OSCER case-management/reporting tool, state-specific portals (Wisconsin MyACCESS and equivalents), plus every legal-aid and Medicaid-navigator nonprofit already funded to do 1:1 enrollee assistance.
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** No viable self-serve motion. The eligible population is capped at ≤138% of the federal poverty level by definition — the same income band Meta's ad standards explicitly restrict from precise targeting (Financial Products Special Ad Category rules bar income/financial-behavior targeting, and Meta's discriminatory-practices policy bars implying "vulnerable financial status"). A B2B pivot (selling to hospitals/FQHCs worried about uncompensated care from coverage loss) is enterprise healthcare procurement — a poor fit regardless.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Effectively zero on a direct-to-consumer model — the target buyer cannot be precisely reached on Meta and has, by eligibility definition, limited ability to pay; free federal/nonprofit alternatives already exist for the exact workflow.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** An MVP reporting-reminder app ships in weeks, but it competes against tools that are federally funded, plugged directly into state eligibility systems (data the tool would need but couldn't access), and staffed by legal-aid caseworkers for the cases that actually matter (appeals, wrongful terminations) — a thin app can't replicate the part of the workflow that actually saves someone's coverage.
- **Sources:** [KFF — Medicaid work requirements tracker](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/medicaid-work-requirements-tracker-overview/), [CHCS — federal work requirements summary](https://www.chcs.org/resource/a-summary-of-national-medicaid-work-requirements/), [Arkansas Advocates for Children and Families — 18,000 lost coverage](https://www.aradvocates.org/publications/losing-coverage-state-medicaid-work-reporting-requirements-stripped-18000-arkansans-of-health-insurance-in-2018/), [CBPP — coverage-loss estimates by state](https://www.cbpp.org/research/health/medicaid-work-requirements-will-take-away-coverage-from-millions-state-and), [Health Affairs — Arkansas two-year impacts](https://www.healthaffairs.org/doi/10.1377/hlthaff.2020.00538), [Code for America — blueprinting Medicaid work requirements](https://codeforamerica.org/news/blueprinting-medicaid-work-requirements/), [CBPP — Medicaid work requirement vendor landscape](https://www.cbpp.org/research/health/assessing-the-medicaid-work-requirement-vendor-landscape), [Meta Transparency Center — financial products & services](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/financial-services/).
- **Kill reason:** Doubly fatal, independent of each other: the exact reporting/documentation failure mode this idea targets is already the specific problem CMS's own tool and a federally-engaged nonprofit (Code for America) are funded to solve ahead of the same deadline, and the buyer population is simultaneously unable to pay (capped at 138% FPL by eligibility rule) and unreachable via precise Meta targeting (Financial Products Special Ad Category + vulnerable-financial-status restrictions).
- **B2C_RULES gate check:** Fails Rule 1 outright (no viable precise Meta targeting for a means-tested population) — not scored further per the ledger's own standard.

### 2. Legal-compliance-change alert subscription for small landlords (1-10 units) — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A paid subscription that monitors a landlord's state (and ideally city) rental-law changes and emails a plain-language summary of what changed and what to update in leases/policies — pitched at small, often out-of-area landlords for whom, per trade commentary, "staying current is a part-time job."
- **Risks:** The exact product already exists, at a price point that leaves no room to compete on price, from a competitor with a head start on the underlying legal-monitoring data pipeline.
- **Consumer/buyer pain (quantified):** Real and recurring — 2026 alone brought California AB628 (mandatory working fridge/stove), AB1414 (tenant opt-out of bundled internet fees), AB2747 (credit-reporting mandate for 16+-unit landlords), New York's new algorithmic rent-setting-tool restrictions, and Washington's 9.683% statutory rent-increase cap, on top of the perennial patchwork of city/county rules. Trade content explicitly frames small, especially out-of-area, landlords as unable to track this without help.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Weak as a "why now" — this is the same annual legislative churn every prior year has produced; nothing about 2026's volume or mechanism is structurally new.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do. TurboTenant, Avail, RentRedi, and Rentec Direct all already bundle "regularly updated by legal professionals" state-specific lease templates as a standard (often free-tier) feature of the broader landlord tools this buyer already uses for rent collection and screening.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it is, by a direct clone at the exact price point:** Tenby's "Landlord Law Watch" is the identical product already live: it monitors state codes and legislative sessions weekly, emails what changed and what to update in a lease, and prices at $29/yr per state — precisely the lightweight, cheap, standalone positioning this idea would need to differentiate on.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** More states pass more landlord-facing laws (the trend is monotonically up), which grows the existing competitor's total addressable market right alongside any new entrant's, with no reason a new player displaces the incumbent already indexed on this exact workflow.
- **Competitors:** Tenby (Landlord Law Watch, $29/yr/state, direct clone), TurboTenant, Avail, RentRedi, Rentec Direct, Buildium/AppFolio (enterprise tier), plus state/local landlord associations (e.g., California Apartment Association) that already publish free compliance-update content to their membership base.
- **Who pays / sales motion:** Self-serve, checkout-compatible in principle — but it's the identical motion Tenby already runs at a price low enough ($29/yr) that undercutting it further leaves no margin, and beating it on content quality requires the same 50-state legal-monitoring pipeline as the incumbent, not a weeks-buildable differentiator.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Thin. Small landlords are a real but modest-ARPU segment already served at commodity pricing; the rent-collection platforms' free bundled compliance content sets a $0 price anchor for the casual landlord, while the serious multi-property landlord is already inside Buildium/AppFolio's compliance-adjacent feature set.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** An alert-email MVP ships in weeks, but sustaining accuracy requires an ongoing weekly state/city legislative-monitoring operation — real, non-trivial content-ops overhead for a solo founder, running in perpetuity against a competitor who has already built and is already running that exact pipeline.
- **Sources:** [Tenby — Landlord Law Watch](https://tenbyapp.com/tools/law-watch), [Property Management Pleasanton — 2026 California landlord law guide](https://www.propertymanagementpleasanton.com/california-landlord-law-resources/the-2026-california-landlord-law-update-a-practical-guide-for-rental-owners), [SJA Property Management — Washington 2026 rental law guide](https://propertymanagersseattle.com/washington-rental-law-compliance-guide/), [WNY Lawyers — NY 2026 landlord compliance update](https://www.wny-lawyers.com/2025/12/new-york-landlord-2026-legal-compliance-update/), [TurboTenant — property management features](https://www.turbotenant.com/features/), [Mesa Properties — 2026 CA rental law compliance guide](https://www.mesaproperties.net/blog/2026-rental-law-updates-and-compliance-guide-for-california-landlords).
- **Kill reason:** A named, live, identically-scoped direct competitor (Tenby's Landlord Law Watch) already runs this exact mechanic at a price ($29/yr/state) that leaves no room to win on price, while the core compliance content is separately given away free as a bundled feature inside every major landlord platform this buyer already pays for — the same "real pain, already-served-free-or-cheap" pattern that has now killed every compliance-alert idea this ledger has tested.
- **Not scored against B2C_RULES** — buyer is a small-business/landlord operator, not a personal consumer.

**Bottom line:** Thirty-fourth straight cycle, zero survivors. Both candidates this cycle had unusually strong, well-documented "why now" triggers — one backed by a real historical failure case (Arkansas's 18,164 wrongful-seeming coverage losses) and hard national projections, the other by routine but real annual law changes — and both still died to this ledger's most durable pattern: whoever has the motive and the data to solve a real problem cheaply usually already has, whether that responder is a federally-funded nonprofit (Code for America, CMS's own tooling) or a $29/yr commercial direct clone (Tenby). The Medicaid candidate is a sharper variant worth remembering: even severe, well-quantified, politically salient consumer pain is not automatically a startup opportunity when the affected population is structurally incapable of paying and structurally excluded from precise paid-acquisition targeting — the two conditions this ledger's own B2C_RULES Rule 1 exists to catch.
