## Ideas

Three candidates deep-dived this cycle, all rejected — the fourteenth straight cycle with zero survivors. Two looked like genuinely fresh tech-driven B2B wedges (a brand-new browser standard, a brand-new Anthropic feature) and one was a quantified B2C pain point; all three collapsed on the same underlying failure mode this project keeps re-finding — either the market is already saturated by faster movers, or the "market" doesn't exist yet because the trigger is too new for real demand to have formed.

### 1. WebMCP "agent-readiness" tooling for websites — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** An audit/checker tool (early-Lighthouse-style) or hosted implementation service that helps website owners expose structured tools to AI browser agents via the new WebMCP (`navigator.modelContext`) standard.
- **Risks:** Spec is a W3C Community Group draft, not standards-track, with no cross-vendor consensus requirement; the core API entry point has already been renamed twice in under a year (`window.agent` → `navigator.modelContext` → `document.modelContext`); Safari and Firefox have made no implementation commitment.
- **Pain (quantified):** Real-world adoption is measured at approximately 0% of live sites as of an independent July 2026 audit; no mainstream agent (Claude, ChatGPT Agent, Perplexity, Gemini) calls `modelContext` tools in production yet. There is currently no pain to solve, only a speculative future one.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** The standard didn't exist 2 years ago (proposed Aug 2025), but the "why now" cuts against the idea as much as for it — by every adoption-curve estimate found, this is 6-18 months too early, not on-time.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do, and moved fast. Google shipped a free WebMCP-compliance audit inside Lighthouse/PageSpeed Insights ("Agentic Browsing" category) within about 3 months of the Chrome 149 origin trial opening — the standard's own vendor already ships the exact audit tool this idea proposes to sell. Cloudflare shipped a free developer-preview edge feature (Aug 2026) that auto-injects a WebMCP bridge via a dashboard toggle, no code required.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it already is, at the free tier:** At least 6 free checker tools/browser extensions are live (webmcp-checker.com, webmcpvalidator.com, webmcpworld.com, webmcpchecker.com, and others), one openly run by an SEO content-farm operator already using it as consulting lead-gen. A populated `awesome-webmcp` GitHub list exists within 3 months of the origin trial opening.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** Chrome is projected to ship WebMCP on-by-default in late 2026, with "meaningful adoption" not expected until 2027; Safari/Firefox positions (both opened only ~3 months ago, unresolved) will likely resolve one way or the other by then, and the spec will either stabilize into a Working Group or stall.
- **Competitors:** webmcp-checker.com, webmcpvalidator.com, webmcpworld.com, webmcpchecker.com, snezzi.com, adaptmarketing.com (free checkers); Conscriba, webmcpify, MCP-B/WebMCP-org, GoogleChromeLabs/webmcp-tools (implementation, free/OSS); Cloudflare "Agent Readiness" (edge auto-injection, free); Google Lighthouse/PageSpeed Insights Agentic Browsing audit (free, vendor-native); Semrush already publishing explainer content, a leading indicator of a future incumbent feature.
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** Unclear — the only paid activity found is ad hoc "done-for-you implementation" consulting bolted onto free checkers by marketing agencies, not recurring SaaS. No self-serve or founder-led motion has demonstrated willingness to pay yet.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** No comparable pricing exists for any WebMCP-specific paid product — nothing to benchmark against. Ceiling reads low while adoption sits near 0% and free tools already dominate every distribution channel (SEO, GitHub, dev communities) a solo founder would use.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** Days for a checker, weeks for a hosted implementation service — build speed isn't the constraint, market timing is. Ongoing maintenance load is real: the spec has already changed its core API twice in under a year, so anything built against it needs constant rework.
- **Sources:** [W3C WebMCP CG spec](https://webmachinelearning.github.io/webmcp/), [awesome-webmcp](https://github.com/webmachinelearning/awesome-webmcp), [WebKit standards-positions #670](https://github.com/WebKit/standards-positions/issues/670), [Mozilla standards-positions #1412](https://github.com/mozilla/standards-positions/issues/1412), [Chrome 149 origin trial](https://ppc.land/chrome-149-origin-trial-puts-webmcp-in-developers-hands-at-last/), [State of WebMCP, July 2026 — ~0% adoption](https://www.spronta.com/blog/state-of-webmcp-july-2026/), [Cloudflare WebMCP](https://blog.cloudflare.com/webmcp/) via [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/08/cloudflare-webmcp/), [Chrome Lighthouse Agentic Browsing scoring](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/lighthouse/agentic-browsing/scoring), [TechCrunch — OpenAI Atlas shutdown](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/09/openai-is-shutting-down-atlas-but-its-ai-browser-ambitions-are-still-growing/).
- **Kill reason:** The spec is a two-browser-engine (Chromium-only) Community Group draft that's already renamed its core API twice in under a year, real-world adoption is measured at ~0%, and — decisively — both the standard's own vendor (Google, via Lighthouse) and a major infra incumbent (Cloudflare) already shipped free versions of the exact audit and implementation tooling this idea proposes, within about 3 months of the origin trial opening, faster than a solo founder could plausibly move.
- **Not scored against B2C_RULES** — B2B idea, buyer is a website owner/developer, not a personal consumer.

### 2. Clean paid alternative to Cozi (family shared-calendar app) — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** An ad-free, no-paywall-tricks, paid shared-calendar app for parents of school/sports-age kids, positioned directly against Cozi's 2024 paywall backlash (free tier cut to a 30-day event-visibility window; Trustpilot average reportedly 2.1 stars, "bait and switch" complaints).
- **Risks:** The exact positioning ("clean alternative to Cozi's paywall") is already the explicit, stated pitch of 6+ live competitors; the two closest direct entrants give the comparable-or-better feature set away free, capping any price a challenger could charge; Google Calendar and Apple Family Sharing already provide the free ambient substitute most families already have.
- **Consumer pain (quantified):** Real — Cozi's free tier was cut to a 30-day calendar view in May 2024, pushing users toward a $39-80/yr paid tier; Trustpilot average corroborated at 2.1 stars via multiple secondary sources (direct fetch of trustpilot.com was blocked by network egress this cycle — flagging for a human spot-check before relying on it further).
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Weak — the triggering event is over two years old (May 2024) and the market has already fully absorbed and monetized it via at least six competing entrants; no fresh 2026 platform shift specific to this vertical was found.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They effectively already do. Maple (VC-backed, $5M+ from Inspired Capital, Goodwater Capital, G9 Ventures, Able Partners) gives the full feature set away free. Sense (getsense.ai) markets directly at Cozi switchers via a `/from-cozi` landing page with a free-forever tier plus AI email/photo parsing — a feature set a solo non-AI entrant would need months to match. Google Calendar and Apple Family Sharing cover the baseline for free.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it already is:** Searching "best Cozi alternative 2026" surfaces 6+ direct competitors (Maple, Sense, Calendara, Nori, Homsy, Nestify, SameNest) with dedicated comparison-content SEO campaigns already live and indexed.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** Meta's detailed targeting keeps eroding toward Advantage+ auto-audiences — parental-status/life-event targeting is now a "suggestion," not a precise filter, as of the Jan 2026 sweep, making the Meta-ads funnel Lance's playbook depends on progressively less precise for this exact audience.
- **B2C gate check (all 9):** Rule 1 (Meta-targetable) — pass, weakly; parenting-stage/life-event categories still exist but precision is degrading post-Jan-2026. Rule 2 (renewing LTV + inflow) — **FAIL**; ~4M US births/year gives real inflow, but Sense and Maple both give the core product away free, capping willingness to pay. Rule 3 (moat) — **FAIL**; no network effects or proprietary data, and free AI-differentiated competitors already out-feature a plain paid entrant. Rule 4 (not thin wrapper) — pass on paper, but cuts against the idea: non-AI is now a feature gap, not a focus, since 2026 entrants lead with AI email/photo parsing. Rule 5 (forced-multiplayer referral) — **FAIL/unverified**; an ACM study (277 survey, 40 interviews, 30-couple intervention) found uneven adoption and friction are the typical real-world outcome, not automatic viral lock-in — forced-multiplayer is a documented churn risk here, not a proven growth lever. Rule 6 (niche) — **FAIL**; "parents of school/sports-age kids" is a broad, well-trodden mass-market category. Rule 7 (reputationally clean) — pass. Rule 8 (tellable story) — **FAIL**; no non-obvious distribution insight, just competent execution of an already-crowded playbook. Rule 9 (over-mined shape) — **FAIL, hard**; "clean paid alternative to an incumbent with a paywall-backlash" is itself the over-mined shape here, independently of the family-calendar vertical, with 6+ named entrants already racing it. **At least 5 of 9 gates fail outright.**
- **Competitors:** Cozi ($0/$39-80/yr), Maple (free, VC-backed), Sense/getsense.ai (free-forever + AI), Calendara ($2.99/mo, AI photo-to-event), Nori (AI family hub + hardware), FamilyWall ($4.99/mo), Skylight Calendar (hardware, $150-630 + $79/yr), TimeTree (free, 60M users, $4.49/mo ad-free), Google Calendar (free), Apple Family Sharing (free).
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** Unclear who would pay when the two nearest direct competitors are free and more feature-rich; no differentiated paid tier identified.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Not confidently modelable given free-competitor pressure on every axis; realistic read is CAC high relative to an achievable price point once free AI-featured alternatives appear in every comparison search, LTV:CAC likely unfavorable, ARR ceiling low without a moat.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** Fast to build technically (weeks) — which is exactly why 6+ others already did it; ongoing load is an SEO/content arms race against incumbents already running the identical "Cozi alternative" content playbook.
- **Sources:** [Cozi Review 2026 — Calendara](https://www.usecalendara.com/blog/cozi-review-2026), [Sense — from Cozi](https://getsense.ai/from-cozi), [Trustpilot — Cozi](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/cozi.com) (secondary-sourced), [Meta detailed targeting 2026 — Jon Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/meta-ads-targeting-2026/), [Maple — Crunchbase](https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/maple-93be), [ACM "Double Incomes, Single Calendar" study](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3757584), [Best Cozi Alternatives — Sense](https://getsense.ai/blog/posts/best-cozi-alternatives-2025), [FamilyWall pricing 2026](https://www.usecalendara.com/blog/familywall-pricing-2026).
- **Kill reason:** At least six live competitors already occupy the exact "clean paid alternative to Cozi's paywall" positioning, one VC-backed at $5M+ and giving the product away free, another built explicitly around AI-parsing features a solo entrant would need months to match — and the idea independently fails 5 of the 9 B2C gates (renewing LTV, moat, niche-vs-broad, tellable story, over-mined shape), not one close call on a single axis.

### 3. Fleet-management/guardrail layer for self-hosted Claude Code runners — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A third-party dashboard/policy layer for companies running Anthropic's newly-launched self-hosted Claude Code runner fleets (public beta, ~Aug 13, 2026) — cross-fleet health, cost/chargeback, org-wide security-policy templates, audit-log rollup, and incident rollback, built on top of the Prometheus metrics and lifecycle hooks Anthropic already exposes.
- **Risks:** The feature is 5-11 days old and off-by-default; near-zero current buyer count; the buyer persona (platform engineers already running Terraform/K8s/Prometheus for other CI fleets) is exactly the group equipped to DIY this from Anthropic's own published alert-rule YAML; the closest mature real-world analog (self-hosted GitHub Actions runner fleet SaaS) just lost two funded players to thin margins.
- **Pain (quantified):** Real but affects a vanishingly small population right now — plausibly low tens of teams globally, gated by Team/Enterprise plan + org-admin opt-in + a genuine network-isolation/compliance need + engineering bandwidth to stand up runners. No adoption numbers published by Anthropic.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Genuinely can't have existed two years ago — the feature launched within the last two weeks, the strongest "why now" of any idea checked this cycle. The flip side: it may also be 6-12 months too early for a market to exist yet.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** Verified empty as of Aug 18, 2026 — LangSmith, Braintrust, Helicone, and Arize have no self-hosted-Claude-Code-runner support. But these are natural, well-capitalized bolt-on candidates the moment a handful of enterprise customers ask, not a technical moat, just a timing head start of unknown length.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — because it's 5-11 days old, not because of unmet demand:** No purpose-built competitor found anywhere (GitHub, Product Hunt, Show HN) — the risk here isn't saturation, it's market non-existence.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** Anthropic shipped ~10 point releases with new self-hosted-runner flags/behavior in under two weeks post-launch, and already ships Prometheus metrics, identity/JWT primitives, a hardening checklist, and an interactive "doctor" debugging agent on day one — a native fleet dashboard on the existing admin page reads as a plausible 6-12 month Anthropic ship, not a 2-year one.
- **Competitors:** None purpose-built today (verified empty). Adjacent bolt-on candidates: LangSmith, Braintrust, Helicone, Arize. Cautionary analog: BuildJet and Cirrus CI (self-hosted GitHub Actions runner fleet management, a market orders of magnitude larger and more mature) both shut down in H1 2026 on thin margins.
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** Founder-led B2B only, no self-serve motion realistic given the buyer count and the security-sensitive nature of the sale (a new vendor touching git credentials and runner secrets). Buyer: platform/DevOps or security leads at Claude Code Team/Enterprise orgs that have also enabled the self-hosted-environments beta.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** High CAC relative to an addressable pool plausibly under 100 companies industry-wide today; realistic pricing would be a flat per-org platform fee ($500-3,000/mo) given tiny seat counts per deployment. ARR ceiling low-single-digit-millions at best in year one, hard-capped by how few companies have both a self-hosted deployment and appetite for a point solution over DIY Grafana.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** Technically fast, 4-8 weeks for a v1 wrapping the existing metrics/hooks in a UI — the bottleneck is finding design partners in a near-empty market, not build time. Ongoing maintenance load is heavier than typical SaaS: the runner API/flag/metric surface already changed across 10 point releases in under two weeks and will keep moving on Anthropic's schedule, not the startup's.
- **Sources:** [Anthropic — self-hosted environments](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/self-hosted-environments), [deploy docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/self-hosted-environments-deploy), [configuration docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/self-hosted-environments-configuration), [Anthropic — permission modes](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/permission-modes), [MLQ.ai — auto mode default](https://mlq.ai/news/anthropic-will-make-claude-codes-auto-mode-the-default-for-pro-max-and-team-users/), [9to5Mac — auto mode PSA](https://9to5mac.com/2026/08/14/psa-claude-code-enabling-auto-mode-as-default-next-week-anthropic-says/), [Northflank — BuildJet/Cirrus CI shutdowns](https://northflank.com/blog/github-pricing-change-self-hosted-alternatives-github-actions).
- **Kill reason:** The pain is real and unusually well-documented straight from Anthropic's own docs, and the "why now" is as clean as this project has seen (feature is under two weeks old) — but the addressable buyer population today is essentially zero, Anthropic shipped the hard 80% of the solution (metrics, identity tokens, hardening docs, a debugging agent) on day one and is iterating every 1-2 days, and the closest mature analog in a vastly larger market (self-hosted CI runner fleet SaaS) just lost two funded players to thin margins — building the same category for a market 1/1000th the size isn't "early," it's premature.

**Bottom line:** Fourteenth straight cycle, zero survivors. Two of this cycle's candidates were about as fresh as this project's four-lens scan can produce — a browser standard mid-origin-trial, an Anthropic feature under two weeks old — and both still lost to faster-moving incumbents or a market that doesn't exist yet, respectively; the third (family calendar) was a quantified pain point that turned out to already have six competitors racing the identical positioning since 2024. A pattern worth naming explicitly: "new tech capability just shipped" is not automatically less contested than "regulation just changed" — in both WebMCP and the self-hosted-runner case, either the standard's own vendor (Google, Anthropic) or an adjacent infra incumbent (Cloudflare) moved to fill the gap within days to weeks, faster than a solo founder's research-to-ship cycle.
