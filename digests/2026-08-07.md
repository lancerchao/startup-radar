## Ideas

Three candidates deep-dived this cycle, all rejected. The pattern rhymes across all three: each looked like an open wedge from a breadth scan and turned out, on a real competitive check, to already have a funded or scaled incumbent sitting exactly on top of it — one of them (subscription cancellation) is the consumer-facing mirror of a B2B compliance-tool shape this project already killed once.

### 1. AI Overview traffic-loss diagnostic for small publishers — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** Self-serve SaaS for small independent publishers/content sites — measures organic search traffic lost to Google AI Overviews and AI Mode, tracks citation vs. blue-link presence, alerts on changes, positioned against this cycle's Top-Stories-inside-AIO rollout.
- **Risks:** Enters a category with 6+ funded or bootstrapped competitors already live at every price tier, including one (NewzDash) already publisher-specific; the core data source (AI Overview impressions) is simultaneously being made free by Google itself.
- **Business pain (quantified):** Real. Ahrefs: 58% CTR reduction on AIO-affected top results (Feb 2026), up from 34.5% a year prior. Pew: 8% CTR with an AI Overview present vs. 15% without. Chartbeat/Reuters Institute: small publishers (1K-10K daily pageviews) lost 60% of search referral traffic over two years, vs. 47% mid-size and 22% large — the steepest hit lands on exactly this idea's target customer. AI-chatbot referral traffic (ChatGPT etc.) is still <1% of total pageviews despite 200%+ growth.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Google's Top-Stories-inside-AIO mechanism only began June 2026, and CTR damage has intensified (34.5%→58% in under a year) — the pain trend is genuinely fresh. But the tool category responding to it is not: Otterly (2023), Profound and Scrunch (2024-2025) predate this window by 1-2 years.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** Google is structurally conflicted (won't quantify its own traffic diversion for publishers) but is shipping the adjacent transparency Google Search Console now has a free native "AI performance" report (Jun 3, 2026) plus a UK opt-out pilot — undercutting exactly the data layer this idea would sell. Ahrefs and Semrush already ship AI-visibility tracking (Brand Radar, AI Toolkit) bundled into suites publishers may already own.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it is:** Enterprise brand GEO is owned by Profound ($150M+ raised, unicorn) and the newly-Sitecore-owned Scrunch ($225M acquisition, Jun 2026). Mid-market self-serve is dominated by Peec AI ($29M+ raised, reportedly at a $200M valuation, $10M ARR in 16 months, ~1,300+ customers). The budget/solo tier this idea targets ($19-30/mo) already has Otterly.ai, Thruuu, Wincher, Dageno, AWR Cloud. The publisher-specific niche is already directly served by NewzDash (used by Forbes, targets sub-$10M-revenue publishers, cites the same 15.5%/17.5% Top-Stories stat used to justify this idea) and Visibly's "Publisher Mode."
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** Google Search Console's free AI reporting will likely deepen (click/query-level data), further commoditizing the core metric; consolidation continues (Scrunch→Sitecore is likely the first of several GEO acquisitions by MarTech/CMS incumbents).
- **Competitors:** Profound, Peec AI, Scrunch AI (acquired by Sitecore), AthenaHQ, Otterly.ai, Goodie AI, Rankscale, NewzDash, Visibly, Ahrefs Brand Radar, Semrush AI Toolkit, plus a long tail (Thruuu, Wincher, Dageno AI, AWR Cloud, QuickSEO, Am I Cited, GetMentioned, meev.ai).
- **Who pays:** Small publishers/content-site owners — but the only tier a solo founder could compete in (budget/self-serve, $19-30/mo) is the most crowded, lowest-margin, and going to the customers least able to afford recurring SaaS as their own ad/affiliate revenue shrinks. No founder-market fit either — Lance's growth channel is Meta ads to consumers; publisher/SEO acquisition runs through content marketing and SEO-community word of mouth, a completely different motion he hasn't run before.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / LTV:CAC / ARR ceiling:** Not modeled — the exact niche and price tier is already occupied by named, funded or scaled competitors; modeling unit economics against a market this saturated would be speculative.
- **Time to first sellable version:** Technically 2-4 weeks for a solo builder (scheduled SERP scraping, AIO/citation detection, dashboard, email alerts) — speed was never the constraint here.
- **Operational load:** Ongoing SERP-scraping infrastructure is a standing liability (Google changes AIO markup, anti-bot arms race, multi-engine coverage multiplies fragility) that funded competitors already absorb with engineering teams a solo founder doesn't have.
- **Sources:** [Search Engine Journal — small publisher traffic down 60%](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/search-referral-traffic-down-60-for-small-publishers-data-shows/569959/), [Medianama — Ahrefs 58% CTR study](https://www.medianama.com/2026/02/223-google-ai-overviews-click-through-rates-58-study/), [Axios — Chartbeat AI chatbot referral share](https://www.axios.com/2026/03/17/chartbeat-search-traffic-ai-chatbots), [Press Gazette — Top Stories inside AI Overviews](https://pressgazette.co.uk/platforms/google-adds-top-stories-links-to-many-news-related-ai-summaries/), [TechCrunch — Google Search opt-out regulation](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/03/publishers-will-be-able-to-opt-out-of-ai-search-thanks-to-new-regulation/), [PikaSEO — Google Search Console AI performance reports](https://pikaseo.com/articles/google-search-console-ai-performance-reports-2026), [TechCrunch — Peec AI $10M ARR](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/23/peec-one-of-berlins-rising-startups-more-than-doubled-annualized-revenue-in-months-to-10m-sources-say/), [Adweek — Scrunch acquired by Sitecore](https://www.adweek.com/media/sitecore-snaps-up-geo-startup-scrunch-for-225m/), [NewzDash — AI visibility for news publishers](https://www.newzdash.com/guide/ai-visibility-tracking-news-publishers-3-layers-matter), [Otterly.ai pricing](https://otterly.ai/pricing).
- **Kill reason:** Every layer of this idea — SERP/AIO scraping, citation tracking, multi-engine coverage, alerting, even the publisher-specific framing and the sub-$30/mo self-serve tier — already has a funded or bootstrapped incumbent live today, including one (NewzDash) targeting the identical publisher niche with the identical seed statistic, while Google itself is simultaneously giving the core data away for free.

### 2. Subscription/gym-membership cancellation concierge — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** Consumer app/service: forward a hard-to-cancel recurring commitment (gym, streaming, meal kit), the company cancels it on your behalf with a completion guarantee — positioned against this week's viral Blink Fitness "prove you moved" cancellation story and the FTC's ongoing LA Fitness/Esporta suit.
- **Risks:** Direct, scaled, trusted incumbent already owns this exact feature; a venture-funded pure-play clone already launched and folded within its funding life; a near-identical $5 clone already exists.
- **Consumer pain (quantified):** Real and fresh — the Blink Fitness Reddit post hit ~65,000 upvotes; FTC's Aug 2025 suit against Fitness International LLC covers 600+ locations and 3.7M members; 81M Americans hold a gym membership (26.1% penetration, $45-46B industry); the average American carries 12+ active subscriptions and 42% forget at least one recurring charge. The FTC's broader click-to-cancel rule remains vacated (8th Circuit, Jul 2025), so this isn't being fixed by regulation near-term.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Timing tailwind is real (fresh viral proof, ongoing FTC suit, no regulatory fix incoming) but not a structural "why now" — the underlying pain and the tools built against it both predate this window by years.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do. Rocket Money (formerly Truebill, acquired by Rocket Companies for ~$1.275B, Dec 2021) ships bank-linked "Cancel This For Me" as a built-in Premium feature and runs SEO content specifically for gym cancellations (LA Fitness, Gold's Gym, Crunch). DoNotPay markets the identical pitch across Planet Fitness, Gold's Gym, LA Fitness — though the FTC permanently fined and restricted it ($193K, Feb 2025) for deceptive "AI lawyer" claims, a direct reputational precedent for this idea's "guaranteed" framing.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it is:** Billshark ($9/cancellation flat fee, Mark Cuban-backed, $10M+ saved claimed since 2016) and Just Cancel ($5 one-time, AI bank-statement scan across 440+ services, direct cancel links) both already ship the exact mechanic at commodity pricing. Chargeback (VC-backed, "AI agent cancels subscriptions for you") launched with the identical pitch and was absorbed into a broader fintech (Flex) and is being sunset entirely by April 2027 — a prior funded attempt at this exact idea that didn't survive standalone.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** The FTC is reportedly working a narrower click-to-cancel rule via ANPRM — if it lands, it compresses this exact pain point through regulation right as a new entrant would be trying to scale into it.
- **Competitors:** Rocket Money (~$1.275B acquisition), DoNotPay (FTC-fined, still operating), Billshark, Just Cancel, Trim (now OneMain/Clearway-owned), Gains App, Gravity, ReSubs, Chargeback (folded into Flex, being sunset).
- **Who pays:** The consumer, per cancellation event — episodic, not a standing need.
- **Expected CAC:** Meta targeting is marginal at best — "actively fighting a cancellation" is a spiky, search-intent moment, not an interest Meta sells; the closest interests (Physical fitness, Exercise equipment, ~16M+) target people currently engaged with fitness, the opposite audience. Expect materially worse CAC than Lance's careerpicai funnel, where the purchase trigger is stable and cacheable.
- **LTV:** Capped near the market's proven one-time price ($5-9) unless the product becomes a recurring subscription-tracker — which is Rocket Money's actual business, at a trust/scale disadvantage no new entrant can close quickly.
- **LTV:CAC / ARR ceiling:** Not modeled — no path to the recurring economics this rubric wants without rebuilding Rocket Money from scratch.
- **Time to first sellable version:** Days to weeks — Just Cancel proves a solo-buildable $5 MVP already exists, which is itself evidence of low defensibility, not opportunity.
- **Operational load:** Higher than it looks — real cancellation execution means impersonating the customer to merchants, handling credentials/POA-like access, absorbing chargeback/fraud risk, and chasing merchant-specific friction that gyms deliberately design to defeat generic bots (the Blink "prove you moved" pattern is exactly this).
- **B2C gate check:** Fails Rule 2 outright — no genuine reason to keep paying past the first cancellation; every comp that tried to monetize this standalone either priced it as a one-time commodity ($5-9) or got absorbed into a broader recurring product (Chargeback→Flex). Also fails Rule 3 (Rocket Money already ships this at acquisition-proven scale, and a funded direct clone already launched and folded) and Rule 9 (this is the consumer-facing mirror of the "click-to-cancel compliance" B2B idea this project already rejected on 2026-08-03 — same underlying shape, re-skinned, now with more named competitors than the B2B version had). Not scored further once three gates fail.

- **Kill reason:** Cancellation is structurally a one-and-done event with no organic renewing-LTV mechanic, Rocket Money already ships the identical feature at ~$1.275B-acquisition scale, a venture-funded direct clone (Chargeback) already tried and folded, and a $5 near-identical clone (Just Cancel) is live today — this is a re-skin of the B2B "click-to-cancel compliance" idea this project rejected in early August, not a new wedge.

### 3. Laid-off tech-worker severance negotiation & rights kit — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** One-time-purchase digital kit for laid-off tech/knowledge workers — AI-assisted severance-offer review, negotiation letter/script generation, and know-your-rights guidance (WARN Act, COBRA, state unemployment filing) — positioned against this cycle's AI-attributed layoff wave.
- **Risks:** Fails the project's own Meta-targetability gate outright, on the same failure pattern ("recently divorced" isn't a real ad category) already established this project; a feature-identical incumbent already exists with public testimonials.
- **Consumer pain (quantified):** Real and verified — AI cited in 39.8% of May 2026 US job cuts, up from 7% in January (Challenger, Gray & Christmas); tech sector leads all industries at 149,023 cuts YTD through July (31% of the 2026 total). Severance is genuinely negotiable in narrow, documented ways (extra RSU vest, COBRA extension, extra weeks) — see the May 2026 Oracle severance-negotiation dispute.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** The AI-attribution share is genuinely rising month over month (7%→10%→25%→26%→40% across Jan-May), a real and current trend, not narrative-fitting after the fact.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do. SimpleSeverance.co ships the identical product — AI fairness score on severance offers, negotiation scripts, counter-offer builder, agreement-language simplification, $99 paid tier, public testimonials ("$25k more negotiated"), and a founder story built on the founders' own layoffs, the exact "tellable story" angle this idea would have leaned on.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it is, relative to its own small niche:** NegotiateSeverance.ai, Chatvisor.ai's Severance Negotiation Helper, NegotiateIt.ai, and Justee.ai (free AI severance-agreement review) all compete on the same pitch; CaseMark sells the equivalent B2B into employment law firms; human flat-fee alternatives run $700-3,000.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** Layoff volume may cool (July 2026 was Challenger's lowest monthly total in two years) or AI-driven cuts could keep compounding — neither outcome fixes the Meta-targeting problem or the crowded-competitor problem.
- **Competitors:** SimpleSeverance.co, NegotiateSeverance.ai, Chatvisor.ai, NegotiateIt.ai, Justee.ai (free), CaseMark (B2B), plus human flat-fee attorneys ($700-3,000).
- **Who pays / Expected CAC / LTV / LTV:CAC / ARR ceiling:** Moot — see kill reason; severance is a one-and-done event for an individual with no renewing mechanic, and the one channel this project's entire model depends on doesn't have the targeting category this idea needs.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** Technically trivial to build — which is exactly why 5+ direct competitors already exist; low defensibility, not an advantage.
- **B2C gate check:** Fails Rule 1 outright — Meta's documented Life Events taxonomy has "New job" (the opposite audience) but no "job loss," "laid off," or "severance" category; the closest available interests ("Application for Employment," "Employment Opportunities") are the generic job-search interests the rule explicitly excludes. This breaks the paid-acquisition channel Lance's entire operating model depends on. Independently would also fail Rule 3 (SimpleSeverance already owns the trust/testimonial moat with zero technical differentiation available to a new entrant) and Rule 9 (a textbook instance of the flagged "tool reacting to a recent market shift" shape — swap the trigger event for any other negotiable-life-moment and the same tool pattern appears). Not scored further once three gates fail.
