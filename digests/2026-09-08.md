## Ideas

Three candidates deep-dived this cycle, all rejected — the thirty-fifth straight cycle with zero survivors.

### 1. Business Hour Delivery Rate compliance dashboard for Amazon B2B sellers — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A monitoring/alerting tool for Amazon sellers to track their Business Hour Delivery Rate (BHDR) ahead of the September 30, 2026 90% threshold, flag at-risk SKUs/carriers, and recommend fulfillment or carrier changes to stay above the line.
- **Risks:** The entire premise (sellers can't see this metric coming) is false — Amazon already shows it to them, for free, inside the dashboard they already use daily.
- **Consumer/buyer pain (quantified):** Real and dated — starting Sept 30, 2026, sellers below 90% BHDR (rolling 14-day window) get a warning; those still below by Oct 30 lose FBM eligibility for Amazon Business customers (FBA/retail unaffected). Seller-forum threads show active anxiety about the metric.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Genuinely fresh — the requirement is brand new for this specific metric, three weeks from binding enforcement at digest time.
- **Why don't existing big players compete — they don't need to, Amazon already ships it free:** Amazon's own Account Health dashboard added BHDR to its "Eligibilities" section as a native, real-time metric before the deadline was even announced as enforceable — the exact monitoring layer this idea would sell is already the free thing sellers check today.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it is, at the tooling layer that matters:** 3PL/multichannel shipping platforms (ShipStation, Ordoro) are already publishing guidance and, per their own product categories, already support delivery-window-aware carrier/service selection — the fulfillment-side fix (not just visibility) is already inside tools this seller segment already uses for shipping, not a gap.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** More BHDR-style operational metrics likely get added to Account Health (Amazon has done this repeatedly — this is the same pattern as the SP-API/Account-Health expansions already logged on this ledger), each one arriving pre-instrumented by Amazon, not by a third party.
- **Competitors:** Amazon's own Account Health / Eligibilities dashboard (the metric itself), ShipStation, Ordoro, and other seller multichannel-shipping platforms covering the fulfillment-side fix.
- **Who pays / sales motion:** Would be self-serve SaaS to Amazon B2B sellers, but there's no wedge — the visibility Amazon gives away free is the entire value proposition of a "dashboard," and the actionable fix (carrier/route selection) already lives inside tools this seller already pays for.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Effectively zero standalone — nothing to charge for once the free-metric fact is accounted for.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** An MVP reading the SP-API and re-displaying BHDR could ship in days, but it would be re-skinning a number Amazon already shows sellers in the same login session, for free.
- **Sources:** [MyAmazonGuy — BHDR overview](https://myamazonguy.com/news/amazon-business-hour-delivery-rate/), [Ordoro — BHDR explainer](https://blog.ordoro.com/2026/08/12/amazon-business-hour-delivery-rate/), [Amazon Seller Forums — "Monitor your business hour delivery rate"](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/228197c9-185b-48c7-879a-c6fc01bb9cbd), [Amazon Seller Forums — BHDR now in Account Health](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/f5fe1fc8-c8bd-441d-94d4-2f1539c38451), [Shopifreaks](https://www.shopifreaks.com/amazon-will-penalize-sellers-who-dont-deliver-90-of-amazon-business-orders-during-buyers-business-hours-starting-september-30/).
- **Kill reason:** Amazon already surfaces the exact metric this idea would sell visibility into, natively and for free, inside the Account Health dashboard sellers already use — the same "platform already gives away the free monitoring layer" pattern that has now killed every Amazon-seller-compliance idea this ledger has tested (ad-spend guardrails, proceeds-deduction forecasting, synthetic-performer tagging).
- **Not scored against B2C_RULES** — B2B, buyer is an Amazon Business seller.

### 2. Household receipt & warranty tracker (Reddit r/BuyItForLife-sourced) — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A consumer app that pulls receipts from email/photos, tracks warranty and return-window expiration dates, and reminds the owner before a warranty lapses — the specific unmet need Reddit's r/BuyItForLife and r/frugal communities keep asking for, per this cycle's pain-mining pass.
- **Risks:** The "why is there no app for this" framing is stale — there are several apps for this, one of them (VaultlyKeep) already matching the exact feature list a Redditor would ask for.
- **Consumer pain (quantified):** Real but modest and not newly emerged — people keep manual spreadsheets of purchase dates and warranty expirations; no fresh 2026 trigger (regulatory, technological, or behavioral) makes this pain bigger or newer than it was in 2023.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** No case. This is an evergreen, not-getting-worse pain point; nothing dates this to 2026.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** Not applicable — this was never a big-tech-adjacent gap; it's a small-utility-app category that's been live for years.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it is:** At least seven named live consumer apps cover this today: VaultlyKeep (receipts, warranty/return-window reminders, insurance-claim reports, resale-provenance packs, shared household vaults — a near-exact match to the Reddit ask), Sortly (warranty fields + maintenance reminders, though priced for small-business inventory at $25–149/mo), Shoeboxed, MrReceipt, WarrantyVault, WarrantyKeep, and warrantytracker.cloud (free tier for 25 items, PRO tier with AI-OCR scanning and family sharing).
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** Nothing structural — this stays a small, permanently-contested utility-app niche with a free tier already at the price point a casual user would pay.
- **Competitors:** VaultlyKeep, Sortly, Shoeboxed, MrReceipt, WarrantyVault, WarrantyKeep, warrantytracker.cloud.
- **Who pays / sales motion:** Self-serve, but the direct clone (VaultlyKeep) already ships the fuller feature set (household sharing, insurance-claim reports, resale provenance) that would be this idea's differentiation.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Low — a free-tier competitor already exists at the exact "record 25 things and get reminders" use case that satisfies most casual users, capping willingness to pay for a new entrant.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** Fast to build (days–weeks for an MVP), which is exactly why seven-plus competitors already exist at every price point from free to business-SaaS.
- **Sources:** [VaultlyKeep — best receipt tracker apps](https://www.vaultlykeep.com/blog/best-receipt-tracker-apps), [Sortly pricing](https://www.sortly.com/pricing/), [Warranty Tracker](https://warrantytracker.cloud/).
- **Kill reason:** Rule 9 tarpit in consumer-utility-app form — "why is there no app for X" is the classic signal of a niche that already has several, and a direct feature-for-feature clone (VaultlyKeep) beat this idea to market with a fuller version of the exact pitch.
- **B2C_RULES gate check:** Fails Rule 1 outright — "people who keep receipts" is not a real Meta interest/behavior/life-event category, only a diffuse demographic-adjacent bucket; rejected without scoring the remaining eight gates, per the file's own instruction to stop at the first failure.

### 3. Loyalty-account takeover protection for frequent travelers — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A subscription monitoring service alerting frequent travelers to suspicious balance changes or logins across their airline miles, hotel points, and credit-card travel-rewards accounts — prompted by this week's Manchester Airports Group breach and the broader rise in credential-stuffing attacks on loyalty programs (~$21B in industry-wide loyalty fraud losses cited this cycle).
- **Risks:** The exact mechanic (balance-change alerting across many loyalty programs) is already a mainstream free feature of the category's dominant aggregator.
- **Consumer pain (quantified):** Real — credential-stuffing attacks let fraudsters change an account's contact email and drain miles/points within minutes, often before the owner is notified; industry-wide loyalty-fraud losses are cited around $21B.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Weak once verified — the Manchester breach itself, despite being this week's freshest trigger, leaked contact/parking data, not loyalty-account credentials, so it doesn't actually supply a "why now" for this specific angle; account-takeover-via-credential-stuffing has been a known, growing loyalty-fraud vector for several years already.
- **Why don't existing big players compete — they don't need to:** AwardWallet, the category's dominant free aggregator, already tracks 680+ loyalty programs in one dashboard and ships "Balance Watch" — free email/push alerts the instant a tracked account's balance changes, explicitly recommended by travel bloggers as the anti-theft mechanism for this exact problem.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it is, on both sides of the buyer:** Consumer side is AwardWallet's free feature; the harder, higher-value side (real-time fraud detection and blocking, not just after-the-fact balance alerts) is already sold direct to the airlines/hotels themselves by dedicated B2B vendors (Memcyco, Transmit Security) with fraud-graph visibility a consumer app could never replicate.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** More loyalty-program breaches (this is now a recurring "market signal," not a one-off), but each one reinforces AwardWallet's free monitoring value and the airlines' own B2B fraud-detection spend rather than opening room beneath either.
- **Competitors:** AwardWallet (free, 680+ programs, balance-change alerts), Memcyco, Transmit Security (B2B airline/hospitality fraud prevention).
- **Who pays / sales motion:** Would be self-serve, targeting "frequent international travelers" — a real, still-available Meta behavior-targeting category (B2C_RULES Rule 1 passes) — but with nothing left to charge for once AwardWallet's free tier is accounted for.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Low — the free incumbent already satisfies the exact use case (get notified when my balance changes) for the segment that would otherwise be this product's buyer.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** An MVP scraping/aggregating a handful of loyalty programs could ship in weeks, but matching AwardWallet's 680-program coverage (built over 15+ years) is a multi-year data-integration undertaking, not a weeks-buildable moat.
- **Sources:** [AwardWallet Balance Watch](https://awardwallet.com/features/awardwallet-balance-watch/), [SecurityWeek — Manchester Airports Group breach](https://www.securityweek.com/manchester-airports-group-data-on-8-8-million-people-leaked-after-ransom-refusal/), [Memcyco — airline loyalty account takeover prevention](https://www.memcyco.com/airline-loyalty-account-takeover-prevention/), [Finovox — airline miles fraud](https://www.finovox.com/en/articles/airline-miles-how-do-fraudsters-exploit-loyalty-accounts).
- **Kill reason:** Passes B2C_RULES Rule 1 (frequent-international-traveler is a real, still-available Meta targeting category) but fails Rule 3 outright — AwardWallet already gives away the exact differentiating mechanic (cross-program balance-change alerting) free at a scale (680+ programs, 15+ years of integrations) a new entrant can't cheaply replicate, and the higher-value fraud-detection layer is already sold B2B to the airlines directly. Independently, the breach that prompted the search doesn't actually supply loyalty credentials, weakening the "why now" on top of the moat failure.
- **B2C_RULES gate check:** Rule 1 passes; rejected on Rule 3 — not scored further per the file's own instruction.

**Bottom line:** Thirty-fifth straight cycle, zero survivors. All three candidates had real, dated hooks (a three-week-out Amazon compliance deadline, an evergreen but genuinely-asked-for Reddit pain point, and a days-old 8.8M-record breach), and all three died to variants of the same two patterns this ledger keeps finding: the platform or category leader already gives the differentiating mechanic away free inside a tool the buyer already uses (Amazon's own Account Health metric, AwardWallet's free balance alerts), or a direct feature-for-feature clone already exists (VaultlyKeep). Nothing scanned this cycle — across tech shifts, fresh regulation, funding signals, or pain-mining — surfaced a candidate where that pattern didn't hold.
