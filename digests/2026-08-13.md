## Ideas

Five candidates deep-dived this cycle, all killed — the ninth straight cycle with zero survivors, the most candidates checked in a single cycle so far. The first three (Amazon ad-billing, Toast fees, Printify/Printful) died fast enough that two backups (Kajabi/Teachable, Meta Ads MCP) were pulled in from the same day's scan. All five share the pattern that's now defined this project: a real, dated, quantified trigger, checked against a competitive field that turns out to already be occupied — twice this cycle by the platform that created the pain shipping the fix itself (Amazon's own lending partner Slope; Meta's own MCP governance rules).

### 1. Amazon ad-spend cash-flow forecasting tool for FBA sellers — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A cash-flow forecasting dashboard (not a lender) for Amazon FBA sellers, modeling payout timing under Amazon's new ad-spend "proceeds deduction" billing system so sellers can see cash gaps coming before they hit.
- **Risks:** Amazon's own official financing partner already markets directly against this exact event; multiple funded fintechs give away the forecasting layer for free as financing lead-gen; the "Pay by Invoice" opt-out blunts urgency further.
- **Consumer pain (quantified):** Real. Amazon moved Sponsored Ads billing from credit card to direct payout-balance deduction, effective Aug 1, 2026 (delayed from April 15 after a seller-organized boycott by the 800-member Million Dollar Sellers community). A community poll cited by multiple outlets: 80% of 7-figure sellers expect a 25%+ hit to available cash from the 30–60 days of lost card float.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Genuinely fresh — the delayed Aug 1 rollout is this month's news, with sellers getting individual Seller Central notices now.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do, aggressively. Slope — Amazon's own official embedded-lending partner (announced Dec 2025, "Line of Credit powered by Slope with Amazon Lending," up to $5M at 8.99% APR) — has published a dedicated blog post on this exact billing change. Wayflyer and Viably both run live, **free** Amazon cash-flow forecasting dashboards as financing lead-gen. SellersFi, Uncapped/Clearco, Payability, and 8fig are all named by industry coverage as the sellers' actual response to this specific gap.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it is:** Dozens of SEO articles on "amazon ad billing change 2026" were already live within days of the Aug 1 rollout, from Nova, SellerSprite, Melio, BellaVix, and others.
- **What will change in 2 years:** Nothing favorable — Amazon's lending ecosystem (Slope, Parafin) only gets more entrenched as the default response to seller cash-flow friction it itself created.
- **Competitors:** Slope (Amazon's own lending partner), Wayflyer, Viably, SellersFi, Uncapped/Clearco, Payability, 8fig, Parafin.
- **Who pays / Expected CAC / LTV / LTV:CAC / ARR ceiling:** A standalone forecasting-only product (the one form Lance could build without becoming a lender) has to compete against free tools from funded fintechs cross-subsidized by financing revenue — an unwinnable price position for a solo entrant with no lending arm.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** Technically fast (weeks) to build a forecasting dashboard against Seller Central APIs — irrelevant given the free-competitor wall.
- **Sources:** [CNBC — seller boycott](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/15/amazon-sellers-boycott-ads-payment-changes.html), [ModernRetail](https://www.modernretail.co/operations/a-group-of-seven-figure-amazon-sellers-is-planning-a-one-day-ad-boycott-as-payment-changes-squeeze-cash-flow/), [EcommerceBytes](https://www.ecommercebytes.com/2026/04/16/amazon-delays-change-that-would-contribute-to-seller-cashflow-crunch/), [Amazon Ads — advertiser payments update](https://advertising.amazon.com/library/news/update-on-advertiser-payments), [Wayflyer Amazon cash flow planner](https://wayflyer.com/en/amazon-cashflow-planning), [Viably](https://runviably.com/), [Slope — Amazon Lending](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251216444256), [Slope blog on this billing change](https://slopepay.com/blog/amazon-ad-billing-change-2026).
- **Kill reason:** Amazon's own embedded-lending partner and at least five other funded fintechs (Wayflyer, Viably, SellersFi, Uncapped, Payability) already ship free forecasting tools and/or dedicated content targeting this exact billing change — the same "sophisticated incumbents already structured for the disruptive-looking change" pattern that killed the 2026-08-04 BSA/FBA-financing idea, now confirmed directly rather than inferred.

### 2. Meta Ads MCP governance/agent tool for agencies and small advertisers — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A governance/permission and audit-trail layer on top of Meta's new first-party Ads MCP server for agencies delegating campaign work to AI agents across client accounts, and/or a packaged "AI agent manages your small business's Meta ad account" product for solo advertisers.
- **Risks:** Meta already shipped the core governance primitive this idea would differentiate on; the underlying MCP plumbing is free and open-source; 10+ named competitors already launched within the trigger's first 15 weeks.
- **Business pain:** Real, fresh platform capability — Meta's Ads MCP server (mcp.facebook.com/ads, 29 tools, read/write) went to open beta Apr 29, 2026, expanded with admin governance ("MCP server rules": budget-change limits, catalog-edit restrictions, approval steps) Jul 16, and folded into Marketing API v26.0 by early August.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Genuinely new — the MCP server and its governance layer are both 2026 releases.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do, on both angles. Meta itself shipped "MCP server rules" — the exact governance/approval-workflow primitive the agency angle would sell. On the packaged-agent angle, Madgicx ($79/mo autonomous budget optimization), Hyper (fully autonomous bid/budget agent), and AdCreative.ai already position as "AI manages your ad account."
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it is:** Named MCP-native entrants already shipping: Pipeboard (460+ GitHub stars, Slack approval workflows, audit trail, Meta Business Partner badge, $29/mo), AdKit ($29/mo, draft-first human approval, multi-platform), Ryze.ai (agency white-label program). Underlying plumbing is free/open-source (GoMarble, MIT-licensed, 225+ stars; several other open MCP servers). A swarm of ~15 SEO content sites were already racing for "Meta ads MCP" search traffic within 3.5 months of the trigger.
- **What will change in 2 years:** Nothing favorable — Meta has shown it extends governance primitives itself (the same absorption pattern seen with Apple/Google's age-verification APIs), and the free-OSS-plumbing baseline only deepens.
- **Competitors:** Pipeboard, AdKit, Ryze.ai, Madgicx, Hyper, AdCreative.ai, GoMarble (OSS), Revealbot (rule-based, an obvious next mover).
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** Mostly self-serve SaaS gated by managed ad spend ($5K/mo+ minimums) — a fit for Lance's skill set in principle, but that same low barrier to entry is exactly why 10+ competitors already exist within the trigger's first few months.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Not modeled in depth — a crowded, fast-follower-friendly niche with free OSS scaffolding caps differentiation before economics are worth testing.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** Weeks to build on the free MCP plumbing — the same speed that let 10+ others beat a new entrant to market already.
- **Sources:** [Meta for Business — Ads AI Connectors](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/meta-ads-ai-connectors), [Pipeboard (GitHub)](https://github.com/pipeboard-co/meta-ads-mcp), [AdKit](https://adkit.so/features/ads-mcp/meta), [Ryze Agency Program](https://www.get-ryze.ai/agency-program), [GoMarble Meta Ads MCP](https://www.gomarble.ai/ads/meta-ads-mcp), [Meta MCP tool comparison](https://claudefa.st/blog/tools/mcp-extensions/meta-ads-mcp-comparison).
- **Kill reason:** Structurally the same "platform shift → build the wrapper" shape already killed twice in this ledger (Ray-Ban glasses, agentic-commerce/AEO checker) — Meta shipped the governance layer itself, the plumbing is free/open-source, and at least 10 named competitors already launched within the same ~15-week window as the trigger.

### 3. Restaurant POS/Toast hidden-fee statement auditor — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A neutral, one-time-fee tool where a restaurant owner uploads their Toast (or other POS) processing statement and gets an automated audit flagging junk fees and rate creep vs. market benchmarks — explicitly not a processor switch or a subscription.
- **Risks:** The "free audit as switch-bait" model is a decades-old, standard ISO/broker tactic already run by many players; a flat one-time fee is worse on risk and completeness than the free/contingency alternatives already on the market.
- **Consumer pain (quantified):** Real, verified on primary sources. BBB: 171–235 Toast complaints in under 3 years, 1.31/5 rating, including a documented unauthorized subscription hike from $35 to $184 without consent. Trustpilot: 3.1–3.2/5 across ~1,400 reviews. Capterra: 92% of negative reviews cite high/unclear fees. A 2023 unauthorized $0.99 order-fee incident triggered a congressional inquiry — shows this is a pattern, not a one-off.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Weak on freshness — this is an escalating pattern, not a single dated 2026 event; the specific "350% search increase" stat traces to a single competitor's marketing blog and could not be independently verified.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do. Merchant Cost Consulting explicitly offers "negotiate lower Toast rates without switching processors" on a contingency (% of verified savings, $0 upfront) model — the exact no-switch positioning this idea would lead with, but with better economics for the buyer.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it is, on both the free and paid sides.** FeeQuery (AI instant statement analysis), Swipesum/Staitment (free audit + a licensable $75/report product, sold mostly to other ISOs), CardFellow (free lifetime monitoring bundled with brokerage), plus half a dozen more ISO-affiliated "free audit" lead-gen sites (Merchant Fee Savers, Verisave, Soltis, LexActum) — this is standard industry practice, not a gap.
- **What will change in 2 years:** Nothing favorable — the contingency/free-audit dynamic is structural to how payment processing sales works, not trend-driven.
- **Competitors:** FeeQuery, Swipesum/Staitment, Merchant Cost Consulting, CardFellow, Labrador AI (funded Toast competitor publishing switch-bait fee content), Merchant Fee Savers, Verisave, Soltis, LexActum.
- **Who pays / Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Weak — Swipesum's own choice to sell its audit software mostly to other ISOs/agents rather than direct-to-merchant signals the direct-to-restaurant-owner paid market is thin; buyers have zero-risk free and contingency alternatives one search away.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** Weeks to build (parse PDF, classify fees, benchmark) — but that's also true for the half-dozen incumbents who built it first and give it away.
- **Sources:** [BBB — Toast complaints](https://www.bbb.org/us/ma/boston/profile/point-of-sale-systems/toast-inc-0021-164973/complaints), [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/toasttab.com), [FeeQuery](https://feequery.net/merchant-statement-audit/), [Swipesum/Staitment](https://www.swipesum.com/audit), [Merchant Cost Consulting — Toast review](https://merchantcostconsulting.com/lower-credit-card-processing-fees/toast-review/), [CardFellow](https://www.cardfellow.com/blog/cardfellow-offers-free-lifetime-monitoring-to-ensure-lowest-rates/), [Labrador AI](https://www.labrador.ai/blog/toast-pos-fees-2026).
- **Kill reason:** "Free audit as switch-bait, or contingency negotiation, beats a flat one-time fee" is the dominant existing model, run by at least 8 named incumbents including a contingency player (Merchant Cost Consulting) offering the identical no-switch positioning at zero upfront risk to the buyer — a structurally worse offer than what's already on the market.

### 4. Kajabi/Teachable "de-SaaS" migration tool for course creators — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A migration service/toolkit helping course creators fed up with Kajabi/Teachable price hikes export their content and set up a lean, largely self-hosted or one-time-tool-based replacement, sold as a one-time service rather than a new subscription platform.
- **Risks:** Migration help is already free (platform-native importers) or a commoditized low-cost gig-economy service; switching to a cheaper hosted competitor beats self-hosting on the buyer's own stated motivation (escaping ongoing maintenance and fees).
- **Consumer pain:** Probably real (multiple secondary sources converge on consistent, specific numbers — Kajabi's Jan 13, 2026 price hike, Teachable's Starter transaction fee) but not independently primary-source-verified; several of the loudest "backlash" sources are competitors' own switch-bait content marketing.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Genuinely dated if the reporting holds — both price changes landed within the last 8 months.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do, as free acquisition bait. GoHighLevel ships a native "Kajabi Importer"; Systeme.io publishes a free migration offer explicitly targeting Kajabi/Kartra/Podia switchers; FG Funnels, Captivation Hub, and Mastermind all ship Kajabi course importers.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it is.** Dozens of Fiverr gigs already sell this exact service for $15–440 (some by self-described ex-Kajabi Implementation Specialists); agencies (GHL Scale Up, FunnelXperts) already productize "Kajabi → GoHighLevel migration" from ~$100.
- **What will change in 2 years:** Nothing favorable — the two viable business models here (free platform-vendor importer, or gig-labor migration) are both already occupied.
- **Competitors:** GoHighLevel (native importer), Systeme.io (free migration offer), FG Funnels, Captivation Hub, Mastermind, plus a live Fiverr/agency gig market ($15–440).
- **Who pays / Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Structurally weak even before competition: this is inherently one-and-done (bad LTV), and the only plausible recurring hook — an ongoing self-hosted-stack maintenance retainer — directly contradicts the customer's stated motivation of escaping recurring fees.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** The hard technical part (bulk video export, since neither platform offers an official export API) is already solved by free open-source tools (serpapps/kajabi-video-downloader and similar), capping how much anyone can charge for it.
- **Sources:** [Ruzuku — Kajabi 2026 pricing](https://www.ruzuku.com/blog), [group.app — Teachable changes](https://group.app/blog), [Systeme.io migration offer](https://systeme.io/), [GoHighLevel Kajabi Importer](https://www.gohighlevel.com/), [serpapps/kajabi-video-downloader (GitHub)](https://github.com/serpapps/kajabi-video-downloader).
- **Kill reason:** Migration help is already given away free by competing platforms as their own acquisition funnel and separately commoditized into a $15–440 Fiverr/agency gig market; independently, switching to a cheaper hosted platform (Podia, Systeme.io) is objectively easier and cheaper for the buyer than the proposed self-hosted alternative, undercutting the idea's own premise.

### 5. POD (Printify/Printful) silent-fulfillment-failure watchdog — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A lightweight monitoring tool connecting to a seller's Printify/Printful/Shopify/Etsy accounts to proactively alert on stalled orders, shipping-status anomalies, and defect-rate spikes before the customer complains.
- **Risks:** Both platforms already push the underlying failure events as free webhooks; a mature, funded horizontal category already owns the "alert before complaint" mechanic; a direct beta competitor already targets the identical buyer and integration set.
- **Consumer pain:** Real and current — sellers report orders marked "shipped" that never ship, valid addresses wrongly flagged and blocked (with Printify support telling one seller to "use another provider" instead of investigating), and 2026-dated quality complaints. A cited "~30% reprint rate" figure is a single anecdote, not an independently verified benchmark.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Weak — this is an ongoing operational pain, not a new dated trigger; Printify/Printful merged into "Fyul" in Jan 2025, which is the closest thing to a dated event and doesn't itself change the pain.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do. Printful's webhook API emits `Order failed`/`on hold`/`cancelled` events; Printify offers webhooks plus a daily digest email and a public Network Fulfillment Status page — the raw "silent failure" signal isn't actually hidden.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it is, twice over.** Wonderment (acquired by Loop Returns, "Track by Loop") does exactly this pitch generically — free to 500 shipments/mo, $99–349/mo paid. AfterShip covers the same ground, free to 50 shipments/mo. A live, in-beta direct competitor, PodVector AI, explicitly targets POD sellers across Shopify/Meta/Google/Printify/Printful with real-time anomaly flagging.
- **What will change in 2 years:** Nothing favorable — the true addressable buyer (multi-provider, high-enough-volume-to-pay sellers) is a sliver of the ~10M combined Printify/Printful account base (mostly dormant/hobbyist), and incumbent free tiers already cover most real sellers' actual order volume.
- **Competitors:** Wonderment/Track by Loop, AfterShip, PodVector AI (live beta, direct competitor).
- **Who pays / Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Weak — no evidence this buyer segment pays past free-tier defaults; true addressable population is much smaller than the oft-cited combined account figure implies.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** Fast to build (webhook listener + alerting UI) — but that speed is exactly why a live beta competitor already exists.
- **Sources:** [Printful API docs — webhooks](https://developers.printful.com/docs/v2-beta/), [Printify Network Fulfillment Status](https://printify.com/network-fulfillment-status/), [Wonderment](https://wonderment.com/), [Wonderment pricing](https://www.wonderment.com/pricing), [PodVector AI](https://podvector.ai/), [Shopify Community thread — address blocking](https://community.shopify.com/t/why-are-my-orders-auto-fulfilling-preventing-shipping-label-creation/137627).
- **Kill reason:** The underlying failure signals are already free/native to both platforms, a funded horizontal incumbent (Wonderment/Loop) already productizes the exact "alert before complaint" mechanic, and a live in-beta direct competitor (PodVector AI) already targets the identical buyer and integration set.

**Bottom line:** Ninth straight cycle, zero survivors — the most candidates checked in one cycle so far (5, after the first 3 died fast enough to justify pulling in 2 backups from the same day's scan). Two of five died to the sharpest version of this project's core recurring finding: the platform that created the pain already shipped the fix itself, for free, as fast or faster than a solo founder could respond (Amazon's own lending partner Slope on the ad-billing change; Meta's own "MCP server rules" on the agent-governance idea). The other three died to the more familiar wall — a mature, sometimes decades-old service category (payment-statement auditing, SaaS-migration gigs, order-tracking apps) already serving the exact niche, often for free as a loss-leader. Process note: for any idea responding to "platform X just shipped new capability Y," check whether X's own roadmap already includes the governance/monetization layer the idea would sell — both platform-shift kills this cycle (Amazon, Meta) had that answer sitting in the same week's news coverage as the trigger itself.
