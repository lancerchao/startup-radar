## Ideas

Two candidates deep-dived this cycle, both rejected — the forty-second straight cycle with zero survivors.

### 1. Distribution/growth copilot for vibe-coded indie apps — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A tool that automates the post-launch distribution work (directory submissions, AI-search/GEO tagging, SEO content, launch scheduling) for the wave of non-technical founders shipping working apps via Lovable, Bolt, Replit, v0, and Base44 — people who can now build in days but have no marketing muscle.
- **Risks:** The exact audience this idea needs to sell to is famous for having no budget and for being hostile to being sold to.
- **Consumer/buyer pain (quantified):** Real and structural — "the median indie hacker is not at $50K MRR, with approximately 50% at $0 to $1K MRR," and "more than half of shipped solo projects never get a single paying customer" (2026 indie-hacker retrospectives). One documented case: a founder sharing a genuinely useful tool in a relevant subreddit was met with reflexive hostility for "vibecoding trash" the moment AI-assisted origin was mentioned — the community itself actively punishes the self-promotion this idea would need to enable.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Partial case only — the *population* of non-technical shippers is new (vibe-coding market at $4.7B in 2026 per vendor estimates, Lovable and peers scaling fast), but the underlying pain — "building is easy, distribution is the bottleneck" — is the founding premise of Indie Hackers itself, a decade-old observation, not a fresh one.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** Lovable/Bolt/Replit deliberately stay out of marketing and distribution by design (their own docs and third-party guides note this explicitly) — but that just means the gap is open to *anyone*, not that it's open to a new entrant specifically.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it is:** A full generic-tool ecosystem already targets this exact buyer: SE Ranking repositioned an "Essential" plan at solo founders in 2025–2026 ($65/mo); 25+ Product-Hunt-alternative directories plus aggregators like makers.page already run the "submit everywhere" playbook; AI SEO/content writers and n8n-based DIY workflows cover the content side. No dedicated "growth copilot for vibe-coded apps" brand was found, but the functional need is fully absorbed by tools that don't care what built the app.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** More non-technical builders enter, but the same generic tools and directories absorb the growth — the buyer pool getting bigger doesn't create a seam if nothing about the *service* is differentiated.
- **Competitors:** SE Ranking (solo-founder tier), 25+ Product Hunt alternative directories, makers.page, generic AI SEO content generators, n8n-based automation, plus the entire IndieHackers/BuildInPublic content ecosystem already teaching this exact playbook for free.
- **Who pays / sales motion:** Self-serve SaaS to solo founders — but the buyer segment self-selects for having little to no marketing budget by definition of the problem.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Not meaningfully scoreable — CAC for reaching indie-hacker communities is itself expensive (ad-hostile, self-promotion-punishing), and LTV is capped by a buyer whose median business is at $0–1K MRR.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** An MVP wrapper (directory-submission automation + AI content drafts) could ship in days — which is exactly why the pieces already exist as separate, longer-established tools.
- **Sources:** [Airticler — indie founder SEO resources](https://www.airticler.com/resources/indie-founders), [Makers.page — where to list your indie product](https://makers.page/blog/where-to-list-your-indie-product), [devtoolpicks — best SEO tools for indie hackers 2026](https://devtoolpicks.com/blog/best-seo-tools-indie-hackers-2026), [Indie Hackers — why indie founders fail](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/why-indie-founders-fail-the-uncomfortable-truths-beyond-build-in-public-b51fd6509b), [Contrary Research — Lovable](https://research.contrary.com/company/lovable).
- **Kill reason:** The buyer's defining trait is "I can't get distribution" — a tool that itself has to reach that buyer through paid or organic channels runs into the identical bottleneck it's pitching a fix for, and every individual mechanism (directories, SEO content, launch scheduling) is already served by generic tools that don't need the "built with AI" hook to work.
- **Not scored against B2C_RULES** — B2B, buyer is a solo app builder.

### 2. VAMP chargeback-ratio tracker for small merchants — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A dashboard for small, self-managed e-commerce/subscription merchants (no dedicated fraud team) tracking their Visa/Mastercard dispute-and-fraud ratio against 2026's repeatedly-tightened VAMP thresholds, with early alerts before tripping the "excessive" tier and templated evidence responses inside the shortened dispute windows.
- **Risks:** The exact merchant tier that would pay for this already has it as a line item inside a tool it already uses.
- **Consumer/buyer pain (quantified):** Real and escalating — Visa cut the "excessive" threshold from 2.2% (pre-2026) to 1.5% (April 1, 2026) to 0.9% later in 2026; merchants in that tier face an $8 fine per flagged transaction (30 events ≈ $240 in network fines alone, before chargeback fees or reserve increases); a full chargeback averages ~$78 in processing cost; response windows have shrunk to as little as ~9 days.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Genuinely dated — the threshold tightened twice within 2026 alone and is still being actively re-cut mid-cycle.
- **Why don't existing big players compete — they already do:** Chargeflow (Shopify-native, success-based pricing at 25–40% of recovered disputes, $29/deflected-dispute alerts), Signifyd and Kount (subscription or % of GMV, enterprise-quoted), and FraudLabs Pro (budget-tier prevention screening) already span the exact small-to-large merchant range this idea targets.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it is, including on the content/SEO layer:** Payments-content site Beancount.io published two dedicated VAMP-ratio guides for small merchants within the past two months (July 25 and August 25, 2026) — the exact keyword and buyer this idea would need to rank for is already being actively raced.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** The threshold will likely keep tightening, but each cut becomes a same-day feature update inside tools that already track the ratio, not a new opening.
- **Competitors:** Chargeflow, Signifyd, Kount, FraudLabs Pro, RefundSentry, ChargeMate, Debutify's own 9-app roundup, Beancount.io (content/positioning).
- **Who pays / sales motion:** Would be self-serve SaaS to independent merchants, but success-fee incumbents (Chargeflow) already remove the price objection for the segment likely to convert.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Not meaningfully scoreable — no price or feature gap between the free-ish native processor tools and the paid incumbents for a new entrant to occupy.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** A ratio-tracking dashboard could ship in days, which is why five-plus incumbents already ship the fuller, automated version.
- **Sources:** [Beancount.io — VAMP is live](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/08/25/visa-vamp-chargeback-ratio-small-merchant-guide), [Beancount.io — VAMP threshold cut](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/07/10/visa-mastercard-vamp-chargeback-monitoring-2026-guide), [Chargeflow — best Shopify chargeback apps 2026](https://www.chargeflow.io/blog/best-chargeback-solutions-for-shopify-2026), [IntelliPay — VAMP guide](https://intellipay.com/what-every-merchant-needs-to-know-about-vamp-before-april-2026/).
- **Kill reason:** Extends this ledger's most over-mined shape ("a fee/regulation/rule just shifted → build a calculator or compliance tool") from government regulation and platform fees to card-network rules — same absorption pattern, same result: incumbents at every price tier already track this ratio, and at least one content operator is already actively racing the exact SEO keyword a new entrant would need.
- **Not scored against B2C_RULES** — B2B, buyer is a small merchant.

**Bottom line:** Forty-second straight cycle, zero survivors. Both candidates had genuinely current triggers (the vibe-coding population boom, Visa's mid-2026 VAMP re-tightening) but died the same two ways this ledger keeps finding: either the mechanism is already absorbed by a generic tool ecosystem that doesn't care about the specific trigger (directories/SEO tools for indie builders), or named incumbents already track the exact regulatory/network metric at every price tier (chargeback platforms for VAMP). The first candidate surfaces a sharper, more general point worth carrying forward: a product whose target buyer's core problem is "I can't get discovered" has an unusually hard time being sold to that buyer at all — the distribution bottleneck is symmetric.
