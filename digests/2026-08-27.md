## Ideas

Three candidates deep-dived this cycle, all rejected — the twenty-third straight cycle with zero survivors. All three had real, quantified triggers, and all three died the same way this run keeps finding: the exact wedge a solo founder would build is already occupied, in one case by the very platforms/vendors named in the trigger, in another by a decades-stable churn pattern that never produces mass switching, and in the third by a trigger that turned out to be a year older than it appeared.

### 1. AI-application-flood triage/verification tool for SMB recruiters — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A B2B triage/verification layer plugging into a recruiter's inbox or ATS that flags AI-mass-applied, duplicate, or fraudulent candidates, sold self-serve or founder-led to SMB employers without enterprise ATS budgets — positioned as the employer-side answer to LinkedIn's application flood (deliberately not a job-seeker-facing product, since that angle already failed B2C Rule 1's Meta life-event-targeting wall on 2026-08-07).
- **Risks:** Saturated at every layer simultaneously — platform-native, ATS-bundled, and dedicated point-solution vendors are all already shipping this.
- **Buyer pain (quantified):** Real. LinkedIn press coverage converges on ~11,000 applications/minute and a ~45% YoY surge (the precise "11,000/min = 45% YoY" framing appears to compress LinkedIn's own more granular multi-year submission-rate data — treat as media-shorthand, not a clean primary statistic). Recruiters report 1,200+ applications for single remote listings; some pull postings entirely. Vendor-claimed (TurboCheck, unaudited): 20-60% of applicant pools are fraudulent.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Genuinely current — candidate-side mass-apply bots matured through 2025-26, and LinkedIn shipping a second countermeasure (the Aug 2026 "underqualified applicant" warning, after an earlier application cap failed) is itself evidence the problem is still live at the platform level.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do. Ashby shipped native "Fraudulent Candidate Detection and Management" in 2026; Metaview ships automation/fraud scoring across 7+ ATS integrations (Greenhouse, Lever, SmartRecruiters, etc.); Manatal bundles duplicate-detection and AI fit-scoring at an explicitly SMB price ($15-55/user/mo).
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it is:** At least three dedicated point-solution vendors already sell this directly — TurboCheck (fraud/deepfake detection, claims 200K+ fake candidates caught, unaudited), Sardine.ai ($70M raised, extended its fintech fraud-intelligence product into hiring in May 2026), and, most damaging to this idea specifically, **Verif_Hire** — a $0-integration Chrome extension for instant candidate verification, explicitly positioned for small-business/solo recruiters, occupying the exact "cheap, no-integration, SMB" wedge this idea would need. The trigger source itself, HeroHunt.ai, is a vendor that has already published a "Top 10 Candidate Identity Verification Solutions" roundup — the space is mapped and being monetized, not undiscovered.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** Platform-native detection (LinkedIn, Indeed) likely commoditizes basic duplicate/bot signals further; expect Greenhouse/Lever/Workable to follow Ashby/Metaview into native fraud-flagging, squeezing standalone tools toward zero differentiation on the "basic triage" use case while funded incumbents (Sardine) push into higher-value niches like deepfake video-interview detection.
- **Competitors:** TurboCheck, Verif_Hire, Sardine.ai ($70M raised), Ashby (native feature), Metaview (native feature), Manatal ($15-55/user/mo), iProov (enterprise deepfake detection).
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** Self-serve is only plausible at Verif_Hire's price point — already taken. Anything requiring ATS integration needs founder-led sales into SMB HR/recruiting teams competing against features already bundled free into the ATS they already pay for.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Poor — core value is being given away bundled by Ashby/Metaview; a standalone tool has no legal/insurance backstop for the false-positive liability risk inherent to fraud-flagging, which real money (Sardine's $70M) is better positioned to absorb.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** A crude duplicate/pattern-match MVP is 2-4 weeks technically, but detection heuristics require continuous, adversarial updating as candidate-side AI improves — an ongoing arms race, not a build-once product — plus support-heavy false-positive dispute handling with real reputational stakes.
- **Sources:** [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/ai-job-applications-linkedin/), [GetCoAI](https://getcoai.com/news/ai-job-applications-flood-linkedin-with-11000-per-minute/), [HeroHunt.ai — application flood playbook](https://www.herohunt.ai/blog/surviving-the-ai-application-flood-2026-playbook/), [HeroHunt.ai — ID verification roundup](https://www.herohunt.ai/blog/candidate-identity-verification-hiring-2026/), [TurboCheck](https://turbocheck.com/homepage/), [Sardine.ai](https://www.sardine.ai/blog/job-application-fraud-detection), [Verif_Hire](https://verif-hire.com/), [Metaview](https://www.metaview.ai/application-review), [Ashby fraud detection — Skima review](https://skima.ai/blog/product-deep-dives/ashby-reviews), [Manatal pricing](https://www.pin.com/blog/manatal-pricing/).
- **Kill reason:** The pain is real and current, but it's simultaneously being monetized by the platform itself (LinkedIn's own countermeasures), by bundled ATS features at the exact SMB price point this idea targets (Ashby, Metaview, Manatal), and by at least three dedicated point-solution vendors — including one (Verif_Hire) already occupying the specific cheap, no-integration, solo-recruiter wedge that would be this idea's only defensible entry point.
- **Not scored against B2C_RULES** — B2B, buyer is the employer/recruiter, not a personal consumer.

### 2. QuickBooks Online migration tool (ride the Aug 2026 price hike) — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A self-serve tool automating QuickBooks Online → Xero/Wave/FreshBooks/Zoho migration (chart-of-accounts mapping, historical transaction import, reconciliation check), marketed to small-business owners and bookkeepers angry about Intuit's latest renewal price increase.
- **Risks:** Switching accounting systems is inherently high-friction (audit-trail continuity, bank-feed re-auth, payroll re-wiring, staff retraining) — the standing reason SMBs historically grumble and stay; a botched migration corrupts a business's books, a high-liability failure mode for an unproven solo vendor in a trust-dependent category.
- **Buyer pain (quantified):** Real but modest in absolute dollars. QBO Plus renewal: $115→$140/mo (+21.7%, +$300/yr). Advanced: $275→$340/mo (+23.6%, +$780/yr). Stacked with a new $2/employee/mo payroll fee from July 2026. This is Intuit's sixth consecutive annual price hike since 2021 (Advanced up ~83% over that span).
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Weak. The Aug 1, 2026 renewal wave is a real, fresh trigger event, but "Intuit raised prices again" has recurred annually since 2021 without ever producing a durable new-entrant migration-tool winner — the pattern itself argues against novelty.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do, aggressively. Xero ships **Jet Convert**, a fully automated, free, current-plus-prior-year migration tool, plus a free CSV Conversion Toolbox for accountants. Intuit itself partners with Dataswitcher to migrate data *into* QBO from competitors, covering up to two years of data at no cost — actively counter-recruiting even while raising its own prices.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it is:** Four layers of existing supply: free native tools (Xero Jet Convert, Dataswitcher), a commodity gig market (5+ active Fiverr sellers at $55-300/migration found live), dedicated commercial conversion services (Dancing Numbers, Certum Solutions, eCloud Experts), and SaaS import tools (SaasAnt from $25/mo). Worse, the SEO content wedge is already gone — Beancount.io has run an active, dedicated content campaign against this exact 2026 hike since April, alongside at least six other outlets (CentsIQ, Certum, Steph's Books, Fourlane, Workforce.com, Business2Community) publishing dedicated Aug 2026 coverage within the past two weeks.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** Likely another QBO price hike around Aug 2027 (the established pattern), continued Intuit ARPU growth outpacing subscriber losses per market commentary (indirect evidence, not disclosed churn data) — i.e., grumbling without mass churn — plus QBO Desktop's phased sunset forcing migrations that gig workers and incumbents already service.
- **Competitors:** Xero Jet Convert (free), Xero Conversion Toolbox (free), Intuit/Dataswitcher (free-to-low-cost, Intuit-sponsored), Dancing Numbers, SaasAnt Transactions ($25/mo+), Movebot ($390+, general-purpose), 5+ active Fiverr sellers ($55-300 flat), Beancount.io (content campaign + alternative product).
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** Nominally the small-business owner, but the platform-choice decision skews to the bookkeeper/accountant, who already has free vendor tools and $55-300 gig options on hand — a solo founder would need founder-led sales into bookkeeping firms for a deal size that barely clears gig-work pricing.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Poor. LTV is a one-time $50-300 transaction (matches the existing Fiverr price ceiling — can't charge much more against free native tools), not recurring; CAC via SEO is already high given the crowded, newsjacked content field.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** Technically buildable in weeks (CSV parsing + CoA mapping isn't novel engineering), but every migration is bespoke and support-heavy — dirty data, edge cases, multi-year history, payroll/inventory add-ons — with outsized liability risk for a solo operator with no accounting credential.
- **Sources:** [Fourlane](https://www.fourlane.com/quickbooks-online-pricing-changes-2026-new-rates-and-fourlane-discounts/), [Beancount.io — migrate before renewal](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/08/17/quickbooks-online-price-hike-2026-migrate-before-renewal-guide), [Beancount.io — switching guide](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/04/25/switching-from-quickbooks-migration-guide-small-business), [CentsIQ](https://centsiq.com/news/quickbooks-online-price-increase-august-2026/), [Certum Solutions](https://www.certumsolutions.com/library/quickbooks-price-increase-august-2026), [Xero — convert from QuickBooks](https://www.xero.com/us/accounting-software/convert-from-quickbooks/), [Intuit/Dataswitcher](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/dataswitcher/), [SaasAnt](https://www.saasant.com/quickbooks-migration/), [Dancing Numbers](https://www.dancingnumbers.com/quickbooks-data-conversion/).
- **Kill reason:** Every layer of this migration wedge — free vendor-native tools (including Intuit's own counter-recruiting partnership), commodity $55-300 gig work, dedicated commercial conversion services, and the SEO content play newsjacking this exact hike — is already occupied by incumbents who moved faster than a new entrant could ship, and Intuit's well-precedented annual price hikes have never historically produced the mass-churn event this idea needs to fund a business, because switching accounting systems carries real audit-trail and workflow risk that keeps SMBs grumbling in place.
- **Not scored against B2C_RULES** — B2B, buyer is a small-business owner/bookkeeper, not a personal consumer.

### 3. OAuth/non-human-identity (NHI) token governance for SMBs — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A self-serve tool for small companies without a security team to discover, audit, and revoke third-party app OAuth grants into Salesforce, Google Workspace, and Slack — the exact attack surface exploited in the Salesloft/Drift breach — priced and packaged for SMB self-serve buying instead of enterprise security-team sales.
- **Risks:** The trigger itself is a year stale; the specific "self-serve, SMB-priced, no enterprise sales" wedge is already occupied by at least two funded, shipping competitors; buyer trust in a first-time security vendor is a slower barrier to clear than sales motion.
- **Buyer pain (quantified):** Real but the trigger date is wrong. Multiple primary sources (SocRadar, Anomali, Obsidian Security, The Register) confirm the actual Salesloft/Drift intrusion ran Aug 9-17, **2025** (not 2026), with Salesloft revoking tokens Aug 20, 2025, and Mandiant's investigation concluding Sept 30, 2025 — 700+ orgs affected (Cloudflare, Google, PagerDuty, Palo Alto Networks confirmed), now generating 15+ class-action filings, but with no fresh 2026 technical development found. The underlying OAuth/NHI risk category is real; this specific incident is a year-old, fully-metabolized news event still circulating in 2026 SEO content as if current.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Weak once corrected — no fresh 2026 catalyst found; the broader NHI-awareness trend (Okta 2023, Snowflake 2024, Salesloft/Drift 2025) has already had 1-3 years to attract funded competitors.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** Platform-native free tooling already covers the basics — Google Workspace admin console, Microsoft Entra ID app governance, and Salesforce's own Connected App OAuth usage reports give baseline visibility at no extra cost. Above that, enterprise NHI vendors (Astrix Security $85M raised, Oasis Security $195M raised, Entro Security $24M, Token Security $27M, Clutch Security $28.5M) skip SMB because enterprise sales economics don't support sub-$50K deals.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it isn't empty, it's thinly but already contested:** **Nudge Security** ($22.5M raised) already ships a directly self-serve product — free trial, no credit card, no sales call — explicitly targeting "lean security teams," the closest analog to this idea and already funded and shipping. **Grip Security** ($76M raised, legacy CASB vendor) backed into an explicit SMB tier at $2/user/month covering OAuth/auth insights and offboarding. A third, lower-confidence entrant (8200.dev, sourced from a single unverified aggregator) reportedly offers a permanent free tier for smaller companies.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** Every funded vendor above is already repositioning toward AI-agent/MCP-server identity governance as the next wave (Oasis's $120M round pitched explicitly around this; Token Security shipped an MCP-server NHI product) — incumbents extending upmarket-funded platforms into the adjacent space faster than a new entrant could build baseline OAuth coverage, plus reported acquisition interest (Cisco reportedly eyeing Astrix) suggesting consolidation ahead.
- **Competitors:** Astrix Security ($85M), Oasis Security ($195M), Entro Security ($24M), Token Security ($27M), Clutch Security ($28.5M), Valence Security ($32M, Microsoft M12-backed), Savvy Security ($30M), Nudge Security ($22.5M, self-serve), Grip Security ($76M, SMB tier at $2/user/mo).
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** A self-serve motion is directionally compatible with Lance's Meta-ads/consumer-funnel skill set — sales-motion mismatch is not the primary blocker here, unusually. The real mismatch is security credibility: buyers of any tool connecting OAuth scopes into their Salesforce/Google/Slack admin consoles expect visible security posture (SOC 2 readiness, responsible-disclosure process) before they'll grant access, a trust bar a solo generalist web developer starts well behind funded, security-pedigreed competitors on.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Poor. Incumbent self-serve pricing already anchors near-free (Nudge's free trial, Grip's $2/user/month ≈ $480-1,200/yr for a 20-50 person company); a new entrant prices against zero. Plausible ACV $300-1,200/yr against likely CAC of $500-2,000/logo (security buyers are skeptical of paid social, favor slow content/trust-building) — marginal LTV:CAC at best.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** Realistically 2-4 months even for a 2-platform MVP (Google Workspace + Slack), not weeks — each platform's admin API has its own auth model and breaking-change cadence, and a bad auto-revoke can break a customer's production integration, a serious liability risk for an unproven vendor. Ongoing: must track multiple vendor APIs indefinitely and absorb reputational risk if a customer is breached on the tool's watch — the same failure mode the pitch itself cites as the trigger.
- **Sources:** [SocRadar — Salesloft Drift breach recap](https://socradar.io/blog/salesloft-drift-breach-everything-you-need-to-know/), [Obsidian Security — UNC6395](https://www.obsidiansecurity.com/blog/unc6395-salesloft), [The Register — Salesforce class actions, Sept 2025](https://www.theregister.com/2025/09/26/salesforce_class_actions/), [Astrix Security — Series B](https://astrix.security/learn/news/astrix-raises-45m-series-b-to-redefine-identity-security-for-the-ai-era/), [Oasis Security — $120M Series B](https://www.accessnewswire.com/newsroom/en/computers-technology-and-internet/oasis-security-raises-120m-series-b-to-secure-the-rise-of-enterpr-1149255), [Nudge Security pricing](https://www.nudgesecurity.com/pricing), [Nudge Security — Series A](https://www.nudgesecurity.com/press/nudge-security-raises-22-5m-series-a-to-secure-workforce-ai-and-saas), [Grip Security — Series B, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/22/grip-security-raises-41m-to-help-enterprises-manage-their-saas-identity-risk).
- **Kill reason:** The trigger is a year-old, fully-litigated breach with no fresh 2026 hook, and the specific wedge this idea targets — self-serve, SMB-priced OAuth/NHI governance without an enterprise sales motion — is already contested by at least two funded, shipping competitors (Nudge Security, Grip Security's SMB tier) pricing near-free, against a buyer-trust barrier that a first-time security vendor can't clear quickly regardless of sales-motion fit.
- **Not scored against B2C_RULES** — B2B, buyer is a company's IT/security function, not a personal consumer.

**Bottom line:** Twenty-third straight cycle, zero survivors. All three candidates this cycle had real, quantified pain and looked distinct from the ledger's most over-mined shapes at first glance (no regulatory-compliance-checker, no senior-focused Meta-ad-wall collision, no thin AI wrapper) — yet all three still died to the recurring core pattern: the party best positioned to close the gap is already closing it, whether that's the platform itself (LinkedIn), the incumbent doing active counter-recruiting (Intuit/Xero), or funded specialists who got there years earlier (Nudge, Grip). One process finding worth carrying forward: verify a "fresh" trigger's actual date against a primary source before spending research time on it — this cycle's third candidate looked current in aggregator coverage but was a year stale on inspection.
