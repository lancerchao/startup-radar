## Ideas

Two candidates deep-dived this cycle, both rejected — the twenty-fifth straight cycle with zero survivors. Both had real, fresh, quantified triggers; both died to a now-familiar pattern — a well-resourced party (a philanthropic consortium in one case, a dedicated point vendor plus the report's own publisher in the other) already closed the exact gap a solo founder would need.

### 1. Take It Down Act compliance-in-a-box for small UGC platforms — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A hosted, zero-DevOps SaaS wrapping StopNCII/HMA hash-matching, a removal-request intake portal, and a 48-hour SLA/audit-log dashboard, sold to small-to-mid UGC platforms (dating apps, photo/video communities, niche forums, gaming platforms) that are legally "covered platforms" under the Take It Down Act but too small to build or self-host their own trust-and-safety stack.
- **Risks:** This is a compliance/liability-adjacent product — a mishandled or delayed report exposes the *customer* to real FTC penalties and exposes the *vendor* to reputational fallout and possible indemnification demands. It also competes directly against a free tool built by a coalition of the largest AI/platform companies specifically to serve this buyer.
- **Consumer pain (quantified):** FTC enforcement began May 19, 2026, and is visibly active: warning letters to 12 "nudify" tool operators and reminder letters to 15 major platforms (Alphabet, Amazon, Apple, Meta, Discord, Match Group, Microsoft, Pinterest, Reddit, SmugMug, Snap, TikTok, X, Automattic, Bumble). Violations carry civil penalties up to $53,088 each. Legal commentary (Orrick, ReedSmith, Wiley) converges on smaller platforms specifically struggling with the "reasonable efforts to remove duplicates" (hash-matching) requirement.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** The law passed May 2025, but the FTC's enforcement teeth are brand new and still ramping — the warning letters cited above are from the past 3.5 months, and legal advisories are still actively urging companies to get compliant.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do, from two directions. Cinder ($41M raised) serves the largest UGC platforms (OpenAI, Spotify, ElevenLabs, Midjourney, Synthesia) — enterprise, not small-platform, pricing and sales motion. Below that tier, ROOST — a nonprofit funded with $27M by Google, OpenAI, Discord, and Roblox explicitly to give under-resourced platforms free trust-and-safety infrastructure — shipped Coop v0 in February 2026: free, open-source, self-hosted software doing hash-matching (via Meta's own HMA), a review queue, and NCMEC reporting, timed roughly three months ahead of the FTC deadline.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet:** It isn't quite empty — ROOST's tool requires real self-hosting/DevOps effort a solo-operator platform may not want, which is technically an opening for a "we host Coop for you" managed layer. But this is the same "regulation just changed → build a compliance tool" shape that has failed on this ledger every time it's been tested (electricians, drone pilots, notaries, tariffs, insurance, EU AI Act disclosure, CRA reporting), and here the free alternative isn't an underfunded hobby project — it's deliberately built and timed by a consortium of the exact companies that would be best positioned to clone or fund a hosted version themselves.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** ROOST's roadmap shows active post-v0 development (a technical design committee, FOSDEM 2026 talks) — the likelier outcome is ROOST or a partner ships a hosted/managed tier itself, closing the remaining self-hosting-friction gap rather than leaving it open for a new entrant.
- **Competitors:** ROOST/Coop (free, self-hosted, OSS, Google/OpenAI/Discord/Roblox-backed), Cinder ($41M raised, enterprise-tier), StopNCII.org (free hash registry), generic moderation APIs (Hive, Moderation API, OpenAI/Google moderation endpoints) that don't handle the NCII-specific workflow or audit trail.
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** Nominally self-serve (a $50–200/mo SaaS pitched at small dating/photo-app operators), but any product touching user intimate-image reports needs a security/compliance trust bar before an operator grants access — a slow, relationship-driven sale, not a Meta-ads consumer funnel.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** LTV is plausible (a genuine recurring compliance need), but ROOST's free, self-hostable alternative anchors price expectations at $0 for any operator willing to run it themselves, capping achievable price and, with it, ARR against a niche buyer pool of platforms that are neither tiny (no UGC, no exposure) nor large (already served by Cinder-tier vendors).
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** A thin wrapper around StopNCII's API plus a ticket/SLA tracker is buildable in weeks, but shipping it "thin" is exactly the liability problem — a defensible audit trail that would hold up to an FTC inquiry is realistically months of work, then ongoing exposure to real legal/reputational risk if a customer is fined despite using the tool.
- **Sources:** [FTC — warning letters, May 2026](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/05/ftc-sends-warning-letters-companies-about-compliance-take-it-down-act), [The Record — 12 firms warned](https://therecord.media/ftc-warns-12-firms-of-violating-take-it-down-act), [ROOST — Meet Coop v0](https://roost.tools/blog/meet-coop-v-0/), [ROOST — Coop 1.0 announcement](https://roost.tools/blog/coop-1-0-world-s-first-free-open-source-child-safety-infrastructure-for-every-platform), [Cinder — customers/funding](https://cinder.ai/), [CDT — NCII hash-matching governance brief](https://cdt.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/2026-04-08-CDT-NCII-Hash-matching-brief-2.pdf), [ReedSmith — covered platforms](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102ms5e/covered-platforms-are-you-ready-to-take-it-down/).
- **Kill reason:** The party best positioned to close this exact gap for small platforms — ROOST, a nonprofit funded by Google, OpenAI, Discord, and Roblox specifically to serve under-resourced operators — already shipped a free, open-source, purpose-built tool three months before the compliance deadline hit. Same "new regulation → build a compliance tool" shape that has died on this ledger every time it's been tested, this time closed off by coordinated industry philanthropy rather than a lone incumbent.

### 2. AI-citation / GEO tool for independent wedding vendors — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A generative-engine-optimization (GEO) tool built specifically for independent wedding vendors (photographers, florists, planners, small venues) to get cited when couples ask ChatGPT/Perplexity/Google AI Overview for vendor recommendations — addressing The Knot/Zola/WeddingWire's near-total lock on AI-generated wedding answers.
- **Risks:** Identical shape to the publisher-focused GEO tool already rejected on this ledger (2026-08-07), just relabeled for a new vertical; a dedicated competitor and the triggering report's own publisher already sell into this exact niche.
- **Consumer pain (quantified):** 5W's Wedding Industry AI Visibility Index 2026 (65+ engaged-couple prompts across ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini, Google AI Overviews) found The Knot/Zola/WeddingWire capture ~73% of wedding-planning AI answers, and ~84% of individual vendors have effectively zero AI citation share. Zola's First Look Report separately puts AI use among engaged couples at 54%, up ~150% YoY.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Genuinely fresh — both reports are 2026-dated, and the underlying behavior shift (AI-assisted wedding planning roughly doubling in a year) is new, not recycled.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They're the ones winning by default. The Knot Worldwide's decades of indexed content, structured data, and brand mentions are exactly what LLMs preferentially cite, and it owns both nominally "competing" cited brands (The Knot and WeddingWire) — there's no incumbent to disrupt, only a moat to climb.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it isn't:** Wedy Pro AI already markets itself directly against this pain ("Wedding Vendor SEO in the Age of AI"); dedicated how-to-rank-on-ChatGPT guides for wedding vendors are already a content genre (Brian Lawrence, Belman & Co, Boda Bliss); and 5W — the report's own publisher — runs a paid GEO practice and is visibly using its "Visibility Index" as a lead-gen hook for that exact service.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** More agencies will copy 5W's playbook (publish a scary "you're invisible" stat, sell the fix), intensifying a content/tooling arms race that a solo entrant is late to rather than early into; the core tactics (schema markup, consistent name/address/phone data, FAQ content) will keep getting commoditized into free guides faster than a paid tool can differentiate.
- **Competitors:** Wedy Pro AI, 5W (agency GEO practice), the previously-rejected publisher-GEO field (Peec AI, Scrunch AI/Sitecore, Profound), plus a growing wave of SEO-agency content specifically targeting wedding vendors (Brian Lawrence, Belman & Co, Boda Bliss).
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** Self-serve SaaS is plausible — wedding vendors already pay for Knot/Zola/WeddingWire placement — but CAC is likely high against a fragmented, low-marketing-sophistication buyer (solo photographers/florists) who is also the target of an already-crowded content field competing for the same attention.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Modest. Vendor spend in this category runs to low hundreds of dollars a year based on comparable directory/ad placements; ARR ceiling is capped by a fragmented buyer pool already being told (for free, in blog form) to just fix their own schema markup and Google Business Profile.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** Weeks — it's the same content-optimization-checker shape already built for publishers; that ease of build is exactly why a vertical-specific clone (Wedy Pro AI) already exists. Ongoing: AI answer-engine citation behavior shifts frequently, requiring continuous content/schema maintenance — the same treadmill that undercut the publisher-GEO idea's appeal.
- **Sources:** [5W — Wedding Industry AI Visibility Index 2026](https://www.5wpr.com/research/wedding-industry-ai-visibility-index-2026/wedding-industry-ai-visibility-index-2026.pdf), [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/73-of-wedding-planning-ai-answers-now-route-to-just-two-platforms--and-84-of-individual-wedding-vendors-have-zero-ai-citation-share-new-5w-report-finds-302766400.html), [Zola First Look Report 2026](https://www.zola.com/expert-advice/the-first-look-report-2026), [Wedy Pro AI](https://www.wedypro.ai/blog/wedding-vendor-seo-ai-discovery-2026), [Brian Lawrence — ranking on ChatGPT](https://www.brianlawrence.com/how-to-rank-on-chatgpt-what-wedding-vendors-need-to-know-about-ai/).
- **Kill reason:** This is the same over-mined GEO/AI-visibility shape rejected on 2026-08-07 for publishers, now relabeled for a new vertical — a dedicated vendor-specific competitor (Wedy Pro AI) and the triggering report's own publisher (5W, selling GEO services off the back of it) already occupy the wedge, and the underlying fix is widely-published DIY advice, not a defensible product.
- **Not scored against B2C_RULES** — B2B, buyer is the wedding-vendor small business, not a personal consumer.

**Bottom line:** Twenty-fifth straight cycle, zero survivors. Both candidates this cycle were fresh and well-quantified — active FTC enforcement with named warning-letter recipients, and a dated 2026 industry report with a clean, surprising stat (84% vendor invisibility) — and both still died to the same core pattern this ledger keeps finding: whoever is best positioned to close a fresh gap usually closes it faster than a solo founder can research it, whether that's a philanthropic consortium of the biggest AI labs (ROOST) or the marketing agency that published the very report used as the trigger (5W).
