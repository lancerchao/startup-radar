## Ideas

Two candidates deep-dived this cycle, both rejected — the thirty-third straight cycle with zero survivors.

### 1. "Verified Human" certification badge for creators (writing, art, music) — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A paid trust-mark/certification service creators (writers, visual artists, musicians) attach to their work to signal it's human-made, riding the 2026 "AI slop" backlash and platforms' own AI-disclosure enforcement (Etsy, Spotify) as proof of demand.
- **Risks:** Entering a category where the price-setting incumbent runs on near-donations, structurally capping what any paid competitor can charge.
- **Consumer/buyer pain (quantified):** Real and well-documented cultural moment — Merriam-Webster named "slop" its 2025 Word of the Year (9x YoY increase in "AI slop" mentions during 2025); CNN predicted 2026 as the year of "100% human" marketing; Etsy alone removed ~12,000 listings and issued ~8,500 warnings for AI-disclosure violations in Q1 2026.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Genuine — AI-content volume and platform enforcement (Etsy's Creativity Standards, Spotify's AI Persona badges) both matured in 2026, creating real creator anxiety about being lumped in with AI slop.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** Platforms solve their *own* disclosure problem (Etsy tags listings, Spotify tags artist profiles) but don't offer a portable, cross-platform trust mark — that gap is filled by third parties already, not open.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it is:** At least four named competitors: VerifiedHuman™ (running 3 years, global creator collective across six continents, became a C2PA Contributor Member January 2026), Artisan (SF startup, $4.2M raised January 2026, launched February), VHC Global (verified-human/AI/AI-assisted badges for businesses and creators), and Not By AI (badge movement/community). Spotify's own "Verified by Spotify" authenticity badges launched 2026 on top of that.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** More platforms ship native labels (further commoditizing the "badge" mechanic itself), and the funded entrant (Artisan) has runway to out-market any new bootstrap competitor.
- **Competitors:** VerifiedHuman™, Artisan, VHC Global, Not By AI, plus platform-native labels (Etsy Creativity Standards, Spotify AI Persona / Verified by Spotify).
- **Who pays / sales motion:** Self-serve, creator pays annually — but VerifiedHuman's pricing (pay-if-you-can, suggested $9/$39/$79/year, free option, "97% funded by member generosity") sets the market's price expectation near zero, which any new paid entrant would have to undercut or out-differentiate against for free.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Low. The category's own oldest and most trusted player deliberately prices near-donation to maximize adoption/legitimacy rather than revenue — a rational new entrant can't out-price that and win trust simultaneously.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** Fast to build the badge/verification mechanic itself (days), but building the *trust* a certification mark requires (the entire value of the product) takes years of community-building — VerifiedHuman's three-year head start is the actual moat, not the software.
- **Sources:** [Nurdd — Made by Humans](https://www.nurdd.club/blogs/made-by-humans-authentic-content-ai-slop), [VerifiedHuman FAQ](https://www.iamverifiedhuman.com/faq), [VerifiedHuman — About](https://www.verifiedhuman.info/about), [VHC Global](https://vhc.global/), [Etsy AI Disclosure Standard — rewarx.com](https://www.rewarx.com/blogs/etsy-ai-disclosure-standard), [TechCrunch — Spotify AI Persona badges](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/11/spotify-will-label-ai-persona-profiles-and-exclude-their-music-from-recommendations/).
- **Kill reason:** Rule 9 tarpit — "AI slop backlash → sell a human-made certification badge" is the obvious response to an obvious trend, and it's already been built, by a 3-year-old trust-first incumbent that deliberately prices near free plus a freshly venture-funded challenger; no room for a new entrant to win on price or trust simultaneously.
- **Not scored against B2C_RULES** — buyer is a creator/small business (writer, artist, musician), not a personal consumer.

### 2. AI-generated-music royalty-fraud detection tool for independent musicians — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A scanning tool independent musicians could run against their own catalog and royalty statements to detect AI-clone tracks, fraudulent-stream dilution of their royalty pool, or misattributed AI-generated "artists" free-riding on their genre/name — marketed direct to indie artists rather than to labels/distributors.
- **Risks:** Third-party detection is a structurally weaker product than what distributors already run, and there's a live commercial ecosystem built specifically to *evade* detection, proving the fight is already adversarial on both sides.
- **Consumer/buyer pain (quantified):** Real and large. Forbes estimates AI-generated music fraud at a $4B "fraud machine" (May 2026); the royalty pool is projected to lose 25% of creator revenue by 2028 (~€4B, per industry estimate); Deezer's own detector flags up to 85% of streams on AI-tagged tracks as fraudulent and demonetizes them; Spotify purged 75M fraudulent/AI-spam tracks in August 2025 alone.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Real momentum — DDEX 5.0 (new industry metadata standard) now ships three mandatory AI-disclosure fields, and Spotify's AI Persona badges are rolling out to profiles this month (mid-September 2026).
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do, and better than a third party structurally could. Deezer's second-generation in-house detector (launched June 2026) claims 99.8% accuracy; Spotify runs its own AI Credits/SongDNA provenance features; detection industry-wide is "a stack of independent systems run by the DSP, the distributor, and third-party vendors with cross-platform visibility" — visibility a single artist's tool never has.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it is, from both directions:** Platform-side detection is well-funded and improving fast; on the other side, commercial watermark-removal/evasion tools (e.g., Undetectr, marketed openly to Suno/Udio users to help AI tracks "pass distributor inspection") show the adversarial fight is already a mature, two-sided commercial market — not an open gap waiting for a first mover.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** Distributors and DSPs keep improving in-house detection using full stream-graph visibility across all artists simultaneously — a structural data advantage a per-artist third-party tool can never replicate, so the gap gets *less* buildable over time, not more.
- **Competitors:** Deezer (in-house, 99.8% claimed accuracy), Spotify (AI Credits/SongDNA, AI Persona badges), YouTube (mandatory AI labels), DDEX 5.0 standard bodies; evasion side: Undetectr and similar.
- **Who pays / sales motion:** Would be self-serve (musician pays to scan their own catalog), but the actual lever an aggrieved indie artist has today is disputing a distributor's royalty statement or lobbying for standard adoption — not running an independently-built detector with a fraction of the data the DSPs already have.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Low ceiling even in the best case — a product that can't match the free, already-shipped in-house detector's accuracy has no real reason for a musician to pay for it standalone.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** Not weeks. Matching even a fraction of Deezer's claimed accuracy needs real audio-ML investment (the opposite of a thin wrapper), against a target that actively adapts (the evasion-tool market proves this is a moving target, not a static detection problem).
- **Sources:** [Forbes — AI-Generated Music $4B Fraud Machine](https://www.forbes.com/sites/virginieberger/2026/05/05/how-ai-generated-music-became-a-4-billion-fraud-machine/), [Deezer Newsroom — AI detection tool](https://newsroom-deezer.com/2026/01/ai-generated-music-deezer-selling-detection-tool/), [Tech Times — streaming platforms back AI music labels](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320433/20260714/streaming-platforms-back-ai-music-labels-fraud-actors-exploit-self-reporting-gap.htm), [Undetectr — is Undetectr a scam](https://undetectr.com/blog/is-undetectr-a-scam), [TechCrunch — Spotify AI Persona badges](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/11/spotify-will-label-ai-persona-profiles-and-exclude-their-music-from-recommendations/).
- **Kill reason:** Fails Rule 4 (needs real audio-ML depth, not a thin LLM wrapper) and Rule 3 (no moat — DSPs have full cross-artist stream-graph visibility a per-artist tool structurally can't replicate) simultaneously; independently confirmed by the existence of a live commercial evasion-tool market (Undetectr) proving this is already a well-resourced two-sided fight, not an open gap.
- **Not scored against B2C_RULES** — buyer is a musician/creator (B2B/creator-economy), not a personal consumer.

**Bottom line:** Thirty-third straight cycle, zero survivors. Both candidates rode genuinely fresh triggers this cycle (Spotify's AI Persona badges going live in profiles this month, the "100% human" marketing counter-trend), but both died to variants of this ledger's most common patterns: an idea shape (certification badge for a backlash trend, third-party detection tool for a platform-level fraud problem) that's obvious enough to have already attracted a funded or trust-anchored incumbent before this cycle could find it. No new candidate cleared even the first screen on distribution/moat grounds today; the Senior Line watch item's trigger conditions were re-checked and remain unchanged (see world watch above).
