## Ideas

Three candidates deep-dived this cycle. Two died the project's usual way — a specific, dated trigger, checked, already occupied by named live competitors. The third is the first candidate in eleven straight cycles not killed outright by an existing player doing the same thing; it's flagged `new` for the weekly review to pressure-test rather than closed here.

### 1. Airbnb host damage-claim evidence/documentation kit — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A mobile app for Airbnb (and VRBO) hosts that captures timestamped, tamper-evident move-in/move-out photos and packages them into a "Legitimate and Verifiable Evidence"-compliant report for AirCover damage claims.
- **Risks:** At least four named competitors already ship this exact mechanism; the differentiator (tamper-evidence, timestamping) is table stakes across all of them, not a moat.
- **Consumer pain (quantified):** Real and dated. Airbnb's April 20, 2026 TOS update defined "Legitimate and Verifiable Evidence" and explicitly banned AI-generated, AI-enhanced, upscaled, or synthetic photos/receipts in damage claims — triggered by a Manhattan superhost caught submitting ~$16,000 in fabricated damage photos (same coffee-table crack, different positions, across submissions). Airbnb now checks photo authenticity, timestamps, causation, and timeline on every claim.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Genuinely dated — a 4-month-old TOS change with an enforcement case attached.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** N/A — this is a third-party tooling opportunity, not something Airbnb itself would build (Airbnb's incentive is fewer successful claims, not better host tooling).
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it is:** TurnSnap (live iOS App Store app, "Rental Damage Tracker," timestamped turnover records), Rental Inspection Report (before/after evidence, room labels, exports that attach directly to AirCover/VRBO Host Guarantee/Hostfully), Property Inspect (tamper-evident timestamped photo/comment/signoff records), and Checkout Shield (paired pre-stay/post-stay photos, GPS- and server-verified timestamps, hashed original files, content explicitly framed around the April 2026 TOS update) all already ship the identical mechanism.
- **What will change in 2 years:** Nothing favorable — Airbnb's own verification bar (authenticity, timestamp, causation, timeline checks) only gets more sophisticated, raising the bar these tools already compete on rather than opening a new one.
- **Competitors:** TurnSnap, Rental Inspection Report, Property Inspect, Checkout Shield, plus Hostfully/PriceLabs-style property-management suites with inspection modules bundled in.
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** Individual hosts and small co-hosting operators — self-serve app-store or direct-website purchase, no Meta life-event or interest category precisely targets "Airbnb host," so acquisition would run through App Store search and host-community content (BiggerPockets, r/airbnb_hosts, host Facebook groups) rather than paid social.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Not modeled in depth — moot given four incumbents already own the mechanism; a fifth clone competing on price alone caps ARR at a sliver of an already-small niche (US short-term-rental hosts who both self-manage and have been burned by a denied/disputed claim).
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** Technically fast (weeks — camera capture, timestamp/hash, PDF export) — irrelevant given the direct-competitor wall.
- **Sources:** [The Host Report](https://www.thehostreport.com/news/airbnb-bans-ai-evidence-for-damage-claims-in-new-terms-of-service-update), [AirROI](https://www.airroi.com/blog/airbnb-april-20-2026-tos-update-ai-evidence-ban), [rentalinspectionreport.com](https://rentalinspectionreport.com/), [Property Inspect](https://propertyinspect.com/blog/how-host-inspections-are-airbnbs-secret-to-success/), [Checkout Shield](https://checkout-shield.ezzon.nl/airbnb-2026-rule-changes), [TurnSnap — App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6758029417).
- **Kill reason:** Four named, live competitors (TurnSnap, Rental Inspection Report, Property Inspect, Checkout Shield) already ship the identical timestamped-evidence mechanism, at least one of them built and marketed explicitly around this same April 2026 TOS change — there's no open mechanism left to differentiate on.

### 2. TikTok Shop seller return-cost / dispute management tool — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A dashboard for TikTok Shop sellers that tracks the new June 2026 return-cost math (seller now bears buyer-fault return costs, sub-$10 items are returnless refunds) and automates the 24-hour dispute-response window TikTok enforces before a seller forfeits the refund fight.
- **Risks:** Both the cost-calculation layer and the dispute-automation layer already have live, named competitors; TikTok's own Seller Center already ships configurable auto-approval rules for the underlying workflow.
- **Consumer pain (quantified):** Real. June 2026 policy: sellers now cover buyer-fault return costs (previously platform-subsidized), with only a 50% subsidy left for select TikTok-arranged cases; items priced $10 or less become returnless refunds — buyer keeps the item, seller eats the cost. Once forward shipping, return shipping, fulfillment-by-TikTok handling, refund admin fees, and commission clawbacks stack up, a $50 return can cost a seller roughly $65.70 (Hyperfocus.tech modeling). Sellers must also answer aftersales disputes within 24 hours or lose the case by default.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Genuinely fresh — a June 2026 policy change, sellers still adjusting.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do. TikTok Shop's own Seller Center ships configurable return/refund/replacement auto-approval rules and, since June 2, initiates some partial refunds on the platform's own initiative.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it is, on both halves of the idea.** Return-cost calculators: Dashboardly, Hyperfocus.tech, FastMoss, Seller Profit Guard, and Futureproof all ship free-to-cheap TikTok Shop fee/return calculators, several explicitly updated for the June 2026 change. Dispute automation: Chargeflow (25%-of-recovery success fee, 4x-ROI guarantee, tracks the 24-hour and 7-day deadlines automatically) and Agentative's "Dispute Defense Agent" (audits incoming disputes against TikTok's terms, auto-generates responses, claims 80% time reduction) already do exactly this for TikTok Shop specifically.
- **What will change in 2 years:** Nothing favorable — TikTok's Shop Performance Score already determines the seller/platform cost split algorithmically, meaning the underlying rules keep getting more automated on TikTok's side, compressing the value a third party can add.
- **Competitors:** Dashboardly, Hyperfocus.tech, FastMoss, Seller Profit Guard, Futureproof (calculators); Chargeflow, Agentative, Webgility (dispute/returns automation).
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** TikTok Shop sellers, mostly via Shopify/TikTok Seller Center app-store discovery or seller-community content (similar channel to the already-rejected Amazon and Toast ideas on this ledger) — not Meta-ads-friendly, B2B self-serve SaaS.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Not modeled — moot given the crowded field; Chargeflow's success-fee model in particular is a structurally better offer (zero cost if you don't win) than a flat subscription tool would be.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** Weeks to build against TikTok's Partner API — same speed that let at least seven named competitors already ship equivalent or better versions.
- **Sources:** [ppc.land — 24-hour dispute rule](https://ppc.land/tiktok-shop-forces-sellers-to-answer-disputes-in-24-hours-or-lose-refund/), [TikTok Seller Center — Policy Pulse June 2026](https://seller-us.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=6747273381791534&lang=en), [Chargeflow — TikTok Shop guide](https://www.chargeflow.io/blog/ultimate-tiktok-shop-chargeback-guide), [Chargeflow pricing](https://www.chargeflow.io/pricing), [Hyperfocus — TikTok Shop returns cost guide](https://hyperfocus.tech/resources/tiktok-shop-returns-disputes), [Dashboardly — TikTok Shop fee calculator](https://www.dashboardly.io/post/tiktok-shop-fee-calculator).
- **Kill reason:** Every layer of the idea is already served — TikTok's own Seller Center ships the configurable rules engine, at least five calculators already model the June 2026 return-cost math, and two named tools (Chargeflow, Agentative) already automate the exact 24-hour dispute workflow, one of them on a success-fee model that's a structurally better deal for the seller than a subscription tool.

### 3. AI/SaaS vendor shutdown-risk monitor for small operators — flagged `new`, not deep-dived to a kill

- **Brief description:** A monitoring service where a small business, agency, or solo SaaS founder lists the AI/SaaS tools their operations depend on; the product tracks public risk signals (funding/M&A news, executive and founder departures, layoff reports, changelog/commit-activity silence, "exploring strategic options" language) across that vendor list and alerts before a shutdown or acquihire — paired with a nagging reminder to keep data exports current regardless.
- **Risks:** Unproven willingness to pay for a low-frequency, high-impact event per individual vendor; the audience (indie SaaS founders, small agencies) is not Meta-targetable and doesn't match Lance's Meta-ads-to-checkout distribution strength; adjacent to this project's most over-mined shape ("something changed → build a tracker for it") even though the specific angle differs from prior kills.
- **Business pain (quantified):** Real and escalating. 254 of 2,718 catalogued AI tools/startups (9.3%) have shut down or been acquired as of Aug 9, 2026 (ToolDirectory.ai). Three documented 2026 acquihire-to-shutdown windows this year alone: Salesforce/Clockwise (announced Mar 19, shut down Mar 27 — 8 days), OpenAI/Hiro (announced Apr 13, app stopped Apr 20, data deleted, not transferred to OpenAI — 7 days), Klaviyo/Agency ($32M raised, announced Aug 5, shuts down Aug 31 — 26 days). Coverage attributes the wave to AI-wrapper gross margins compressing below 20% plus hyperscalers/frontier labs absorbing roughly two-thirds of Q1 2026 AI funding via acquihire rather than growth financing — meaning the target getting swallowed is often a startup a small customer had no way to vet as "financially fragile" in advance.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Genuinely fresh — this is a 2026-specific consolidation rate, not a recycled trend; the shortest documented notice-to-shutdown window this year is 7 days.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** Not directly. Enterprise third-party/vendor-risk-management platforms (Onspring, Panorays, Riskonnect) exist but are security/compliance-oriented (breach, SOC2, supply-chain-attack risk) rather than continuity/shutdown-risk-oriented, and priced for enterprise buyers — Onspring quotes run ~$20K–78K/yr, Panorays contracts cluster $30K–60K/yr on Vendr — a poor structural fit for a 3-person agency paying for five AI tools.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet:** Three free trackers exist (Killed by AI — 106 documented dead AI products; The AI Cemetery — 95; postmortem.io — a general startup-shutdown postmortem library), but all three are retrospective catalogs you have to think to check, not a personalized, proactive alert tied to the specific vendors a given business actually depends on. No product doing that personalized/proactive version was found in this search pass — the closest adjacent finding was an indie-hacker "founder-dependency audit" post with no shipped product behind it.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** Likely favorable if the acquihire wave continues at this rate — more vendors, more historical shutdown data to train risk signals on, and a stronger case for the product's core pitch; unfavorable if 2026's consolidation rate is a temporary bubble-correction spike rather than a new steady state, which would shrink the addressable event rate back down.
- **Competitors:** Killed by AI, The AI Cemetery, postmortem.io (free, retrospective, not proactive); Onspring, Panorays, Riskonnect (enterprise vendor-risk, wrong price tier and wrong risk category).
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** Solo SaaS founders, small agencies, and ops-heavy small businesses stacking multiple niche AI tools — reached via content/SEO ("is [vendor] shutting down"), Indie Hackers, Hacker News, and founder-community newsletters, not Meta ads. This is a founder-led-sales/content motion, a genuine mismatch with Lance's stated Meta-ads-to-checkout strength — worth naming plainly rather than glossing over.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Not modeled — too early; the honest open question is whether "peace of mind about a rare per-vendor event" clears a recurring-subscription bar on its own, or needs bundling into something with more frequent daily utility (e.g., a general SaaS-stack cost/renewal tracker) to justify the recurring charge.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** A v1 (manually curated risk directory covering the ~50-100 most-used indie/SMB AI tools + a weekly digest email) is buildable in 1-2 weeks; a real signal-aggregation pipeline (funding-news scraping, LinkedIn departure tracking, changelog-silence detection) is a multi-month build and the part that would create a defensible data moat if it works.
- **Sources:** [ToolDirectory.ai — AI graveyard report](https://tooldirectory.ai/blog/ai-graveyard-report-what-kills-ai-tools-2026), [TechCrunch — Klaviyo/Agency](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/05/klaviyo-acquires-elias-torres-agency-in-full-circle-reunion-for-tech-founders/), [Killed by AI](https://mixtpatrik.github.io/killedbyai/), [The AI Cemetery](https://theaicemetery.com/), [postmortem.io](https://postmortem.io/shutdowns/), [Onspring pricing — SmartSuite](https://www.smartsuite.com/blog/onspring-pricing), [Panorays pricing — Vendr](https://www.vendr.com/marketplace/panorays).
- **Not scored against B2C_RULES** — buyer is a small business/founder, not a personal consumer; honest sales motion above is founder-led content/SEO, not self-serve consumer checkout.
- **Most likely way this fails (not a kill — a flag for the weekly review):** the per-vendor event is too rare and too diffuse across someone's actual tool stack to sustain a standalone recurring subscription — the real product might turn out to be a feature bolted onto a broader "SaaS stack manager" (subscription-cost tracking, renewal reminders) rather than a standalone company, the same "fold into an existing product" pattern several ideas on this ledger have hit before.

**Bottom line:** Eleventh straight cycle for two of three candidates, killed the same way — a fresh, dated platform-policy trigger, checked, and already occupied by 4-7 named live competitors within weeks of the trigger. The third candidate breaks that specific pattern (no direct competitor doing the personalized/proactive version was found) but carries a different, equally real problem: a distribution motion that doesn't match Lance's proven Meta-ads-to-checkout channel, and an unproven case that the underlying event is frequent enough per customer to sustain a subscription on its own. Flagged `new` rather than closed, for the weekly review's adversarial pass — specifically: verify no proactive competitor exists (this search pass could have missed one), and stress-test whether "peace of mind" alone clears the willingness-to-pay bar without a higher-frequency companion feature.
