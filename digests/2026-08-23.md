## Ideas

Three candidates deep-dived this cycle, all rejected — the nineteenth straight cycle with zero survivors. Same recurring failure modes as prior cycles: a free DIY tool already fully solves the compliance task (Amazon), a well-funded incumbent already serves the exact buyer segment at an individual-affordable price (voice-clone protection), and a free competitor plus a health/disability-adjacent Meta targeting wall kill the third (IEP advocate) outright.

### 1. "Synthetic performer" AI-image compliance tagging tool for Amazon sellers — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A tool that batch-tags an Amazon seller's product photos/videos with the required "contains-synthetic-performer" IPTC/XMP metadata field whenever a photorealistic AI-generated model appears in the image, keeping listings compliant with Amazon's new marketplace-wide rule and avoiding ASIN removal.
- **Risks:** The entire deliverable is one free, decades-old command-line operation away from being solved by the seller themselves.
- **Consumer/buyer pain (quantified):** Real but narrow. Amazon has ~500,000 active US sellers (1.65M active worldwide) as of 2026; a broader ~900,000 sellers reportedly use AI tools somewhere in their listing workflow, but that figure covers AI copywriting/background-removal broadly, not specifically photorealistic AI-generated human models — the actual affected subset (sellers using AI "influencer" or model photos) is materially smaller and unquantified in any source found. Non-compliance risk is real: Amazon moderator guidance warns of listing removal and account-level enforcement for repeat violations.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Genuinely fresh — NY's law took effect June 9, 2026, Amazon rolled the marketplace-wide rule out roughly a month later (reported July 25, 2026), and it's spreading (CA, IL, TX, WA drafting similar bills).
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They don't need to — the compliance action itself is a single metadata write. ExifTool (free, open-source since 2003, actively maintained) writes custom IPTC/XMP keywords to the exact `dc:subject` field Amazon specifies, natively supports CSV-driven batch tagging across thousands of files in one command, and is the standard tool professional photographers already use for metadata workflows.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it can't sustain a paid product, the free fix is one command:** At least eight agency/blog posts (GeekSeller, Forbes, MLQ, AI Weekly, ecomclips, masonry, goatconsulting, Omega) already exist purely to explain the free fix to sellers — the same "market organized around interpreting a free/DIY solution, not around paying for an alternative" signature that killed the Shopify agentic-commerce-readiness idea last cycle.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** More states adopting synthetic-performer laws increases the *number of jurisdictions* requiring the tag, but does nothing to the *difficulty* of adding it — still one metadata field, forever free to write.
- **Competitors:** No dedicated paid vendor found. ExifTool (free) does the entire technical job; Amazon's own generative-AI listing tools (used by ~1.3M sellers per Q3 2025 data) are a plausible candidate to auto-embed the tag natively, further closing any gap.
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** Would be self-serve B2B SaaS sold to Amazon sellers running AI-model photography — but the realistic buyer response to "pay $X/mo to add one metadata field" is "run the free command line tool once, or ask whoever generated the AI photos to do it."
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Poor — no defensible technical moat over a free, widely-known utility; addressable buyer pool (sellers using photorealistic AI model images specifically) is an unquantified small slice of an already-shrinking active-seller base (2.4M in 2021 → 1.65M by end of 2025).
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** Days to build a wrapper UI around ExifTool — irrelevant, since the underlying task doesn't need a product at all.
- **Sources:** [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/gabrielalinzainescu/2026/07/25/amazon-requires-sellers-to-label-ai-generated-people-in-listing-images/), [GeekSeller](https://www.geekseller.com/blog/amazon-introduces-new-rules-for-ai-generated-images-july-2026/), [MLQ News](https://mlq.ai/news/amazon-requires-sellers-to-tag-ai-generated-people-in-product-images-after-new-york-law/), [Cooley — NY law analysis](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-29-new-york-enacts-synthetic-performer-disclosure-law-for-advertisements-including-those-using-generative-ai), [ArentFox Schiff — penalties](https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/the-fine-print/ai-actors-real-penalties-new-yorks-first-the-nation-advertising-law-now), [ExifTool](https://exiftool.org/), [IPTC — ExifTool IPTC/XMP command-line guide](https://iptc.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/PMD/pages/649330691/Reading+and+writing+IPTC+metadata+on+the+command+line+using+ExifTool), [SmartScout — Amazon seller stats](https://www.smartscout.com/blog/amazon-seller-statistics), [Red Stag — active seller counts](https://redstagfulfillment.com/how-many-third-party-sellers-are-on-amazon/), [Influencers Time — state compliance matrix](https://www.influencers-time.com/synthetic-performer-disclosure-laws-building-a-state-complia/).
- **Kill reason:** The compliance task is a single free, open-source, one-command metadata write (ExifTool) that already handles batch operations at scale — there is no product-shaped gap between "regulation exists" and "seller is compliant," only a five-minute DIY fix, the same free-DIY failure mode that has now killed variants of this shape roughly a dozen times on this ledger.
- **Not scored against B2C_RULES** — B2B, buyer is an Amazon seller/small business, not a personal consumer.

### 2. AI voice-clone detection & protection service for independent creators — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A subscription service for podcasters, voice actors, and YouTube/audiobook creators that fingerprints their voice, continuously scans the web for unauthorized AI-cloned uses, and handles takedown/cease-and-desist drafting when a match is found — riding the NO FAKES Act's advance and the live NY synthetic-performer law.
- **Risks:** The exact feature (voiceprint + detection) is already sold direct to individual creators, not just enterprises, by a funded incumbent at an affordable monthly price.
- **Consumer pain (quantified):** Real but early-stage — no hard incidence numbers found (no dataset of "X creators found clones of themselves this year"), only qualitative concern documented across multiple 2026 creator-advice guides and SAG-AFTRA's 16,000+-signature open letter (June 2026) pushing Congress on the NO FAKES Act.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Real momentum — NO FAKES Act cleared Senate Judiciary Committee unanimously in June 2026 (still not law), NY's synthetic-performer disclosure law has been live since June 9, 2026, and Illinois/California/Texas/Washington are drafting their own digital-replica bills.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do, at the individual-creator tier specifically. Resemble AI's 2026 pricing includes a $29/mo Creator plan and $99/mo Professional plan with full API access "from day one, including voice cloning and Detect" — its DETECT-3B Omni model claims 98% accuracy across 38+ languages, backed by a $13M round (Sony Innovation Fund, Google AI Futures Fund, Dec 2025). ElevenLabs embeds an inaudible AudioSeal watermark in all synthetic outputs by default.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — the buildable version isn't buildable by a solo dev:** Voiceprint-matching-across-the-open-web is a genuine, hard audio ML/crawling engineering problem — not a thin LLM-wrapper, but also not a "few weeks, one founder" build; the incumbents that do it well took real funding and a specialized ML team to reach 98% claimed accuracy.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** More state/federal law increases *demand* for detection, but Resemble and ElevenLabs — both already selling to individual creators — are the ones positioned to capture it, not a new entrant starting from zero on the hard technical part.
- **B2C gate check (all 9):** Rule 1 (Meta-targetable) — plausible pass; "podcasting," "voice acting," "content creator" are real interest/behavior categories, unverified against the ads-manager size estimator. Rule 2 (LTV + inflow) — pass; genuine recurring monitoring need, continuous inflow of new creators. Rule 3 (moat) — **FAIL**; Resemble AI already sells the identical feature set to individual creators at $29–99/mo with a funded, working 98%-accuracy detection model — nothing a solo build can defend against that. Rule 4 (not thin wrapper) — passes the letter (real ML engineering, not an API call) but fails the rubric's separate "buildable in weeks" criterion — the credible version of this product is a multi-quarter ML engineering effort, not a weeks-long build. Rule 5 (referral mechanic) — weak/fail; protecting your own voice has no natural double-sided or forced-multiplayer sharing loop. Rule 6 (niche) — pass. Rule 7 (reputationally clean) — pass. Rule 9 (over-mined shape) — borderline fail; "build a detector for a new AI-driven risk" is the same recurring shape behind the already-rejected AI-content-compliance, vibe-coding-security-scanner, agentic-browser-security, and reasoning-trace-secrets-scanner ideas. **Fails decisively on Rule 3 alone.**
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** Would be self-serve consumer subscription — but competing against a funded incumbent already selling the same thing to the same buyer at a price point ($29/mo) hard to undercut profitably.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Weak — no technical differentiation reachable in a solo-founder timeframe, and Resemble's API-first, credits-never-expire pricing model directly targets cost-conscious individual creators, the exact segment being pitched.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** Months at minimum for a credible detection model (the hard part), versus zero for a customer to just sign up for Resemble's existing Creator tier today.
- **Sources:** [Kaufman & Canoles — NO FAKES Act status](https://www.kaufcan.com/newsroom/news/the-no-fakes-act-moves-forward-how-ai-voice-and-likeness-rights-could-reshape-entertainment-deals), [SAG-AFTRA — AI resources](https://www.sagaftra.org/contracts-industry-resources/member-resources/artificial-intelligence), [Recording Law — state deepfake/voice-clone law matrix](https://www.recordinglaw.com/us-laws/deepfake-laws/), [Voiceflow — Resemble AI review 2026](https://www.voiceflow.com/blog/resemble-ai), [CheckThat.ai — Resemble AI pricing 2026](https://checkthat.ai/brands/resemble-ai/pricing), [Percify — AI voice cloning and copyright 2026](https://percify.io/blog/ai-voice-cloning-and-copyright-in-2026-what-creators-need-to-know), [Soundverse — protect your voice from AI cloning](https://www.soundverse.ai/blog/article/how-to-protect-your-voice-from-ai-cloning-1051).
- **Kill reason:** Resemble AI already sells voiceprint-based clone detection directly to individual creators at $29–99/month with a funded, working ML model behind it — the hard engineering part of this idea is a solved, already-commercialized problem, and the credible technical bar to compete is months of ML work, not a weeks-long solo build.

### 3. AI IEP/special-education advocate app for parents — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** An AI tool that reads a child's IEP, evaluation reports, and school emails, flags vague goals or missing legally-required services, drafts formal request letters with proper IDEA citations, and preps parents for IEP meetings.
- **Risks:** A free, full-featured direct competitor already exists, and the buyer audience sits on the wrong side of Meta's health/disability-condition ad-targeting wall.
- **Consumer pain (quantified):** Real. 8,194,424 US students ages 3–21 had IEPs as of the latest IDEA data (2024), roughly 15% of the K-12 population — a large, continuously renewing population (new evaluations and IEPs every school year).
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Weak — no fresh regulatory or technical trigger this cycle; the one dated development is a *new competitor* (a free multilingual tool launched by a special-ed teacher, August 2026), which cuts against the idea rather than for it.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do, and one of them is free. EveryIEP is free for parents, drafts evaluation requests and formal letters with proper legal citations, flags vague goals/missing services, and builds meeting agendas — functionally the entire proposed product, at $0. Kidvokit, Arloa, and IEP Advocate.ai ($30/mo) compete at the paid tier.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it already is:** Five distinct tools (EveryIEP, Kidvokit, Arloa, IEP Advocate.ai, and the new S.S. Education) now serve this exact audience, with the free option covering the core feature set claimed for this idea.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** More free entrants, if the S.S. Education launch pattern (built by a practitioner, given away) repeats — downward pressure on any paid tier, not upward.
- **B2C gate check (all 9):** Rule 1 (Meta-targetable) — **FAIL**; the addressable audience is defined by a child's disability/health-adjacent condition (autism, ADHD, speech/language impairment, learning disability), the same category Meta stripped from interest targeting in its January 2022 sensitive-categories sweep — the identical wall that killed the ADHD task-app (2026-08-12) and GLP-1 (2026-08-09) ideas; "parents" alone is too broad to reach this specific sub-population precisely. Rule 2 (LTV + inflow) — pass; annual IEP cycles create real retention, continuous new diagnoses create real inflow. Rule 3 (moat) — **FAIL**; EveryIEP is a free, feature-complete competitor. Rule 5 (referral mechanic) — weak/fail; no designed double-sided or forced-multiplayer sharing loop, though organic word-of-mouth in parent support groups is plausible. Rule 6 (niche) — pass. Rule 7 (reputationally clean) — pass. Rule 9 (over-mined shape) — fail; "AI reads your paperwork and drafts an appeal/request letter with legal citations" is the same template behind the already-rejected insurance-denial-fight-kit, disability/PIP appeal-letter tool, and hospital-bill-negotiation ideas, now applied to special education. **Fails on Rule 1 alone**, reinforced by Rules 3 and 9.
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** Self-serve consumer purchase, competing against a free incumbent with the identical feature set — a structurally difficult sell regardless of execution quality.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Poor — no precise Meta ad channel (Rule 1 fail) and no price umbrella above a free competitor (Rule 3 fail).
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** Weeks to build a comparable document-parsing tool — irrelevant given the free competitor already ships the same feature set.
- **Sources:** [PRWeb — free multilingual IEP tool, Aug 2026](https://www.prweb.com/releases/special-education-teacher-launches-free-multilingual-ai-tool-to-help-families-understand-ieps-302848745.html), [EveryIEP](https://www.everyiep.com/), [Kidvokit](https://kidvokit.com/), [IEP Advocate.ai](https://iepadvocate.ai/), [Arloa](https://www.arloa.ai/lp/iep), [EdWeek — special education enrollment history](https://www.edweek.org/teaching-learning/the-number-of-students-in-special-education-has-doubled-in-the-past-45-years/2023/07), [The Advocacy Institute — IDEA-eligible student data](https://www.advocacyinstitute.org/blog/).
- **Kill reason:** EveryIEP already gives away the entire proposed feature set free, and the buyer audience is defined by a child's disability/health-adjacent condition — the same Meta interest-targeting wall (Jan 2022 sensitive-categories sweep) that has now killed three separate ideas on this ledger (ADHD app, GLP-1 products, and this one).

**Bottom line:** Nineteenth straight cycle, zero survivors. This cycle's pattern skewed toward "the hard/valuable part of the idea is already commercialized by a funded specialist" (Resemble AI's individual-creator voice-clone detection) and "the easy part needs no product at all" (Amazon's metadata tag, solved by a 20-year-old free CLI tool) rather than last cycle's "platform ships it free" pattern — a reminder that fresh regulatory triggers (NY synthetic-performer law, NO FAKES Act) are necessary but not sufficient when the technical bar is either trivial (free DIY) or too high for a solo weeks-long build. The IEP candidate is the pattern that keeps recurring most often on this ledger: real, quantified pain: plus a free full-featured competitor plus a Meta health-adjacent targeting wall, now the third such combination (after ADHD, GLP-1) to die exactly the same way.
