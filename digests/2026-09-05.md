## Ideas

Two candidates deep-dived this cycle, both rejected — the thirty-second straight cycle with zero survivors.

### 1. Document/medical-identity breach protection for seniors (responding to the IDScan.net/Nexus leak) — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A subscription identity-protection product specifically for the document-scan and medical-identity-fraud angle of the Nexus breach — monitoring for new accounts opened using a stolen driver's-license image (not just SSN/credit-file monitoring) and Medicare/medical-claim fraud alerts, marketed to the adult-child buyer of an aging parent.
- **Risks:** Every differentiating feature this idea would lead with is already a standard line item at the category leaders.
- **Consumer pain (quantified):** Real and fresh. 153M+ driver's licenses, 10M+ ID cards, 3M+ travel documents, and 579,000 medical cards for sale as of August 31, 2026 (KrebsOnSecurity, FBI investigating as of September 2). A clear license scan is enough to open new credit accounts or pass some identity-verification checks; medical identity theft via stolen Medicare numbers is independently confirmed as one of the fastest-growing fraud categories against seniors.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Genuinely fresh trigger (days old), but the response infrastructure isn't new — the entire identity-protection industry exists precisely to respond to breaches like this one, and has for over a decade.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do, explicitly, for this exact buyer. Aura ($25/mo family plan, $1M insurance/adult), LifeLock (up to $3M coverage), Identity Guard, Coveron, and EverSafe (20% discount for 60+, stacks with a 30% family discount) are all independently ranked in 2026 "best identity theft protection for seniors" roundups, and dark-web monitoring plus dedicated medical-ID monitoring are already standard features across them, not a gap to fill.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it is:** At least 5 named vendors run senior-specific pricing/marketing today. Independently ironic: Aura, the most-recommended vendor for this exact buyer, disclosed its own breach (~900K records, voice-phishing of an employee) in March 2026 — proof the sector's dominant trust-brand isn't invulnerable, which if anything favors the incumbent's continued dominance (customers already accepted the risk and stayed) rather than opening room for a new entrant.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** More breaches, more incumbent feature parity, tighter incumbent-vs-incumbent price competition — nothing that structurally opens room below the leaders.
- **Competitors:** Aura, LifeLock, Identity Guard, Coveron, EverSafe, NortonLifeLock, Experian, IdentityIQ.
- **Who pays / sales motion:** Would be self-serve/checkout-compatible in principle (adult child buying for a parent, same buyer as the in-sheet ideas), but this is exactly why the category is this crowded — it's the same easy-to-build, easy-to-market shape every incumbent already executes.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Meta classifies identity-theft-protection ads under its Financial Products and Services Special Ad Category — self-identification required, no ZIP-code targeting, mandatory 18–65+ age range, all genders included. This kills the precise senior-only targeting the idea depends on, the same wall that blocked the Social Security claiming report and Medicare Advantage decoder ideas on this ledger.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** An MVP dark-web-scan dashboard ships in weeks, but real identity-restoration service (the actual value seniors pay for after a breach) requires insurance backing and a resolution-specialist operation neither incumbent nor a new entrant can stand up cheaply — the incumbents' $1–3M insurance backing is a real cost structure, not a marketing claim.
- **Sources:** [Krebs on Security](https://krebsonsecurity.com/2026/09/fbi-probes-service-selling-153m-drivers-licenses/), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/02/it-sure-looks-like-hackers-breached-a-major-id-card-verification-service/), [Retirement Living — best ID theft protection for seniors 2026](https://www.retirementliving.com/best-identity-theft-protection), [Cloudwards — EverSafe review](https://www.cloudwards.net/eversafe-review/), [Aura data breach — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aura_data_breach), [Jon Loomer — Meta Special Ad Categories](https://www.jonloomer.com/special-ad-categories-meta-ads/).
- **Kill reason:** Fails on saturation, not on the pain being real — at least five named vendors already run senior-specific pricing and already ship the document/medical-ID-specific monitoring this idea would lead with, and Meta's Financial Products Special Ad Category kills the precise senior targeting the funnel depends on. Same two-part failure pattern (real incumbent + Meta financial-category wall) that has now killed every senior-fintech-adjacent idea this ledger has tested.
- **B2C_RULES gate check:** Rejected on Rule 1 (Special Ad Category strips precise targeting) independently of Rule 3 (no defensible moat against five funded incumbents already serving this buyer) — fails two gates, not scored further.

### 2. Paid skill/plugin marketplace for AI coding agents (Claude Code / SKILL.md ecosystem) — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** Build and sell packaged skills/plugins (e.g. via Agensi or a similar marketplace) for AI coding agents — one-time $3–25 purchases that save a developer setup/configuration time, distributed through the emerging SKILL.md-standard marketplace ecosystem rather than Meta ads.
- **Risks:** The market is real but the earnings distribution is a power law with a near-zero median, and the growth channel is audience-building, not paid acquisition — the opposite of Lance's demonstrated strength.
- **Consumer/buyer pain (quantified):** Real but modest — developers save configuration/setup time; a $5–15 skill "pays for itself immediately" if it saves an hour, per marketplace marketing content.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Genuine and dated. SKILL.md standardized as a cross-agent format in 2025–2026 (20+ coding agents now support it), creating enough installed base for a real marketplace economy for the first time — Agensi launched and crossed 2,000 listed skills with 200+ creators and 50,000 monthly visitors within three months (milestone dated July 9, 2026).
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They partially do (Anthropic's own official repo exists) but haven't built a paid storefront — Anthropic's github.com/anthropics/skills holds only 17 reference skills and is free/open-source, leaving commercial packaging to third-party marketplaces (Agensi, ClaudeSkills.info, skills.sh, SkillsMP) rather than the platform itself.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet:** It's young (three months old) but moving fast — 2,000+ skills already listed on one marketplace alone, plus multiple competing catalogs.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** More agents adopt SKILL.md, the creator base grows, and — per the pattern of every other 2026 AI-tooling category on this ledger — the easy-to-build ideas get filled first and fastest, compressing the window for a new entrant.
- **Competitors:** Agensi (paid, security-reviewed, 70/30 or 80/20 creator split via Stripe Connect), ClaudeSkills.info, skills.sh, SkillsMP, claudemarketplaces.com, plus Anthropic's own free official repo undercutting price expectations at the low end.
- **Who pays / sales motion:** Self-serve marketplace listing — buyer discovery happens via marketplace search and creator reputation/audience, not Meta ads. Lance would state this honestly: no compatible paid-acquisition channel exists for this category; growth is organic/audience-driven, which is the opposite of the funnel Lance has actually proven out.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Reported earnings are a hard power law — top individual skills generate $500–3,000/month, but the median listed skill earns under $50/month, with revenue concentrated in the top ~10% of listings who already have a following. One-time pricing ($3–25/skill) also caps LTV per customer at a single purchase unless bundled into an ongoing collection.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** Genuinely fast — a single skill can ship in days. But that ease of building is exactly why the median outcome is near-zero revenue; standing out requires building an audience first, which is months of work with no guaranteed payoff, not a "sellable in weeks" outcome in the sense this ledger's rubric means.
- **Sources:** [Agensi — 2,000 skills milestone](https://www.newsfilecorp.com/release/304200/Agensi-Crosses-2000-Skills-Three-Months-After-Launching-Its-AI-Agent-Marketplace), [anthropics/skills — GitHub](https://github.com/anthropics/skills), [Agensi — monetize SKILL.md skills guide](https://www.agensi.io/learn/how-to-monetize-skill-md-skills-developer-guide-2026), [Agensi — best AI agent skills marketplaces 2026](https://www.agensi.io/learn/best-ai-agent-skills-marketplaces-2026).
- **Kill reason:** No distribution answer that fits Lance's actual playbook (PROMPT.md's own no-ship-without-distribution rule) — the channel is organic audience-building among developers, not paid Meta acquisition — and the earnings data show most participants land near $0/month regardless of channel, since the category is a power law dominated by creators who already had an audience before listing.
- **Not scored against B2C_RULES** — B2B/creator-economy, buyer is a developer, not a personal consumer.

**Bottom line:** Thirty-second straight cycle, zero survivors. Both candidates were the freshest possible triggers available this cycle — a days-old 153M-record breach and a three-month-old real-money marketplace milestone — and both still died to this ledger's two most common failure patterns: a funded incumbent already shipping the exact differentiator (five senior-specific identity-protection vendors, in this case), and a channel mismatch between where the buyer actually is and the Meta-ads funnel this founder's playbook depends on (the skills marketplace's audience-driven, not paid-acquisition, growth path). Nothing scanned this cycle contradicts the standing pattern: visible, well-quantified pain is now essentially always already being served by the time it's visible enough to notice.
