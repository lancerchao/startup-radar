## Ideas

Two candidates deep-dived this cycle, both killed — the seventh straight cycle with zero survivors. Both had a genuinely fresh, dated trigger (a new open developer platform, a new attack campaign) but died to the same structural pattern the ledger keeps re-confirming: the infrastructure needed to get paid isn't built yet (glasses), or it's already been built for free by whoever created the problem (security).

### 1. Third-party utility app for Meta Ray-Ban Display AI glasses — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A single-purpose paid utility app for Meta's Ray-Ban Display smart glasses (the AR/HUD variant), built as a standalone web app per Meta's new developer toolkit — e.g., a live captions/translation overlay, a transit/wayfinding companion, or a step-by-step cooking-guide overlay — sold as a one-time purchase via a companion phone-app listing.
- **Risks:** No app-store, payment, or discovery layer exists yet on the platform at all — Meta has shipped the SDK but not the commerce infrastructure a paid app needs; the installed base for the Display (screen) variant specifically is a small fraction of total Ray-Ban Meta sales.
- **Consumer pain:** Real but not yet well-characterized — early third-party demos (smart-home control, flight lookup by gaze, live transit info) show plausible utility, but no willingness-to-pay data exists because nobody has been able to charge for an app on this platform yet.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Genuinely fresh — Meta only opened the Display glasses to third-party developers in May 2026 (native Swift/Kotlin SDK or plain HTML/CSS/JS web apps), and the underlying hardware only shipped September 2025.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They're the platform, not a competitor yet — Meta explicitly says it's outsourcing the "killer app" problem to outside developers via grants (5x $200K Catalyst Grants, 25 Accelerator Grants announced Jan 2026) rather than building every app itself.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it's too early to tell, and that's the problem, not the opportunity:** Per Next Reality's developer-preview analysis, "no public information currently exists on app review or approval, a future storefront, monetization for developers, or discoverability beyond URL sharing" — the platform is "structurally incomplete in ways that matter to anyone considering serious investment." Nobody's building a sustainable business here yet because there's no mechanism to.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** Meta will very likely ship real app-store/payment/discovery infrastructure as adoption scales (EssilorLuxottica is discussing doubling annual production toward 20M units/year with Meta) — but the correct move is to wait for that infrastructure to exist, not build ahead of it on spec.
- **Competitors:** None yet in the commercial sense — early entrants are hobbyist/demo apps (Tetris via Neural Band, a smart-home-control app, an agentic Starbucks-ordering assistant called "Super") shared by URL, not sold.
- **Who pays / Expected CAC / LTV / LTV:CAC / ARR ceiling:** Unmodelable — there is no payment rail for a third-party app on this device today. Even a working app can't yet be legally sold to end users on the platform.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** Technically fast to prototype (a web app, the same skill Lance already has) — but "sellable" is blocked entirely on Meta shipping commerce infrastructure it hasn't announced a date for.
- **B2C gate check (informational, not a live selection since the platform gate kills it first):** Would likely fail Rule 1 today — "owns Ray-Ban Display glasses" isn't a Meta ad-targetable audience at any real precision given the small installed base of the Display (vs. audio-only) variant specifically. Rule 6 (niche) plausibly passes once the SDK matures. Not scored further — moot until a storefront exists.
- **Sources:** [Next Reality — SDK paths and platform gaps](https://virtual.reality.news/news/meta-ray-ban-display-developer-preview-sdk-paths-and-platform-gaps/), [UploadVR — early apps](https://www.uploadvr.com/meta-ray-ban-display-amazing-hud-apps/), [Gizmodo — early apps going off the rails](https://gizmodo.com/the-meta-ray-ban-display-can-now-unlock-your-car-i-guess-2000764501), [CNBC — EssilorLuxottica 2025 sales](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/11/ray-ban-maker-essilorluxottica-triples-sales-of-meta-ai-glasses.html), [TrendForce — AR glasses shipment forecast](https://www.trendforce.com/presscenter/news/20260130-12908.html), [gHacks — Wearables Toolkit](https://www.ghacks.net/2026/05/18/meta-opens-ray-ban-display-glasses-to-third-party-developers-through-wearables-toolkit/).
- **Kill reason:** The platform itself confirms it hasn't shipped app-store, payment, or discovery infrastructure — there is no legal, scalable way to charge an end user for a third-party app on this device yet. Revisit once Meta announces a storefront/payment API; building ahead of that on spec means shipping into a platform that structurally can't pay you back.

### 2. AI-coding-agent config-file poisoning scanner for solo/indie developers — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A cheap, one-time-purchase or low-cost-monthly CLI/desktop tool that scans a repo's `CLAUDE.md`, `.cursorrules`, `SKILL.md`, and MCP server configs for hidden-instruction prompt-injection payloads (zero-width Unicode, "ignore previous instructions" patterns) before a developer opens the project in Claude Code, Cursor, or similar — targeted at solo developers and small teams too small to buy enterprise AppSec tooling.
- **Risks:** The exact detection mechanism this idea proposes is already shipped, free, and backed by better threat-intel than a solo entrant could match; the buyer segment (indie developers) is documented to actively prefer free/open-source tools and resist recurring fees.
- **Business pain (quantified):** Real and escalating fast. Phoenix Security counted 37 malicious-package campaigns and 497 indexed malicious packages in H1 2026 alone (2.6x the full-year campaign count of the prior year); AI-agent-specific delivery (MCP server injection, `.cursorrules`/`CLAUDE.md` poisoning, SessionStart hook abuse) appeared in at least 14 of 59 tracked campaigns. The Aug 4, 2026 keyv/cacheable + TrapDoor campaigns are the freshest, highest-profile instances.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Genuinely new attack surface — AI coding agents with auto-run/auto-install permissions reading project config files as trusted instructions is a 2025–2026 phenomenon, not a repackaged old problem.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They're the ones who built the detection tooling first. Snyk shipped `agent-scan`/`mcp-scan` (open source, auto-discovers Claude/Cursor/Windsurf/Gemini CLI configs, checks 15+ risk categories including prompt injection and tool poisoning) and published a dedicated "ToxicSkills" research study (36% prompt-injection rate, 1,467 malicious payloads found in an agent-skills marketplace). Aikido Security discovered and open-sourced detection rules for the related "PromptPwnd" CI/CD pattern. Lasso Security ships an open-source MCP security gateway.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it isn't:** ClawGuard MCP already offers a free tier (100 scans/day) aimed at exactly this indie/solo use case. Snyk's tools are free and open source with no seat-based gate for the core scanning function. Multiple specialized research teams (HiddenLayer, Repello AI) are publishing detection techniques in the open.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** The attacker/defender arms race keeps escalating (new campaign families roughly quarterly this year), which grows the raw pain — but every major AppSec vendor already treats "AI agent config poisoning" as core roadmap territory, so free/open coverage only gets deeper, not thinner.
- **Competitors:** Snyk (agent-scan, mcp-scan — free/OSS), Lasso Security (open-source MCP gateway), ClawGuard MCP (free tier), Aikido Security (PromptPwnd, open-sourced Opengrep rules), HiddenLayer and Repello AI (research + commercial detection).
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** Indie developers and small teams, self-serve/PLG — a channel Lance could technically execute (content/SEO, dev-tool Product Hunt launch), but indie-hacker tooling surveys this cycle show this exact buyer explicitly optimizes for generous free tiers and resists recurring fees, and the best free option here comes from a company with far deeper threat-intel resources than a solo entrant.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / LTV:CAC / ARR ceiling:** Not modeled in depth — a credible zero-cost substitute from a well-resourced incumbent (Snyk) caps willingness to pay before unit economics are worth testing.
- **Time to first sellable version:** Weeks (pattern-match known injection signatures against local config files) — but that speed is the risk, not the reassurance, since Snyk and others shipped the same thing first and gave it away.
- **Operational load:** Moderate and ongoing — detection signatures need continuous updates as new obfuscation techniques (zero-width Unicode, encoded payloads) emerge, an arms race a solo maintainer would be permanently behind funded research teams on.
- **Sources:** [Wiz — keyv/cacheable attack](https://www.wiz.io/blog/keyv-and-cacheable-npm-supply-chain-attack), [stateofsurveillance.org — TrapDoor campaign](https://stateofsurveillance.org/news/trapdoor-supply-chain-attack-ai-coding-assistant-poisoning-claude-cursor-2026/), [Phoenix Security — 2026 supply chain attack acceleration](https://phoenix.security/accelerating-supply-chain-attacks-npm-pypi-vsx-ai-enabled-2026/), [Snyk — agent-scan (GitHub)](https://github.com/snyk/agent-scan), [Snyk — ToxicSkills research](https://snyk.io/blog/toxicskills-malicious-ai-agent-skills-clawhub/), [Aikido — PromptPwnd](https://www.aikido.dev/blog/promptpwnd-github-actions-ai-agents), [ClawGuard MCP](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/@joergmichno/clawguard-mcp), [devtoolpicks — indie hacker tool preferences](https://devtoolpicks.com/blog/best-sentry-alternatives-indie-hackers-2026).
- **Kill reason:** Snyk already ships the identical detection mechanism (config auto-discovery + prompt-injection/tool-poisoning scanning across Claude/Cursor/Windsurf/Gemini CLI) free and open-source, backed by dedicated research the size of a solo founder's entire company; a second free-tier competitor (ClawGuard) already targets the exact indie-developer buyer this idea proposes, and that buyer segment is documented to actively resist paying for tooling with a credible free option.

**Bottom line:** Seventh straight cycle, zero survivors. Both kills this cycle share a shape distinct from most prior rejections: neither died to "a free incumbent already serves this need" in the everyday sense — one died to a platform that hasn't finished building the plumbing to let anyone get paid at all (revisit later, this one could open), the other died to a free tool from the same company class that created the underlying research problem, shipped before a solo entrant could move. Worth tracking: Meta's Ray-Ban Display storefront/payment API announcement, whenever it lands, as a trigger to revisit idea #1 specifically — it's a rare case where the idea's core logic (platform opened, distribution-ready audience, skills match) holds and only the timing failed.
