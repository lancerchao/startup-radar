## Ideas

Two candidates deep-dived this cycle, both rejected — the twenty-fourth straight cycle with zero survivors. Both had real, quantified, currently-accelerating triggers; both died to the same recurring pattern — a cluster of funded competitors, or the platform itself, already occupies the exact wedge a solo founder would need.

### 1. Backup-childcare benefit-in-a-box for SMB employers hit by 5-day RTO mandates — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A founder-led-sales product selling backup-childcare benefit access to 100-1,000-employee companies rolling out 5-day RTO who are too small for Bright Horizons' enterprise contracts — a pay-as-you-go booking layer connecting employees to vetted in-home/center backup care, billed only for hours actually used.
- **Risks:** Requires building caregiver-vetting, background-check, and liability infrastructure before a single sale — this is a marketplace-and-trust build, not a software wrapper. Already-funded competitors are undercutting on price in exactly this segment.
- **Consumer pain (quantified):** Real and accelerating. 55% of Fortune 100 now mandate 5-day RTO (vs. 5% in 2021); 3.2M child care slots at risk nationally; state-level waitlists spiking in real time (Indiana 34,000+, Missouri +60% in one week, West Virginia ~28,000-child gap under age 6); 455,000+ women left the US workforce in H1 2025 citing childcare.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** The collision is new, not either trend alone — 5-day enforcement (37% of companies actively enforcing vs. 17% in 2024) is landing directly on top of an already-strained 2026 childcare-capacity crunch.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They do, at the top of the market — Bright Horizons already owns Fortune 500-scale backup-care contracts, which is precisely why this idea targets the tier below it.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it is:** At least four funded, "tech-first," explicitly cheaper, app-based competitors are already marketing directly into this mid-market/SMB gap: **Helpr** (starts at $12.95/employee, use-based pricing), **Vivvi** (global backup child/elder care, "employer sponsored child care, reinvented"), **CorporateCARE Solutions** (pay-as-you-go, zero-waste billing, "organizations of any size"), and **Cariloop** (care-navigation across childcare/eldercare/backup/pet care from $12.95/employee for small business). Coverage explicitly frames all of them as positioned against "legacy" providers for mid-market employers adopting the benefit for the first time.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** RTO enforcement keeps climbing as more Fortune 100 companies follow Instagram/PNC/Fidelity; expect the four incumbents above to keep pushing further downmarket toward true small business (<100 employees) — the segment least served today — squeezing this idea's remaining runway rather than opening it.
- **Competitors:** Bright Horizons (enterprise), Helpr, Vivvi, CorporateCARE Solutions, Cariloop, Care.com/CareBenefits, LifeCare.
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** Employer HR/benefits buyer, founder-led sales through benefits brokers — no self-serve precedent exists in this category; sales cycles in employee benefits typically run 3-6+ months, a poor match for Lance's self-serve consumer-checkout skillset.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** High CAC (relationship-driven broker-channel sales), and any pricing edge is already claimed by four incumbents pricing near $12.95/employee.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** Months, not weeks — a credible offering needs a vetted caregiver network, background checks, and liability coverage before any employer will sign, then ongoing high-touch care-coordination support.
- **Sources:** [June Care Co.](https://www.junecare.co/blog/end-of-hybrid-work-intensifies-childcare-crisis), [Center for American Progress](https://www.americanprogress.org/article/americas-licensed-child-care-deserts/), [Skedda RTO tracker](https://www.skedda.com/insights/return-to-office-policies-2026-company-tracker), [Helpr](https://www.hellohelpr.com/resources/for-benefits-leaders/what-is-backup-care), [Vivvi](https://employers.vivvi.com/), [CorporateCARE Solutions](https://www.corporatecaresolutions.com/), [Cariloop](https://cariloop.com/solutions/backup-care), [601 Capital — Bright Horizons](https://601capital.substack.com/p/bright-horizons-childcares-hidden).
- **Kill reason:** The pain is real, fresh, and accelerating, but the exact SMB/mid-market gap below Bright Horizons is already being actively filled by at least four funded, tech-first, cheaper competitors — and closing it requires a caregiver-vetting/liability/marketplace build that's months of work with no self-serve distribution channel, not the weeks-to-first-sellable-version bar Lance needs.
- **Not scored against B2C_RULES** — B2B, buyer is the employer, not a personal consumer.

### 2. AI-fluency portfolio/credential for new grads facing the entry-level hiring collapse — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A one-time-purchase, self-serve product for college students/recent grads: a guided, project-based portfolio builder producing verifiable "I used AI tools to actually ship X" case studies — the applied-skill signal employers say they want in place of a degree — sold direct to job-hunters navigating the collapsed entry-level market.
- **Risks:** The category (AI resume/job-search tools) is already one of the most crowded consumer-software niches on the internet; the platform best positioned to own the specific "prove you can use AI" signal is now giving it away free.
- **Consumer pain (quantified):** Real and NY-Fed-verified. Recent-grad (22-27) unemployment hit 5.6-5.7% in Q1 2026 — above the national rate, a reversal from the below-national-rate pattern that had held almost continuously since 1990. Entry-level ("no experience") job postings are down ~73% from the 2021-22 peak. 41.5% of recent grads were underemployed in Q1 2026.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** The specific divergence — grad unemployment now exceeding the national rate — is a fresh, still-worsening 2026 data point per the NY Fed's Q1 release, not a recycled trend.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They're the ones closing the gap. OpenAI opened its three "AI Foundations" certificate courses to the public in June 2026 — free, no employer sponsor, no waitlist — targeting exactly the "prove you can apply AI" signal this idea would sell.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it's one of the most saturated shapes checked this cycle:** 2026 "best AI job-search tool" roundups already name 10+ live competitors — Kickresume, aiApply, Rezi, Teal HQ, Jobscan, Resume Worded, Enhancv, Chiaro, Yoodli, Final Round AI, Google's own free Interview Warmup — before counting OpenAI's free credential entering the exact "applied AI skill" niche.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** OpenAI's not-yet-launched proctored certification exam (currently in employer pilots) will likely ship free-or-cheap and become a default, expected-free credential line — the same trajectory "Google Analytics Certified" took — closing this window further rather than opening it.
- **Competitors:** Kickresume, aiApply, Rezi, Teal HQ, Jobscan, Resume Worded, Enhancv, Chiaro, Yoodli, Final Round AI, Google Interview Warmup, OpenAI AI Foundations (free).
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** The student/grad themselves, self-serve — mechanically compatible with Lance's Meta-ads playbook (a "recent graduate" interest/behavior audience is targetable), the one part of this idea that actually fits.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** LTV is capped hard regardless of competition — a job search is inherently one-and-done once the person is hired, and OpenAI's free credential sets the market price anchor at $0.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** Weeks, technically easy to build — which is exactly why 10+ competitors already exist; ongoing content refresh needed as "what employers want to see" shifts with each new AI capability.
- **Sources:** [NY Fed — College Labor Market](https://www.newyorkfed.org/research/college-labor-market), [CNBC — remote work & youth unemployment](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/01/remote-work-youth-unemployment-new-york-fed.html), [CNBC — grad job market](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/06/college-graduates-job-market-unemployment.html), [FindSkill.ai — OpenAI certification guide](https://findskill.ai/blog/openai-certification-guide/), [4dayweek.io — AI resume builders](https://4dayweek.io/career-advice/best-ai-resume-builders), [Resumly.ai — AI tools for new grads](https://www.resumly.ai/best/best-ai-job-search-tools-for-new-grads).
- **Kill reason:** The underlying pain is real, fresh, and quantified, but the product shape is one of the most saturated categories checked this cycle, and the party best positioned to own its core signal (OpenAI) is already giving it away free — capping the ceiling at zero. Independently fails B2C_RULES Rule 2 (no renewing LTV: a job search ends at hire, and the pursuit here is a one-time purchase against a population that isn't the renewing kind the rule requires) — would be rejected on B2C grounds alone even setting the saturation problem aside.

**Bottom line:** Twenty-fourth straight cycle, zero survivors. Both candidates this cycle had genuinely fresh, well-quantified, primary-sourced triggers — a real collision of trends (RTO enforcement hitting a childcare capacity crisis) and a real reversal in NY Fed data (grad unemployment exceeding the national rate for the first time in decades) — yet both died to the same pattern as most of this ledger: funded, tech-first competitors already occupy the specific SMB-priced wedge a solo founder would need, or the platform most likely to own the core signal (OpenAI, on applied-AI credentialing) is giving it away for free. A third pass today (Amazon's AI title-rewrite disruption, logged in World Watch) died even faster — six named listing-monitor vendors already alert on exactly that change, not worth a full write-up.
