## Ideas

Three candidates deep-dived this cycle, all killed — the sixth straight cycle with zero survivors. All three follow the same underlying failure mode the ledger keeps re-finding: a fresh, real, quantified trigger (an insurer earnings call, a new state law, a fraud-statistics report) that turns out to already have either a free incumbent solution, a platform that solved it at the source, or 2+ funded dedicated competitors by the time it's investigated.

### 1. Medicare Advantage non-renewal / ANOC "plain-English decoder" kit — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A $19–29 one-time-purchase digital product: send a photo of your Medicare Advantage non-renewal letter or Annual Notice of Change (ANOC), get a plain-English translation of what's changing, a checklist of deadlines (AEP Oct 15–Dec 7, SEP windows), and a worksheet to bring to a licensed agent or SHIP counselor — deliberately *not* a plan-recommendation or enrollment product, to sidestep CMS TPMO/broker-licensing rules.
- **Risks:** Meta classifies insurance-adjacent ads under Special Ad Category, which strips the precise senior-age targeting the idea depends on; a free, well-established human alternative (SHIP counselors) already does exactly this job at scale.
- **Consumer pain (quantified):** Real and newly escalating. Humana alone is displacing 600,000 MA members for 2027 (announced Aug 2, 2026 earnings call); Aetna already dropped 90 plans across 34 states for 2026; UnitedHealthcare exited 225+ counties while entering only 14. Letters land by end of September 2026; the AEP decision window is Oct 15–Dec 7.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** MA plan exits are accelerating specifically because CMS is squeezing insurer margins and carriers are explicitly prioritizing profitability over enrollment for the 2027 contract year — this is a fresh, dated escalation (Humana's Aug 2 call), not a recycled trend.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They don't need to — the "competitor" here is a government-funded free service. State Health Insurance Assistance Programs (SHIP) gave 4.3 million beneficiaries, family members, and caregivers direct help in 2022, with 1.7 million receiving one-on-one counseling averaging 33 minutes per session (vs. 9.5 minutes for a 1-800-MEDICARE call) — a slower, human, free alternative already absorbing exactly this need.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it's structurally hard to reach the buyer, not unaddressed:** No dedicated "decode my ANOC letter" consumer product was found; the space is instead occupied by insurance-agent blogs (explaining the letters to drive their own broker leads) and the free SHIP program. The gap isn't unowned — it's owned by a channel (licensed agents, whose business model requires *not* charging the consumer directly) and a free public service, neither of which a solo paid product beats on cost.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** MA exits keep escalating (CMS margin pressure isn't reversing), which grows the raw number of affected people — but Meta's insurance-ad targeting restrictions and SHIP's free-service model are both structural, not trend-driven, so neither loosens as the underlying pain grows.
- **Competitors:** SHIP (free, 54 state/territory programs), 1-800-MEDICARE, insurance-agent content sites (myplanfit.com, prepareformedicare.com) that explain non-renewal letters as broker-lead-gen, eHealth/GoHealth (licensed brokers, commission model — the exact structure this idea is trying to avoid).
- **Who pays / Expected CAC / LTV / LTV:CAC / ARR ceiling:** Not modeled in depth — no reachable precise paid-acquisition channel once the Special Ad Category targeting restriction applies; broad 65+ targeting at 15-mile-minimum radius would carry consumer-funnel-grade CAC against a $19–29 one-time product, a weak ratio before any conversion data exists.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** Technically fast (weeks) — an LLM call against a well-known document type — but that speed cuts against it too: it's easy for a SHIP-adjacent nonprofit or an existing Medicare-content publisher to add for free.
- **B2C gate check:** Fails multiple gates outright. **Rule 1 (Meta-targetable) — FAIL:** insurance-adjacent ads fall under Meta's Special Ad Category, losing age targeting entirely (ads must reach all of 18–65+) and geographic precision (15-mile minimum radius); "just received a non-renewal letter" isn't a Meta-knowable signal at all. **Rule 3 (moat) — FAIL:** SHIP already gives away free, live, one-on-one human counseling at meaningful scale (4.3M/yr) — no proprietary edge over a free human alternative. **Rule 6 (niche) — FAIL:** "any of the tens of millions of MA enrollees receiving any letter this fall" is broad, not a defined niche. **Rule 9 (over-mined shape) — FAIL:** this is another instance of the already-flagged "a regulation/market shift just happened → build a calculator/decoder for it" pattern, now failed on click-to-cancel, EU AI Act, EU CRA, tariffs, insurance-navigation, and Meta AI-disclosure tools in prior cycles. **Rule 2 (LTV/renewing) — weak fail:** the product is used once per letter, at most annually; inflow (new MA-exit-affected people each year) is real but retention doesn't follow from the product itself. **Rule 4 (not thin wrapper) — weak fail:** "translate this document + generate a checklist" is close to a bare LLM-call wrapper with no accumulated per-user context. **Rule 5 (referral) — weak:** no natural double-sided mechanic. **Rule 7 (reputationally clean) — PASS.** **Rule 8 (tellable story) — soft pass:** "helped seniors avoid a coverage gap" is fine but not a distinctive distribution lesson. 1 clean pass out of 9.
- **Sources:** [Becker's Payer — Humana 2027 exits](https://www.beckerspayer.com/payer/medicare-advantage/humana-to-exit-medicare-advantage-plans-covering-600000-members-in-2027/), [Forbes — MA plan exits escalating](https://www.forbes.com/sites/brucejapsen/2026/08/02/thousands-of-seniors-may-lose-medicare-advantage-as-plan-exits-escalate/), [myplanfit — Aetna exits](https://myplanfit.com/aetna-is-exiting-medicare-markets/), [leadgen-economy — 2026 AEP carrier exits](https://www.leadgen-economy.com/blog/medicare-advantage-2026-aep-carrier-exits-uhc-humana-aetna/), [KFF — role of SHIPs](https://www.kff.org/medicare/the-role-of-ships-in-helping-people-with-medicare-navigate-their-coverage/), [Agent CRM — Meta Special Ad Category / insurance](https://blog.agent-crm.com/navigating-meta-ads-restrictions-for-insurance-agents-facebook-and-instagram-marketing-updates/), [24/7 Wall St. — MA letters this fall](https://247wallst.com/personal-finance/2026/07/31/medicare-advantage-insurers-are-redrawing-the-map-for-2027-members-find-out-by-letter-this-fall/).
- **Kill reason:** Meta's Special Ad Category classification for insurance-adjacent ads strips the precise senior-targeting the idea's economics depend on — the same wall that killed the "Social Security claiming report" watch item — and independently, SHIP's free, government-funded, live one-on-one counseling (4.3M people/year) already serves the exact underlying need at a cost point (zero) no paid product beats.

### 2. App Store age-verification compliance wrapper for small/indie developers — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A hosted compliance layer + drop-in SDK wrapping Apple's Declared Age Range API and Google's Play Age Signals API, giving small app publishers a turnkey way to meet Texas/Utah/Louisiana App Store Accountability Act requirements (age-tier detection, parental-consent flows, automatic privacy-setting restriction for minors) without building the logic themselves.
- **Risks:** The two platforms that created the compliance burden already shipped the actual hard part (the age-signal source of truth) as free native OS APIs; a turnkey third-party wrapper for exactly this use case already exists.
- **Business pain (quantified):** Real but narrowing fast. Three states have enacted App Store Accountability Acts and are now all live: Texas (Jan 1, 2026), Utah (May 7, 2026), Louisiana (Jul 1, 2026), each with different specifics (Utah leans hardest into mandatory parental-consent-per-feature; Texas focuses on 18+/harmful-content gating). More states have introduced similar bills this legislative session, but developer pushback (cited by Axios) means the count and final requirements are still moving.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** The laws are new — Texas's Jan 1, 2026 effective date is the first hard compliance deadline in this category, so this is a genuinely fresh trigger, not a recycled one.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do, and they're the platforms themselves. Apple shipped the Declared Age Range API plus a Significant Change API; Google shipped the Play Age Signals API (beta) — both released specifically to let *any* developer comply without building age-verification infrastructure from scratch, which is the entire value proposition this idea would charge for.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it already isn't unowned:** Median.co's "AgeSafety" native plugin already ships a single JavaScript integration that wraps both Apple's and Google's APIs for exactly this compliance need, bundled inside a broader (already-paid) native-app-wrapper product. Multiple law firms (FKKS, Loeb & Loeb, Wiley, McDermott) publish free compliance guides and comparison charts covering the state-by-state differences.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** More states will likely pass similar laws given the TX/UT/LA precedent, growing the nominal addressable market — but Apple and Google have shown they extend their own native APIs proactively (already covering 3 states plus Australia), which keeps shrinking a third-party wrapper's differentiation rather than growing it.
- **Competitors:** Apple (Declared Age Range API, Significant Change API — free, native), Google (Play Age Signals API, beta — free, native), Median.co (AgeSafety plugin, bundled into a paid product), assorted law-firm compliance guides (free, informational).
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** Small-to-mid app publishers building fully native (non-webview) apps who can't build the consent-flow UI themselves — a thin buyer pool once Median.co's existing customers and Apple/Google's own documentation are subtracted out; founder-led content/SEO sales (targeting "app store accountability act compliance" searches), not self-serve at scale, and a structurally different motion than Lance's Meta-ads-to-checkout playbook.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Not modeled in depth — the free-API-plus-one-existing-wrapper baseline caps willingness to pay before any pricing test is worth running.
- **Time to first sellable version:** Weeks (call two documented, well-supported platform APIs, render a settings/consent screen) — the same fast-build/fast-replicate risk pattern already seen with the Shieldstral wrapper kill: anyone, including the platforms themselves, can ship the same thing in the same window.
- **Operational load:** Moderate — mostly staying current with each new state's specific parental-consent workflow variant as more states pass their own version of the law with different thresholds and mechanics.
- **Sources:** [FKKS — countdown to Jan 1, 2026 compliance](https://technologylaw.fkks.com/post/102lxsp/countdown-to-jan-1-2026-mobile-developers-must-adopt-apple-google-apis-to-com), [ComplianceHub.Wiki — Google/Apple age-check tech](https://compliancehub.wiki/google-adds-age-check-tech-as-texas-utah-and-louisiana-enforce-digital-id-laws/), [Median.co — age verification laws guide](https://median.co/blog/new-age-verification-laws-2026), [Median.co — AgeSafety plugin docs](https://docs.median.co/docs/age-safety), [Axios — developer pushback on app store age-verification bill](https://www.axios.com/local/huntsville/2026/01/29/developers-push-back-on-app-store-age-verification-bill).
- **Kill reason:** Apple and Google already ship the actually-regulated part (the age-signal source of truth) as free native OS APIs across all three enacted states, and a turnkey third-party wrapper for indie developers (Median.co's AgeSafety plugin) already exists — the sixth instance this project has found of the B2C_RULES Rule 9 "a regulation just changed → build a compliance tool for it" shape failing (after click-to-cancel, EU AI Act, EU CRA, Meta AI-disclosure, and the tariff/insurance-navigation sweep). Not formally scored against B2C_RULES since this is a B2B idea, but the Rule 9 logic — "is this shape one the market has already piled into" — applies directly and kills it before unit economics are worth modeling.

### 3. AI interview-fraud / deepfake-candidate detection for employers — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** Real-time proctoring/detection layer for remote video interviews that flags AI-generated live answers, hidden-earpiece coaching, and full face-swap/voice-clone synthetic candidates, sold to mid-market employers and staffing agencies running high-volume remote hiring.
- **Risks:** Already a multi-vendor category with a live per-interview price anchor and at least one enterprise-focused dedicated competitor shipping near-identical detection claims; the buyer (mid-market/enterprise TA teams via ATS integrations) requires a slower sales motion Lance has no track record in.
- **Business pain (quantified, vendor-reported — flagged as such):** 38.5% of candidates were flagged for AI-cheating behavior across 19,368 tracked interviews (Jul 2025–Jan 2026), a rate industry blogs describe as having tripled in three months; deepfake fraud attempts in hiring reportedly up 1,300% YoY; 31% of hiring managers say they've already interviewed someone they believed was synthetic; 62% of hiring professionals admit candidates are now better at faking with AI than recruiters are at catching it; unaided humans reportedly spot deepfakes at only ~55.5% accuracy, barely above chance. A documented Feb 2026 near-miss (cybersecurity firm Evoke AI nearly hired a fully synthetic candidate) is cited as the canonical real-world case.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Real-time face-swap and voice-clone injection into live video calls, combined with LLM-generated live coaching, crossed a usability threshold in roughly the last 12–18 months — genuinely new capability, not a repackaged old problem.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They're the ones building it. This isn't a gap major ATS platforms left open — several point-solution vendors already moved fast and shipped dedicated products, and the underlying video platforms (Zoom, Teams, Meet) could add basic detection as a feature at will since they own the base video layer.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it already is:** Sherlock launched on Product Hunt and prices at $2/interview for a full "interview intelligence suite" (fraud detection + AI-fluency scoring + auto-notes, integrated directly with Zoom/Meet/Teams). Talview ships a dedicated "Deepfake Interviews Detection" enterprise product claiming a 60% improvement in real-time fraud detection [vendor-claimed]. Metaview (an interview-intelligence incumbent) is publishing deepfake-detection content and features into its existing product. Truffle/QBS, Incruiter, and Alex AI all cover the same ground this same season.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** The generation/detection arms race continues indefinitely — a new entrant would be permanently behind funded specialists doing nothing else full-time; video platforms may also bundle basic detection natively, the same absorption pattern already seen this cycle with OpenAI folding its Atlas browser back into core ChatGPT/Chrome.
- **Competitors:** Sherlock ($2/interview, Zoom/Meet/Teams integration), Talview (enterprise deepfake-interview detection), Metaview (interview intelligence + detection content), Truffle/QBS, Incruiter, Alex AI.
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** Mid-market/enterprise talent-acquisition teams and staffing agencies, sold via ATS integrations and founder-led/channel sales — a fundamentally different, slower motion than Lance's Meta-ads-to-web-checkout consumer funnel, in a market where 2+ funded specialists already have integration and distribution head starts.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Not modeled — a per-interview-priced, integration-dependent enterprise sale doesn't map to any channel Lance currently has, and the existing $2/interview anchor from Sherlock caps pricing room before a build is justified.
- **Time to first sellable version:** Weeks for a shallow version, but reaching parity with the multi-layer detection already shipped (keyboard dynamics, eye-tracking, audio-environment analysis) is a real ML engineering lift, not a weekend wrapper — and even at parity, it would arrive into an already-crowded field.
- **Operational load:** Moderate-high — real-time video/audio inference infrastructure, plus false-positive-rate tuning, since wrongly flagging an honest candidate is a legal and reputational risk for any vendor selling into hiring decisions.
- **Sources:** [The Interview Guys — State of Hiring Fraud 2026](https://blog.theinterviewguys.com/the-state-of-hiring-fraud-2026-when-38-5-of-candidates-are-cheating/), [Metaview — deepfake interviews blog](https://www.metaview.ai/resources/blog/deepfake-interviews), [Sherlock — pricing](https://www.sherlock.sh/pricing), [Sherlock — Product Hunt launch](https://www.producthunt.com/products/sherlock-5), [Talview — deepfake interviews detection](https://www.talview.com/en/deepfake-interviews-detection), [InterviewMan — AI interview cheating statistics 2026](https://interviewman.com/blog/ai-interview-cheating-statistics-2026).
- **Kill reason:** The category is already multi-vendor and price-anchored — Sherlock ships a full detection suite at $2/interview with direct Zoom/Meet/Teams integration, and Talview already runs a dedicated enterprise deepfake-detection product making near-identical claims to what this idea would pitch — so the "why isn't it saturated" test fails outright before the sales-motion mismatch (mid-market/enterprise TA teams via ATS integrations, nothing like Lance's proven consumer-checkout funnel) is even weighed.

**Bottom line:** Sixth straight cycle, zero survivors — but the pattern in *how* these three failed is worth naming precisely, since it's now showing up consistently enough to change how candidates get triaged before a deep-dive: two of three died to a "the platform/regulator that created the pain already gave away the fix for free" wall (Apple/Google's own compliance APIs; SHIP's free counseling), and the third died to "funded point-solution vendors already move faster than a solo entrant can, in a category the underlying platforms could also absorb at will" (interview-fraud detection, echoing the earlier ROOST/ContentGuard kill of the moderation-wrapper idea). Going forward, before committing deep-dive time: for any regulation-triggered idea, check what the regulated platform itself shipped in response before checking for third-party competitors — in two entries this cycle, that single check would have killed the idea in five minutes instead of thirty.
