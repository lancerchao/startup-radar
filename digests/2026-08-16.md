## Ideas

Three candidates deep-dived this cycle, all rejected — the twelfth straight cycle with zero survivors. Each looked genuinely fresh from the breadth scan (a new App Store policy, a hard API-deprecation deadline, a widely-covered demographic report) and died the way this project keeps re-finding: the headline framing overstated the real opportunity once traced to primary sources, and/or a near-identical competitor was already live.

### 1. App Store delisting-risk monitor for indie/portfolio publishers — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A dashboard/alert tool for indie and portfolio iOS publishers that tracks each app's update cadence and download trend against Apple's delisting-risk criteria, warning before removal.
- **Risks:** No moat — incumbents already track the needed signals; redundant with Apple's own free 90-day warning for the well-defined half of the risk; the genuinely new half has no published threshold to build against; target population skews unable/unwilling to pay; the fix is a one-time update, undermining subscription retention.
- **Pain (quantified):** Real trigger, correctly dated (June 8, 2026 Guideline 4.3(b) change), but conflates two mechanisms. The monitorable one — 3+ years no update AND near-zero downloads over a rolling 12-month window — has existed since 2016; Apple already emails a 90-day warning before removing under it, and has removed ~2.8M apps this way over 6 years. The new discretionary half (4.3(b), saturated novelty categories) has no published thresholds or appeal process at all — nothing to monitor against. ~500-650k Apple apps meet the "abandoned" (2-3yr no update) definition (Pixalate), but median indie iOS app revenue is <$500/mo and 81% never reach $1,000 MRR (RevenueCat 2025) — weak ability to pay in exactly the population most "at risk."
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Partially — the discretionary 4.3(b) clause is new, but it has no monitorable mechanics; the actually-buildable half (the 2016-era Improvements process) was equally buildable two years ago.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** Apple has no incentive (runs its own free warning system already). ASO incumbents don't ship this as a named feature yet, but already own the exact data (update timelines, download trends) and the paying multi-app-portfolio customer segment.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it effectively is:** No dedicated "delisting risk alert" product found, but Appfigures ($0-179/mo) already tracks update timelines and portfolio analytics for the identical customer, and AppFollow ($139+/mo) tracks the review/ranking side — a checkbox feature away from shipping this themselves, with distribution and trust a new entrant lacks. Adjacent content plays (ourcodeworld, appscalelab, enzuzo, krensh, capgo.app) already claimed the "App Store 2026 policy" SEO terms within weeks of the trigger.
- **What will change in 2 years:** Apple will likely publish clearer 4.3(b) thresholds under developer pressure (it did this for the Improvements process in 2022) — once public, this becomes a trivial incumbent feature, closing the window further.
- **Competitors:** Appfigures, AppFollow, Sensor Tower/Apptweak (enterprise tier), capgo.app, plus SEO-established content sites (ourcodeworld, appscalelab, enzuzo, krensh).
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** Only portfolio owners/app-flippers with 10+ monetized long-tail apps (low thousands of individuals/studios globally) — reached via SEO/content/indie-dev newsletters, not Meta-ads-reachable.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** CAC ~$50-150 via content (no paid-ads channel); LTV weak (one-time-fix dynamics); realistic ARR ceiling in the low hundreds of thousands even in a good scenario, overlapping almost entirely with Appfigures' existing customer base.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** 4-6 weeks technically — the same speed that lets an incumbent copy it in a sprint. Low build maintenance, but real support load from anxious owners near removal deadlines.
- **Sources:** [Apple Developer — Guidelines 4.3(b)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), [Apple Developer — App Store Improvements](https://developer.apple.com/support/app-store-improvements/), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/09/apple-says-it-may-remove-apps-from-the-app-store-if-they-dont-attract-users/), [Pixalate Q4 2023 Abandoned Apps Report](https://www.pixalate.com/blog/pixalates-q4-2023-abandoned-mobile-apps-report-apple-app-store-google-play-store), [Appfigures pricing](https://appfigures.com/platform/pricing), [AppFollow vs Appfigures](https://appfollow.io/compare-appfollow-vs-appfigures), [capgo.app](https://capgo.app/blog/capacitor-ota-updates-staying-compliant/).
- **Kill reason:** The monitorable half of "delisting risk" is a mechanism Apple has run free since 2016 with its own 90-day warning; the genuinely new half has no published threshold to monitor against; and the signals a startup would need are already owned by Appfigures/AppFollow, who also already have the only customer segment with money to pay — leaving the startup with the poorest, most-likely-already-abandoned population, i.e. no one left to bill.

### 2. Assistants API → Responses API migration tool — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A tool to automate converting an app's OpenAI Assistants-API integration (threads, runs, file search, code interpreter, function calling) to the Responses API, sold to developers scrambling before the Aug 26, 2026 shutdown.
- **Risks:** The urgent window already closed months before this digest cycle; a live, better-positioned competitor exists; the product is structurally one-time, not recurring; a competent developer can likely self-serve the migration with an LLM faster than vetting and paying a new tool.
- **Pain (quantified):** Real and dated — Aug 26, 2026 is a hard, permanent cutoff (announced Aug 26, 2025, so a full year's notice, not a surprise). No published count of Assistants-API-specific production apps exists, but third-party platforms are independently unwinding integrations: Zapier has already deprecated all Assistants-API ChatGPT steps (breaks Aug 26, manual rebuild, no auto-migration); Zoho's community deprecation thread has 100+ replies.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** The deadline is real, but the acute "build a business around this" window was 6-12 months ago — official OpenAI migration guides and third-party wire-compatible alternatives were already live by January-March 2026, 4-6 months before this cycle.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** OpenAI already ships an official migration guide with full request/response mapping and has feature-complete parity in Responses API; it also ships an official Codex-CLI-driven auto-migration tool for Chat Completions → Responses (106 GitHub stars) — it simply hasn't extended that specific tool to cover Assistants' stateful thread/run migration, a narrow, legitimate gap (thread history is customer data OpenAI can't safely auto-migrate).
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it is:** Ragwalla already ships a live, wire-compatible drop-in replacement — point existing Assistants-API code at their base URL with zero rewrite, "indefinitely" supported — strictly better for an anxious developer than a migration tool with regression risk. Dozens of free SEO-optimized migration guides are already live (ClonePartner, Ragwalla, Syntackle, UXContinuum, APIScout, and others). Two free OSS migration tools exist for this exact problem with 0-1 GitHub stars each — direct evidence of negligible pull even at $0.
- **What will change in 2 years:** Nothing favorable for this specific window — it self-destructs Aug 26, 2026. The pattern (provider forces an API migration) will recur with future API version bumps, but each recurrence needs a fresh, narrow, time-boxed tool; no durable platform sits under this specific instance.
- **Competitors:** Ragwalla (wire-compatible drop-in replacement, live since ~Jan 2026), OpenAI's own official migration guide + Codex migration pack, ClonePartner/Syntackle/UXContinuum/APIScout/SocialCrawl/Developers Digest (free guides), sudo7Raghav/DeprecationBot and muratcankuruoffical's migration repo (free OSS, near-zero adoption).
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** Straggler developers who haven't migrated in 6-12 months of runway and don't want the Ragwalla shim — a narrow, shrinking-by-the-day, low-willingness-to-pay segment; no time left to build SEO or outbound distribution before the deadline passes.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** LTV effectively one-time (no recurring need post-migration). CAC high relative to deal size given zero runway for organic channels. ARR ceiling: low five figures at best, then the market permanently disappears Aug 26.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** 3-7 days to an MVP, leaving 0-3 days to find and close paying customers before the window closes — high risk of shipping after demand evaporates. Each engagement likely needs hands-on debugging of framework-specific code (LangChain wrappers, custom polling), closer to consulting than scalable SaaS.
- **Sources:** [OpenAI — Migration guide](https://platform.openai.com/docs/guides/migrate-to-responses), [OpenAI — Assistants migration docs](https://platform.openai.com/docs/assistants/migration), [OpenAI Developer Community — deprecation notice](https://community.openai.com/t/assistants-api-beta-deprecation-august-26-2026-sunset/1354666), [openai/completions-responses-migration-pack (GitHub)](https://github.com/openai/completions-responses-migration-pack), [Ragwalla — migration guide](https://ragwalla.com/docs/guides/openai-assistants-api-deprecation-2026-migration-guide-wire-compatible-alternatives), [ClonePartner](https://clonepartner.com/blog/openai-assistants-api-shutdown-the-2026-migration-guide/), [Zoho community deprecation thread](https://help.zoho.com/portal/en/community/topic/deprecation-notice-openai-assistants-api-will-be-shut-down-on-august-26-2026).
- **Kill reason:** The scramble-driven opportunity already closed 4-6 months before this cycle — official guides, a live wire-compatible competitor with a head start, and multiple free migration guides were all in place by Q1 2026, and even free OSS tools built for this exact problem show 0-1 GitHub stars, i.e. negligible pull even at zero cost. At T-10 days, remaining demand is a shrinking, low-pay straggler population and the product is structurally non-recurring.

### 3. Succession-readiness kit for 55+ small business owners — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A self-serve, one-time-purchase digital product (course + templates + light valuation/checklist) sold directly to small-business owners 55+ with no succession plan, helping them get "sale-ready."
- **Risks:** A near-identical product is already live at the target price point; the project's own core distribution channel for this exact audience closed in January 2026; one-time purchase with no renewal mechanic; the documented real friction is emotional avoidance, not lack of a checklist.
- **Consumer pain (quantified):** Real demographic shift, overstated headline. McKinsey's "Great Ownership Transfer" (Feb 2026): of ~6M businesses facing ownership transition by 2035, only ~1M are judged "viable for sale or employee ownership" — the $5T figure belongs to that ~1M subset, not the 6M headline widely quoted. 92% of 2022 small-business exits ended in closure, not sale. The "no succession plan" rate ranges 32%-54% "have a plan" depending on source (Exit Planning Institute survey vs. an unrelated U.S. Bank survey commonly cited alongside the McKinsey number) — a 20+ point unreconciled spread.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Weak — the McKinsey report is a fresh Feb 2026 press hook, but the "silver tsunami" succession narrative has circulated in the industry since the late 2010s; nothing structurally new for the product itself.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do, on every side — SBA/SCORE and BizBuySell serve the free end at real scale (SCORE: 300K+ mentoring clients/yr), SuccessionSuite already serves the exact paid self-serve end, and CEPA-certified CPAs/wealth advisors serve the professional-services end, often bundled free as a client-retention play.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it is:** SuccessionSuite (successionsuite.com) already ships a free "Exit Readiness Toolkit" lead magnet plus $199 and $499 paid tiers — checklist, financial scorecard, templates, AI prompt generator — the identical product at the identical price band. "Exit Ready" ($449 course) is a second direct comp. BizBuySell, Transworld, and Sunbelt all give away comp-based valuations as broker lead-gen.
- **What will change in 2 years:** The trigger intensifies (transition wave peaking 2027-28), which raises competitive intensity, not distribution ease — and Meta's targeting restrictions are trending tighter, not looser.
- **Competitors:** SuccessionSuite, "Exit Ready" course, BizBuySell, Transworld Business Advisors, Sunbelt Business Brokers, Exit Planning Institute / CEPA advisor network, SCORE/SBA, CPA/wealth-advisory firms (CLA, Focus Partners, FP Transitions, Rehmann).
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** The individual owner, self-funded, one-time, at a $0-499 price band already set by SuccessionSuite — but the acquisition channel that would make this Lance's kind of play is gone: Meta removed "Small Business Owners" as a detailed-targeting interest category in January 2026 (see World watch), leaving only contested SEO terms where business brokers and M&A advisors — backed by five/six-figure commission economics — can outbid a $200-500 product's margin.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** CAC high and rising (no precise paid-social targeting left). LTV capped at a one-time price. Realistic ARR ceiling low-to-mid six figures at best against a true addressable pool closer to ~1M "viable for sale" owners, further narrowed by those not already served by an accountant/broker/SCORE.
- **B2C gate check (all 9):** Rule 1 (Meta-targetable) — **FAIL**, the qualifying category was removed Jan 2026. Rule 2 (renewing LTV + inflow) — **FAIL** on retention, one-and-done purchase. Rule 3 (moat) — **FAIL**, SuccessionSuite is a live clone. Rule 4 (not thin wrapper) — marginal, the valuation layer is easy to AI-wrap but free comp-based versions already exist. Rule 5 (referral mechanic) — **FAIL**, no aligned/forced-multiplayer/shareable-artifact loop. Rule 6 (niche) — **FAIL**, spans roughly half of all US small businesses. Rule 7 (reputationally clean) — pass, with a liability-framing caution (don't imply substitute for licensed valuation/legal advice). Rule 8 (tellable story) — weak, replays an already-run Gumroad playbook. Rule 9 (over-mined shape) — **FAIL**, the same "headline-stat → checklist/kit" pattern already killed 5+ times on this ledger. **0 of 9 clear passes; fails 6 outright.**
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** 2-4 weeks technically — the same speed that let SuccessionSuite already exist. Low content maintenance; real liability-adjacent support risk given the product touches retirement-stakes financial decisions.
- **Sources:** [McKinsey — The Great Ownership Transfer](https://www.mckinsey.com/institute-for-economic-mobility/our-insights/the-great-ownership-transfer-a-new-era-of-business-stewardship), [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/martinadilicosa/2026/02/26/millions-of-small-businesses-soon-changing-hands-as-baby-boomers-retire-in-great-ownership-transfer-report-says/), [U.S. Bancorp Small Business Survey 2025](https://ir.usbank.com/news-events/news/news-details/2025/U-S--Bank-Small-Business-Survey-Finds-More-Than-One-Third-of-Gen-Z-and-Millennial-Owners-Plan-to-Acquire-a-Business-From-a-Retiring-Owner--But-Many-Older-Owners-Arent-Ready/default.aspx), [Gallup](https://news.gallup.com/poll/657362/small-business-owners-lack-succession-plan.aspx), [SuccessionSuite](https://www.successionsuite.com/), [SuccessionSuite+ ($199)](https://successionsuite.gumroad.com/l/succession-suite-plus), [SuccessionSuite Elite ($499)](https://successionsuite.gumroad.com/l/succession-suite-elite), [BizBuySell — Guide to Selling Your Business](https://www.bizbuysell.com/seller/guide/selling-a-business/), [Exit Planning Institute — CEPA](https://exit-planning-institute.org/program/certified-exit-planning-advisor), [SCORE — Succession Planning e-guide](https://www.score.org/e-guides/small-business-owners-guide-succession-planning/), [Jon Loomer — Meta Ads Targeting 2026](https://www.jonloomer.com/meta-ads-targeting-2026/), [Psychology Today — Why Business Owners Delay Exit Planning](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/leadership-diversity-and-wellness/202604/why-business-owners-delay-exit-planning).
