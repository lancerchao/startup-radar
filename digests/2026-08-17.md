### 1. Section 338 Canada tariff HS-code exposure scanner — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A tool that ingests a small importer's HS/HTS codes and product catalog, cross-references against the July 2026 Section 338 proclamations' Annex II lists, and flags exposure plus estimated new landed cost.
- **Risks:** The underlying tariff is explicitly framed by trade counsel as a negotiating "opening bid," not a stable rule; untested legal authority invites near-certain litigation; the market already filled in within weeks of signing; single-country, single-proclamation scope is trivial for incumbents to add as a feature.
- **Pain (quantified):** USTR-sourced: ~$20B of the $382B in 2025 US goods imports from Canada affected (~5.2%), pushing Canada's average US tariff rate to 6.27%. ~500-569 HTSUS subheadings across dairy, alcohol, autos, furniture, and more. No source — primary or secondary — quantifies the number of *small* US importer businesses affected or the per-business dollar impact; much of the named $20B (autos, national dairy processors, major beverage importers) flows through large enterprises already running Avalara/Thomson Reuters-class compliance stacks, so the true SMB slice is an unquantified fraction of an already-narrow number.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Genuinely new — first-ever invocation of the dormant Section 338 authority, chosen as a fallback after the Supreme Court struck down IEEPA tariffs in Feb 2026. But trade counsel (Holland & Knight) assesses two of the three underlying disputes as "procedurally simple" for Canada to resolve inside the mandatory USMCA Article 34.7 review already underway, with USTR targeting interim arrangements by year-end 2026 — this reads as a fast-closing window, the same pattern that's killed prior tariff-reaction ideas on this ledger.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do, trivially — adding one more HTSUS subheading list to an existing Chapter-99 duty-stacking engine is a data update, not new engineering. Avalara and Thomson Reuters ONESOURCE both published Section 338 guidance within days and already sell AI-assisted HS classification at enterprise scale.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it already is:** A free, no-signup "Tariff Impact Checker" covering Section 301/232/338 exposure was live and indexed within 4 weeks of signing (aacb.com), alongside a dedicated calculator naming the Canada Section 338 duty explicitly (tariffcalculator2026.com), HTSUS-subheading-level explainer content, and multiple customs-broker/consultant sites running Section 338 guidance as advisory lead-gen — all found in a single search pass, 2 days before the tariff's own effective date.
- **What will change in 2 years:** Most likely partial or full rollback tied to the USMCA review; second most likely, litigation (called "near-certain" by trade-policy watchers since no court has ever construed Section 338's "discrimination" trigger); less likely but real, escalation to a full import ban under Section 338(b). Any path obsoletes a narrowly-scoped point solution.
- **Competitors:** aacb.com Tariff Impact Checker, tariffcalculator2026.com, CustomsGenius, tariffstool.com, carraglobe.com (free calculators/content); Avalara, Thomson Reuters ONESOURCE, Flexport, Zonos (enterprise trade-compliance incumbents); GHY International, Crane Worldwide, Anderinger, Peacock Tariff Consulting (brokers/consultants running this as lead-gen).
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** Ambiguous, likely nobody durably — free tools already answer "am I exposed," and a small importer's own customs broker will answer it free as a relationship-building service. Any real payment would be for deeper mitigation consulting, which brokers already offer; not Meta-targetable, SEO-only distribution against incumbents with years of accumulated trade-topic domain authority.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** CAC high and rising (SEO-only, already contested). LTV low — the tariff itself may not outlive 6-12 months per trade counsel's own timeline. ARR ceiling low as a point solution; reframing as a general multi-program tariff monitor raises the ceiling but then competes head-on with Avalara/Thomson Reuters/Descartes rather than filling a gap.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** Technically days (match a catalog against a published list), but shipping now lands at least 4 weeks behind a dozen-plus already-indexed free competitors — the lost distribution window, not the build, is the real bottleneck. Ongoing maintenance load is high relative to any plausible revenue: must track Federal Register amendments, CBP rulings, and any injunction or rollback, each capable of invalidating the tool's output overnight.
- **Sources:** [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/23/2026-14997/imposing-additional-duties-to-offset-canadian-discrimination-against-the-commerce-of-the-united), [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/trump-administration-imposes-50-tariffs-certain-canadian-products-first-use-section), [Holland & Knight — "opening bid"](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/07/50-percent-opening-bid-canadian-imports-subject-to-section-338-tariffs), [Global Trade Alert](https://globaltradealert.org/blog/section-338-canada-august-2026), [Volokh/Reason — litigation risk](https://reason.com/volokh/2026/08/03/prospective-legal-challenges-to-trumps-section-338-tariffs-against-canada/), [Tech Times — IEEPA→338 pivot](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/321171/20260721/section-338-invoked-trumps-50-canada-tariffs-bypass-courts-that-killed-ieepa.htm), [aacb.com Tariff Impact Checker](https://www.aacb.com/tariff-impact-checker), [Avalara — Section 338 explainer](https://www.avalara.com/blog/en/north-america/2026/07/what-are-section-338-tariffs.html.html).
- **Kill reason:** Free, no-signup exposure checkers built by parties with far more distribution (law firms, brokers, freight forwarders using it as lead-gen) were already live within weeks of signing and days before the tariff's own effective date — and the underlying tariff is explicitly framed by trade counsel as a fast-closing negotiating position facing near-certain litigation, leaving no realistic runway to build a defensible paying customer base before either the SEO window closes or the tariff itself changes.

### 2. SMB/DTC vendor-breach exposure monitor — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A monitoring service where a small business or DTC brand lists the vendors it depends on (3PL, payments, marketing tools); the product tracks public breach disclosures across that vendor list and alerts the customer when one is compromised, priced below enterprise vendor-risk-management platforms.
- **Risks:** Breach-disclosure data is inherently reactive — a vendor has already disclosed to press/regulators before this product can "alert" anyone, so it competes with free news coverage rather than preventing anything; false-negative liability if the tool misses a quiet disclosure; the closest direct analog has stayed tiny for 8 years, a strong signal the category itself doesn't monetize rather than that no one built it well.
- **Pain (quantified):** Real but diffuse. Verizon's 2025 DBIR: third-party involvement in breaches doubled from 15% to 30% of all breaches year over year. Post-breach incident-response cost for a small business runs $15K-50K for recovery, $50K-200K if deep forensics are needed — but that's the cost of *being* breached, not the marginal cost of hearing about a vendor's breach a few days later than free press coverage would tell you, which is the actual value this product adds.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Weaker than it first looked. Two of the three "August 2026 cluster" breaches (Framework, and Trezor via its vendor ShipMonk) trace to the same single Metabase zero-day (CVE-2026-72898) — one shared-dependency incident, not a structural rise in vendor-breach rate. The third cited breach ("Marquis") is misdated — the actual intrusion was Aug 14, 2025, with notices going out March 2026, not an August 2026 event at all.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do, just bundled into products the target buyer is already drifting toward for other reasons. Vanta ships a "TPRM Agent" that continuously monitors a customer's vendor landscape for breaches and auto-drafts remediation, bundled into the compliance platform many small SaaS/e-commerce-adjacent companies already buy for SOC 2. Whistic ships an identically-pitched "Vendor Monitoring" feature. SpyCloud launched enterprise-priced "Supply Chain Threat Protection" in Jan 2026 for the same signal.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it's been tried and stalled, not left open:** HackNotice is the closest real analog — personalized real-time third-party breach alerts for businesses — and eight years and a $7M Series A (2022) later is still ~19-24 employees on ~$2.1M revenue (2025). That's a company that found this niche's ceiling, not one proving it's wide open.
- **What will change in 2 years:** Third-party-breach share of total breaches likely keeps climbing per the DBIR trend, but Vanta/Whistic-style bundling of breach monitoring into compliance suites will likely keep pushing this feature further downmarket for free, compressing any standalone product's price ceiling further.
- **Competitors:** HackNotice (SMB-tier, stalled ~$2M ARR); Vanta TPRM Agent, Whistic Vendor Monitoring (bundled, free-to-existing-customers); SpyCloud Supply Chain Threat Protection, UpGuard, SecurityScorecard, Panorays, Onspring, Riskonnect (enterprise, $20K-78K/yr — the last four already surveyed and rejected for this ledger's adjacent "AI/SaaS vendor shutdown-risk monitor" entry, killed 2026-08-16); a 20-minute Google Alerts setup as the free substitute, alongside the fact every breach verified this cycle got fast, broad tech-press coverage within days.
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** DTC brand owners / small e-commerce operators — a real but low-urgency, insurance-like buyer closer to a compliance checkbox than an active pain; content/SEO distribution ("was your vendor in the X breach"), not Meta-ads-native.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** CAC ~$200-400 (security/compliance content converts worse than lifestyle/ops tools). LTV low — per-customer trigger events are infrequent, so post-signup urgency fades fast, the same churn dynamic that killed the sibling shutdown-risk idea; roughly $150-350 LTV at $20-40/mo against plausible 50-70%+ annual churn. LTV:CAC roughly 1:1 to 1.5:1 — not a fundable ratio without a much cheaper channel. ARR ceiling low single-digit millions at best — HackNotice's $2.1M after 8 years and $7M raised is the closest real data point.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** 4-8 weeks for an MVP (vendor-list intake matched against public breach feeds); the hard part is data-source coverage and precision, not the build. Ongoing operational load is closer to running a small research/media operation than a set-and-forget SaaS — continuous curation across fragmented state AG portals, press, and vendor-taxonomy matching, with real liability if a miss reaches a customer who relied on it.
- **Sources:** [BleepingComputer — Trezor breach](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/trezor-discloses-data-breach-affecting-nearly-14-000-customers/), [TheHackerNews — Metabase CVE-2026-72898](https://thehackernews.com/2026/08/metabase-zero-day-exploited-in-wild.html), [The Register — Framework/Metabase](https://www.theregister.com/personal-tech/2026/08/10/framework-loses-customer-data-in-metabase-zero-day-attack/5285302), [TechCrunch — Marquis, correctly dated](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/18/marquis-says-over-672000-people-had-personal-and-financial-data-stolen-in-ransomware-attack/), [Verizon 2025 DBIR](https://www.verizon.com/about/news/2025-data-breach-investigations-report), [Vanta TPRM Agent](https://help.vanta.com/en/articles/12524282-continuous-monitoring-of-vendor-risk), [Whistic Vendor Monitoring](https://www.whistic.com/vendor-monitoring), [SpyCloud launch](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/01/14/spycloud-supply-chain-threat-protection/), [HackNotice — Crunchbase](https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/hacknotice), [HackNotice revenue — Getlatka](https://getlatka.com/companies/hacknotice.com).
- **Kill reason:** The paid tier is already occupied by tools the target buyer is drifting toward for other reasons (Vanta/Whistic bundle this into compliance platforms), the free substitute (fast press coverage plus a 20-minute Google Alerts setup) captures most of the actual value, and the closest SMB-tier precedent (HackNotice) spent 8 years and a $7M raise proving the standalone category caps out around $2M ARR — the same "too rare per customer, incumbent already ships it as a feature" failure mode that killed this ledger's sibling shutdown-risk idea one cycle earlier, via the same two incumbents (Vanta, Whistic). These two ideas should likely be treated as one already-rejected product shape, not two.
- **Not scored against B2C_RULES** — B2B idea, buyer is a small business, not a personal consumer.

Payability, 8fig, Viably, SellersFi, and Wayflyer already cover the smaller-seller/faster-underwriting end. No eligibility gap, ticket-size gap, or distribution angle left uncovered — not re-added to the ledger.

**Bottom line:** Thirteenth straight cycle, zero survivors. Both full deep-dives this cycle looked genuinely fresh from the breadth scan — a first-ever use of a dormant tariff authority, a cluster of vendor breaches — and both collapsed once traced to primary sources: the tariff scanner lost to free tools built by parties with structurally more distribution (law firms, brokers) within a month of the trigger, and the breach monitor turned out to be the same failure mode as a sibling idea this ledger killed one cycle earlier, via the same two incumbents. Building on the correction pattern flagged in the 2026-08-16 digest: two claims from other outlets needed real correction this cycle too (the "Marquis" breach was misdated by a full year in an earlier pass; "Slopepay" is not a new competitor, it's Slope under its own domain) — a reminder to verify company identity and event dates against primary sources before treating a breadth-scan finding as fresh.
