## Ideas

Two candidates deep-dived this cycle, both killed — the fifth straight cycle with zero survivors. Both looked genuinely fresh from the breadth scan (a newly-crossed adoption threshold, a newly-open-sourced model) and both died to a wall that had nothing to do with technical feasibility: one to a Meta ad-targeting policy that's been closed since 2022 and got stricter this year, one to prior art that's already ten months old plus a well-funded nonprofit giving the same thing away free.

### 1. GLP-1-driven consumer transition products (45–70 cohort) — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** One-time-purchase products for the surge in GLP-1 (Ozempic/Wegovy/Zepbound/Mounjaro) users needing help with rapid body-composition change — wardrobe/clothing-fit guidance, loose-skin/firming guidance, and nutrient-deficiency/muscle-preservation tracking, aimed at Lance's exact senior-skewed demographic.
- **Risks:** No viable Meta ad-targeting path exists for this audience at all; two of the three sub-niches are already free via scaled incumbents; the one genuinely open sub-niche (wardrobe) is a physical-goods fulfillment business, not a solo-founder-buildable digital product.
- **Consumer pain (quantified):** Real and large. Gallup (Jun 2026, n=5,065): 12.4% of US adults currently on a GLP-1, up from 5.8% in Feb 2024. JPMorgan: 25–30M US users by 2030 vs. ~10M today [bank-research projection]. 21% of US households have a GLP-1 user (May 2026, up from 9% Jan 2025). Circana survey (via CNBC): 80% of GLP-1 users expect to need new clothing. Up to 40% of weight lost on GLP-1s can be lean muscle — a documented clinical concern.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Adoption is genuinely accelerating (5.8%→12.4% in under 2.5 years) but this is a slow multi-year demographic trend, not a sharp discrete trigger — and it's one of the most heavily covered consumer trends of the last 12 months (CNBC, CBS News, PwC, eMarketer, Bernstein all published GLP-1-consumer-behavior analyses), not an underexplored corner.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do. Noom shipped a free "GLP-1 Companion" with "Muscle Defense™" (Jun 2026) open to any GLP-1 user regardless of provider — protein tracking, muscle-focused movement plans, side-effect support, at $0. MyFitnessPal shipped dedicated GLP-1 medication tracking. Stitch Fix and the tailoring/resale industry are already visibly adjusting for the wardrobe shift (alteration volume up 2x YoY per CNBC). SkinMedica, IMAGE Skincare, and the med-spa/derm industry have run GLP-1-specific firming campaigns since Aug 2025.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it isn't, in 2 of 3 sub-niches:** Nutrition/muscle tracking already has 5+ live apps (Pep, StrideFuel, Nutrola, Lina GLP-1 Tracker) plus Noom's free offering. Skincare/firming is incumbent-owned. Wardrobe is the one open gap, but it's a service/fulfillment business (tailoring, sizing logistics), a different execution shape than Lance's software-margin consumer funnel.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** Adoption keeps climbing toward JPMorgan's 25-30M estimate, but Meta's health-ad restrictions are tightening in the same window (see kill reason) — the regulatory trend moves the wrong direction for this idea's core assumption, not the right one.
- **Competitors:** Noom (GLP-1 Companion, free), MyFitnessPal, Pep, StrideFuel, Nutrola, Lina GLP-1 Tracker, SkinMedica/IMAGE Skincare/med-spa firming lines, Stitch Fix, tailors/resale shops.
- **Who pays / Expected CAC / LTV / LTV:CAC / ARR ceiling:** Not modeled — no reachable paid-acquisition channel to price against once the targeting gate fails.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** Not evaluated — moot given the targeting gate.
- **B2C gate check:** Fails Rule 1 decisively — Meta removed all health-condition/medication interest categories network-wide in January 2022 (no "Ozempic"/"Wegovy"/"GLP-1" targeting has existed since, independent of this trend), and 2026 made it stricter still: a new restricted "Weight Management" sub-category (Housing/Employment/Credit-style limits — no age/gender/zip exclusion, stripped detailed targeting), a Personal Attributes policy banning creative that implies knowledge of a viewer's health/body/weight status, and LegitScript certification plus Meta's prior written authorization required to reference GLP-1 drugs by name. The only legal path is broad, non-health-implying targeting indistinguishable from any general 45-70 wellness play — which defeats the idea's own premise. Independently fails Rule 2 (one-time-purchase by design, no renewing hook), Rule 3 (nothing proprietary; Noom already cloned the best piece for free), Rule 5 (no approved referral mechanic — the one shareable-artifact angle, before/after transformation content, runs into Meta's own weight-loss-imagery ban), Rule 6 (12.4% of all US adults is a broad demographic, not a niche), and Rule 9 (textbook instance of "a demographic/market shift just happened → build a tracker/guide/kit for it," the same reflexive pattern the ledger has already found saturated 5-for-5 in other verticals). 1 soft-pass (Rule 7) out of 9. Not scored further on unit economics once the targeting gate fails.
- **Sources:** [Forbes Health / Gallup — GLP-1 adoption stats](https://www.forbes.com/health/weight-loss/glp-1-statistics/), [JPMorgan — obesity-drug research](https://www.jpmorgan.com/insights/global-research/current-events/obesity-drugs), [Meta Transparency Center — health & wellness restricted-goods policy](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/), [CNBC — GLP-1 apparel impact](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/), [Noom — GLP-1 Companion / Muscle Defense](https://www.noom.com/), [MyFitnessPal GLP-1 tools](https://www.myfitnesspal.com/).
- **Kill reason:** Meta has had no health-condition or medication interest-targeting category since a January 2022 network-wide removal — "GLP-1 users" was never a targetable audience, and Meta's 2026 policy changes (a new restricted "Weight Management" sub-category, a ban on creative implying knowledge of viewer health status, LegitScript gating on drug-name references) move further toward restriction, not away from it — collapsing the idea's entire "new addressable audience" premise into generic broad targeting; independently, the two most defensible sub-niches (muscle-preservation tracking, skin-firming) are already served free or cheap by Noom, MyFitnessPal, and the med-spa/skincare industry.

### 2. Policy-adaptive content-moderation-as-a-service for indie dating/companion apps (built on Mistral's Shieldstral) — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** Hosted API + dashboard wrapping Mistral's newly open-sourced Shieldstral (Aug 4, 2026) — a self-hostable, prompt-configurable safety classifier — with policy templates for harassment/self-harm/scam detection, sold to small dating, AI-companion, and UGC app operators who can't afford enterprise trust-and-safety vendors.
- **Risks:** The "policy-as-prompt, no retraining" mechanism isn't new — it's roughly the fourth major open-weight guard model to ship it in under two years; a $9/mo indie-targeted wrapper already exists; a $27M-funded nonprofit backed by the same labs is giving the wrapper layer away free to this exact buyer segment.
- **Business pain (quantified):** Real but thin. ~337 active revenue-generating AI companion apps worldwide (128 launched in 2025); the top 10% capture 89% of revenue, leaving roughly 300 small operators splitting ~$18M of H1-2026 revenue combined — averaging ~$120K ARR/operator, a cash-poor buyer pool. 12 states now legally require companion-chatbot operators to run self-harm/suicidal-ideation detection protocols, creating genuine compliance pressure.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Shieldstral (Aug 4) is real and is a genuine infra improvement — smaller and cheaper to self-host than prior guard models. But the underlying capability isn't new: OpenAI shipped gpt-oss-safeguard-120b/20b (Oct 28, 2025, Apache-2.0) with the identical policy-as-prompt mechanism ten months earlier, and Google's ShieldGemma (Aug 2024) supports custom policy text on open weights too. Shieldstral changes which open model is cheapest to run, not whether the capability exists.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They're the ones shipping it. Mistral, OpenAI, and Google all now ship open-weight, policy-adaptive guard models directly. ROOST — a $27M-funded nonprofit backed by Google, OpenAI, Roblox, Discord, and Match Group as a partner — gives away free open-source trust-and-safety infrastructure (Coop: moderation review + NCMEC-compliant CSAM reporting workflow; Osprey: incident response) explicitly aimed at "startups who lack access to the safety tools available to large tech companies" — this candidate's exact stated buyer.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it is:** ContentGuard, a $9/mo indie-targeted moderation wrapper, launched on Product Hunt in Feb 2026 — months before Shieldstral existed. LocalMod (free, open-source, self-hosted) is already on GitHub. OpenAI's Moderation API and Google's Perspective API are free; Sightengine ($29/mo) and Hive (self-serve, pay-as-you-go) cover the cheap-paid tier; AWS Rekognition has a free tier. Two Hat/Community Sift (Microsoft-acquired 2021) and Spectrum Labs (ActiveFence-acquired 2023) show the enterprise tier has already consolidated into a few vendors, evidence the category rewards a small number of well-capitalized players, not many solo entrants.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** More open guard models will keep shipping on an annual cadence across at least three labs (Google, OpenAI, Mistral) — price/performance keeps improving, which erodes a cost-based wrapper's differentiation further, not less; well-funded AI-safety capital ($660M across 27 deals, Aug 2025–Jul 2026) is concentrated in enterprise agent-governance and could move down-market into this segment at will.
- **Competitors:** OpenAI Moderation API (free), Google Perspective API (free), AWS Rekognition (free tier + cheap), Sightengine ($29/mo+), Hive (self-serve), ContentGuard ($9/mo), LocalMod (free/OSS), ROOST/Coop (free/nonprofit), Mistral's own Moderation endpoint ($0.10/1M tokens), Two Hat/Community Sift (Microsoft), Spectrum Labs (ActiveFence).
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** Small dating/companion/UGC app operators, thin wallets (~$120K ARR/operator avg in the companion segment) — founder-led outbound/content (Indie Hackers, r/SaaS, companion-app-builder Discords), not self-serve at meaningful scale, and a structurally different motion than Lance's Meta-ads-to-consumer-checkout playbook.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / LTV:CAC / ARR ceiling:** CAC likely $50–150 via outbound/content given no paid-ads channel available; LTV low given thin buyer revenue and high indie-app churn; LTV:CAC marginal at best; ARR ceiling likely well under $1M given buyer-pool size and free-tier competition on every side.
- **Time to first sellable version:** Weeks — which is the risk here, not the reassurance: anyone else (including a nonprofit already doing it for free) can build it in the same window.
- **Operational load:** High relative to revenue — GPU inference hosting, and if self-harm/CSAM-adjacent detection is marketed as a feature, NCMEC mandatory-reporting compliance infrastructure (18 U.S.C. §2258A) a solo operator isn't set up to run correctly.
- **Sources:** [Mistral AI — Shieldstral announcement](https://mistral.ai/news/shieldstral/), [OpenAI — gpt-oss-safeguard](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-oss-safeguard/), [Google — ShieldGemma](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/shieldgemma), [Hive pricing](https://docs.thehive.ai/), [Sightengine pricing](https://sightengine.com/pricing), [ROOST](https://www.roost.tools/), [Platformer — ROOST funding](https://www.platformer.news/), [Multistate.ai — companion-chatbot state laws](https://www.multistate.ai/updates/vol-105-state-ai-companion-chatbot-laws).
- **Kill reason:** The "open-weight guard model, policy supplied as a prompt" shape isn't new — OpenAI's gpt-oss-safeguard shipped the identical mechanism ten months before Shieldstral — and the specific product (a hosted wrapper for indie operators) is already crowded top to bottom: a $9/mo Product-Hunt-launched competitor (ContentGuard) predates Shieldstral entirely, and ROOST, a $27M-funded nonprofit backed by Google/OpenAI/Discord/Roblox/Match Group, gives the same wrapper layer away free to the identical underserved buyer this idea targets. Not logged as a new ledger entry — the self-harm-detection function it proposes is the same technical mechanism behind the already-tracked "Compliance-as-a-service for AI companion-chatbot operators" watch item; logged as a material update there instead.

**Bottom line:** Zero survivors for the fifth straight cycle, and the two kills fail for structurally different reasons worth naming precisely: one dies to an ad-platform policy wall that predates and is independent of the trend that motivated it (Meta's 2022 health-targeting removal, tightened further in 2026), the other dies to prior art that's already ten months old plus a well-funded nonprofit actively giving the same product away free to the exact underserved buyer it targets. Neither kill traces to "not enough demand" — both underlying trends (GLP-1 adoption, small AI-companion-app operators needing cheap trust-and-safety tooling) are real and quantified. The lesson for future cycles is the same one this project keeps re-learning in new clothes: a fresh-looking trigger (a newly-crossed adoption threshold, a newly-open-sourced model) is not the same thing as an open wedge, and the gap between "this just changed" and "nobody has built the obvious response yet" needs to be checked before, not after, the deep-dive.
