## Ideas

Three candidates were selected from today's scan and deep-dived with adversarial verification. All three failed — logged here in full because the research has value even (especially) when it kills the idea, and because it keeps the ledger from re-litigating the same pitches later.

### 1. Digital government-identity setup kit for seniors — REJECTED

- **Brief description:** Guided video course + PDF checklist + optional light chat support walking seniors (or adult children buying for them) through creating/verifying SSA.gov, Login.gov, ID.me, and Medicare.gov accounts, sold one-time.
- **Risks:** Underlying enforcement deadline has already slipped twice in 12 months; .gov UI changes make video content perishable; liability exposure if guidance causes a benefit lockout; ad-account risk from keyword adjacency to senior tech-support scams.
- **Consumer pain:** Real but narrow — a one-time, 10–20 minute task a subset of seniors get stuck on.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Login.gov/ID.me became the mandatory SSA sign-in path in mid-2025; a further phone-channel/MFA restriction is reported (not independently verified) around Aug 18, 2026.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** AARP already publishes free step-by-step guides; ID.me/Login.gov are incentivized to make free self-service work since it's their own compliance mandate, not a profit center.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet:** It already is, just fragmented and low-quality (SEO "account locked" support-mill sites, general senior-tech concierges) rather than one clean product.
- **What changes in 2 years:** More federal programs going online-only would widen the task list; credible only if SSA holds a deadline without reversing again.
- **Competitors:** ID.me free video-chat agents, AARP free guides, Candoo Tech ($75–228, general senior-tech concierge, vendor-stated), Claimyr ($20–60 one-time phone-hold bypass), a cottage industry of SEO support-mill sites.
- **Who pays:** Most likely the adult child, as a burden-relief gift purchase.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / LTV:CAC / ARR ceiling:** No verified figures found; directionally weak — cheap 55+ CPMs likely offset by "why would I pay, it's free" objections and Meta's personal-attributes ad-policy friction on vulnerability-framed copy.
- **Time to first sellable version:** 1–3 weeks technically.
- **Operational load:** Higher than a typical "record once, sell forever" digital SKU — content needs re-shooting every time SSA/Login.gov/ID.me change their UI (has happened 3+ times in 18 months), and a live-support add-on turns this into an ongoing labor business, cutting against Lance's one-time-purchase preference.
- **Sources:** [Nextgov](https://www.nextgov.com/digital-government/2025/07/social-security-plans-new-restrictions-its-phone-line-citing-fraud/407087/), [AARP](https://www.aarp.org/social-security/phone-id-verification-to-end/), [GAO-26-109261](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-109261), [ID.me Network](https://network.id.me/how-id-me-video-chat-agents-help-people/), [Candoo Tech](https://www.candootech.com/), [Meta Transparency Center — Personal Attributes](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/privacy-violations-personal-attributes/)
- **Kill reason:** The government's own mandated ID-verification vendor (ID.me) already gives away free, live, human, elderly-tailored support for the exact task this would charge for.

### 2. Sibling eldercare cost-splitting app — REJECTED

- **Brief description:** Narrow Splitwise-style ledger for adult siblings to log and settle out-of-pocket eldercare costs for a shared aging parent.
- **Risks:** Multiplayer adoption (needs 2+ siblings to actually use it); entrenched free substitutes (Splitwise, Venmo Groups, spreadsheets); a "tracking" tool can itself read as an accusation inside a family.
- **Consumer pain:** Real — AARP: family caregivers report avg $7,242/yr out-of-pocket (78% report regular costs, n≈2,400). The specific claim that money is "the #1 trigger" of sibling conflict traces to marketing blogs repeating each other, not a primary AARP ranking — flag that framing as unverified.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** No genuine fresh trigger found. This is a timeless demographic pain with no recent regulatory/technological/behavioral shift specific to it — doesn't belong in a digest built around recent change.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** Too small a niche for Splitwise/Venmo relative to their general TAM; big eldercare platforms (A Place for Mom, Care.com) monetize via referral/lead-gen, not a utility feature.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet:** It isn't saturated — it's already occupied. At least four competitors: CareSplit, Ask Felix, SupportPay (PR-verified traction, employer-benefit channel, ~$1–3/user pricing), and TendTo, plus free substitutes including Venmo Groups' 2023 shared-expense feature.
- **What changes in 2 years:** Nothing structural — aging-population tailwind is multi-decade, not a 2-year catalyst.
- **Competitors:** CareSplit, Ask Felix, SupportPay, TendTo, Splitwise, Venmo Groups, plain spreadsheets (AARP's own recommended fallback).
- **Who pays:** Ambiguous — typically the already-burdened primary caregiver sibling would buy it, then has to convince siblings (who may feel less burdened or resent being tracked) to adopt it too.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / LTV:CAC / ARR ceiling:** No hard figures found; directionally weak — multiplayer-adoption CAC inflation against a bounded 2–5 year usage window per family.
- **Time to first sellable version:** 4–8 weeks for a solo founder — the easy part of this idea.
- **Operational load:** Underrated for the app's simplicity — support tickets skew emotionally loaded (grief, sibling distrust), and the product handles sensitive financial data about a vulnerable third party.
- **Note on cited "failures":** CareZone wasn't a product failure — it was acquired by Walmart for $200M in 2020 (3.5M users) and later folded into Walmart's own stack; its 2023 sunset was corporate consolidation, not evidence against this narrower idea.
- **Sources:** [AARP caregiver cost survey](https://www.aarp.org/pri/topics/ltss/family-caregiving/family-caregivers-cost-survey/), [Home Instead "50-50 Rule" research](https://caregiversns.org/images/uploads/tiac/TiAC%2020180125%20The50-50RuleHelpingSiblingsOvercomeConflict.pdf), [SupportPay](https://www.prweb.com/releases/supportpay-eliminates-financial-conflict-with-the-first-app-to-help-families-share-caregiving-expenses-automatically-302084981.html), [Venmo Groups](https://techcrunch.com/2023/11/14/venmo-gets-a-new-way-to-split-expenses-among-groups-like-clubs-teams-trip-buddies-and-more)
- **Kill reason:** Multiplayer adoption — the one sibling who feels the pain has to talk the others into installing and self-reporting into new software; most families default to Venmo or a spreadsheet instead, and no fresh event forces the switch.

### 3. Meta AI-ad-content compliance/detection tool — REJECTED

- **Brief description:** Browser extension/SaaS scanning ad creative for AI origin and helping advertisers self-declare it in Meta Ads Manager.
- **Risks:** Core premise doesn't survive scrutiny (see below); single-platform dependency; AI-detection heuristics carry false positive/negative risk that undermines trust in the tool itself.
- **Consumer pain:** Low. Real friction, if any, is narrower than the pitch: ambiguity about what counts as "substantial AI modification" vs. cosmetic touch-up, and agencies tracking checkbox status across many ad accounts — not a submission-blocking problem.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** The trigger event is real (Meta expanded AI labeling from political-only to commercial ads, June 2026) — but it's a self-declared checkbox plus automatic C2PA detection, not the "14% of rejections / strike-then-suspension" enforcement ladder that a cluster of ~15 SEO content-farm sites (auditsocials.com, 1clickreport.com, and similar) repeat verbatim with no attributable source. That claim did not survive verification against Meta's own pages or independent trade press (Jon Loomer Digital, Marketing Dive).
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do, minorly — AdCreative.ai bundles an "Instant AI Ad Compliance Checker" into its broader creative suite; Hoopoz scans for undisclosed disclosures.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet:** Because there isn't enough of a standalone problem to saturate — ticking a box you already know the answer to doesn't justify dedicated tooling.
- **What changes in 2 years:** Meta will keep improving native detection/UI nudges, shrinking any third-party wedge further.
- **Competitors:** AdCreative.ai, Hoopoz, AdStellar. No dedicated funded startup found for this alone — because it's a feature, not a category.
- **Who pays:** If anyone, agencies juggling many client ad accounts — not solo advertisers, for whom the box takes seconds.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / LTV:CAC / ARR ceiling:** No demand signal found (no active buyer discussion surfaced in ad-buyer/PPC communities) — any figure would be a pure guess, so omitted rather than fabricated.
- **Time to first sellable version:** 2–4 weeks technically — irrelevant given the weak demand signal.
- **Operational load:** Continuous tracking of Meta policy/API changes; single point of platform risk.
- **Sources:** [Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/sociable-meta-adds-updated-disclosure-tags-for-ai-generated-ads/824833/), [Jon Loomer Digital](https://www.jonloomer.com/ai-disclosure-checkbox-chatgpt-attribution/), [AdCreative.ai Compliance Checker](https://www.adcreative.ai/compliance-checker)
- **Kill reason:** Meta already auto-detects AI creative via C2PA metadata and gives advertisers a free one-click disclosure toggle — once the fabricated "enforcement ladder" claim is stripped out, there's no real detection problem left for a paid third party to solve, and the sliver that remains is already bundled free into incumbent ad-creative tools.

**Bottom line:** No survivors today. Two process notes for tomorrow's run: (1) a platform-policy "why now" trigger needs to be checked against what the policy actually requires — a disclosure checkbox is not the same shape of problem as an enforcement gate, and a cluster of SEO sites repeating identical unsourced stats is itself a red flag worth checking for before a candidate gets selected, not after; (2) a real, well-quantified pain (eldercare cost-splitting) still needs a genuine recent trigger to belong in this digest — timeless pains with no fresh catalyst and existing incumbents are lower-priority than they look at first pass.
