## Ideas

Three candidates deep-dived this cycle, all rejected — the thirty-ninth straight cycle with zero survivors.

### 1. Rental fee-disclosure compliance tool for small independent landlords — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A tool that generates the itemized, all-in fee disclosures now legally required on rental listings and lease quotes (Colorado, Austin, Massachusetts, Connecticut, Nevada, New Mexico, Minnesota, New York), sized for landlords with 5-49 units who list directly rather than through a large property-management platform.
- **Risks:** The largest rental-listing distribution platform already ships the exact compliance feature for free to the target buyer.
- **Consumer/buyer pain (quantified):** Real and dated — Austin's ordinance (passed May 28, 2026) requires 50+ unit operators to comply October 1, 2026 and 5-49 unit operators by January 1, 2027; seven states now mandate all-in pricing or full fee disclosure in advertising as of 2025-2026, with Colorado's HB25-1090 backed by real AG enforcement (a July 2026 settlement with rent-payment processor Domuso over unlawful card fees).
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Genuinely fresh and multiplying — this specific state/city wave of "total price" mandates is 2025-2026 legislation, with Austin's deadline landing in three weeks.
- **Why don't existing big players compete — they don't need to, the largest listing platform already ships it free:** Zumper, through a 2025 partnership with Engrain, syndicates mandatory-fee data to display a "Total Monthly Leasing Price" across its own listings and partner sites, explicitly built and marketed as compliance with the new all-in-pricing laws.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it is, at the exact segment this idea targets:** Zumper listing is free for landlords with fewer than 10 properties in most markets (5 free in the largest metros) — precisely the small-independent-landlord segment a new compliance tool would target; TurboTenant, Avail, and RentRedi (already covering the adjacent compliance-alert shape this ledger rejected 2026-09-07) round out the rest of the self-serve landlord-software market.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** More states adopt total-price mandates, but Zumper (and Engrain, which syndicates to other listing sites) absorbs each one as a free feature update, the same pattern that's killed every "regulation just changed → build a compliance tool" idea on this ledger.
- **Competitors:** Zumper (free, Engrain-powered fee sync), TurboTenant, Avail, RentRedi.
- **Who pays / sales motion:** Would be self-serve SaaS to independent landlords, but the free incumbent already occupies the exact target segment (sub-10-property landlords) with a first-party feature built for this specific law.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Near zero — no price a new entrant could charge undercuts a free, syndicated, already-compliant listing platform.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** An MVP generating itemized disclosures could ship in days, which only underlines why Zumper already shipped the more valuable version (syndicated across multiple listing sites) as a byproduct of its existing product.
- **Sources:** [KXAN — Austin ordinance](https://www.kxan.com/news/local/austin/austin-to-require-landlords-to-disclose-hidden-fees-during-leasing-process/), [Otten Johnson — Colorado HB25-1090](https://www.ottenjohnson.com/news/hb25-1090-colorados-residential-landlords-must-eliminate-junk-fees-in-2026-including-cam-charges/), [Zumper — multifamily fee transparency guide](https://www.zumper.com/manage/resources/multifamily-fee-transparency-guide/), [Zumper x Engrain partnership](https://www.zumper.com/manage/resources/zumper-and-engrain-simplify-fee-transparency/), [Colorado AG — Domuso settlement](https://coag.gov/press-releases/rent-payment-processing-company-domuso-agrees-to-comply-with-colorado-consumer-and-credit-laws/).
- **Kill reason:** Zumper already ships the exact itemized "Total Monthly Leasing Price" compliance feature free to landlords with under 10 properties — precisely the buyer segment this idea would target — built via a 2025 Engrain partnership explicitly in response to the same state-law wave; the ninth confirmation on this ledger of the "regulation just changed → build a compliance tool" shape dying to a free incumbent.
- **Not scored against B2C_RULES** — B2B, buyer is a small independent landlord, not a personal consumer.

### 2. Check-fraud / mail-theft protection service for seniors — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A subscription service for adult children to protect an aging parent from mail-theft-related check fraud and check washing — monitoring incoming/outgoing mail, alerting on suspicious check-clearing activity, and coaching the switch away from paper checks.
- **Risks:** Both halves of the mechanism (mail-theft prevention, bank-account fraud monitoring) are already free or already bundled into existing senior-targeted competitors on this ledger.
- **Consumer/buyer pain (quantified):** Real and rising — 63% of financial institutions reported check fraud attempts in the past 12 months per the Federal Reserve's 2026 Risk Officer Report (21% saw increased check washing specifically); mail-collection-box theft rose 139% from FY2019-2023; industry estimates put total check-fraud losses as high as $30B if unchecked, versus $21B in 2023; USPS itself, as of July 2026, is telling the public to stop using blue collection boxes for outgoing checks.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Weak once verified — check fraud and mail theft have been a rising, publicized problem since at least 2023 (the FBI/FINRA alerts predate this cycle by 1-3 years); July 2026 USPS guidance is the freshest dated element, but it's an incremental escalation of an old trend, not a new mechanism or new regulation.
- **Why don't existing big players compete — they already do, at the exact target buyer:** Aura and Norton LifeLock — both already established senior-market identity-protection competitors on this ledger (per the 2026-09-05 document/medical-ID breach protection rejection) — bundle bank-account transaction monitoring (including checking accounts) with alerts on unusual withdrawals or transfers as a standard plan feature; USPS's own free Informed Delivery service lets any consumer preview incoming mail by photo, directly addressing the mail-theft-prevention half for free.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it is, and this is now a pattern, not a one-off:** This is the seventh distinct flavor of "build a fraud-protection product for a senior/family buyer" this ledger has evaluated and rejected — following the AI voice-clone family-defense kit, recovery-scam verification service, document/medical-identity breach protection, deepfake video-call scam detection, the "Is this a scam?" checker, and the Elder Fraud Freeze concierge. Every prior instance died to the same combination: a free bank/USPS-native mechanism, an already-bundled Aura/LifeLock-style competitor, or the Meta targeting wall below.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** Continued gradual shift away from paper checks reduces the addressable problem over time rather than growing it, unlike most of this ledger's "why now" cases.
- **Competitors:** USPS Informed Delivery (free), Aura, Norton LifeLock, EverSafe (all already senior-market identity/fraud monitoring incumbents).
- **Who pays / sales motion:** Would be self-serve subscription (adult child paying for a parent) — the same buyer this ledger has repeatedly found has no precise Meta interest/life-event targeting category (Financial Products Special Ad Category blocks precision here, same wall as the Medicare/insurance/fintech senior kills).
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Not meaningfully scoreable — kill precedes it; Meta's Financial Products Special Ad Category restriction alone caps CAC efficiency below what this ledger's other senior-fintech ideas already found unworkable.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** An MVP wrapping bank-alert aggregation and mail-photo monitoring could ship in weeks, but would ship directly behind Aura/LifeLock's existing bundled feature and USPS's free mail-preview service.
- **Sources:** [Forbes — check theft surging](https://www.forbes.com/sites/steveweisman/2026/01/25/from-mailbox-to-bank-fraud-check-theft-is-surging-and-how-to-stop-it/), [Federal Reserve Fed360 — 2026 Risk Officer Report](https://www.frbservices.org/news/fed360/issues/051426/risk-management-2026-risk-officer-report), [Cautellus — check washing 2026](https://cautellus.com/blog/check-washing-scam-2026), [Security.org — Aura vs LifeLock 2026](https://www.security.org/identity-theft/lifelock-vs-aura/), [ClickOrlando — USPS Informed Delivery](https://www.clickorlando.com/news/local/2026/04/10/informed-delivery-a-way-to-combat-mail-theft/).
- **Kill reason:** Free (USPS Informed Delivery) and already-bundled (Aura, LifeLock) solutions already cover both halves of the mechanism for the exact senior/family buyer this idea targets, and the underlying "why now" is an old, gradually-escalating trend rather than a fresh trigger — the seventh confirmation that "fraud-protection product for seniors" is now an over-mined shape on this ledger, on top of failing the Meta targeting wall that killed six prior instances of it.
- **B2C_RULES gate check:** Fails Rule 1 — no precise Meta interest/life-event targeting category survives the Financial Products Special Ad Category restriction for this buyer, the same wall as the Medicare/insurance/fintech senior kills; rejected without scoring the remaining eight gates.

### 3. Product-recall refund-tracking app for consumers — REJECTED (new, brief pass)

- **Brief description:** An app that matches a household's owned products against CPSC/FDA recalls and walks the user through claiming the free refund, replacement, or repair.
- **Risks:** Multiple free tools already do exactly this, including one built specifically around home-inventory matching.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it is:** "kept" (a home-inventory app) already auto-matches a user's registered products against CPSC recalls and flags matches; Recall Refunds and OpenClassActions already maintain free, continuously updated recall databases with direct links to claim free remedies; CPSC itself ships a free recall-alert app and daily email list. The adjacent claims-filing category (class-action settlements) shows the identical pattern one level over: Class Action Buddy, Catch, Payout, and Settlemate already auto-fill and submit claims for free, and the category's own consumer-safety norm is explicit — "the legitimate claim site is free, always," which makes a paid version of this mechanic actively suspect to the buyer, not just uncompetitive.
- **Kill reason:** Not a "why now" event — no fresh regulatory or volume trigger was found this cycle — and the mechanic is already free at every layer (detection, matching, and claim-filing) from multiple live competitors; a paid entrant would be competing against a category where "free" is also the trust signal.
- **Sources:** [getkeptapp.com — CPSC recall alerts](https://getkeptapp.com/blog/cpsc-recall-alerts/), [OpenClassActions — recalls database](https://openclassactions.com/recalls.php), [Settlemate — DoNotPay alternatives](https://www.settlemate.io/blog/donotpay-alternatives).
- **Not scored against B2C_RULES** — killed on Rule 9 (over-mined shape: free-tool-covers-every-layer) before reaching the full gate check; noted briefly rather than given a full deep-dive since the saturation was unambiguous on first pass.

**Bottom line:** Thirty-ninth straight cycle, zero survivors. This cycle's two fully-worked candidates both had genuinely dated triggers (Austin's fee-disclosure ordinance, USPS's July 2026 blue-collection-box warning) but died to the same recurring pattern: whoever already owns the distribution channel or the adjacent bundle (Zumper for rental listings, Aura/LifeLock for senior fraud monitoring) absorbs the new regulatory or trend trigger as a feature update before a solo founder could ship a standalone product. The check-fraud idea additionally confirms that "fraud-protection product for seniors" — like "regulation → compliance tool" and "AI-coding-agent ecosystem tooling" before it — is now a recognizable over-mined shape on this ledger, having failed the same way seven separate times.
