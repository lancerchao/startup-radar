## Ideas

Two candidates deep-dived this cycle, both rejected — the twentieth straight cycle with zero survivors. Both failed on the same underlying shape as prior kills: a fresh, real, quantified trigger (a much bigger tax credit; a large invisible-support population) led straight to either a free calculator/incumbent platform already positioned to capture it, or a federally-funded nonprofit network already serving the exact audience for $0.

### 1. Employer-Provided Childcare Tax Credit (Section 45F) capture/advisory service for small businesses — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A service helping small-business owners identify, structure, and file for the newly-quadrupled Section 45F credit — advising on which spending qualifies (on-site facility, resource/referral services, or the newly-eligible third-party intermediary route) and preparing Form 8882.
- **Risks:** The calculator half of the product already exists free; the intermediary-platform half is already occupied by funded incumbents; the advisory half is a commodity CPA-firm service, not software.
- **Consumer/buyer pain (quantified):** Real money on the table — the credit cap just went from $150K to $500K/$600K and the rate from 25% to 40%/50%, for any business under the $32M gross-receipts small-business threshold (a large population; most small employers in the US clear that bar). Most small business owners are unaware their qualifying-expense options just widened (backup care now qualifies up to $45K/child/year; intermediary contracts now count without owning a facility) — awareness gap, not access gap.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Genuinely fresh — OBBBA passed July 2025, the new rules apply to tax year 2026 and later, and the IRS's own "tax year 2026 and later" guidance page is new this year.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do, on both sides of the exact wedge this idea would need. TOOTRiS explicitly markets itself as "the nation's leading real-time Child Care Benefits platform" and positions itself as the 45F-qualifying intermediary — "employers no longer need to build or operate their own Child Care facilities to qualify" is TOOTRiS's own pitch, word for word matching this idea's premise. Bright Horizons, Vivvi, and WeeCare all run adjacent versions aimed at the same small/mid-size employer. Separately, a free public tool (45f.org, "2026 Section 45F Child Care Tax Credit Calculator") already exists to do the awareness/calculation half, and at least eight CPA firms (Mize, Haynie, Keiter, Civitas Strategies, Beancount.io, Catalyst CPA, Foncannon, DPC Advisors) have already published 2026-specific 45F guides — the market is already organized around interpreting this exact change.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it can't sustain a new entrant, both ends are already claimed:** The two things a customer would actually buy — "be the qualifying intermediary" and "tell me if I qualify and how much" — are respectively a funded platform's core pitch and a free tool plus every CPA firm's SEO content.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** More small businesses will learn about the expanded credit, growing TOOTRiS/Bright Horizons/Vivvi's addressable market directly — they're the ones set up to capture new demand, not a new entrant.
- **Competitors:** TOOTRiS, Bright Horizons, Vivvi, WeeCare (intermediary platforms); 45f.org (free calculator); 8+ CPA firms publishing dedicated 2026 guides.
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** Would be self-serve B2B SaaS or founder-led advisory sold to small-business owners/office managers — but the realistic response to a pitch is "my CPA already mentioned this" or "I'll just use TOOTRiS."
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling / reputational note:** Poor differentiation ceiling; also worth flagging directly (not a formal B2C gate here, but the same judgment call) — "aggressively help small businesses claim a government tax credit for a fee" is the exact shape of the Employee Retention Credit (ERC) mill wave that triggered an IRS processing moratorium in Sept 2023 and ~28,000 disallowed claims worth ~$5B by Aug 2024. Any credit-capture business model invites that comparison and the scrutiny that comes with it.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** Days for a calculator (redundant with 45f.org); the only route with actual differentiation — becoming a Form 8882-ready intermediary provider — requires standing up real childcare-services infrastructure, months not weeks.
- **Sources:** [IRS — 45F credit, tax year 2026 and later](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/employer-provided-child-care-credit-tax-year-2026-and-later), [Bipartisan Policy Center — 45F 2026 guide](https://bipartisanpolicy.org/explainer/45f-employer-provided-child-care-tax-credit-2026-guide/), [TOOTRiS — 45F executive guide](https://tootris.com/edu/blog/news/the-executive-guide-to-leveraging-child-care-tax-incentives-to-maximize-workforce-roi/), [Bright Horizons — 45F credit expansion](https://www.brighthorizons.com/employers/benefits/employer-provided-child-care-credit), [45f.org — free 2026 calculator](https://45f.org/), [Beancount.io — Section 45F 2026 guide](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/05/09/section-45f-employer-childcare-credit-obbba-2026-expansion-40-percent-500k-cap-guide), [PBMares/Accounting Today — ERC fraud/moratorium history](https://www.accountingtoday.com/news/irs-learned-lessons-from-erc-fraud-wave).
- **Kill reason:** TOOTRiS already markets itself as the exact 45F-qualifying intermediary this idea would build, a free calculator (45f.org) already covers the awareness/math half, and at least 8 CPA firms already publish dedicated 2026 guidance — no piece of the value chain is unclaimed, and the closest analogous business model (fee-based government-credit capture) carries real ERC-mill-style regulatory/reputational baggage.
- **Not scored against B2C_RULES** — B2B, buyer is a small-business owner/operator, not a personal consumer.

### 2. Kinship-caregiver ("grandparents raising grandchildren") support kit — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A paid, self-serve kit for grandparents and other relatives who've suddenly taken in a grandchild — legal-guardianship paperwork checklist, benefits-eligibility navigator, school-enrollment/custody-affidavit templates — targeted at the senior-skewed demographic adjacent to Lance's existing careerpicai.com buyer.
- **Risks:** The dominant version of support here is already free, federally-funded, and state-delivered — including in Lance's own state — and the defining trait of the audience isn't a clean Meta targeting category.
- **Consumer pain (quantified):** Real and large. Census/Annie E. Casey data puts kinship care at roughly 2-2.5 million grandparents with primary responsibility for grandchildren nationally (about 3% of all US children), driven by parental addiction, incarceration, and mental-health crises — a population that skews older, financially strained (many on fixed incomes), and more likely to have a disability or be out of the labor force than non-caregivers.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Weak — no fresh dated trigger. The pain is structural and rising gradually with the underlying drivers (addiction, incarceration), not a discontinuity this cycle.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They don't need to — federal and state government already fund free navigator infrastructure covering this exact need. As of January 2026, 11 states plus Puerto Rico run federally-approved (Title IV-E) evidence-based Kinship Navigator Programs (Colorado, Delaware, Iowa, Minnesota, Nebraska, Nevada, Ohio, South Carolina, Utah, Virginia, **and Washington** — Lance's own state), with more informal state programs beyond that list. The Grandfamilies & Kinship Support Network (GKS Network) + USAging provide free national technical assistance and resource guides; AARP runs a dedicated free grandfamilies resource hub and support community.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it's the wrong kind of "not saturated," it's government-subsidized:** There's no commercial market to be undersaturated in — the entire category is designed to be delivered free through public/nonprofit channels, funded by Title IV-B/IV-E federal dollars specifically because the population is disproportionately low-income.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** More states will likely stand up their own Title IV-E navigator programs (11 states now vs. presumably fewer a few years ago), shrinking any commercial gap further, not opening one.
- **B2C gate check (all 9):** Rule 1 (Meta-targetable) — **FAIL/unverified-and-doubtful**; no evidence "grandparents raising grandchildren" or "kinship caregiver" exists as a Meta interest/behavior/life-event category (Meta's life-event list covers things like "new parent" and "moved recently," not family-structure crises) — same wall pattern as the "recently divorced" and "bereaved spouse" kills. Rule 2 (LTV + inflow) — mixed; real inflow (new kinship placements form continuously) but caregivers are disproportionately low-income and fixed-income, a poor fit for a paid product regardless of targeting. Rule 3 (moat) — **FAIL**; an 11-state federally-funded navigator network plus AARP's free hub already serves this population at $0. Rule 6 (niche) — pass, genuinely narrow. Rule 7 (reputationally clean) — pass. Rule 9 (over-mined shape) — new shape on this ledger, not previously tested, but structurally identical to the already-twice-killed "life-crisis population + no Meta life-event category + free nonprofit/government incumbent" pattern (bereaved spouse 2026-08-22, and directionally the solo-ager/eldercare items). **Fails decisively on Rule 1 and Rule 3 together.**
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** Would need to be self-serve consumer purchase from a caregiver population that is disproportionately eligible for the free public alternative already — a structurally worse position than competing against a paid incumbent.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Poor — no precise ad channel, and the buyer's realistic next step after a paid pitch is "check if my state has a kinship navigator program" (11 do, including Lance's own).
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** Weeks for a checklist/template product — irrelevant given the free, government-funded alternative already reaches the exact buyer.
- **Sources:** [Census Bureau — Grandparents Living With Grandchildren, March 2024](https://www.census.gov/library/stories/2024/03/grandparents-living-with-grandchildren.html), [GKS Network — kinship/grandfamilies data](https://www.gksnetwork.org/kinship-data/), [GKS Network — Kinship Navigator Programs Around the US](https://www.gksnetwork.org/resources/kinship-navigator-programs-around-the-united-states/), [GKS Network — Washington State Kinship Program Services](https://www.gksnetwork.org/resources/washington-state-kinship-program-services/), [ACF — Kinship Navigator Program](https://acf.gov/cb/grant-funding/kinship-navigator-program), [AARP — grandfamilies resources](https://www.aarp.org/), [Psychology Today — grandparents raising grandchildren](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/the-right-side-of-40/202504/the-hidden-epidemic-grandparents-raising-grandchildren).
- **Kill reason:** An 11-state, federally-funded (Title IV-E) Kinship Navigator network — including one in Lance's own state of Washington — plus AARP's free national resource hub already serve this exact population at $0, and there's no evidence Meta offers precise interest/behavior targeting for "kinship caregiver," the same life-event-targeting wall that killed the bereaved-spouse idea two cycles ago.

**Bottom line:** Twentieth straight cycle, zero survivors. Both candidates this cycle had genuinely fresh, quantified "why now" triggers (a quadrupled tax credit; a large, real caregiver population) — stronger openers than several recent cycles' theses — but both died the same way: the exact wedge a solo founder would build (be the intermediary; be the navigator) is already staffed, in one case by venture-backed platforms actively marketing themselves as that intermediary, in the other by federal grant dollars funding the identical service for free, in the caregiver's own state. The pattern worth naming: "a policy change just made an existing incentive/support program much bigger" is not the same "why now" as "a policy change just created a new gap" — the former tends to flow straight to whoever was already positioned as the delivery mechanism, which is rarely a new entrant.
