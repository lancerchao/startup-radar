## Ideas

Three candidates deep-dived this cycle, all rejected — the seventeenth straight cycle with zero survivors. All three shared a pattern distinct from prior cycles' failure mode: rather than one free open-source tool covering the gap, each idea turned out to already be a small, mature *multi-vendor* market (4-8+ named competitors, in the vibe-coding and Amazon-float cases) or already claimed by an entrenched incumbent brand moving into the exact niche the same week (Guardio's AI-browser feature).

### 1. Vibe-coding pre-deployment security scanner for indie/solo AI-app builders — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** An automated scanner (or scan-as-a-service) that checks apps built on AI app-builders — Lovable, Bolt, Replit Agent, v0, Base44, Cursor+Supabase — for exposed API keys, broken auth, and open database rules before a non-technical builder ships to real users with real user data.
- **Risks:** Buyer (solo/non-technical vibe coder) is price-sensitive and structurally one-and-done — scan before launch, maybe once more before a funding round, then gone.
- **Pain (quantified):** Red Access scanned 5,000+ publicly deployed vibe-coded apps in May 2026; ~40% exposed sensitive data (medical, financial, corporate presentations, customer records). Escape.tech's own research catalogs 2,000+ vulnerabilities discovered across vibe-coded apps. Wiz Research found a critical Base44 auth-bypass — a non-secret `app_id` value against an undocumented endpoint let an attacker create a verified account with full access to private enterprise apps. Lovable itself ($13.2B valuation Aug 12, 2026, up from $6.6B six months earlier; ~$500M ARR; ~8M users) has had 3 documented security incidents, including a BOLA vulnerability left open 48 days after the company closed the bug-bounty report without escalation.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Genuinely now — the vibe-coding tools market hit an estimated $4.7B in 2026 (38% YoY growth), and Lovable's valuation doubled in the six months to Aug 12, 2026, meaning the underlying builder population (and its accumulated exposed-app surface area) is large and still growing fast.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do, at every price point. Automated scanners: Revibed (Lovable/Replit/Bolt/Cursor/v0/Windsurf/Base44), Vibe App Scanner/"vas" (150+ checks across DB/auth/API), safevibe.codes (Supabase/Lovable/Bolt/Base44-specific), Scanbee (Lovable/Bolt/Supabase/Cursor). Human-audit tier: VibeSecure ($199, 48hr turnaround), Sherlock Forensics ($1,500+), Varyence and Beesoul ($2,500+, pen-test focus / 12-page structured audits). Wiz (enterprise) already owns the thought-leadership high ground with its own Base44 vulnerability research.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it already is:** A dedicated third-party ranking page ("Best Vibe Code Audit Agencies 2026") already exists to help buyers choose between competitors — evidence of a mature, comparison-shopped market, not an open gap.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** AI app-builders will likely fold basic scanning into their own platforms as a trust/retention feature — Lovable and Base44 are both under public pressure after named incidents — commoditizing the automated-scan tier this idea would compete in from below, on top of the 8 competitors already live.
- **Competitors:** Revibed, Vibe App Scanner (vas), safevibe.codes, Scanbee, VibeSecure ($199), Sherlock Forensics ($1,500+), Varyence, Beesoul ($2,500+), plus Wiz/Snyk covering the same CVE classes at enterprise scale.
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** Self-serve, low/one-time purchase from solo non-technical builders — but that buyer already has 8 named options, and the category is explicitly marketed as "10-50x cheaper than traditional security consultants," meaning price is already compressed to commodity levels.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Weak across the board. One-and-done usage pattern caps LTV; CAC would compete against 8 incumbents already running SEO/content against the identical long-tail "is [platform] secure" search terms a new entrant would need.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** Days to build a first version — which is exactly the problem, since it was equally fast for the 8 competitors that got there first.
- **Sources:** [Revibed](https://revibed.io/security-scan/lovable), [Vibe App Scanner](https://vibeappscanner.com/), [Vibe Code Scanner](https://vibe-eval.com/vibe-code-scanner/), [Scanbee](https://stackshare.io/scanbee-no-frills-less-verbose-security-scanner-for-ai-era-builders), [Wiz — Base44 vulnerability](https://www.wiz.io/blog/critical-vulnerability-base44), [Escape.tech methodology](https://escape.tech/blog/methodology-how-we-discovered-vulnerabilities-apps-built-with-vibe-coding/), [VibeSecure](https://www.vibesecure.org/), [Sherlock Forensics](https://www.sherlockforensics.com/pages/vibe-coding-security.html), [Vibe Code Audit Agencies ranking](https://vibecoding.app/blog/best-vibe-code-audit-agencies), [thenextweb — Lovable security crisis](https://thenextweb.com/news/lovable-vibe-coding-security-crisis-exposed), [Value Add VC — Lovable valuation](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/lovable-valuation-2026-13-2b-and-500m-arr-how-vibe-coding-actually-makes-money).
- **Kill reason:** At least 8 named live competitors (4 automated scanners, 4+ human-audit shops) already serve this exact buyer at every price point from free checks to $199-$3,000, in a market with its own comparison-shopping content ecosystem — a ninth entrant has no differentiation path.
- **Not scored against B2C_RULES** — B2B/dev-tooling idea, buyer is an indie app builder, not a personal consumer.

### 2. Agentic AI-browser security add-on for prosumers/small business — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A lightweight browser extension protecting users of agentic AI browsers (ChatGPT Atlas, Perplexity Comet) from indirect prompt injection, data exfiltration, and unsafe autonomous actions during everyday tasks — positioned below enterprise-grade offerings, for solo professionals and small teams.
- **Risks:** The addressable niche (people actively using agentic AI browsers for real tasks) is still small and early-adopter-skewed, and both the mainstream-consumer tier and the SMB/enterprise tier are already claimed by credible, funded incumbents, leaving no gap in between.
- **Pain (quantified):** LayerX testing found ChatGPT Atlas blocks only 5.8% of phishing attempts vs. Chrome's 47% and Edge's 53% — Atlas users face "90% more exposure" than traditional browser users. LayerX separately demonstrated a single weaponized URL can exfiltrate email content and meeting metadata from Perplexity Comet's assistant. OWASP's 2026 LLM Security Report puts prompt-injection attack growth at 340% YoY, and Brave's security team called indirect prompt injection "a systemic challenge facing the entire category of AI-powered browsers" at Black Hat USA 2026.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Fresh — Black Hat USA 2026 coverage, current-generation Atlas/Comet adoption, and an EU deadline (high-risk AI systems must have documented, operational prompt-injection controls by Aug 2026) all land this month.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do, at both tiers simultaneously. Consumer: Guardio — an established consumer browser-security brand ($9.99-$34.99/mo across Individual/Duo/Family tiers) — has already shipped "Safe Browsing for AI," explicit real-time protection for AI browsers, agents, and GenAI platforms. SMB/enterprise: LayerX explicitly serves the small-business market (not just enterprise) via a browser extension with tested Atlas/Comet/Dia/Genspark compatibility; Seraphic (mid-market) and Menlo Security (remote browser isolation) round out the field.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it isn't:** Guardio alone already owns the self-serve consumer/prosumer price point this idea would target, backed by an established brand and a multi-year track record in adjacent scam/phishing protection.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** Atlas and Comet themselves will likely close their biggest gaps natively once the 5.8%-vs-47% comparison gets wider press coverage, shrinking third-party opportunity further; meanwhile Guardio's head start compounds via brand trust with exactly the privacy-conscious demographic a new entrant would need to win.
- **Competitors:** Guardio ($9.99-$34.99/mo, consumer, ships AI-agent protection today), LayerX (SMB-focused, quote-based), Seraphic (mid-market, quote-based), Menlo Security (enterprise, quote-based).
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** Self-serve consumer/prosumer subscription — the exact motion Guardio already runs, with an existing base and brand recognition a new entrant lacks.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Unfavorable — competing head-on against an incumbent consumer brand already inside this niche with a reputation and distribution advantage that's hard to out-CAC.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** Weeks for a baseline extension, but ongoing operational load is high and permanent — prompt-injection defense is a continuous arms race requiring constant signature/heuristic updates, which is exactly why the surviving players are funded security companies, not solo tools.
- **Sources:** [LayerX — Atlas vs Comet](https://www.humansecurity.com/learn/blog/chatgpt-atlas-vs-perplexity-comet-agentic-browsers/), [Seraphic Security — Top 5 Agentic Browsers 2026](https://seraphicsecurity.com/learn/ai-browser/top-5-agentic-browsers-in-2026-capabilities-and-security-risks/), [research.aimultiple — AI browser security risks](https://research.aimultiple.com/ai-browser-security/), [Guardio — Safe Browsing for AI](https://guard.io/safe-browsing), [Security.org — Guardio pricing](https://www.security.org/antivirus/guardio/), [LayerX vs Seraphic](https://layerxsecurity.com/layerx-extension-vs-seraphic/), [LayerX — best agentic browser security platforms](https://layerxsecurity.com/learn/best-agentic-browser-security-platforms/).
- **Kill reason:** Guardio already ships this exact feature to its existing consumer base ($9.99-$34.99/mo) today, while LayerX already serves the small-business segment with tested Atlas/Comet compatibility — the self-serve buyer this idea would target is already claimed at both ends of the market.
- **Not scored against B2C_RULES** — killed on direct, immediate competitive saturation (an established incumbent already ships the identical feature to its existing base) before the gate check would matter; sales motion is closer to prosumer/small-business than a pure consumer play in any case.

### 3. Amazon ad-spend float/card-rewards arbitrage tool for FBA/PPC sellers — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A tool/service helping Amazon advertisers preserve credit-card float and rewards after Amazon's Aug 1, 2026 shift to auto-deducting ad spend directly from seller proceeds — automating enrollment in Amazon's own Pay-by-Invoice option and routing the resulting Net-30 invoice through a card-based bill-pay flow.
- **Risks:** The entire mechanism already exists as a combination of two free/cheap, well-known building blocks (Amazon's own Pay-by-Invoice option plus a generic bill-pay-by-card platform) — nothing about this requires new infrastructure, only awareness, making it a thin and easily-copied wrapper.
- **Pain (quantified):** Real and sizable. Amazon began auto-deducting ad spend directly from seller proceeds and delaying payout to 7 days post-delivery, effective Aug 1, 2026 (delayed from an original April 15 date after an April 2026 seller boycott). At $15K/mo ad spend, combined card-rewards loss (~2-2.5%) plus float value totals ~$4,500-$5,250/yr; at $50K/mo, ~$12,000/yr; at $100K/mo, ~$24,000/yr; at $250K/mo, ~$60,000/yr.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Genuinely fresh and dated — the migration deadline was Aug 1, 2026, so non-opted-in sellers are mid-transition right now.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They don't need to — Amazon itself already ships the fix (Pay by Invoice, Net-30, opt-in before migration), and multiple well-funded, general-purpose bill-pay-by-card platforms already let any business pay any Net-30 invoice by card today, Amazon-specific or not: Melio (flat 2.9% card fee, direct Amazon Business integration), Plastiq (explicitly positioned around "maximizing credit card rewards or managing cash flow through card float").
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — there's no product-shaped gap to saturate:** This is fundamentally a two-step user-education problem (opt into Pay by Invoice; pay that invoice through an existing card-enabled bill-pay tool), already documented publicly by agency content (e.g., "Amazon PPC Credit Card Workaround: Keep Earning 2X Points on Ad Spend in 2026").
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** Nothing structural improves this — the arbitrage math is fixed by Amazon's Net-30 term and whichever bill-pay platform's fee, both already set; if anything, Amazon could close the loophole by requiring proceeds-deduction even under Pay by Invoice.
- **Competitors:** Melio (2.9% card fee, direct Amazon Business integration), Plastiq (rewards/float-focused positioning), Ramp, BILL, Resolve — all general-purpose, all already usable today with zero Amazon-specific tooling required. Existing cash-flow financing incumbents (Payability, Parafin, Slope, Wayflyer, Viably, SellersFi, Uncapped) already cover the adjacent forecasting/financing angle, per this ledger's Aug 13/20 rejections of related Amazon-billing ideas.
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** Self-serve, but the "product" competes against a free ~20-minute setup task using tools serious sellers likely already run (many FBA sellers already carry a Brex/Ramp card).
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Poor — no recurring value beyond initial setup, and the underlying financial trick belongs to Amazon (the Net-30 term) and Melio/Plastiq (the card-pay mechanism), not to any new entrant.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** Days — again the tell, since the "build" is really a content/consulting product wearing software's clothes, not software with a moat.
- **Sources:** [Scaledon — Amazon delays ad credit card policy to Aug 2026](https://scaledon.com/amazon-delays-ad-credit-card-policy-to-august-2026-what-sellers-need-to-know/), [Seller Sprite — Amazon ad billing auto-deduct 2026](https://www.sellersprite.com/en/blog/amazon-ad-billing-auto-deduct-2026), [SlopePay — Amazon ad billing change](https://slopepay.com/blog/amazon-ad-billing-change-2026), [Hire All Stars — Amazon PPC credit card workaround](https://www.hireallstars.com/blog/amazon-ppc-credit-card-workaround), [Melio — Amazon Business integration](https://melio.com/integrations/amazon-business/), [Melio — Amazon Ads payment methods](https://melio.com/blog/amazon-ads-payment-method-changes/), [Resolve Pay — Melio alternatives](https://resolvepay.com/blog/best-melio-alternatives).
- **Kill reason:** The entire mechanism is already free-to-cheap and DIY-assemblable from Amazon's own Pay by Invoice option plus existing multi-vendor bill-pay-by-card platforms (Melio, Plastiq, Ramp, BILL) — there's no software gap, only an underused combination of existing tools that a blog post solves as well as a product would.
- **Not scored against B2C_RULES** — B2B, buyer is an Amazon seller/advertiser.

**Bottom line:** Seventeenth straight cycle, zero survivors. This cycle's failure mode was subtly different from the usual single-free-tool kill: two of the three candidates (vibe-coding security, Amazon float arbitrage) died against an already-crowded *multi-vendor* market rather than one dominant free tool, and the third (agentic-browser security) died because an established consumer brand (Guardio) had already shipped the exact feature into its existing base days-to-weeks before this cycle, not years. Recurring lesson: by the time a "why now" trigger is fresh enough to write up, the market has frequently had the same amount of lead time to notice it — and a market with real, quantified pain (vibe-coding's 40% exposure rate; Amazon's $60K/yr sellers-tier loss) is exactly the kind of pain multiple funded teams independently spot and build for at once.
