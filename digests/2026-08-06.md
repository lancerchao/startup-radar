## Ideas

Two candidates deep-dived, both rejected — the same underlying lesson twice in one cycle: a fresh-looking regulatory or behavioral trigger doesn't open a wedge when a condition-agnostic incumbent already absorbs it (menopause accommodations) or a cheap, already-reviewed indie competitor already ships the exact consumer pitch (dating-photo verification).

### 1. Menopause/perimenopause workplace-accommodation compliance tool for HR teams — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A guided accommodation-request intake tool for HR/benefits teams — policy templates, an EEOC-style interactive-process wizard, and a jurisdiction tracker — for employers with staff in Rhode Island or Philadelphia (soon possibly more), where menstruation/perimenopause/menopause symptoms are now a legally protected accommodation category.
- **Risks:** The enacted buyer pool is much smaller than the "wave" framing in law-firm content implies (Virginia's version failed to pass); the actual workflow gap is already covered by generic, condition-agnostic accommodation-management software that doesn't need new code to add a new named condition.
- **Consumer pain (B2B, employer-side):** Real once triggered — Philadelphia's ordinance applies to any employer with even one employee working in the city regardless of HQ location, and covers accommodation requests where symptoms "substantially interfere" with job functions (undefined threshold, real compliance-uncertainty pain). But it's a narrow, two-jurisdiction trigger today, not a broad multi-state mandate.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Genuinely new — Rhode Island's law (June 2025) and Philadelphia's ordinance (Dec 2025, effective Jan 1, 2027) are the first of their kind in the US, and the federal Menopausal Workers' Fairness Act (H.R. 9671) was introduced July 14, 2026. But "why now" is weaker than it first looks: Virginia's parallel bill died this session in favor of a study due 2028, so the trend is two jurisdictions with real teeth plus one stalled federal bill, not a fast-multiplying patchwork like the AI-companion-chatbot laws.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They don't need to build anything new. AbsenceSoft — a leave/accommodation SaaS already tracking 240+ statutory policies (FMLA, ADA, PWFA) across 7M+ employees, with a prebuilt EEOC-compliant "interactive process wizard" — handles any new protected condition as just another case type in an existing workflow. Separately, the Menopause Friendly Accreditation body (UK-origin, expanded to the US in 2025, CVS Health its first US-accredited employer) already owns the training/culture/certification positioning via an independent assessment panel.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet:** It is, structurally, even without a menopause-labeled competitor: the accommodation-*request* workflow a Philadelphia HR team needs the moment this takes effect is generic, and any employer already running AbsenceSoft (or a peer like Sedgwick/Unum's leave platforms) gets menopause-request handling for free the day someone files one — no vendor has to ship a "menopause module" for the software to already work.
- **What will change in next 2 years:** More cities/states may follow Rhode Island and Philadelphia, but Virginia's stall shows the pace is slower and less certain than the AI-companion-chatbot precedent; meanwhile AbsenceSoft-class platforms keep adding named conditions to their existing engines at zero marginal product cost, widening their lead rather than opening room for a point solution.
- **Competitors:** AbsenceSoft (condition-agnostic leave/accommodation SaaS, 7M+ employees tracked), Sedgwick and Unum (leave-administration incumbents with similar generic accommodation modules), Menopause Friendly Accreditation/Henpicked (training + certification, live in the US, CVS Health accredited).
- **Who pays:** HR/benefits teams — but the realistic buyer either already owns AbsenceSoft-class software (no new purchase needed) or is small enough that a free EEOC-template PDF plus a lawyer's one-time policy review covers it, the same "free-fix-at-the-source" pattern that killed the Elder Fraud Freeze concierge (2026-08-02).
- **Expected CAC / LTV / LTV:CAC / ARR ceiling:** Not modeled — no differentiated wedge once the incumbent-absorption finding is accounted for.
- **Time to first sellable version:** Weeks technically (policy templates + a request-tracking form), moot given the competitive findings.
- **Operational load:** Not evaluated — moot.
- **Sources:** [Duane Morris — Philadelphia ordinance](https://www.duanemorris.com/alerts/philadelphia_enacts_new_workplace_protections_menstruation_menopause_0126.html), [Fisher Phillips — Philadelphia 5 steps for employers](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/insights/insights/workplace-protections-related-to-menstruation-perimenopause-and-menopause), [McKnight's Senior Living — Rhode Island first state](https://www.mcknightsseniorliving.com/news/rhode-island-first-state-to-mandate-workplace-protections-for-menopause-related-medical-conditions/), [BillTrack50 — RI S0361](https://www.billtrack50.com/billdetail/1841683), [Disability Leave Law — Virginia study not law](https://www.disabilityleavelaw.com/2026/04/articles/paid-sick-leave-2/virginia-governor-spanberger-proposes-amendments-to-paid-sick-leave-paid-family-and-medical-leave-and-menopause-accommodations-legislation/), [Congress.gov — H.R. 9671 text](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/9671/text/ih?overview=closed&format=txt), [AbsenceSoft — accommodations/ADA interactive process](https://absencesoft.com/accommodations/), [Menopause Friendly US accreditation](https://menopausefriendlyus.com/).
- **Kill reason:** The enacted regulatory trigger is real but narrower than the coverage implies (Virginia's bill failed; it's Rhode Island plus one city, not a multi-state wave), and the workflow gap it creates is already absorbed at zero marginal cost by condition-agnostic accommodation-management incumbents (AbsenceSoft, 7M+ employees tracked) that don't need a menopause-specific feature to already work — the same "generic infrastructure swallows the new regulatory category" pattern that has now killed this idea shape repeatedly this project.

### 2. AI-generated/stolen dating-photo verification for daters 50+ (catfish/deepfake checker) — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A cheap, pay-per-scan consumer tool — paste a match's photo or profile link, get a verdict on AI-generation artifacts, reverse-image stolen-photo hits, and a cross-reference against known-scammer photo databases — marketed at daters 50+ or their adult children, positioned against this year's jump in AI-generated catfishing that defeats old-fashioned reverse-image search.
- **Risks:** Direct, live, well-reviewed consumer competitor already on the identical pitch; the space shows the same multiple-indie-clones-zero-breakout pattern the project killed once already this week (Yuka-for-a-vertical, 2026-08-05) — squarely inside B2C_RULES.md's flagged "AI photo app" meta-category.
- **Consumer pain:** Real and freshly quantified — FBI IC3 2025: $929.3M in romance-scam losses (+38% YoY), median loss for the 55-64 cohort ~$9,000 (highest of any age band), over-60s carrying >50% of total dollar losses. Scammers this cycle moved to AI-generated faces (defeats plain reverse-image search) and real-time deepfake video-call filters — the "broken English, blurry photo" tells are gone.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Genuinely sharp on the capability side — AI face-generation and real-time deepfake video filters good enough to pass a casual video call are a this-cycle development, not older news.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** The biggest dating platforms already build this in-house for their own users (Bumble's Deception Detector blocks ~95% of flagged scam/spam accounts; Tinder's Face Check went mandatory for new US accounts Oct 2025) — they're not a target buyer, and their in-app coverage reduces the addressable pain for anyone already on those two apps specifically.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet:** It isn't unsaturated — it's a live, populated niche. VerifyDating.net is already shipping the exact pitch (flags AI-generated/stolen photos "even when Google reverse image search returns nothing," cross-references scammer-photo blacklists), priced at $4.99 for 5 scans, claiming 4.9/5 across 2,847 reviews. A cluster of adjacent Google Play/App Store apps compete on nearly the same mechanic — Cheat AI (4.29/5, 1.6K ratings, pulled from the store Feb 2026), Catch A Cheater Buster AI, Catfish Scan, Cheaterbuster — mixed quality, at least one user review calling a sibling app "a scam" for returning generic lookalike photos instead of a real match. TruthScan is the one credible funded name in the space, but it sells B2B into dating platforms and enterprises (Professional plan $49/mo+), not to consumers directly — so it validates the underlying detection tech is becoming a commodity API other apps already wrap, rather than being a competitor to a consumer product.
- **What will change in next 2 years:** Detection APIs (the kind TruthScan sells) keep getting cheaper and more accurate, which lowers the barrier for the next indie clone as much as it would help a new entrant — no compounding advantage accrues to whoever enters next.
- **Competitors:** VerifyDating.net (live, reviewed, direct pitch match), Cheat AI/Catch A Cheater Buster AI/Catfish Scan/Cheaterbuster (Google Play/App Store cluster, mixed quality), Bumble Deception Detector and Tinder Face Check (in-platform, reduces addressable pain on the two largest apps), TruthScan (B2B detection API, not a direct consumer competitor but commoditizes the core mechanism).
- **Who pays:** Consumers (the dater or a worried adult child), pay-per-scan or small subscription — but see kill reason on pricing/differentiation.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / LTV:CAC / ARR ceiling:** Not modeled — a live, well-reviewed, cheap direct competitor already occupies the exact price point and pitch.
- **Time to first sellable version:** Days to weeks technically (wrap an existing AI-detection API plus a shared scam-photo blacklist) — fast and cheap precisely because it's a thin wrapper, which is why the niche already has four-plus indie entrants.
- **Operational load:** Ongoing blacklist/database upkeep without VerifyDating's review-count head start.
- **B2C gate check:** Fails Rule 4 (thin wrapper — the mechanism is an AI-detection API call plus a shared photo blacklist, no proprietary data moat achievable at entry) and Rule 9 (squarely the flagged "AI photo app" meta-category, and the app-store cluster shows the identical multiple-clones-zero-breakout signature the Yuka-vertical finding named two days ago) simultaneously. Rule 3 is also weak — VerifyDating's review volume and on-message pitch is a real, not hypothetical, moat-less competitor. Not scored further once three gates fail.
- **Sources:** [FBI IC3 2025 elder fraud summary](https://seniors.hcsk.org/elder-fraud-statistics-research/), [KnowBe4 — 2026 AI romance scams](https://blog.knowbe4.com/love-in-the-age-of-ai-why-2026-romance-scams-are-almost-impossible-to-spot), [VerifyDating.net](https://verifydating.net/), [AppBrain — Cheat AI listing/removal](https://www.appbrain.com/app/cheat-ai-catch-a-cheater/com.decent.demure), [Google Play — Cheating AI Catch a Cheater](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.dream.catfish&hl=en-US), [TruthScan — dating CRO/fraud detection](https://truthscan.com/solutions/dating-cro-fraud-detection), [TruthScan pricing](https://truthscan.com/pricing).
- **Kill reason:** VerifyDating.net already ships the exact consumer pitch (cheap per-scan pricing, works where reverse-image search fails, strong review count) with no visible gap to differentiate into, and the surrounding app-store cluster shows this is the same over-mined "AI photo app" shape the ledger killed once already this week — a thin API wrapper with several live indie entrants and zero breakout, not an open door.

**Bottom line:** Zero survivors today, and both kills rhyme with findings already on the ledger from the last 48 hours: a fresh regulatory trigger (menopause accommodation law) gets absorbed by generic incumbent infrastructure the way the AI-companion-chatbot and click-to-cancel compliance ideas weren't (the difference: those buyer categories needed vertical-specific logic; this one doesn't), and a fresh capability shift (AI-generated catfish photos) gets colonized by cheap indie apps just as fast as the Yuka-vertical scan-and-score shape was two days ago. Process note for future cycles: when a "why now" trigger is a capability shift (AI got better at X) rather than a hard deadline, check app-store search results for the obvious consumer wrapper before the deep-dive, not after — the entry cost for a thin wrapper is exactly as low for competitors as it is for us.
