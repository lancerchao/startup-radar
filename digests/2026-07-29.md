### 1. Insurance-denial fight kit — STATUS CHANGE: in-sheet → REJECTED

- **What changed:** This idea was promoted to Lance's evaluation spreadsheet yesterday (2026-07-29) on the strength of the 80.7%-appeals-win / 11.5%-filed gap. Today's research found **Claimable (getclaimable.com)** already executing almost exactly this product.
- **Claimable's actual position (verified):** $39.95/appeal, flat fee, no subscription; company-claimed ~80–85% success rate (marketing figure, not independently audited); founded ~2023, publicly launched Oct 2024, seed round ~$1.7M (Walkabout Ventures, Humanrace Capital); Trustpilot 4.5/5 but only 14 reviews (thin signal). Distribution leans heavy earned-media/PR (Yahoo Finance, Bloomberg, Axios, IBTimes, GlobeNewswire) plus condition-specific SEO landing pages (/rheumatology, /glp-1-appeals) — i.e., already running the exact SEO-content playbook this idea proposed.
- **Scope correction:** Claimable is narrower than assumed — ~85 conditions/90 treatments, concentrated in specialty/high-cost drugs (GLP-1s, rheumatology biologics, oncology). No dental, vision, disability, auto/PIP, or life-insurance coverage.
- **Competitive field is crowded, not 2-3 players:** at minimum Claimable (paid), Counterforce Health (nonprofit, free, ~70% claimed success), Fight Health Insurance (free), AppealArmor (free), AppealGen (free-to-start), Jenova AI (broader denial-type agent), Counterclaim.help, FightClaimDenial, DocuGov.ai, plus Aegis (YC-backed, B2B provider-side variant).
- **B2B fallback already taken:** Claimable launched a dedicated enterprise platform (April 2026) selling to pharma manufacturers, health systems, and access partners — the exact "sell to advocates/employers" pivot that would have been this idea's fallback wedge.
- **Sources:** [Claimable](https://www.getclaimable.com/), [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/getclaimable.com), [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/artificial-intelligence-2/2026/insurance-denials-meet-their-match-in-ai-powered-appeals/), [StreetInsider — Claimable enterprise launch](https://www.streetinsider.com/Globe+Newswire/Claimable+Debuts+AI-Powered+Enterprise+Patient+Access+Platform+for+Pharma+Manufacturers,+Health+Systems+and+Access+Partners+to+Automate+Appeals+at+Scale/26356462.html), [Axios — Counterforce Health](https://www.axios.com/local/raleigh/2025/08/20/using-ai-to-fight-back-against-insurance-denials-counteforce)
- **Kill reason:** As specified (AI health-insurance appeal letters, ~$39/appeal, SEO distribution), the idea is fully occupied — Claimable is funded, PR-visible, already executing, and has already claimed the B2B fallback; at least four free competitors further commoditize the core mechanic.
- **Note — a distinct, unserved wedge exists:** none of the reviewed players (Claimable, Counterforce, Fight Health Insurance, AppealArmor, Jenova) touch dental, vision, disability (ERISA-governed LTD/STD), or auto/no-fault PIP denials — structurally different appeal regimes needing different domain content. Logged to the ledger as a new, distinct `new` item for a future day's dedicated deep-dive, not a revival of this one.

### 2. AI voice-clone family-defense kit — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** App/checklist helping families set up and practice a codeword/challenge-question protocol specifically against AI voice-clone "grandparent scam" calls, sold one-time or as a low-cost family plan — distinct from the existing in-sheet "is this a scam?" text/screenshot checker.
- **Risks:** Core mechanism is free public advice; a funded, better-built competitor beat it to market by three weeks; the stats motivating urgency are unsupported.
- **Consumer pain:** Real but smaller than the headline claims — FBI IC3 2025: $352M in AI-tagged losses among 60+ victims (not the fabricated $2.3B/2026 figure circulating; see World Watch). ~1-in-4 adults report encountering an AI voice scam (McAfee survey, vendor-claimed).
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Genuine — voice cloning from ~3 seconds of audio has been cheap/consumer-accessible only since ~2023–24. But this "why now" already produced funded competitors; it didn't leave a gap.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They do. Cloaked ($375M Series B, Call Guard feature, $119.99/yr), Truecaller (Family Protection), Norton, McAfee, Aura, Bitdefender all ship voice/call-scam features.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet:** It's saturating in real time — Savi Security ($7M seed, $8/mo family plan, real-time in-call detection) launched July 7, 2026, days before this scan.
- **What changes in 2 years:** Behavioral/audio-biometric detection gets cheaper and better, widening the gap between a static codeword checklist and real-time detection — pushes further against the thin-wrapper version.
- **Competitors:** Savi Security, Cloaked, Truecaller, Norton/McAfee/Aura/Bitdefender, VoiceProtector.com, plus free FTC/AARP/FBI-published codeword advice.
- **Who pays:** Adult child buying protection for an aging parent — real precedent exists (Savi $8/mo, Cloaked $120/yr, SilverShield $5/mo subsidized) but all subscription; no one-time SKU comp found.
- **Expected CAC/LTV/LTV:CAC/ARR ceiling:** No credible figures found for this specific niche — omitted rather than fabricated.
- **Time to first sellable version:** 1–2 weeks for the thin checklist version; the differentiated (real-time detection) version is a multi-month ML/audio effort outside near-term solo scope.
- **Operational load:** Low if kept static — but that's the version already outflanked by active-detection subscriptions.
- **Sources:** [FTC 2025 imposter-scam data](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/06/ftc-data-show-people-reported-losing-3-point-5-billion-imposter-scams-2025), [FBI IC3 2025 Annual Report](https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2025_IC3Report.pdf), [AARP emergency-scam guide](https://states.aarp.org/colorado/beware-emergency-scams), [Savi Security — TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/07/savis-app-aims-to-protect-consumers-from-realistic-ai-scams-like-kidnappers-demanding-ransom/), [Cloaked $375M — TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/19/consumer-focused-privacy-company-cloaked-raises-375m-as-it-expands-to-enterprise/)
- **Kill reason:** The free mechanism (family codeword) is already standard FTC/AARP advice needing no product, and the paid differentiated version (real-time voice-clone detection) was funded and shipped by a better-resourced competitor three weeks before this analysis — no defensible space between "free" and "already built by someone else."

### 3. Senior digital-legacy / genetic-data cleanup kit (23andMe fallout) — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** Guided one-time service helping (mostly 50+, genealogy-skewing) consumers delete/lock down 23andMe account data after its 2025 bankruptcy, potentially generalizing to a "your data after a company goes bankrupt" playbook.
- **Risks:** Same failure pattern as the already-rejected government-ID setup kit — free, official, step-by-step guidance already exists everywhere; trigger event is 12–16 months stale.
- **Consumer pain:** Real but shrinking and mostly already self-served — 1.9M of ~15M customers (≈15%) had requested deletion via the free flow by June 2025, within about 3 months of the trigger. No reported cases of actual data sale to a bad actor since the bankruptcy.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** The only genuinely "now" news is a July 14, 2026 multistate AG settlement ($18M) that reads as case-closed/restitution, not fresh danger — undercuts urgency-based marketing right as a product would launch.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already effectively do — EFF, CBS, NBC, MIT Technology Review, Fortune, and every relevant state AG's office published free, near-identical step-by-step deletion guides within days of the trigger.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet:** It's saturated on the education side; the unsaturated sliver (login/email-recovery-locked users) is small and would require an open-ended labor-heavy concierge model, not a course.
- **What changes in 2 years:** Little for 23andMe specifically — TTAM's privacy guardrails and AG oversight are now locked in by settlement. A generalized "future bankruptcy" playbook is speculative and event-driven.
- **Competitors:** DeleteMe ($129–139/yr), Incogni ($7.99/mo), Optery ($3.99–24.99/mo) — broker-removal category, doesn't currently cover this single-site task, but only because the task (one settings page, one confirmation email) is too simple to need their automation model. Real competitor is the free-guide ecosystem (EFF et al.).
- **Who pays:** Adult child buying peace-of-mind for a parent — same buyer profile as the rejected ID.me idea.
- **Expected CAC/LTV/LTV:CAC/ARR ceiling:** No reliable figures found — directionally weak; "why pay, it's one click" objection mirrors the ID.me case exactly.
- **Time to first sellable version:** 1–2 weeks technically (content + checklist).
- **Operational load:** Low for pure content; high and open-ended if a live-support/account-recovery concierge tier is added.
- **Sources:** [EFF — How to Delete Your 23andMe Data](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2025/03/how-delete-your-23andme-data), [TechCrunch — 15% requested deletion](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/11/23andme-says-15-of-customers-asked-to-delete-their-genetic-data-since-bankruptcy/), [CNBC — Wojcicki buys back 23andMe](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/06/13/anne-wojcicki-to-buy-back-23andme-and-its-data-for-305-million.html), [NY AG — $18M settlement, July 2026](https://ag.ny.gov/press-release/2026/attorney-general-james-secures-18-million-23andme-failing-protect-customers), [Security.org — DeleteMe/Incogni/Optery pricing](https://www.security.org/data-removal/best/)
- **Kill reason:** Free, authoritative, step-by-step deletion guidance (EFF, every major outlet, every relevant state AG) already existed within days of the trigger, and 15% of the 15M-customer base self-served through it over a year ago — the identical "free official help already solves it" failure mode that killed the ID.me government-identity kit.

**Bottom line:** No survivors today, but a real save — catching that Claimable already owns the insurance-appeal-kit idea before Lance spent time on it is exactly what this process is for. Process note: two of today's three candidates (voice-clone defense, 23andMe cleanup) died to the same pattern already seen once before (free official help fully covers the task) — worth checking for that pattern *before* selecting a candidate, not after, especially for anything in the "help a senior deal with an official/institutional process" shape.
