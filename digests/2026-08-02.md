## Ideas

Three candidates investigated, all rejected — each kill is a genuine, checkable finding (existing free incumbent, existing direct competitor, or the underlying fix being trivially free), not a demand or timing problem. All three trace back to the two Aug 1/fresh-law world-watch items above, which looked promising enough on a first pass to warrant the deep-dive.

### 1. Elder Fraud Freeze / Trusted Contact registration concierge — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A guided service for adult children that inventories every financial account an aging parent holds (bank, credit union, brokerage) and gets a Trusted Contact Person designated on each one, to avoid getting caught in the new 14–15 business day exploitation-hold freezes.
- **Risks:** Core fix is free and fast at the source; no evidence multi-account aggregation is a real bottleneck (most seniors hold 2–4 accounts, not dozens); no existing player to benchmark pricing against.
- **Consumer pain:** Real and freshly regulatory — over half of US states now have Safe Harbor hold laws in effect since Jan 1, 2026, and frustrated-customer reports describe freezes lasting from 48 hours up to several weeks, disrupting mortgage/prescription/utility payments for households living paycheck to paycheck.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Genuinely sharp — this wave of state hold laws is dated to Jan 1, 2026, confirmed by an FTC survey report, not recycled older news.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They don't need to — each individual bank/brokerage already offers Trusted Contact designation as a built-in, no-cost feature (FINRA Rule 4512 has required brokerages to ask since 2018); there's no institutional incentive to build a cross-institution aggregator.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet:** No dedicated multi-bank Trusted Contact concierge product was found — but that's because the underlying task doesn't need one: designating a Trusted Contact takes under 5 minutes by phone at virtually every major bank, per FINRA's own investor guidance.
- **What will change in next 2 years:** More states will likely add hold laws (Minnesota, Florida already strengthened theirs for 2026), keeping the underlying pain real — but doesn't change that the fix stays free and fast at the source.
- **Competitors:** None found as a direct multi-bank Trusted Contact aggregator; adjacent overlap with the already in-sheet "Solo-ager readiness organizer" (broader legal/who-acts-for-me kit) and general senior concierge services (GoGoGrandparent, task-based, not financial-paperwork-specific).
- **Who pays:** Would be the adult child, but see kill reason — no clear reason to pay for what's a free 5-minute call per account.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / LTV:CAC / ARR ceiling:** Not modeled — no viable price point identified before the unit economics question is even reachable.
- **Time to first sellable version:** Days (a checklist + call-script product), irrelevant given the pricing ceiling problem.
- **Operational load:** Low, but moot.
- **Sources:** [FTC — State Hold Laws and Elder Financial Exploitation Prevention survey (PDF, 2025)](https://consumer.ftc.gov/system/files/consumer_ftc_gov/pdf/State%20Hold%20Laws%20and%20Elder%20Financial%20Exploitation%20Prevention%20(2025).pdf), [SavingAdvice — Elder Fraud Freeze explainer](https://www.savingadvice.com/articles/2026/01/15/10714740_the-elder-fraud-freeze-why-banks-are-now-legally-allowed-to-hold-your-senior-transfer-for-14-business-days.html), [FINRA — Trusted Contact investor bulletin](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/trusted-contact), [FINRA Rule 4512 background](https://bhseclaw.com/blog/finra-trusted-contact-rule/)
- **Kill reason:** The regulatory trigger is real and fresh, but the actual fix (a free, ~5-minute phone call per account, already built into every major bank's account-opening flow since a 2018 FINRA rule) is too cheap and fast to support a paid product standing alone — this is a checklist item to fold into an existing senior-safety offering (e.g. as a one-time setup step inside the in-sheet Senior Line or Solo-ager organizer), not a business by itself.

### 2. Meta ad rejection / account-health pre-flight checker (AI-content focus) — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A tool advertisers run creative through before spending budget, predicting whether Meta's new proactive MARS scanning system will flag it for undisclosed AI content, health claims, or other rejection triggers — positioned to reduce the 14%-of-rejections currently coming from AI-disclosure misses.
- **Risks:** Direct competitor already live; Meta's own compliance toggle already addresses the core AI-disclosure case for free; thin, narrow feature surface easily folded into existing ad-management suites.
- **Business pain:** Real and Lance has direct founder-market fit here (careerpicai runs exactly this kind of AI-generated-creative Meta funnel) — health/beauty advertisers saw rejection rates jump 34% overnight under the new MARS system, and undisclosed AI content is now the 3rd-largest rejection category.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Sharp — MARS's shift to proactive, pre-impression scanning across 5 data layers is a 2026 change, and the mandatory AI-disclosure label requirement is new this cycle.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do. auditsocials.com runs a live "Meta Ad Rejection Predictor" tool that pre-screens ad copy/creative against 40+ known Meta policy triggers; Revealbot already automates the reactive side (auto-pause + Slack/email alert + backup-campaign trigger on rejection).
- **Why isn't it saturated yet:** It isn't fully saturated as a dedicated AI-disclosure-specific predictor, but the feature surface is thin enough that it reads as a checklist add-on to existing tools (Revealbot, AdEspresso-class platforms) rather than a standalone wedge — same conclusion the ledger already reached rejecting the broader "Meta AI-ad compliance/detection tool" idea on 2026-07-28.
- **What will change in next 2 years:** Nothing that reopens the wedge — Meta's own one-click AI-disclosure toggle inside Ads Manager already discharges the main compliance burden for free, and MARS will keep getting more automated, not less.
- **Competitors:** auditsocials.com (Meta Ad Rejection Predictor, live), Revealbot (rejection automation/alerting), Meta's own native AI-disclosure toggle, broader ad-management suites (AdEspresso/Hootsuite) that could bolt this on as a feature.
- **Who pays:** Advertisers running AI-generated creative at meaningful spend — but see kill reason on why the ceiling here is low.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / LTV:CAC / ARR ceiling:** Not modeled — narrow feature surface with a free-tier direct competitor already live caps pricing before a funnel is worth building.
- **Time to first sellable version:** Technically fast (an API-based checklist scorer), moot given competitive findings.
- **Operational load:** Low, but moot.
- **Sources:** [AuditSocials — Meta ad policy 2026 guide](https://www.auditsocials.com/blog/meta-ad-policy-updates-2026-guide), [ScalingJournal — Meta advertising policies / rejection triggers](https://scalingjournal.com/meta-advertising-policies-explained/), [AdStellar — Meta Ads Policy Checker Software roundup](https://www.adstellar.ai/blog/meta-ads-policy-checker-software), [Servo — Meta Ads Automation Tools 2026](https://servoad.com/blog/best-meta-ads-automation-tools-2026)
- **Kill reason:** This repeats the ledger's 2026-07-28 finding in a new packaging — a named, live competitor (auditsocials.com's own Meta Ad Rejection Predictor) already ships the exact pre-flight check, Meta's native toggle already handles disclosure for free, and the reactive side is already automated by Revealbot, leaving no open feature surface worth a standalone product.

### 3. Recovery-scam verification/protection service (targeting prior fraud victims) — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A service for families of a senior who was just scammed, verifying whether a subsequent "we can recover your funds" contact is legitimate or a follow-up recovery scam, given fraud victims frequently end up on resold "sucker lists."
- **Risks:** Core intervention is informational and already given away free by multiple established channels; the addressable moment (right after a fresh scam, before a recovery scammer strikes) is narrow and hard to reach via paid ads; monetizing a person who just lost money is a bad first-touch economic and trust proposition.
- **Consumer pain:** Real and freshly quantified — FBI IC3's 2025 annual report puts recovery-scam losses at $540M for the year, and FTC estimates ~40% of digital scam victims get scammed again while trying to recover funds.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** The $540M IC3 figure is new (2025 data, released in the 2026 annual report) and "experts predict recovery scams will be a top fraud category in 2026," but the underlying mechanism (scammers trading victim lists) is decades old, not a fresh phenomenon — the "why now" is a new number on an old problem, not a new problem.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do, for free. AARP's Fraud Watch Network Helpline (877-908-3360, free, open to non-members, live specialist guidance) exists specifically for "what do I do next" after a scam. Unscammed AI already offers free AI-assisted fraud reporting and bank-letter drafting for victims (UK and US).
- **Why isn't it saturated yet:** It's covered on the free/informational side; the gap, if any, is proactive outreach in the narrow post-scam window — but no plausible cheap distribution channel to reach a specific senior in the hours/days right after their scam was found.
- **What will change in next 2 years:** Recovery-scam dollar volume likely keeps growing alongside overall elder fraud, but that doesn't change the free-incumbent or distribution-timing problems.
- **Competitors:** AARP Fraud Watch Network Helpline (free), Unscammed AI (free core reporting/paperwork assistance), FTC/FBI IC3 public guidance, state AG consumer-protection lines.
- **Who pays:** Adult children, in theory — but seconds after a family member has already lost money is a uniquely bad moment to sell a new subscription, and the free-helpline alternative is a live objection at checkout.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / LTV:CAC / ARR ceiling:** Not modeled — the distribution-timing problem (no channel to reach a just-victimized family in the relevant window) kills the funnel before unit economics are reachable.
- **Time to first sellable version:** Not evaluated — moot given the above.
- **Operational load:** Not evaluated — moot given the above.
- **Sources:** [AARP — Recovery scams explainer](https://www.aarp.org/money/scams-fraud/recovery-scams/), [AARP Fraud Watch Network Helpline](https://www.aarp.org/money/scams-fraud/helpline/), [Unscammed AI](https://unscammed.com/us/online-safety/already-a-victim-don-t-let-scam-recovery-services-scam-you-again), [FBI IC3 2025 elder fraud data summary](https://seniors.hcsk.org/elder-fraud-statistics-research/)
- **Kill reason:** The advice a paid product would sell ("never pay an upfront fee to a recovery service, verify independently") is already given away free by AARP's live helpline and Unscammed AI's reporting assistant, and there's no credible paid-acquisition channel to reach a specific family in the narrow post-scam window before a recovery scammer does — this is better handled as a feature/talking-point inside the in-sheet Senior Line's existing family-briefing mechanic than as a standalone product.

**Bottom line:** Zero survivors today. Process note: two of three kills (Trusted Contact concierge, recovery-scam verification) share a pattern worth naming for future cycles — a real, freshly-regulatory or freshly-quantified pain point whose actual remedy is *already free and fast* at the source (a bank phone call, a nonprofit helpline) doesn't become a product just because the pain is real; the gap has to be in execution speed/aggregation/reach, not in access to the underlying advice. Both ideas are logged here as fold-in features for the already in-sheet Senior Line rather than independent product bets.
