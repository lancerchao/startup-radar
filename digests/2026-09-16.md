## Ideas

Three candidates deep-dived this cycle, all rejected — the forty-third straight cycle with zero survivors.

### 1. Meta "value rules" placement spend-leak auditor — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A continuous monitoring tool for small/DTC Meta advertisers that detects placement-level ad-spend leakage now that Meta has replaced hard, per-ad-set placement exclusion with "value rules" — bid-value suppression capped at -90%, never full exclusion — comparing actual spend-by-placement against the advertiser's intent and alerting on drift.
- **Risks:** Reads as a feature, not a company — the exact buyer is already served by tools that could ship this specific check in a sprint.
- **Consumer/buyer pain (quantified):** Thin. No dollar figures, %-of-spend estimates, or forum/Reddit complaint volume found anywhere; the rollout is only ~3 weeks old (staggered start ~Aug 25, 2026) and what exists is trade-press explainer coverage (PPC.land, Jon Loomer Digital, TheOptimizer), not advertiser outrage yet. Meta also kept a full-block escape hatch at the account level (Advertising Settings → Placement Controls), which blunts the worst-case framing — the real gap is narrower: advertisers who need *different* rules per campaign, or don't know the account-level toggle exists.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Genuinely fresh — Marketing API v26.0 changelog July 29, 2026; advertisers started publicly noticing the missing per-ad-set control around Aug 25.
- **Why don't existing big players compete — they're already circling it:** TheOptimizer has already published a 2026 blog specifically on "how to actually block placements, it's not as simple anymore," and already sells rule-based automated placement blocking. Revealbot and Madgicx serve the identical self-serve buyer with rule-based automation one API query away from shipping the same alert.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it effectively already is:** No tool markets this exact "value-rule leak" feature by name yet, but the technical build (Meta Insights API broken out by `publisher_platform`/`platform_position`, diffed against configured intent) isn't defensible — it's a standard query incumbents with existing distribution could add in days.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** The market confusion (advertisers not realizing "value rule ≠ exclusion") likely fades within months as trade press and Meta's own UI copy catch up — that part is temporary. The residual leak is durable by design (a -90% rule structurally guarantees some spend still lands in the "excluded" placement forever), but that's a thin, feature-sized value, not a company-sized one.
- **Competitors:** TheOptimizer, Revealbot, Madgicx (self-serve, same buyer); Triple Whale, Northbeam, Smartly.io (enterprise-adjacent, already touch placement/attribution data for larger accounts).
- **Who pays / sales motion:** Would be self-serve SaaS to small/DTC advertisers and boutique agencies — Revealbot starts at $99/mo to this exact buyer.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Not meaningfully scoreable — no price or feature gap between what a new entrant could charge and what incumbents could ship free as a feature update.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** Days to build — which is exactly why 3+ better-distributed incumbents are one sprint away from doing the same thing.
- **Sources:** [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/some-meta-advertisers-lose-placement-controls-with-bid-cuts-capped-at-90/), [Jon Loomer Digital](https://www.jonloomer.com/meta-removing-placement-controls-ad-sets/), [TheOptimizer](https://theoptimizer.io/blog/meta-ads-placement-control-in-2026-how-to-actually-block-placements-its-not-as-simple-anymore), [Coinis](https://coinis.com/blog/meta-removing-placement-exclusions-ad-sets), [Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/news-2/meta-remove-placement-controls-from-ad-sets-12401398).
- **Kill reason:** The mechanic and timing are genuinely fresh, but this is a sprint-sized feature already within reach of at least three existing self-serve tools that already write content about the exact keyword, and the pain evidence is still unverified three weeks post-rollout — no Reddit/forum outrage found despite active searching.
- **Not scored against B2C_RULES** — B2B, buyer is a small/DTC advertiser.

### 2. OSS code-provenance / "Turnitin for code" (Big Tech vs. small-OSS attribution theft) — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A code-fingerprinting and license-compliance monitoring service pointed in the *reverse* direction from standard compliance tools — helping open-source maintainers (or better, VC-funded OSS-core startups) detect when a larger, better-resourced company forks or copies their code/design and relaunches it without attribution, triggered by this week's viral Minitap-vs-Google dispute.
- **Risks:** The sympathetic buyer (unpaid OSS maintainers) structurally can't or won't pay; the only buyer with real money reacts with lawyers and relicensing, not a SaaS subscription.
- **Consumer/buyer pain (quantified):** Real and recurring as a 2026 pattern, not a one-off — beyond Minitap/Google, at least two comparable disputes this year: Corgi (YC-backed) accused of cloning open-source Papermark, and Delve (YC-backed) accused of forking Apache-licensed Sim.ai/SimStudio without attribution. But it's clustered narrowly in fast-moving, thinly-resourced AI-agent-tooling OSS projects getting outrun by better-funded look-alikes — not a broad, cross-industry epidemic.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** The wave of AI-agent-tooling OSS projects colliding with well-funded competitors chasing the same space is itself a 2026 phenomenon — three named disputes inside one year.
- **Why don't existing big players compete — they already exist, pointed the other way:** FOSSA, Black Duck, Mend, and Snyk Open Source all help a company that *consumes* open source verify it isn't violating licenses in its own product (the buyer is the copier's legal team, not the original author). The one tool built for the actual "was my code vendored elsewhere" direction — Trail of Bits' Vendetect — is free and open source (AGPLv3).
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — the core tech already is, for free:** Vendetect covers the detection mechanism at no cost; no dedicated product exists on the victim's side specifically because there's no budget there, not because of a technical gap.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** More disputes will likely surface as the AI-tooling OSS wave continues, but the buyer-side economics don't change — there still isn't anyone positioned to pay recurring fees for this.
- **Competitors:** Vendetect (free, open source, Trail of Bits); FOSSA, Black Duck, Mend, Snyk Open Source (all serve the opposite buyer direction).
- **Who pays / sales motion:** No credible recurring payer identified. Software Freedom Conservancy — the standard-bearer for GPL enforcement — raised only about $50K in public donations for one enforcement push, confirming unpaid maintainers won't fund this. The one buyer with money (VC-funded OSS-core startups) responds to exactly this scenario with legal action or license changes (MongoDB/Redis/Elastic's move to SSPL against cloud giants), not a subscription purchase.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Not meaningfully scoreable — no viable recurring buyer identified.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** Technically fast to build on top of free/open-source fingerprinting tech — irrelevant given no buyer would pay for it.
- **Sources:** [Minitap — "I expected better from Google"](https://www.minitap.ai/blog/i-expected-better-from-google), [Hacker News thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49716265), [TechCrunch — Corgi/Papermark](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/26/corgi-the-buzzy-y-combinator-backed-insurance-tech-startup-says-it-didnt-steal-an-open-source-product/), [Open Source For You — Delve/Sim.ai](https://www.opensourceforu.com/2026/04/delve-accused-of-passing-open-source-ai-tool-as-its-own/), [Trail of Bits — Vendetect](https://github.com/trailofbits/vendetect), [Software Freedom Conservancy — enforcement strategy](https://sfconservancy.org/copyleft-compliance/enforcement-strategy.html).
- **Kill reason:** The sympathetic buyer (unpaid OSS maintainers) has, by the Software Freedom Conservancy's own fundraising numbers, proven it won't or can't pay for this; the one buyer with real money responds to this exact scenario with lawyers and license changes instead of a SaaS subscription — no recurring-revenue model survives contact with either buyer segment.
- **Not scored against B2C_RULES** — B2B, and killed on "no viable payer" before a sales-motion question was even relevant.

### 3. Faire wholesale credit-line suspension/appeal service for independent retail shops — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A service for small independent retail shop owners who buy inventory via Faire's net-terms credit to help them avoid, appeal, or recover from sudden credit-line suspension or account rejection — modeled on the existing Amazon-seller-suspension-appeal consulting industry.
- **Risks:** Every dimension needed to validate this as a business — severity, buyer-pool size, recurrence — is unverified or absent from accessible sources.
- **Consumer/buyer pain (quantified):** Directionally real but unquantified. Faire's Retailer Terms of Service reportedly reserve the right to suspend or terminate accounts "without prior notice," "with or without cause"; BBB complaints describe cancelled orders and accounts flagged "unverified" despite submitted EIN letters and resale certificates, with support channels that dead-end. No dollar figures (credit-line sizes, lost-order value) or complaint-volume counts were found — direct Reddit and BBB page fetches were blocked in this research environment, so this rests on search-engine-summarized secondary sources, not verified primary text.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** No fresh trigger identified — no evidence this is a new-in-2026 policy or a recent spike in incidence; unclear how long the pattern has existed.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** Faire does publish a suspended-account appeal process, but it appears aimed at brand/seller suspensions, not retailer credit-line decisions specifically — a plausible gap on the buyer side, but unverified in scope.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet:** No direct competitor found, but the closest analog — the mature Amazon-seller-suspension-appeal consulting industry (Riverbend Consulting, AmazonSellersLawyer, SellerAppeal) — suggests the natural model here is a one-off paid crisis consult, not a subscription product.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** Unclear — no evidence found of a trend making this better or worse. Two Faire-adjacent wholesale-credit competitors (Tundra, Abound) have already shut down, narrowing rather than widening the space.
- **Competitors:** None found addressing Faire credit specifically; closest analog is the Amazon suspension-appeal consulting category, which proves people will pay for one-off crisis rescue, not that they'll pay for ongoing monitoring.
- **Who pays / sales motion:** Would be one-off paid service to individual shop owners in an active crisis, not a recurring subscription — most shops likely wouldn't perceive risk until already suspended, undercutting any "monitor continuously" pitch.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Not meaningfully scoreable — no data found on what share of Faire's reported 500K–700K active retailers actually use net-terms credit, or on suspension incidence rate.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** A landing-page/cold-outreach smoke test could run in days — that's the appropriate next step to even validate demand, not a build-ready product.
- **Sources:** [Faire Retailer TOS](https://cdn.faire.com/static/Retailer+TOS+-+May+10+2021+-+ENGLISH.pdf), [Faire BBB profile/complaints](https://www.bbb.org/us/ca/san-francisco/profile/wholesale-jewelry/faire-1116-930351/complaints), [Faire suspended-accounts help article](https://www.faire.com/support/articles/360019040671), [ResolvePay — Tundra shutdown](https://resolvepay.com/blog/tundra-shut-down), [ResolvePay — Abound wholesale payment terms](https://resolvepay.com/blog/abound-wholesale-payment-terms), [Shopify — What is Faire Wholesale](https://www.shopify.com/blog/what-is-faire-wholesale).
- **Kill reason:** Every dimension needed to size this as a business — dollar impact, buyer-pool size, recurrence — is unverified or absent, and the closest real-world analog is structurally a rare, acute, one-time paid rescue service rather than a recurring subscription; no evidence of a Meta-targetable "independent retail shop owner" audience either.
- **Not scored against B2C_RULES** — ambiguous B2B/prosumer buyer (small business owner), and killed on unproven pain/market sizing before a rules check would add information.

**Bottom line:** Forty-third straight cycle, zero survivors. All three candidates this cycle had genuinely fresh or under-explored triggers (Meta's 3-week-old placement-control change, this week's viral OSS-attribution dispute, a plausible-but-undocumented Faire pain point), and none were killed by a named direct clone — a change of pattern from many recent cycles. Instead all three died on more fundamental structural tests: the Meta idea is a sprint-sized feature already in reach of better-distributed incumbents; the OSS-provenance idea has real detection tech already free but no buyer segment that can or will pay; the Faire idea has a plausible pain point but no evidence it's frequent, costly, or recurring enough to support anything beyond a one-off consulting play. Worth carrying forward as a general lesson: "is there a payer" and "is this recurring" are now filtering out ideas as often as "is there a competitor."
