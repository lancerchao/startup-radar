## Ideas

Three candidates deep-dived this cycle, all rejected — the eighteenth straight cycle with zero survivors. Recurring pattern held again: every fresh "why now" trigger led straight to a well-funded incumbent (Shopify itself, in one case) that had already shipped the free or near-free version of the exact product.

### 1. Agentic-commerce readiness audit/fix-it service for small Shopify merchants — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A paid service (audit + guided fixes) helping small/solo Shopify merchants get their product catalog properly structured (JSON-LD schema, shipping/return policy exposure, LLM.txt files) so AI shopping agents (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Copilot) can discover, trust, and transact against their store.
- **Risks:** The exact deliverable — a scored audit identifying missing schema fields — is already shipped free by the platform the merchant is already on.
- **Consumer/buyer pain (quantified):** Real. ChatGPT Instant Checkout adoption stalled at under 30 Shopify merchants before OpenAI sunset the original integration in March 2026; only 4% of shoppers trust AI at the payment stage even though 63% want AI for product discovery, and Adobe Analytics measured 42% higher conversion among AI-referred shoppers in Q1 2026 — real upside being left on the table by unprepared stores.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Genuinely fresh infrastructure-wise — Shopify's Spring '26 Edition (June 17, 2026) turned on Agentic Storefronts/MCP by default, and PayPal's ACP server (announced 2026) is bringing its 430M+ active accounts into ChatGPT commerce this year.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do, for free, at the platform level. Shopify's own commerce-readiness.shopify.io (live since ~April 26, 2026) runs all 31 of the relevant checks on any public store URL with no login and no cost. Perplexity's Merchant Program is also a free, five-minute, no-minimum-revenue signup that improves indexing and unlocks in-chat checkout.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it can't be, the free tier already covers the whole job:** A crop of agency content (digitalapplied, aiadvantageagency, askphill, No Hacks) already exists purely to explain Shopify's own free tool to merchants — the market has organized around interpreting a free product, not around a paid alternative to it.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** Shopify will keep expanding the free tool's coverage as agentic-checkout adoption grows (it's core platform retention for them), continuously shrinking any paid wrapper's differentiation.
- **Competitors:** Shopify's own Agentic Commerce Readiness Audit (free), PayPal ACP server (free to merchants), Perplexity Merchant Program (free), plus a wave of agency blog content repackaging the same checklist (digitalapplied.com, aiadvantageagency.com, askphill.com, craftshift.com, tacey.app).
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** Would-be self-serve B2B SaaS or productized-consulting sale to solo Shopify merchants — but the merchant's actual next step after running Shopify's free scanner is "fix the flagged items myself or hire a $50 Fiverr gig," not "pay for a second scanner."
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Poor. No paid wedge survives a free, platform-native competitor that already owns full technical access to the exact data (product catalog, schema) being scored.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** Days for a comparable scanner — irrelevant, since Shopify shipped one first and owns the distribution advantage of being the platform merchants are already logged into.
- **Sources:** [Shopify community thread](https://community.shopify.com/t/shopify-launched-a-free-agentic-commerce-readiness-scanner-here-is-what-it-found-and-what-to-do-next/614767), [Ecommerce Fastlane — readiness tool guide](https://ecommercefastlane.com/shopify-commerce-readiness-tool-guide/), [Ask Phill — Shopify agentic commerce guide](https://askphill.com/blogs/blog/agentic-commerce-for-shopify-protocols-platforms-and-what-to-prioritize-in-2026), [Website Builder Expert — Perplexity Merchant Program](https://www.websitebuilderexpert.com/news/perplexity-merchant-program/), [laioutr — ChatGPT Instant Checkout adoption stalled](https://www.laioutr.com/en/blog/chatgpt-instant-checkout-merchant-adoption-agentic-readiness-2026).
- **Kill reason:** Shopify itself already ships the exact scored-audit product free, with no login, directly against the merchant's real store data, on the platform the merchant already runs on — no paid wrapper has a wedge against that.
- **Not scored against B2C_RULES** — B2B, buyer is a Shopify merchant/e-commerce seller, not a personal consumer.

### 2. Post-loss financial & account-transition concierge kit (for the surviving spouse) — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A paid, self-serve kit walking a newly widowed person through the practical aftermath — retitling accounts, updating beneficiaries, notifying institutions, canceling subscriptions/memberships in the deceased's name — targeted at the same senior-skewed buyer as Lance's existing careerpicai.com funnel.
- **Risks:** The dominant version of this product is already free to the buyer (paid by an employer or insurer), and the audience-defining life event isn't Meta-targetable at all.
- **Consumer pain (quantified):** Real. ~11M widowed women in the US (Census Bureau); the paperwork burden (retitling accounts, beneficiary updates, income restructuring) is well-documented as a top complaint in the immediate aftermath of a spouse's death.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Weak — this is a structural, evergreen pain point with no fresh dated trigger; nothing this cycle changed the underlying need or the competitive landscape.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do, and they've captured the paying side of the market. Empathy (Series B, $47M, backed by Allianz/MassMutual/MetLife life-insurance carriers, $90M+ total raised) delivers exactly this — including a "LifeVault" account-transition tool and active-cancellation service for the deceased's subscriptions — free to the end user, monetized through employer bereavement-leave benefits and life-insurance riders covering 5M+ lives. Ever Loved and Betterleave run adjacent versions of the same model.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it already is, and the free/paid-by-someone-else model beats a consumer price point:** A widow being asked to pay out of pocket for something a competitor gives away as an insurance/employer benefit is a hard sell regardless of feature quality.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** Empathy's capital and carrier partnerships (three major life insurers as investors) mean it will keep expanding coverage of the exact population a new entrant would target, not shrinking.
- **B2C gate check (all 9):** Rule 1 (Meta-targetable) — **FAIL**; Meta's life-event targeting list (recently moved, new job, newly engaged, newlywed, new parents, anniversary, upcoming birthday) has no bereavement/widowed category — grief and loss have been treated as a "sensitive" excluded category since Meta's January 2022 policy sweep, the same wall that killed the "recently divorced" co-parenting idea (2026-08-04). Rule 2 (LTV + inflow) — weak/borderline; genuine inflow (~2.5M US deaths/year create new widows continuously) but retention is naturally short (the acute paperwork window is weeks, not months). Rule 3 (moat) — **FAIL**; nothing differentiable that a funded incumbent with carrier partnerships can't outspend. Rule 6 (niche) — pass, genuinely narrow. Rule 7 (reputationally clean) — pass. Rule 9 (over-mined shape) — pass, this specific shape (bereavement concierge) hasn't come up on this ledger before. **Fails decisively on Rule 1 alone**, per B2C_RULES's "fail one, it's a pass" standard.
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** Would need to be self-serve consumer purchase — but the credible competing offer is $0 via an employer or insurer, which is a structurally worse position than competing against a paid incumbent.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Unfavorable — no Meta ad channel to reach the buyer precisely, and the free incumbent already covers 5M+ lives.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** Weeks for a checklist-and-template product, but the audience-acquisition problem (no precise ad channel, buyer is in an acute grief window and hard to reach ethically via cold outbound) is the real blocker, not build time.
- **Sources:** [Index Ventures — Empathy $47M Series B](https://www.indexventures.com/perspectives/empathy-raises-47m-in-series-b-funding-to-lead-the-compassionate-economy/), [Empathy — managing accounts after a loss](https://www.empathy.com/managing-accounts/managing-accounts-after-a-loss), [Tracxn — Ever Loved competitors](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/ever-loved/alternatives-competitors), [Perspective Ministries — new widow checklist](https://perspectiveministries.org/the-new-widow-checklist/).
- **Kill reason:** Meta has no bereavement/widowed life-event targeting category (fails B2C Rule 1 outright, same wall as the "recently divorced" kill), and the credible competing product is already free to the buyer via a $90M+-funded startup's employer/insurer benefit model covering 5M+ lives.

### 3. AI-assisted hospital-bill negotiation/dispute service — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A service scanning itemized hospital bills for coding errors and charity-care eligibility, then negotiating down the balance on the patient's behalf, timed to the current wave of ACA-subsidy-driven self-pay growth.
- **Risks:** A mature, multi-vendor market with settled fee structures already serves this exact buyer, and the targeting channel is walled off by Meta's financial-services ad restrictions.
- **Pain (quantified):** Real and growing. ~20M Americans already carry medical debt over $250; ACA enhanced-subsidy expiration is projected to push ~5M people into uninsured status this year with average marketplace premiums up ~26%, and hospitals face a projected $32.1B self-pay revenue shortfall by end of 2026 as more patients can't pay upfront.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Real but incremental — this is an intensifying version of a pain that's existed for years (medical debt is already the #1 cause of US personal bankruptcy), not a fresh discontinuity.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do, at every price model. Goodbill (20% of savings, $1,000 cap, $5.4M raised since ~2022), Resolve (10-25% tiered, $249-499 deposit, $3.3M seed), CareRoute (18-25%, $1,000 cap), mediloop (flat fee: $69 EOB review / $129 single bill / $499/yr for six bills, no percentage cut), and Dollar For (free nonprofit, charity-care applications only) already span the full spectrum from percentage-of-savings to flat-fee to free.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it already is:** mediloop runs dedicated head-to-head comparison pages against Goodbill, Resolve, and Dollar For — the same "comparison-shopped, mature market" signature that killed the vibe-coding-security idea two cycles ago.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** More self-pay patients means more addressable bills for the five incumbents already in market, not an opening for a sixth.
- **B2C gate check:** Rule 1 (Meta-targetable) — **FAIL**; Meta's expanded "Financial Products and Services" special ad category (Jan 2025) now covers health-insurance-adjacent products, blocks precise targeting (states only, no ZIP, full age range required), and bans messaging that implies someone is "in debt" — nearly impossible to advertise a debt-negotiation product without tripping the exact language the policy prohibits. Rule 9 (over-mined shape) — borderline; adjacent to the already-rejected insurance-denial-fight-kit (2026-07-29) and disability/PIP appeal-letter kill (2026-07-29), reinforcing a pattern of "AI dispute/negotiation service for medical-adjacent paperwork" being a repeatedly-mined shape on this ledger. **Fails on Rule 1 alone.**
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** Self-serve consumer, but competing against five incumbents already running the same playbook at every price point from free to 25%-of-savings.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Weak — no Meta ad channel for precise targeting, one-and-done usage pattern per bill caps LTV, and price competition against a nonprofit (Dollar For) and a no-percentage flat-fee player (mediloop) compresses margin further.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** Weeks to build, but ongoing operational load is real and recurring (each case needs human-in-the-loop negotiation with a hospital billing department) — exactly why the five incumbents differentiate on service model (advocate-assigned vs. flat-fee vs. nonprofit) rather than on technology alone.
- **Sources:** [CNBC — ACA subsidy expiration effects](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/24/aca-enhanced-subsidy-expiration-effects.html), [Definitive Healthcare — ACA subsidy expiration provider impact](https://www.definitivehc.com/blog/provider-impact-of-enhanced-aca-subsidies-expiration), [CareRoute — medical bill negotiation services compared](https://www.careroute.ai/bill-defense/compare-medical-bill-negotiation-services), [mediloop — mediloop vs Goodbill](https://mediloop.ai/blog/mediloop-vs-goodbill), [mediloop — mediloop vs Resolve](https://mediloop.ai/blog/mediloop-vs-resolve), [GeekWire — Goodbill funding](https://www.geekwire.com/2024/goodbill-raises-2m-and-expands-customer-base-to-unearth-and-challenge-medical-billing-errors/), [BusinessWire — Resolve seed funding](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220718005136/en/Resolve-Raises-3.3M-in-Seed-Funding), [wetracked.io — Meta sensitive ad categories 2026](https://www.wetracked.io/post/meta-ads-new-sensitive-categories-restrictions).
- **Kill reason:** Five named competitors (Goodbill, Resolve, CareRoute, mediloop, Dollar For) already span the full fee-model spectrum from free to 25%-of-savings with a comparison-shopped market of their own, and Meta's expanded Financial Products/Services special ad category blocks the precise targeting and debt-referencing messaging this product would need to advertise.

**Bottom line:** Eighteenth straight cycle, zero survivors. All three candidates this cycle shared a specific failure mode: a platform or funded incumbent had already turned the exact deliverable into a free feature or free-to-the-end-user benefit (Shopify's own scanner, Empathy's insurer-funded model), which is a harder kill than "a competitor charges for this too" — there's no price point low enough to compete against $0. The medical-bill-negotiation candidate was the exception (real paid competitors, not free), but died on the now-familiar combination of a mature multi-vendor market plus a Meta targeting wall. Recurring lesson holds: strong, quantified "why now" pain (medical debt, agentic-commerce upside, bereavement paperwork) keeps being necessary but not sufficient — the same forces creating the pain are visible to, and often already acted on by, better-capitalized players first.
