## Ideas

Two candidates deep-dived this cycle, both rejected — the twenty-eighth straight cycle with zero survivors. Both looked like genuine "why now" triggers from the breadth scan (a real regulatory deadline with real financial stakes; a viral fairness complaint), and both died differently from the ledger's usual pattern of "a funded competitor already built it" — one to the specific combination of thin buyer economics and free incumbent-adjacent content, the other to a founding trigger that turned out to be fabricated plus a core mechanism that isn't technically buildable at all.

### 1. Apple EU App Store fee-tier decision/optimization tool for iOS developers — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A calculator-plus-advisory tool (and optionally implementation service) that models an iOS developer's actual EU revenue under Apple's new four-tier fee structure (26% IAP / 20% own processing / 15% link-out / 5% third-party distribution) given their specific refund rate, chargeback rate, processor fees, and expected conversion-rate delta for external payment links, then recommends a tier and helps implement the switch (StoreKit External Purchase Links, etc.) before the Oct 1, 2026 deadline and 12-month lock-in.
- **Risks:** The complexity that would motivate a developer to pay for help is the same complexity that plausibly pushes most developers toward inertia — defaulting to staying on Apple IAP rather than researching a switch.
- **Buyer pain (quantified):** Real financial stakes for the subset of developers with meaningful EU revenue: a wrong tier choice costs real margin for 12 months, and Apple's own manual-spreadsheet estimation tool explicitly stops applying Oct 1, 2026 with no confirmed replacement published as of this research.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Weak. Verified this is the third major rewrite of the same underlying EU compliance decision since Jan 2024 (original Core Technology Fee → June 2025 multi-fee stack → this Aug 2026 flat-tier structure) — developers have faced a version of this choice for two and a half years; only the lock-in mechanic and consolidated rate table are new.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do, informally — RevenueCat and Adapty both publish detailed explainer content on each EU fee change to their existing subscription-infrastructure customer base, a natural free upsell path that caps a standalone tool's pricing power.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — thin gap, not an empty one:** No live, verified, EU-2026-tier-specific calculator with a recommendation engine was found — third-party attempts are either stale (a 0-star/0-fork abandoned GitHub calculator built for 2024 rates) or unverifiable in depth (appstorefeepro.com claims 2026 EU coverage but couldn't be confirmed to model refund rates, processor fees, or give a tier recommendation). A real gap exists in *execution quality*, not in whether anyone has touched the space.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** Apple has rewritten these terms three times in under three years and Epic Games publicly called the new terms "junk fees" days after they were announced — further rule changes are likely, which favors an ongoing advisory relationship over a one-time tool, but also means anything built now needs re-work on Apple's schedule, not the founder's.
- **Competitors:** RevenueCat (general App Store revenue calculator, unconfirmed EU-tier depth), Adapty (published EU fee-change analysis as content), appstorefeepro.com (claims 2026 EU coverage, unverified depth), several stale/generic flat-rate calculators (theswiftk.it.com, fintoolsuite.com and similar) still quoting pre-2025 rates.
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** Founder-led or self-serve sale to indie/small iOS app businesses with real EU revenue — mechanically compatible with a web-checkout motion for the calculator itself, but any higher-ticket implementation-as-a-service component (StoreKit link integration) is closer to founder-led consulting than self-serve SaaS.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Buyer pool is a narrow niche of a narrow market: Apple's own Nov 2025 commissioned study (41M transactions, 21,000+ affected apps) found 88% of EU developers pay zero Apple commission and 75% are already on reduced-rate programs; EU App Store revenue is only ~7% of Apple's global App Store revenue. The realistic paying segment — developers with real EU revenue and genuine tier ambiguity — is likely low thousands of apps, capping ARR hard for a standalone tool.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** A calculator alone is buildable in days to weeks but has classic bad-LTV, solve-once economics (the graveyard of abandoned free calculators is the evidence); a defensible ongoing-advisory or implementation-service version needs real subject-matter depth (7-day link-out attribution windows, small-business/program discounts, CTC waiver thresholds) and would take longer to build genuine trust with a skeptical, EU-App-Store-only buyer.
- **Sources:** [TechCrunch — Aug 18 announcement](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/18/apple-overhauls-its-eu-app-store-fees-loosens-rules-for-alternative-app-stores/), [Apple DMA support page](https://developer.apple.com/support/dma-and-apps-in-the-eu/), [Apple CTF estimation page (manual spreadsheet process, expires Oct 1 2026)](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/understanding-the-core-technology-fee/estimate-fees-for-alternative-business-terms), [9to5Mac — Apple-commissioned DMA study, Nov 12 2025](https://9to5mac.com/2025/11/12/apple-says-study-proves-the-eu-is-wrong-about-lowering-app-prices/), [AppleInsider — Epic "junk fees" reaction](https://appleinsider.com/articles/26/08/19/epic-games-disagrees-with-apples-junk-eu-fee-changes), [Apple Newsroom Jan 2024 original terms](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2024/01/apple-announces-changes-to-ios-safari-and-the-app-store-in-the-european-union/).
- **Kill reason:** Third rewrite of a two-and-a-half-year-old decision, not a first-time novel one; Apple's own data shows 88% of EU developers pay zero commission and 75% are already on reduced rates, leaving a thin realistic buyer pool inside an already-small (~7% of global revenue) market; and RevenueCat/Adapty already give the core analysis away as content to their existing customer base, capping what a standalone tool could charge.
- **Not scored against B2C_RULES** — B2B, buyer is an iOS app developer/business, not a personal consumer.

### 2. Gig-driver per-trip pay-transparency/audit tool (DoorDash/Uber/Instacart/Lyft) — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A subscription tool for gig drivers that reconciles actual per-trip pay against platform-disclosed pay structures and legal minimums, flags underpayment or fee-skimming patterns, tracks it over time, and helps build a documented record — pitched as an ongoing pay-transparency product (recurring weekly checks), explicitly not the one-time deactivation-appeal shape this ledger already rejected on 2026-08-31.
- **Risks:** Two independent, each-sufficient kill risks: the founding trigger is fabricated, and the core mechanism may not be technically buildable at all.
- **Consumer pain (quantified):** The specific viral triggering claim (an alleged internal "priority fee"/"desperation score" leak, 80,000+ Reddit upvotes, Jan 2026) was confirmed by TechCrunch and others to be an AI-generated hoax by Jan 6, 2026 — the specific evidence was fabricated and DoorDash denied the mechanism ever existed. The broader opacity problem is real and separately documented, though: a $16.75M NY AG settlement (Feb 2025) for using tips to offset guaranteed pay, and NYC DCWP's Jan 2026 finding that tip-prompt dark patterns cost workers ~$550M (average per-delivery tip fell from $3.66 to $0.93 within a week of the city's minimum-pay law).
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Mixed. The specific trigger cited is fabricated and dateless as evidence; but NYC's enforcement blitz (Jan 2026) and Washington State's new itemized-receipt export requirement (effective July 1, 2026) are genuinely fresh and, unusually for this ledger, point toward new data availability rather than just new pain.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** Not applicable in the usual sense — the platforms themselves are the subject of the audit, so no big player would build this against their own interest.
- **Why isn't it saturated — it's not, but it may be un-buildable:** No existing tool (Gridwise, Solo, Para, Everlance, Gigwage) does per-trip pay-structure auditing against disclosed policy; Solo's "Pay Guarantee" is the closest analog but backs a prediction-vs-actual model, not a policy-compliance audit. The reason no one has built the strong version of this: the platforms' base-pay figure is generated by an undisclosed, proprietary algorithm with no published formula or rate card — there is no public ground truth for a third party to reconcile actual pay against, which is the exact opacity problem regulators and Human Rights Watch (\"The Gig Trap,\" May 2025) are already complaining about. A narrower version — checking disclosed per-trip receipts against legally mandated minimums and known-illegal patterns like tip-offsetting — is technically buildable, but only in the handful of jurisdictions (WA/Seattle, NYC) that currently mandate itemized, exportable receipt data.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** More cities may adopt NYC/Seattle-style minimum-pay and disclosure laws, incrementally widening the buildable-narrow-version footprint — but nothing on record suggests platforms will voluntarily publish the base-pay algorithm itself, so the broad "verify your pay was calculated correctly" version stays structurally unbuildable regardless of timeframe.
- **Competitors:** Gridwise (earnings/tax aggregation), Para (pre-acceptance pay estimates), Solo (pay-guarantee floor product), Everlance (mileage/tax), Gigwage (contractor payroll infrastructure, not consumer-facing) — none doing the specific audited-reconciliation mechanic, but all already own driver mindshare and distribution for anything adjacent.
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** The driver, self-serve — mechanically similar to Lance's checkout-funnel playbook, but this cycle's research could not reconfirm the prior ledger's claim of a clean, discrete Meta "gig/rideshare driver" interest-targeting category (Meta consolidated many granular interests into broader groups starting mid-2025); needs live verification in Ads Manager, not assumption, before any further work.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Poor as scoped. The only technically honest version of the product (a WA/Seattle/NYC minimum-pay-floor and tip-offset checker) would return "no violation" on most weekly checks for most drivers most of the time — the same weak-recurring-value problem, just spread across more check-ins instead of concentrated in one crisis moment, that undercut this ledger's already-rejected deactivation-appeal idea on Rule 2 grounds.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** The narrow, legally-grounded version is buildable in weeks against two to three jurisdictions' published minimum-pay rules; the broad version pitched in the trigger is not buildable at all without platform cooperation that has no reason to happen.
- **Sources:** [TechCrunch — Reddit post confirmed AI-generated hoax, Jan 6 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/06/a-viral-reddit-post-alleging-fraud-from-a-food-delivery-app-turned-out-to-be-ai-generated), [NY AG — $16.75M DoorDash settlement](https://ag.ny.gov/press-release/2025/attorney-general-james-secures-1675-million-doordash-cheating-delivery-workers), [NYC DCWP report — $550M tip-diversion finding](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dca/news/005-26/dcwp-report-shows-uber-doordash-drove-550-million-delivery-worker-pay-losses), [Human Rights Watch — "The Gig Trap," May 2025](https://www.hrw.org/report/2025/05/12/the-gig-trap/algorithmic-wage-and-labor-exploitation-in-platform-work-in-the-us), [Washington State L&I — itemized trip-receipt export rule, effective Jul 1 2026](https://lni.wa.gov/workers-rights/industry-specific-requirements/transportation-network-company-drivers-rights/getting-paid), [GeekWire — Seattle PayUp report](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/seattle-report-says-gig-worker-pay-law-is-working-countering-claims-by-doordash-and-uber/).
- **Kill reason:** The specific viral post that motivates this idea is a confirmed AI-generated hoax, not evidence; and the strong-form product (auditing whether algorithmic base pay was calculated correctly) is not technically buildable because the underlying pay algorithm is proprietary and undisclosed everywhere — there is no public ground truth to check against. The narrow, technically real version (a WA/Seattle/NYC minimum-pay-floor and tip-offset checker) is geographically thin and fails the same renewing-value test (Rule 2) that killed this ledger's prior gig-driver idea, just distributed across more low-signal check-ins instead of one crisis moment.
- **B2C_RULES gate check:** Fails **Rule 2** (weak renewing value — most checks return "no violation," same underlying problem as the rejected deactivation-appeal kit) and **Rule 3** (no moat — a minimum-pay-floor checker built on now-public WA/NYC receipt data is easily cloned by Gridwise/Para/Solo, who already own driver distribution) outright; **Rule 1** (Meta targeting) could not be reconfirmed this cycle and needs live verification, not assumption. Rejected on Rules 2 and 3 alone — did not proceed to score the remaining gates.

**Bottom line:** Twenty-eighth straight cycle, zero survivors. Neither candidate died to "a funded competitor already built the exact thing" — this cycle's failure modes were different: one idea's addressable buyer pool turned out to be a thin slice of an already-small market with the analysis already given away as content by adjacent incumbents (Apple EU fees), and the other's founding evidence was outright fabricated, with the honest core mechanism landing on a wall of platform algorithmic opacity that no regulation yet fully cracks open (gig pay-audit). The more durable finding this cycle is Washington State's new itemized-trip-receipt export rule (effective Jul 1, 2026) — the first regulation seen on this ledger that hands third parties real structured ground-truth data on gig pay, worth revisiting if more states or cities pass the same disclosure requirement and the buildable footprint widens past a two-to-three-city niche.
