## Ideas

Three candidates deep-dived this cycle, all rejected — the eighth straight cycle with zero survivors. All three looked fresh from the breadth scan (a recycled-but-dated backlash, an incumbent shipping a niche feature, a live Supreme Court case) and died the same way this project keeps re-finding: a well-resourced incumbent (funded, free, or platform-native) already occupies the exact wedge, usually for free.

### 1. Clean, paid Splitwise alternative — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A clean, ad-free, one-time-purchase (or low-cost) shared-expense/bill-splitting app for roommates, couples, and trip groups, positioned against Splitwise's daily free-tier expense caps, unskippable ads, and paywalled receipt-scan/search.
- **Risks:** Near-zero demonstrated willingness to pay for bill-splitting specifically; 15+ live competitors, most free; Venmo ships the identical feature natively for free; no defensible moat once cloned.
- **Consumer pain:** Real but not fresh — the daily-expense-cap/ad-unlock mechanic was introduced in **2023**, not 2026; current "2026 backlash" content is old grievances re-dated by SEO farms. Splitwise's own Play Store rating (4.17/5, ~190K reviews) contradicts a mass-exodus framing sourced from a small, self-selecting Trustpilot sample.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** No real trigger — nothing structurally changed in 2026; the pain predates this cycle by three years.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do, for free. Venmo shipped a native Groups feature (unlimited expenses, up to 32 people, 20 groups) in Nov 2023; Splitwise itself is a funded ($20M Series A, 2021), active, ~53-employee incumbent, not a neglected target.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it is:** Tricount (17M+ users, 100% free, bunq-acquired) and Spliit (free, open-source) dominate the free tier; Cino, a **€3.5M-seeded 2025 entrant** (Balderton Capital), already runs the identical "clean alternative to Splitwise" pitch with real growth (claimed 100% MoM in Finland/Italy). At least 13 more named indie competitors (Splitser, Split Circle, GoodShare, Kittysplit, ReceiptSplit, and others) run the same playbook against the same trigger this same year.
- **What will change in 2 years:** Nothing favorable — Meta's 2026 shift toward Advantage+ Audience-as-default further erodes precise, cheap targeting for a broad utility app like this.
- **Competitors:** Splitwise, Tricount, Venmo Groups, Cash App Pools, Spliit, Settle Up, Cino, plus 13+ smaller named indie clones.
- **Who pays / Expected CAC / LTV / LTV:CAC / ARR ceiling:** Unproven — the dominant incumbent's own decade of data implies near-zero conversion to paid once free alternatives are one search away; CAC likely $15–40+ on Meta with no precisely-targetable audience; a one-time-purchase model (Lance's stated preference) conflicts directly with Rule 2's renewing-LTV requirement; realistic ARR ceiling well under $500K given the category leader itself only pulls single-digit-million revenue after a decade.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** Fast to build (weeks) — the one point in its favor — but low operational load doesn't offset the economics.
- **B2C gate check:** **1 clear pass out of 9.** Rule 1 (Meta-targetable) — FAIL: "roommates" isn't a verified interest category, "recently moved" is a loose proxy dominated by home-services intent. Rule 2 (renewing LTV) — FAIL: one-time-purchase model has no accumulating hook. Rule 3 (moat) — FAIL: every differentiator is trivially cloneable and already cloned. Rule 4 (not thin wrapper) — marginal pass (core product isn't an LLM call). Rule 5 (referral) — FAIL: no aligned double-sided reward, no forced-multiplayer mechanic, no shareable artifact. Rule 6 (niche) — FAIL: "people who share expenses" is broad, not a defined niche. Rule 7 (reputationally clean) — PASS. Rule 8 (tellable story) — FAIL: no non-obvious distribution angle in a space already SEO-blanketed by 15+ competitors running the same playbook. Rule 9 (over-mined shape) — FAIL, clear-cut: at least 15 named live "Splitwise alternative" products plus a freshly-funded entrant.

### 2. ADHD-specific task-management app — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A paid planner built around ADHD cognition (task-initiation friction, time blindness, emotional regulation) rather than generic linear to-do lists, positioned against mainstream tools like Todoist.
- **Risks:** Crowded niche with an Apple-anointed category leader; the core AI differentiator is already free inside the mainstream incumbent it's positioned against; Meta ad targeting for the condition is restricted the same way health-condition targeting has failed repeatedly on this ledger.
- **Consumer pain (quantified):** Real. CDC Rapid Surveys System: adult ADHD diagnosis now 6.0% of US adults (~15.5M), up from a prior 4.4% estimate. A vendor-run survey claims 73% of ADHD adults struggle with task initiation — flagged as vendor-claimed, not independently verified.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Weak — adult ADHD diagnosis has risen steadily for years, not a 2026-specific inflection, and AI task-breakdown/voice capture has been commodity capability since 2023.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do. Todoist shipped **Ramble**, a voice brain-dump-to-structured-task feature explicitly marketed at ADHD task-capture friction, directly inside its mainstream product — the exact differentiator this idea would lead with.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it is:** Tiimo (Copenhagen, $4.8–6M raised, 1M+ users, 3M+ downloads, **Apple's 2025 iPhone App of the Year**) already owns the category, growing via TikTok creator partnerships rather than Meta ads. At least 10 more named competitors (Fluidwave, Sunsama, Goblin Tools, Llama Life, Saner.ai, Shimmer, Structured, Finch, Focusmate, HabitBox) surfaced in one research pass.
- **What will change in 2 years:** Nothing favorable — incumbents keep absorbing the differentiating AI features as free add-ons, compressing the wedge further.
- **Competitors:** Tiimo, Fluidwave, Sunsama, Goblin Tools, Llama Life, Saner.ai, Shimmer, Structured, Finch, Focusmate, HabitBox, EndeavorRx (FDA-cleared, adjacent clinical tier).
- **Who pays / Expected CAC / LTV / LTV:CAC / ARR ceiling:** Self-pay subscription, no employer/insurer channel; CAC likely $30–80 given no precise Meta targeting is available, forcing broad/creative-led acquisition; LTV roughly $150–250 against $54–240/yr competitor pricing, with churn risk plausibly worse than generic productivity apps (reviewers describe the required daily ritual becoming "another thing to fail at" for this population); ARR ceiling low-to-mid single digit millions — Tiimo, the category winner with Apple's own marketing megaphone, has raised under $6M total.

Rule 6 (niche) — moot: real niche, but undermined by Rule 1's targeting failure. Rule 7 (reputationally clean) — PASS. Rule 8 (tellable story) — FAIL: the only proven channel (Tiimo's TikTok creator strategy) is already the known playbook, not a novel insight, and isn't Lance's strength. Rule 9 (over-mined shape) — FAIL: 10+ named competitors, one of which is Apple's own App of the Year.

### 3. Compliance tool for Apple's external-purchase-link ruling — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A drop-in, compliant external-checkout tool/guidance service for small US iOS developers to route around Apple's in-app-purchase commission under the ongoing Epic v. Apple anti-steering ruling.
- **Risks:** No entitlement or approval process actually exists to solve for on the US storefront; the developer infrastructure this buyer already runs ships the same capability free; the underlying $0 commission is legally unsettled pending a Supreme Court merits ruling.
- **Business pain (quantified):** Real underlying dispute — Apple was found in willful contempt (Apr 30, 2025) for a 27% external-purchase commission; the Ninth Circuit upheld contempt but called an outright fee ban overbroad (Dec 2025) and remanded for a "cost-based" fee; SCOTUS granted cert on the contempt question Jun 30, 2026, with a merits ruling expected ~2027. Commission sits at $0 today, on the US storefront only.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Genuinely live litigation this cycle (SCOTUS cert, Ninth Circuit remand) — but that also means the economic premise (a $0 fee) is explicitly provisional, not a stable foundation to build a business on.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do, for free. Apple's own App Review Guidelines (3.1.1(a), 3.1.3(a)) require no entitlement for US external links — just a link. RevenueCat (115,000+ subscription apps on its platform) ships Web Purchase Button/Web Purchase Links free up to $2,500 MTR; Superwall ships App-to-Web Checkout free on its base plan.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it is:** Beyond RevenueCat and Superwall, Stripe, Dodo Payments, and PayProGlobal all market directly against this exact use case, and at least 6 agency/vendor blogs already published detailed 2026 "how to add external purchase links" implementation guides.
- **What will change in 2 years:** A SCOTUS merits ruling (~2027) will likely reinstate some fee or set a new cost-based one — shrinking or eliminating the arbitrage this pitch depends on.
- **Competitors:** RevenueCat, Superwall, Stripe, Dodo Payments, PayProGlobal, plus 6+ SEO/agency implementation guides.
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** Small US iOS developers not already on RevenueCat or Superwall — a shrinking pool given RevenueCat's 115K-app footprint — reached via dev-community/SEO content or founder-led dev-tool sales, a distribution motion with no equivalent to Lance's Meta-ads targeting precision.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** High CAC (content/SEO race against entrenched, already-ranking incumbents); low LTV (a one-time integration decision, no accumulating moat); realistic ARR ceiling plausibly sub-$200K. For the modal small-developer buyer (Apple Small Business Program, 15% commission), the arbitrage math is often negative once conversion-rate drop (20–40%, per Superwall's own early data) and lost merchant-of-record tax handling are counted.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** The wrapper itself is 1–2 weeks; reaching parity with free incumbents on multi-jurisdiction tax/merchant-of-record handling is a much longer lift, and the tool would carry ongoing payment-facilitator liability tracking a still-litigating guideline.
- **Sources:** [App Review Guidelines — Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), [Ninth Circuit ruling — MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/12/11/apple-app-store-fees-external-payment-links/), [SCOTUS cert grant — TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/22/apple-says-epic-lawsuit-shouldnt-reshape-app-store-rules-for-all-developers/), [RevenueCat — anti-steering ruling response](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/apple-anti-steering-ruling-monetization-strategy), [RevenueCat — State of Subscription Apps 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps), [Superwall — App-to-Web Checkout](https://superwall.com/features/app-to-web-checkout), [Superwall — early App2Web conversion data](https://superwall.com/blog/initial-data-is-in-app-to-web-conversion-rates-after-the-app-store-ruling).
- **Kill reason:** Apple's own US rule requires no entitlement and is trivial to self-implement, and RevenueCat/Superwall — which already own this exact developer base — ship the compliant checkout free or bundled; the underlying $0 commission is also Supreme-Court-pending and could reverse in 2027, leaving neither a wedge nor a stable economic premise. Not scored against B2C_RULES (B2B candidate) but the file's Rule 9 logic — "a regulation just changed → build a compliance tool for it" — applies directly and is now the project's seventh confirmed instance of that shape failing.

**Bottom line:** Eighth straight cycle, zero survivors. All three kills this cycle share one root cause worth naming precisely: each trigger looked fresh from the breadth scan (a recycled backlash re-dated by SEO content, an incumbent's new feature framed as a gap, a live Supreme Court case) but a focused competitive check found a well-resourced player — funded, free, or the platform itself — already sitting on the exact wedge, in two of three cases giving the core mechanism away for free. Process note: "SEO content calls this a 2026 trend" is not the same claim as "this changed in 2026" — the Splitwise kill specifically traces to treating recycled content as a fresh trigger without checking the underlying feature's actual ship date first; worth a five-minute date-check on any "backlash" or "complaint wave" trigger before committing deep-dive time going forward.
