## Ideas

Three candidates deep-dived this cycle, all rejected — the thirtieth straight cycle with zero survivors. Two follow this ledger's most common pattern (a fresh, dated trigger whose exact mechanic is already live elsewhere); the third is a different and sharper failure mode — the trigger is real and the market gap is real, but a contemporaneous security finding shows the product's core mechanism cannot deliver what it would be sold to prove.

### 1. Self-serve flexible micro-task platform for indie AI builders and small businesses (post-Mechanical Turk) — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A lightweight, self-serve replacement for MTurk's specific mechanic — post an arbitrary task template (not just a structured survey), set a per-task price, recruit anonymous workers at scale, run your own QC — aimed at solo AI developers needing quick human-eval/preference data and small businesses needing ad-hoc labeling, as Amazon shuts the original down Sept 30, 2026.
- **Risks:** "A platform shuts down → build the replacement" is the exact shape that killed the Stocky-refugee Shopify-app idea one cycle ago (2026-09-02); the lesson didn't change in seven days.
- **Consumer pain (quantified):** Real — MTurk served 500,000+ workers at its peak and closed new signups July 30, 2026, two months ahead of full shutdown, giving requesters a hard deadline to migrate workflows.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Genuinely fresh trigger (shutdown announced Aug 25, effective Sept 30), but the competitive response was equally fast.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do, explicitly. Amazon's own shutdown announcement cited AI-native labeling startups (Scale AI, Mercor, Prolific) as the reason MTurk's model was displaced.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it is:** At least 10 named alternatives already have dedicated "MTurk replacement" content live: Prolific (300,000+ vetted participants, 38 countries, explicit "MTurk vs. Prolific" comparison page pre-dating this shutdown), CloudResearch/Connect AI, Toloka, Surge AI, Scale AI, Labelbox, SuperAnnotate, Appen, Clickworker, LXT, iMerit — several publishing comparison guides ("6 alternatives compared") within days of the announcement. Distinct buyer segments (survey/behavioral research vs. bulk labeling vs. RLHF) are each already served by a specialist.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** Nothing favorable — the segment MTurk uniquely served (arbitrary, unstructured HITs at rock-bottom price) is the one incumbents are actively de-emphasizing in favor of higher-quality, higher-priced panels; that gap narrows, not widens.
- **Competitors:** Prolific, CloudResearch/Connect AI, Toloka, Surge AI, Scale AI, Labelbox, SuperAnnotate, Appen, Clickworker, LXT, iMerit.
- **Who pays / sales motion:** Self-serve, mechanically compatible with a checkout flow — the one honest positive on this idea — but competing on price/flexibility against a dozen funded incumbents with years of trust-and-safety and QC infrastructure already built.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Low differentiation caps pricing power; realistic ceiling is a thin reseller margin on top of a workforce this founder doesn't own, in a market segment (cheap unstructured HITs) the whole industry is moving away from.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** A thin task-posting UI ships in weeks; the hard part — a trustworthy anonymous worker pool with fraud/quality controls — is the multi-year moat every named competitor already has and a new entrant does not.
- **Sources:** [TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-amazon-mechanical-turk-shutdown/), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/25/amazon-service-that-jeff-bezos-called-artificial-ai-is-shutting-down.html), [Prolific vs MTurk](https://www.prolific.com/prolific-vs-mturk), [ecorpit.com — 6 alternatives compared](https://ecorpit.com/mturk-closing-human-data-labeling-alternatives-2026/), [Troveo — Surge AI alternatives](https://www.troveo.ai/resources/surge-ai-alternatives).
- **Kill reason:** Same over-mined "incumbent shuts down → I'll build the replacement" shape rejected here seven days ago for Shopify/Stocky — except here the competitive response (10+ specialist alternatives, several already publishing comparison content) was already live before the shutdown even completed.
- **Not scored against B2C_RULES** — B2B/developer-tool, buyer is a business or indie developer, not a personal consumer.

### 2. Vulnerable-adult financial-exploitation transaction-delay compliance tool for community banks and credit unions (Colorado ASSET Act) — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A workflow tool for community banks/credit unions to detect, document, and act on Colorado's new ASSET Act (and ~25 similar state laws) — flag a suspicious disbursement, delay it, generate the required written notification within 2 business days, and track the 90/180-day resolution clock, for institutions too small to run a full enterprise fraud platform.
- **Risks:** Near-identical shape to this ledger's 2026-08-30 rejection (RIA/mutual-fund trusted-contact tooling) four days ago — same underlying regulatory pattern (extending FINRA-2165-style holds to a new institution type), same conclusion likely applies.
- **Buyer pain (quantified):** Real — Colorado adults 60+ lost $7.7B+ to scams in 2025 (59% YoY increase nationally), and ASSET Act gives banks/credit unions real new legal cover (good-faith immunity) plus real new operational burden (mandatory 2-business-day written notice, 90/180-day case clock) they didn't have before this law took effect.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Colorado's law is dated (effective Aug 2026), but the underlying pattern — bank hold-authority statutes modeled on FINRA Rule 2165 (2018) — is 8 years old and already at ~25-26 states; Colorado is a late joiner to an established category, not a first mover.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do, and specifically at this buyer tier. Verafin (Nasdaq-owned) explicitly targets community banks and credit unions "without heavy infrastructure," with native integrations into the core banking systems (FIS, Fiserv, Jack Henry) this exact buyer already runs, plus auto-generated/e-filed SARs. NICE Actimize ships an "AML Essentials" tier aimed at the same regional/community-institution segment.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it is:** Verafin and NICE Actimize between them already cover elder-financial-exploitation detection, case escalation, and SAR-filing workflow for the community-bank/credit-union tier specifically — not just the large-bank tier, which was this idea's working assumption going in.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** More states adopt ASSET-Act-style laws (the trend line is clearly up), but Verafin/NICE Actimize absorb each new state's specific notice/timeline requirements as a routine rules-engine update, the same way they already handle 50-state variation in other compliance workflows.
- **Competitors:** Verafin (Nasdaq), NICE Actimize, plus generalist BSA/AML platforms already sold into this buyer tier.
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** Enterprise/institutional — core-banking-system integration, security/compliance procurement review, likely 6+ month sales cycles; a poor fit for a solo founder with no enterprise sales motion, independent of the competitive picture.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Not reached — enterprise sales motion mismatch is disqualifying on its own regardless of ARR potential.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** Core-banking integrations alone (FIS/Fiserv/Jack Henry) are a months-long undertaking before a single customer could go live, on top of building trust with a security-conscious regulated buyer that already has an incumbent vendor relationship.
- **Sources:** [CPR News — Colorado ASSET Act](https://www.cpr.org/2026/08/25/colorado-senior-fraud-protection-law/), [Colorado General Assembly — HB26-1110](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/HB26-1110), [Denver7](https://www.denver7.com/news/local-news/colorado-asset-act-would-give-banks-power-to-pause-transactions-to-protect-older-adults-from-financial-fraud), [NICE Actimize — elder financial exploitation](https://www.niceactimize.com/blog/fraud-product-team-elder-financial-exploitation-a-growing-crisis-banks-cant-ignore), [Verafin community-bank positioning via CULytics fraud-tech roundup](https://culytics.com/blogs/top-fraud-management-technologies).
- **Kill reason:** Same regulatory-extension pattern as the 2026-08-30 RIA-focused rejection, now confirmed to also cover the retail bank/credit union tier this idea targeted — Verafin and NICE Actimize already integrate into the exact core banking systems and SAR workflows a community bank would need, and the sales motion (institutional procurement, core-system integration) doesn't fit a solo founder regardless of competitive gaps.
- **Not scored against B2C_RULES** — B2B, buyer is a regulated financial institution, not a personal consumer.

### 3. AI-cheating-accusation defense / writing-process authorship verification tool for students — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A tool students (or their parents) run while writing schoolwork that logs keystrokes, pauses, and revision history and exports a timestamped "authorship report" to defend against a false AI-plagiarism accusation — positioned around *Matter of Newby v. Adelphi Univ.* (Feb 2026), where a court found a university's Turnitin-based finding "without valid basis" for lack of due process.
- **Risks:** Two independent, each-sufficient kill risks: the product category is already occupied by a dominant free incumbent, and a contemporaneous security finding shows the core mechanism doesn't prove what it would be sold to prove.
- **Consumer pain (quantified):** Real. Turnitin admits a 4% false-positive rate on full documents (Vanderbilt's own math: ~750 wrongly flagged papers/year at that rate); a Stanford study found detectors flagged 61.3% of non-native-English TOEFL essays as AI-written; Newby is the first case to reach a court ruling, with the judge calling the university's finding "completely false" and ordering the record expunged — real legal teeth now exist behind a false accusation.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Genuinely fresh legal precedent (Feb 2026) that materially changes what evidence matters in an appeal — but the "process tracker" product response to it isn't new at all; it predates the ruling.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do, dominantly. Grammarly — with an existing massive installed base — shipped Authorship as a free/bundled feature (GA in Google Docs, beta in Word) specifically marketed as letting students "prove their writing is theirs," aimed at exactly this use case.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it is:** At minimum 5 more named competitors already run the identical "process tracker/authorship certificate" mechanic: WritersLogic/WritersProof, TypeOS, Honorwrite, WriteStamp, plus Turnitin's own Clarity and GPTZero extending into process-tracking. This is already a named mini-category ("writing process trackers"), not a gap.
- **What will change in the next 2 years — nothing that helps, something that actively hurts:** A 2026 security analysis found the entire mechanism is broken: mutual information between keystroke timing and true authorship is exactly zero under a simple spoofing/type-out attack, and forged sessions evaded five keystroke classifiers at 99.8%+ rates with >0.993 mean "human" confidence. A tool sold to "prove" authorship using this signal is selling evidence that has been shown, in the same news cycle as the trigger, not to prove authorship at all — a worse defect than mere competition, since it undermines the core value claim regardless of who builds it.
- **Competitors:** Grammarly Authorship, WritersLogic/WritersProof, TypeOS, Honorwrite, WriteStamp, Turnitin Clarity, GPTZero (process-tracking features).
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** Self-serve, parent- or student-paid — the one part of this idea that would have mechanically fit Lance's checkout-funnel playbook.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Moot given the mechanism defect, but even setting that aside: Grammarly's existing free/bundled Authorship feature inside a tool most students already use for other reasons sets a $0 price anchor a standalone paid product can't clear.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** A keystroke-logging MVP ships in weeks — irrelevant, since the output it would generate is not evidence of the thing it claims to prove, creating real liability if a family relies on it in an actual academic-integrity hearing and it's shown (as the 2026 research already has) to be spoofable or non-probative.
- **Sources:** [Justia — Matter of Newby v. Adelphi Univ.](https://law.justia.com/cases/new-york/other-courts/2026/2026-ny-slip-op-26021.html), [Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/quick-takes/2026/02/11/adelphi-student-wins-ai-plagiarism-lawsuit), [Plagiarism Today](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2026/02/11/adelphi-student-wins-ai-plagiarism-lawsuit/), [Grammarly Authorship](https://www.grammarly.com/authorship), [detectiondrama.com — writing process trackers](https://detectiondrama.com/writing-process-trackers-what-they-prove/), [GradPilot — AI detection lawsuits tracker](https://gradpilot.com/news/ai-cheating-lawsuits-tracker).
- **B2C_RULES gate check:** 1 of 9 gates confirmed PASS (Rule 1 — Meta's "Parents with teenagers 13-18" parental-status targeting is a real, live category). Fails Rule 2 (episodic, per-incident use, not a renewing habit), Rule 3 (Grammarly's free bundled feature is an uncloneable-away price anchor, not a moat gap), Rule 4 (the entire product is a thin log-and-export wrapper with no defensible depth), Rule 5 (single-player utility, no referral mechanic), Rule 7 (marginal — "help my kid beat an AI-cheating accusation" reads fine, but selling evidence a 2026 study calls non-probative, to a family who may rely on it in a real disciplinary hearing, is a real reputational and liability exposure), Rule 8 (no distribution insight to tell), Rule 9 (decisively over-mined — already a named "process tracker" mini-category with a dominant free incumbent). Rejected outright per the ledger's own rule: fail one gate, it's a pass — this one fails at least six.

**Bottom line:** Thirtieth straight cycle, zero survivors. Two of today's three candidates died the familiar way — a regulation or shutdown created a real, dated trigger, and the exact mechanic it calls for was already live at the buyer's tier (MTurk's specialist replacements moved faster than the week-old Shopify/Stocky precedent even suggested was possible; Verafin/NICE Actimize already reach down to the community-bank tier this cycle's compliance idea assumed was open). The third died a more instructive way: the trigger (a real court ruling with real due-process teeth) and the market gap (a dominant but free incumbent aside, this is a genuinely painful, quantified problem) were both real, but a security finding published in the same window shows the product's central promise — "this log proves you wrote it" — is not something the underlying data can actually deliver. Worth remembering as a distinct failure mode going forward, separate from "already built" and "already saturated": sometimes the mechanism itself doesn't work, independent of competition.
