## Ideas

Two candidates deep-dived this cycle, both rejected — the twenty-seventh straight cycle with zero survivors. Both had genuinely fresh, dated triggers (a platform policy reversal three weeks old; a quantified, current fairness complaint); both died to the two failure modes this ledger keeps finding — a cluster of existing tools already occupying the exact wedge, and (for the second candidate) a B2C economics fail independent of competition.

### 1. AI original-design generator for Etsy sellers hit by the Aug 11 "no purchased templates" rule — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A tool for Etsy POD/laser-cutter/sublimation sellers that generates AI original artwork on demand, vectorizes it to production-ready SVG/DXF, and (optionally) timestamps/documents originality — positioned as the fast fix for sellers who built their catalogs on now-banned purchased templates and clipart.
- **Risks:** The category this idea would need to win — "AI-generate original art, export to SVG/DXF for laser/Cricut/sublimation" — was already a maturing product space serving this exact seller base before the rule change; the trigger accelerates existing demand rather than creating a new, undefended one.
- **Buyer pain (quantified):** Real and dated. Etsy's Creativity Standards change took effect Aug 11, 2026: any product made with a computerized tool (laser cutter, sublimation printer, Cricut, embroidery digitizer) must now use the seller's own original design — a purchased SVG or licensed clipart no longer qualifies as handmade even under a valid commercial license, with listing removal or full shop suspension as the enforcement mechanism. Prior Etsy guidance explicitly permitted the purchased-template workflow, so this is a real, sudden compliance gap for sellers who built catalogs on it.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Genuinely fresh — the policy took effect three weeks before this cycle and reverses Etsy's own long-standing prior guidance, not a recycled trigger.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** Not a big-player-vs-solo-founder story — a cluster of small, already-live AI-native tools serve exactly this seller segment.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it is:** At least five named, live tools already market AI-generate-to-production-format workflows squarely at this buyer: Kittl Pro (vectorization + templates + commercial licensing for POD/Etsy/merch sellers), Svg Craft AI (text/image prompt → SVG for Cricut/laser/POD), SVGMaker (AI SVG/EPS/AI/PDF generation), VectoSolve (AI generation → vectorization → SVG+DXF+PNG+G-code export in one pipeline, credit-based pricing, explicitly built for laser production), and Vector Witch (15-style AI SVG generator for laser engraving/Cricut/POD). The full "prompt to production file in under a minute" workflow this idea would sell is already assembled and shipping.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** More Etsy-seller-focused content and tooling will explicitly market against this rule (the pattern already visible in ShieldMyShop's and similar sellers' guides), and the named AI-SVG vendors will keep adding Etsy-specific originality/documentation features as a retention hook — closing the remaining "prove it's original" gap further rather than opening it.
- **Competitors:** Kittl Pro, Svg Craft AI, SVGMaker, VectoSolve, Vector Witch, plus general AI vectorizers (Vectorizer.ai-style tools) sellers can already repurpose.
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** Self-serve SaaS/credits sold direct to solo Etsy sellers — mechanically compatible with a Meta-ads funnel, but pricing already anchors low (credit-based, sub-subscription) against five live competitors.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Poor differentiation ceiling — the generation+vectorization pipeline is now a commodity feature across multiple tools; any new entrant competes on price into an already-thin-margin credit model, not a defensible position.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** A thin AI-generate-then-vectorize wrapper is buildable in days to weeks, which is exactly why five versions of it already exist; a defensible product would need Etsy-specific originality documentation/audit-trail features not yet confirmed live anywhere, but that's an unverified, thin differentiation to bet a build on.
- **Sources:** [MSN — Etsy's 2026 policy overhaul](https://www.msn.com/en-us/news/other/etsys-2026-policy-overhaul-forces-handmade-sellers-to-rethink-their-business-models/gm-GM59437274), [ShieldMyShop — Aug 2026 policy changes for POD/digital-download sellers](https://www.shieldmyshop.com/blog/2026-04-28-etsy-august-2026-policy-changes-original-design-pod-sellers), [theartisanbusinesscompany.com — what the rule change means for makers](https://theartisanbusinesscompany.com/etsys-august-rule-change-and-what-it-means-for-your-business-model/), [Kittl](https://www.kittl.com/), [Svg Craft AI](https://svgcraft.ai/), [SVGMaker](https://svgmaker.io/), [VectoSolve](https://vectosolve.com/blog/ai-image-to-svg-workflow-2026), [Vector Witch](https://vectorwitch.com/).
- **Kill reason:** The exact workflow this idea would sell — AI-generate original art, vectorize it, export to SVG/DXF for laser/Cricut/sublimation production — is already live and explicitly marketed to Etsy/POD sellers from at least five named tools (Kittl Pro, Svg Craft AI, SVGMaker, VectoSolve, Vector Witch); the Aug 11 policy change accelerates demand into an already-served market rather than opening an empty one.
- **Not scored against B2C_RULES** — B2B, buyer is a solo Etsy seller running a small business, not a personal consumer.

### 2. Gig-platform deactivation appeal/demand-letter kit for rideshare and delivery drivers — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A self-serve product that helps deactivated Uber/Lyft/DoorDash/Instacart drivers assemble a documented appeal — pulling in-app record data, drafting an appeal or demand letter citing the platform's own stated policy and, where applicable, Prop 22-style contractual obligations — sold direct to affected drivers.
- **Risks:** This is a compliance-adjacent legal-document product for a one-time crisis event, the same failure shape (thin LTV, adversarial dispute liability) that has killed every "appeal letter" idea tested on this ledger to date (insurance denials, pet-insurance denials, disability/PIP/dental denials).
- **Consumer pain (quantified):** Real. A Feb 2026 Asian Law Caucus report found 30% of deactivated drivers got no explanation at all, and of those given a reason, 42% cited a single customer complaint; nearly half of deactivated drivers believed customer discrimination played a role, with drivers of color and non-native-English speakers disproportionately affected. Drivers are actively suing Uber over the adequacy of Prop 22's appeal mechanism.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Weak-to-moderate. The Asian Law Caucus report and active Prop 22 litigation are 2026-dated, but the underlying deactivation-appeal problem is multi-year and platforms are already visibly responding (DoorDash shipped in-app appeals in its Dasher app in March 2026).
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** The platforms are the ones adjudicating deactivations, so there's no big-player feature to lose to here — but see below, a dedicated point-solution already fills the gap.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it isn't fully occupied, but the exact wedge is already taken:** GigAppeal already sells appeal-and-demand-letter services specifically for Uber/Lyft deactivations, and general legal-services sites (terms.law) already publish deactivation-appeal FAQs and sell demand-letter drafting for California rideshare drivers specifically. The self-serve, direct-to-driver wedge this idea needs is not empty.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** Platforms keep adding native in-app appeal flows (DoorDash's March 2026 launch is the leading edge), which commoditizes the "submit an appeal" mechanic further and leaves only the "make the appeal persuasive" layer contestable — the exact layer GigAppeal already sells into.
- **Competitors:** GigAppeal (dedicated appeal + demand-letter service), terms.law (deactivation-appeal FAQ + demand-letter drafting), plus each platform's now-native in-app appeal flow (DoorDash, Uber, Instacart's trust-and-safety email intake).
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** The driver, self-serve, at the moment of crisis — mechanically compatible with a Meta-ads funnel (rideshare/delivery-driver interest categories exist), but this is the one part of the idea that actually fits Lance's playbook.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Poor. A deactivation is a single, non-recurring crisis event for a given driver — no accumulating data, no ongoing service relationship, nothing to renew into. Fails B2C_RULES Rule 2 (no renewing LTV) on the same structural grounds as the already-rejected cancellation-concierge and severance-negotiation-kit ideas, independent of the competitive picture.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** A templated appeal-letter generator is buildable in weeks, but every case is emotionally charged, adversarial, and outcome-uncertain — high support load and real reputational risk if a paid appeal fails, for a one-time transaction that can't fund that support cost.
- **Sources:** [Gridwise — deactivation appeal guide](https://gridwise.io/blog/gig-driver-deactivation-appeal), [terms.law — deactivation appeals FAQ](https://terms.law/FAQ/gig-economy/deactivation-appeals-faq.html), [CalMatters — Uber Prop 22 lawsuit](https://calmatters.org/economy/2026/04/uber-proposition22-lawsuit/), [terms.law — California rideshare deactivation demand letter](https://terms.law/Demand-Letters/Employment/california-rideshare-deactivation-demand.html), [Para — DoorDash/UberEats/GrubHub deactivation reasons](https://www.withpara.com/blog/doordash-ubereats-grubhub-deactivation).
- **Kill reason:** Fails B2C_RULES Rule 2 outright — a deactivation appeal is a single, non-renewing crisis transaction with no accumulating relationship, the same structural failure that killed the cancellation-concierge and severance-negotiation-kit ideas on this ledger — and independently, GigAppeal already occupies the specific self-serve, direct-to-driver appeal-and-demand-letter wedge this idea would need.

**Bottom line:** Twenty-seventh straight cycle, zero survivors. Both candidates rode real, dated triggers — a three-week-old Etsy policy reversal and a quantified 2026 fairness report on gig deactivations — but died to this ledger's two most common patterns: a cluster of already-shipping point tools occupying the exact technical wedge (five AI-SVG vendors for the Etsy idea), and a B2C structural fail independent of competition (no renewing LTV for the deactivation-appeal idea, the same shape that killed the cancellation-concierge and severance-kit ideas). The more durable finding this cycle is the Washington/Oregon companion-chatbot law taking effect Jan 1, 2027 — logged as a ledger update, not a new idea, since the buyer-pool economics that capped that watch item haven't changed.
