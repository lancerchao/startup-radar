## Ideas

Two candidates deep-dived this cycle, both rejected — the twenty-sixth straight cycle with zero survivors. Both had genuine, dated 2026 regulatory triggers; both died the same way this ledger keeps finding on B2B compliance ideas — a well-funded incumbent already sits on the exact buyer segment a solo founder would need, with distribution (platform integrations, or thousands of existing RIA customers) a new entrant can't easily replicate.

### 1. Portable-benefits compliance/administration layer for small gig and creator platforms — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A hosted product that lets a small-to-mid-size gig, marketplace, or creator platform (the kind newly caught by California's expanded AB 212 or the growing list of state portable-benefits laws) calculate per-worker contributions, route them into a compliant benefits account, and produce the audit trail regulators would want — without the platform building fund-administration infrastructure itself.
- **Risks:** This is fund administration, not a SaaS dashboard — it touches real worker money (health, paid-leave, disability contributions), which means fiduciary-adjacent obligations, state-by-state variation in what counts as compliant, and reputational/legal exposure if a contribution is mishandled.
- **Consumer pain (quantified):** Real and spreading. 14+ states now have some form of portable-benefits legislation, up from 3 in 2023 (Utah, Alabama, Tennessee, Georgia, West Virginia, Colorado, Maine, Oregon, New Jersey, and more); California's AB 212 was expanded in 2026 to reach any platform above $100K in annual platform revenue per worker — a threshold that catches platforms well below Uber/DoorDash scale.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** The state-law count nearly quintupled in three years and the California threshold expansion is a 2026 change — a smaller platform crossing $100K/worker for the first time this year is a genuinely fresh trigger, not a recycled one.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do — see below. This isn't a gap the giants are ignoring; a funded specialist already built exactly this and sells it to companies of the target size.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it isn't:** Stride Health already partners with 140+ gig-economy and payroll platforms spanning from Uber, DoorDash, and Amazon Flex down to Etsy, Patreon, and Gopuff — i.e., already serving platforms far smaller than the giants, the exact tier this idea targets. Stride was acquired by Integrity (a 600,000-agent insurance distributor) in July 2026, giving it more distribution reach, not less. Alia separately owns the adjacent "household employer" niche (nannies, housecleaners) with the same funded-benefits-account mechanic.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** More states pass portable-benefits laws (the trend is accelerating, not slowing), which is a tailwind for Stride's existing 140-platform network and integration playbook, not an opening for a new entrant — each new state is another market Stride can sell its already-built infrastructure into.
- **Competitors:** Stride Health (140+ platform integrations, post-Integrity-acquisition distribution), Alia (household-employer niche), Trolley (gig payout infrastructure, adjacent), plus the DoorDash/Mastercard-style direct platform partnerships Stride has already built.
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** The platform operator, sold through relationship-driven B2B integration deals (weeks-to-months sales cycles, an API/data integration to build) — not remotely a self-serve consumer-checkout motion.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** LTV could be real (ongoing per-worker contribution flow), but CAC is high (integration-heavy enterprise sale) against a buyer pool where the highest-value targets are already Stride customers.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** Months, not weeks — fund administration compliant across a patchwork of 14+ state regimes is not a weekend build, and the ongoing operational load (holding and correctly routing other people's benefit money) is substantial and continuous.
- **Sources:** [Beancount.io — state law guide](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/07/10/portable-benefits-independent-contractors-state-laws-guide), [Damongo — 2026 state programs guide](https://damongo.com/portable-benefits-for-freelancers-in-2026-your-complete-guide-to-state-programs-platform-coverage-and-how-to-secure-your-safety-net/), [FreightWaves — Stride portable benefits](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/stride-aims-to-give-every-gig-worker-a-portable-benefits-account), [PRNewswire — Stride 140+ platforms](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/stride-partners-with-100-flexible-work-platforms-and-employers-in-support-of-gig-workers-week-of-action-to-boost-health-insurance-enrollment-before-dec-15th-deadline-301438491.html), [Insurance Business — Integrity acquires Stride](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/benefits/integritys-stride-deal-takes-a-medicare-advantage-distributor-into-gig-worker-health-enrollment-581703.aspx).
- **Kill reason:** Stride Health already runs exactly this business, already integrated with 140+ platforms spanning from giants down to Etsy/Patreon-scale operations (the precise tier this idea targets), and just gained more distribution muscle via its July 2026 acquisition by a 600,000-agent insurance distributor. A new entrant would be racing a funded specialist that already owns the customer relationships and the compliance infrastructure.
- **Not scored against B2C_RULES** — B2B, buyer is the platform operator, not a personal consumer.

### 2. Trusted-contact / elder-exploitation compliance tooling for RIAs and mutual funds — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A compliance module for small-to-mid registered investment advisers (RIAs) and mutual-fund transfer agents to collect and manage "trusted contact" data, flag and document suspected financial exploitation of senior/impaired clients, and execute the transaction/redemption holds FINRA Rule 2165 (broker-dealers, since 2018) and the pending Financial Exploitation Prevention Act (mutual funds, passed House 414-2 on June 25, 2026) require.
- **Risks:** Deep compliance/liability surface — a firm that mishandles a hold (wrongly freezing a legitimate transaction, or failing to freeze a fraudulent one) faces real regulatory and client-relationship consequences; any tool touching this needs to be trusted enough for a fiduciary to rely on it under exam scrutiny.
- **Consumer pain (quantified):** Real but not fresh at the mechanism level. Seniors lost $7.75B to fraud in 2025 per the House Financial Services Committee; the new bill passed 414-2, giving mutual funds/transfer agents the same 15(+10)-day hold power broker-dealers have had under FINRA Rule 2165 since 2018.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** The House vote is a genuinely 2026 event and closes a real regulatory gap (funds previously lacked the hold authority broker-dealers already had) — but the underlying compliance workflow (collect trusted contact, flag exploitation, document a hold) is an 8-year-old, well-understood pattern on the broker-dealer side already.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They're already the default choice. COMPLY (RIA in a Box) alone serves 2,800+ RIA firms with an all-in-one compliance platform that already automates regulatory-requirement tracking, code-of-ethics, and registration/filing workflows — the natural home for a new trusted-contact/exploitation module, not a gap next to it.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet:** It may genuinely have room — a specific public trusted-contact/2165-hold feature inside COMPLY or peers (Smarsh, Global Relay) wasn't confirmed in this pass — but the addressable market is thin regardless: ~16,500 SEC-registered RIAs plus a comparable state-registered population, and the fund side of the new bill's obligation lands on transfer agents and mutual-fund complexes, not the RIA long tail at all.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** If the bill clears the Senate, expect incumbent compliance platforms (COMPLY, Smarsh, Global Relay) to ship or expand a fund-specific hold/trusted-contact module as a routine feature update for existing customers — the standard pattern this ledger has seen every time a new disclosure/hold requirement lands on a market an incumbent compliance vendor already owns.
- **Competitors:** COMPLY/RIA in a Box (2,800+ RIA customers, full regulatory-automation platform), Smarsh, Global Relay, plus in-house compliance teams at large fund complexes who will build this internally rather than buy from a new vendor.
- **Who pays / sales motion (honest):** Compliance officers at RIAs, broker-dealers, or fund transfer agents — a founder-led enterprise sale into a heavily regulated buyer that demands vendor due diligence, nothing close to Lance's self-serve consumer-checkout motion.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** High CAC (compliance-software sales cycles run months, with real trust-building overhead), ARR ceiling capped hard by a buyer pool in the low tens of thousands of firms, most of which already pay an incumbent for adjacent compliance workflows and would rather add a module than add a vendor.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** Months — a defensible compliance tool here needs real subject-matter depth (state-by-state exploitation-reporting variation, FINRA/SEC exam-readiness) before a compliance officer would trust it, not a weeks-buildable wrapper.
- **Sources:** [ThinkAdvisor — House passage](https://www.thinkadvisor.com/2026/06/26/house-passes-financial-exploitation-prevention-act/), [CNBC — bill details](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/02/lawmakers-approved-bipartisan-bill-to-battle-fraud-among-older-adults/), [Congress.gov H.R.2478](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/2478), [FINRA Rule 2165](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/2165), [FINRA — trusted contact persons](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/guidance/reports/2026-finra-annual-regulatory-oversight-report/trusted-contact-persons), [COMPLY — RIA compliance software](https://www.comply.com/products/comply-ria/), [ThinkAdvisor — RIA count record](https://www.thinkadvisor.com/2026/06/03/number-of-rias-sets-new-record-report/).
- **Kill reason:** The workflow this idea would sell (trusted-contact collection, exploitation flagging, transaction holds) is an 8-year-old, well-understood compliance pattern already living inside incumbent platforms like COMPLY that serve thousands of the exact buyer this idea would target — a new bill extending the requirement to mutual funds is far more likely to become a feature update at COMPLY/Smarsh than an opening for a standalone new vendor, against a buyer pool (tens of thousands of firms) too small and too enterprise-sales-heavy to fit a solo founder's playbook regardless.
- **Not scored against B2C_RULES** — B2B, buyer is the RIA/fund compliance officer, not a personal consumer.

**Bottom line:** Twenty-sixth straight cycle, zero survivors. Both candidates rode real, dated 2026 regulatory events (a 414-2 House vote extending elder-exploitation holds to mutual funds, and California's AB 212 threshold expansion reaching smaller platforms) — but both died to the same shape this ledger sees constantly on B2B compliance ideas: a funded specialist (Stride, 140+ platform integrations) or an entrenched incumbent (COMPLY, 2,800+ RIA customers) already owns the buyer relationship the new regulation would otherwise create demand for. The more structurally interesting finding this cycle wasn't either idea — it's that Meta's own targeting product is quietly drifting away from the precise interest/behavior mechanism B2C_RULES Rule 1 relies on to gate consumer candidates, which is worth re-checking live in Ads Manager the next time a B2C idea gets this far.
