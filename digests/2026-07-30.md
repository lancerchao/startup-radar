## Ideas

Three candidates investigated today, all rejected. Logged in full — a clean kill (especially one that pre-empts wasted build time on an already-occupied wedge) is the point of this process, not a failure of it.

### 1. AI appeal-letter tool for disability/auto-PIP/dental/vision denials — REJECTED (resolves 2026-07-29 `new` item)

- **Brief description:** Consumer AI tool generating appeal letters for insurance-denial regimes distinct from the health-insurance/specialty-drug space Claimable et al. already occupy — ERISA long-term disability, no-fault auto PIP, dental, vision.
- **Risks:** Free universal competitors already technically cover the mechanic; ERISA disability appeals carry real unauthorized-practice-of-law-adjacent exposure; dental/vision dollar amounts are too small to support a paid SKU; auto-PIP is state-fragmented (no-fault regimes vary by state — FL, NY, MI, NJ, PA, etc. — with different procedural rules).
- **Consumer pain:** Real for disability specifically — SSDI initial approval is only 36% nationally (2025), reconsideration-stage denial is 84% — but that's *government* SSDI, not the *private* ERISA-governed LTD insurance this idea targets; conflating the two would misstate the pitch. No reliable win-rate figure found for private ERISA LTD appeals specifically. Dental/vision denials are typically small-dollar (a few hundred dollars per claim) — thin motivation to seek a paid tool over a free one.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** No fresh external trigger. This candidate originated from finding a "gap" while killing the insurance-denial-fight-kit idea yesterday, not from a new regulatory or market event this week — a process smell worth naming, since "why now" is supposed to be a real catalyst, not a gap noticed in passing.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do, generically. AppealArmor's homepage explicitly offers a free "universal" generator for "any insurance denial" (verified directly on appealarmor.com), not limited to health/specialty-drug conditions. AppealGen and Jenova AI similarly aren't hard-scoped to only health insurance.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet:** Because building *vertically correct* content (ERISA procedural rules, state-by-state PIP no-fault statutes, dental/vision plan-language patterns) is real work relative to the small dollar amounts at stake for three of the four regimes — that's a legitimate reason nobody's built a dedicated product, but it cuts against this idea too: the effort-to-payoff ratio is bad for a solo builder as well.
- **What would change in 2 years:** Little structurally — ERISA and state no-fault statutes are not in active overhaul; ARR ceiling for dental/vision stays capped by the underlying claim amounts.
- **Competitors:** AppealArmor (free, universal + paid Pro $29/mo, Practice $99/mo tiers), AppealGen (free-to-start, health-insurance-scoped), Claimable ($39.95/appeal, specialty drugs, has enterprise/B2B), Counterforce Health (free, nonprofit), Jenova AI (broader denial agent). Dental-specific automation exists but is B2B/provider-side (Ventus AI, Overjet — recovering revenue for dental practices, not helping patients dispute denials).
- **Who pays:** Would be the patient/family member disputing a specific denial — same one-time-purchase-shaped buyer as Claimable, if a paid tier could be justified at all.
- **Expected CAC/LTV/LTV:CAC/ARR ceiling:** Not modeled — the free-competitor and low-dollar-claim problems make any figure moot before it's worth computing.
- **Time to first sellable version:** 1–2 weeks for a thin wrapper — irrelevant given the above.
- **Operational load:** Would require ongoing tracking of ERISA case law and state-by-state no-fault statute changes to stay accurate — a real ongoing content-maintenance burden for low payoff.
- **Sources:** [AppealArmor](https://appealarmor.com/), [Disability Insurance Law Firm — AI risk to ERISA appeals](https://www.disabilityinsurancelawfirm.com/how-ai-can-destroy-your-long-term-disability-appeal/), [Ortiz Law — SSDI FY2025 approval stats](https://www.nickortizlaw.com/what-ssa-fy-2025-disability-data-tells-you-about-your-chances/), [Ventus AI — dental appeal automation](https://www.ventus.ai/blog/dental-claim-appeal-automation-dso-denied-claims-recovery/)
- **Kill reason:** A free "universal" generator (AppealArmor) already nominally covers every regime this idea targets, and the genuine gap in vertical-specific content exists precisely because the payoff (small dental/vision claims, legally fraught ERISA disability appeals) doesn't justify the build effort — for anyone, including a solo founder.

### 2. AI beauty/skincare before-after transformation photo app — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** AI-generated before/after beauty or skincare-outcome simulation photos, sold one-time, riding the same creative format and ad-funnel playbook as careerpicai, timed to Meta's newly loosened before/after ad enforcement.
- **Risks:** Category is already saturated by a 200M-download incumbent (Facetune) plus a constant churn of viral AI-photo-trend apps; no defensible wedge distinguishes this from what already exists; before/after cosmetic-transformation imagery still risks Meta's "misleading efficacy" claim enforcement even under the loosened policy.
- **Consumer pain:** Weak as stated — no quantified unmet need found; this is a "why now" (policy change) in search of a problem, not a problem in search of a solution.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Real trigger — Meta's July 22 policy shift makes before/after cosmetic imagery reviewable by claim rather than auto-rejected by category. Genuine loosening of ad surface.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They do, heavily. Facetune (Lightricks) has 200M+ downloads and already bundles AI headshots, makeup/hairstyle try-on, and fashion styling into a $19.99/mo premium tier.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet:** It is saturated — Pew's 2026 survey found 67% of social-media users already use at least one AI photo-editing tool, and new viral AI-photo trend formats (toy-ification, future-baby generators, hair-color try-ons) launch and cycle through TikTok/Instagram on a roughly monthly cadence already.
- **What would change in 2 years:** Nothing structural in Lance's favor — the ad-policy loosening helps every incumbent equally; it's not a moat.
- **Competitors:** Facetune/Lightricks (200M+ downloads, $19.99/mo), plus a long tail of viral single-purpose AI photo-trend apps that appear and disappear monthly — a fundamentally different (trend-cycle, not durable-SKU) business shape than careerpicai's evergreen professional-headshot use case.
- **Who pays:** Unclear differentiation from existing purchasers of Facetune/similar tools — no distinct buyer segment identified.
- **Expected CAC/LTV/LTV:CAC/ARR ceiling:** Not modeled — no wedge to model against.
- **Time to first sellable version:** Technically fast (1–2 weeks, reusing careerpicai's image-gen stack) — irrelevant given the lack of differentiation.
- **Operational load:** Would require constant creative refresh to keep pace with trend cycles — a genuinely different (higher, content-treadmill) operational shape than a one-time-purchase SKU.
- **Sources:** [AuditSocials — Meta beauty/before-after ad rules 2026](https://www.auditsocials.com/blog/meta-beauty-cosmetic-ads-before-after-photos-body-image-policy-2026), [ExpandedRamblings — Facetune 200M+ downloads](https://expandedramblings.com/index.php/facetune-2-statistics-facts/), [Nothron — viral AI photo trends 2026](https://www.nothron.com/en/blog/top-10-viral-ai-photo-trends-2026)
- **Kill reason:** The Meta ad-policy change is real, but it lowers the barrier equally for a 200M-download incumbent and every trend-app clone already in the category — a loosened ad rule isn't a wedge when the product itself has no differentiation and the category shape (viral trend cycles) doesn't fit a one-time-purchase SKU anyway.

### 3. AI doctor-visit note-taker/summarizer for seniors — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** Consumer app recording/transcribing doctor visits and producing a plain-language summary plus follow-up questions, targeted at solo-ager/senior patients who attend appointments alone.
- **Risks:** Already directly occupied, including by a senior-specific competitor; HIPAA/consent-to-record considerations in two-party-consent states; no differentiated angle found.
- **Consumer pain:** Real and quantified — studies cited across the space show patients forget 40–80% of what's discussed in a visit, and nearly half of what's retained is recalled incorrectly. Genuine problem, but already being solved.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Ambient-transcription AI (Whisper-class models, cheap real-time voice-to-text) has made this technically trivial only in the last ~2 years — real "why now," but it already produced a full competitive field, not a gap.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They do — Abridge (the dominant clinician-side ambient-AI vendor) ships its own "Abridge for Patients" companion app; this isn't an underserved adjacency, it's a shipped feature of the market leader.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet:** It's saturated. Direct competitors found: Kin Health (free, $9M seed, physician-founded), Patiently, DataHippo (explicitly built and marketed for seniors — the exact audience this idea targets), My Medical Records.ai, DrClarity — at least five consumer apps doing this today, most free.
- **What would change in 2 years:** Little in this idea's favor — the trend is toward these features being bundled free by health systems/EHR vendors and clinician-side tools, compressing any standalone paid product's ceiling further.
- **Competitors:** Abridge (patient app), Kin Health, Patiently, DataHippo (senior-targeted), My Medical Records.ai, DrClarity, VisitRecall, Advoca Health, AlignCare.
- **Who pays:** Unclear — every direct competitor found is free; no paid-tier precedent to anchor a pricing model against.
- **Expected CAC/LTV/LTV:CAC/ARR ceiling:** Not modeled — an all-free competitive field makes any paid-SKU figure speculative to the point of not being worth fabricating.
- **Time to first sellable version:** Technically 2–4 weeks (ambient transcription APIs are commodity) — irrelevant given the field.
- **Operational load:** Would need ongoing HIPAA/consent-law tracking across two-party-consent states — real compliance overhead for a product with no clear paid differentiation.
- **Sources:** [TechCrunch — Kin Health $9M seed](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/18/kin-health-raises-9m-to-build-an-ai-notetaker-for-patients/), [DataHippo — App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/data-hippo-ai-summary-notes/id6738997275), [Abridge — Patient Visit Summaries](https://www.abridge.com/blog/patient-visit-summaries--now-generated-in-real-time)
- **Kill reason:** A senior-specific competitor (DataHippo) already ships the exact product for free, and the category's dominant clinician-side vendor (Abridge) already ships a patient-facing companion app — there is no gap left between "free consumer app" and "bundled feature of the market leader."

**Bottom line:** No survivors today — three candidates, three kills, each for a different reason (free universal competitor + legal risk; loosened ad policy with no product differentiation; free senior-specific incumbent). Process note: two of today's three kills (appeal-letter wedge, doctor-visit notetaker) died to "a free competitor already does this specific thing, just not under this exact marketing label" — worth a harder look at whether a "universal"/generic free tool already covers a proposed niche *before* selecting it as a candidate, since that pattern has now killed ideas on 2026-07-29 and 2026-07-30 both.
