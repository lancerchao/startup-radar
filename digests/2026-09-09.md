## Ideas

Three candidates deep-dived this cycle, all rejected — the thirty-sixth straight cycle with zero survivors.

### 1. AI shopping-agent / AEO catalog-readiness tool for small Shopify merchants — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A tool helping small DTC/Shopify merchants make their product catalogs machine-readable and visible to AI shopping agents (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google's Universal Cart/UCP) — visibility tracking, listing-content generation, and catalog-readiness fixes.
- **Risks:** Entering a category that's already fully priced out from free to enterprise, funded to nine figures at the top end.
- **Consumer/buyer pain (quantified):** Real and growing — 34% of US online shoppers used an AI agent to assist a purchase decision in 2026, up from 9% in 2024 (McKinsey AI Commerce Index); merchants with incomplete/non-machine-readable product data reportedly get skipped by shopping agents in favor of better-structured competitors.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Genuinely fresh distribution shift — Google's Universal Cart/UCP update this cycle extends AI-agent shopping from ChatGPT/Perplexity into Google Search and Gemini, widening the surface merchants need to optimize for.
- **Why don't existing big players compete — they don't need to, they already ship the onboarding:** Google itself is "simplifying UCP onboarding in Merchant Center" for free; the platforms driving the shift are also the ones giving away the integration layer.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it is, top to bottom:** Alhena AI (free tier, $199+/mo, live Shopify/WooCommerce/Magento/Salesforce catalog sync, SKU-level AI visibility), Profound ($99–$5,000+/mo, $96M Series C at ~$1B valuation, 1,800+ customers), Otterly ($29/mo budget entry), Scrunch, Peec AI, and Frizerly (positioned explicitly for 1–3-store SMB Shopify founders) already span every price point a new entrant could target.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** More AI-shopping endpoints (deeper ChatGPT commerce, Perplexity's Merchant Program) get added, but each arrives with the existing vendors already shipping same-week integrations — this is now their core business model, not a side feature.
- **Competitors:** Alhena AI, Profound, Scrunch, Peec AI, Otterly, Frizerly, Anagram.
- **Who pays / sales motion:** Self-serve SaaS to Shopify/DTC merchants — but no price floor left to undercut; Otterly already owns the $29/mo budget tier and Alhena gives away a usable free tier.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Low ceiling for a new entrant — price-sensitive segment already served free-to-cheap, enterprise segment locked behind Profound's citation-data scale and funding.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** An MVP tracking basic AI-mention visibility could ship in weeks, but matching Alhena's live catalog-sync depth or Profound's citation-data scale is a funded, multi-year build.
- **Sources:** [McKinsey-cited AI Commerce Index via Toolient](https://www.toolient.com/2026/03/product-feed-optimization-ai-agents.html), [Google UCP updates](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/shopping/ucp-updates/), [Alhena AI vs Profound comparison](https://alhena.ai/blog/alhena-ai-visibility-vs-profound/), [Profound alternatives pricing survey](https://alhena.ai/blog/profound-alternatives/).
- **Kill reason:** Third confirmation this ledger of the GEO/AEO shape being a fully-saturated meta-category — after publishers (2026-08-07) and wedding vendors (2026-08-29), this cycle's ecommerce-specific version dies the same way, complete with a funded leader (Profound), a Shopify-native incumbent with catalog sync (Alhena), and a $29/mo budget option (Otterly) already covering the entire price ladder.
- **Not scored against B2C_RULES** — B2B, buyer is a Shopify/DTC merchant.

### 2. Real-time deepfake video-call scam detection for families protecting aging parents — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A consumer app/browser extension running real-time deepfake detection on video calls (Zoom, FaceTime, WhatsApp) to flag an AI-cloned "relationship" or "advisor" persona mid-pig-butchering-scam, marketed to adult children paying to protect an aging parent.
- **Risks:** The core differentiating mechanism is a hard, capital-intensive ML/systems problem already being solved by funded, hardware-partnered competitors.
- **Consumer pain (quantified):** Real and sharply rising — investment/pig-butchering fraud losses jumped from $1.834B (2024) to $3.519B (2025), a 92% YoY increase, now ~45% of all elder-fraud losses; total elder fraud hit $7.7B in 2025 (+37% YoY); Singapore alone logged 1,200+ deepfaked live-video scam cases in January 2026, versus 43 a year earlier.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Real escalation — 2026 sources specifically describe scammers now running fabricated live personas on video calls (not just cloned voice or text), a step up from the romance/impersonation scams already on this ledger.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** This gap is real — Zoom, Teams, and Meet haven't shipped native deepfake detection as of this cycle.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it is, at the exact mechanism:** Scam.ai launched "Halo" in August 2026 — real-time, on-device deepfake detection for live video meetings, built in partnership with Qualcomm, live now for Windows with more platforms planned. Pindrop Pulse for Meetings ships continuous identity verification and deepfake detection on Zoom/Teams/Webex. Fraunhofer has a working local-running audio+video prototype. All three shipped or were reported on in the same eight-week window as this digest.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** This becomes a bundled feature inside video-call platforms or endpoint-security suites — Halo's Qualcomm hardware partnership is explicitly aimed at platform-level integration, squeezing out room for a standalone consumer app further, not opening it.
- **Competitors:** Scam.ai (Halo), Pindrop, Fraunhofer (prototype).
- **Who pays / sales motion:** Would be self-serve subscription (adult child paying to protect a parent) — but "adult child of an aging parent" isn't a targetable Meta interest/life-event category, the same gap that killed the 2026-08-24 kinship-caregiver idea.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Not scored — kill precedes this.
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** Real-time on-device deepfake detection at usable accuracy is a months-to-years ML/systems build backed by a hardware partnership (per Scam.ai's own Qualcomm collaboration) — not a weeks-buildable MVP.
- **Sources:** [AARP — pig butchering scams](https://www.aarp.org/money/scams-fraud/what-are-pig-butchering-scams/), [HCSK Stolen Trust 2026 study](https://seniors.hcsk.org/special-study-2026/read/), [Scam.ai Halo launch — BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260805515597/en/Scam.ai-Launches-Halo-Real-Time-On-Device-Deepfake-Detection-for-Video-Calls), [Pindrop Pulse for Meetings](https://www.pindrop.com/article/deepfake-video-call-scam-continuous-identity-verification/), [Biometric Update — Fraunhofer](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202608/fraunhofer-develops-real-time-deepfake-detector-for-video-calls).
- **Kill reason:** The exact differentiating mechanism (real-time, on-device video deepfake detection) already shipped commercially, weeks before this digest, from a hardware-partnered, funded competitor — a Rule 4 thin-moat failure compounded by no clean Meta targeting category for the buyer.
- **B2C_RULES gate check:** Fails Rule 1 outright — no real Meta interest/life-event category for "adult child protecting an aging parent from fraud," the same gap that killed the kinship-caregiver idea; rejected without scoring the remaining eight gates.

### 3. Auto dealer price-transparency / CARS Act compliance checker for car buyers — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A consumer tool letting car buyers check a dealer's advertised price against the true all-in price (fees, add-ons) before visiting, riding California's CARS Act (effective October 1, 2026), which mandates all-in pricing, add-on disclosure, and a 3-business-day return right on used vehicles under $50k.
- **Risks:** An identical free tool, from a more trusted incumbent, already exists and was built in direct response to the same enforcement trigger.
- **Consumer pain (quantified):** Real and freshly enforced — the FTC sent warning letters to 97 auto dealer groups in March 2026 over hidden fees; junk fees are a perennial top complaint in car buying.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Dated and real — California's CARS Act binds October 1, 2026, three weeks out at digest time, with misrepresentation liability and 2-year dealer record-retention requirements giving it real teeth.
- **Why don't existing big players compete — they don't need to, they already rebuilt around it:** TrueCar restructured its own pricing model to fold dealer fees into advertised prices specifically in response to the March 2026 FTC scrutiny; Edmunds already publishes "True Market Value" data from real transactions.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet — it is:** CarEdge already ships a free, public "Dealer Transparency Index" scoring every dealer on add-on behavior and markup — built explicitly in response to the same March 2026 FTC warning-letter wave that motivates this idea — live today at caredge.com/dealer-ratings.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** More states copy California's CARS Act (Connecticut already has similar advertising rules), but CarEdge, TrueCar, and Edmunds absorb each one at the same free tier, having already built the scoring infrastructure.
- **Competitors:** CarEdge (Dealer Transparency Index, free), TrueCar, Edmunds (True Market Value).
- **Who pays / sales motion:** Would be self-serve consumer — but no wedge left; CarEdge's tool is free and already carries more car-buying trust than a new entrant could build in weeks.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / ARR ceiling:** Near zero — nothing to charge for once the free incumbent tool is accounted for; independently fails B2C Rule 2 (one-time transaction, no renewing LTV).
- **Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** An MVP scraping dealer ads and diffing against fee-disclosure requirements could ship in days — which is exactly why CarEdge already built and shipped the identical scored tool ahead of the deadline.
- **Sources:** [CalLawyers — CARS Act summary](https://calawyers.org/business-law/california-combating-auto-retail-scams-cars-act-signed-into-law/), [CarEdge — FTC warning + Dealer Transparency Index](https://caredge.com/guides/ftc-car-dealer-warning-march-2026), [Auto Remarketing — TrueCar FTC response](https://www.autoremarketing.com/ar/retail/truecar-responds-to-ftcs-increased-scrutiny-with-updated-pricing-standards/).
- **Kill reason:** CarEdge already ships the exact free, dealer-scored transparency tool this idea would sell, built in direct response to the same March 2026 FTC enforcement wave that motivates it, three weeks ahead of the CARS Act deadline that would make it locally binding.
- **B2C_RULES gate check:** Fails Rule 2 outright — a car purchase is a one-and-done transaction with no renewing reason to keep paying the same tool; rejected without scoring the remaining eight gates.

**Bottom line:** Thirty-sixth straight cycle, zero survivors. All three candidates had genuinely fresh, dated hooks this cycle (Google's Universal Cart/UCP expansion, a 92%-YoY spike in deepfake-video pig-butchering losses plus a just-launched detection competitor, and a three-weeks-out CARS Act deadline with an already-live free compliance tool) — and all three died to the same two patterns this ledger keeps finding: a well-funded or hardware-backed incumbent already ships the exact differentiating mechanism (Alhena/Profound/Otterly, Scam.ai's Halo, CarEdge's Dealer Transparency Index), or the underlying shape is one the market piled into the moment the trigger appeared (GEO/AEO tooling, third confirmation; deepfake detection, an active funded arms race; regulation-triggered compliance checker, the single most over-mined shape on this ledger).
