## Ideas

Three candidates investigated, all rejected — two to the same "regulation/policy just changed → build a compliance/financing tool for it" pattern this project keeps re-verifying and killing, one B2C candidate to a factual error in its own targeting premise.

### 1. BSA-compliance financing/marketplace for Amazon FBA sellers ($100K–$1M/mo) — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A compliant financing or deal-structuring product for Amazon sellers whose existing revenue-based-lending or account-sale/aggregator deals relied on pledging future Amazon disbursements as collateral — a structure Amazon's amended Business Solutions Agreement bans outright effective Aug 24, 2026.
- **Risks:** The disruption is real but the exposed structure (direct payout-right pledges) is already a minority practice; the unserved segment is shrinking, not growing; no self-serve software wedge survives contact with what it would actually take to serve it (capital or a law license).
- **Business pain (quantified):** ~2% of Amazon sellers do $100K+/month (out of an estimated 1-2M active sellers), and >75,000 crossed $1M/year in 2025 — but only the subset with pledge-structured (not receivables-purchase-structured) financing faces real exposure, a minority of that minority.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Genuinely new deadline (Aug 24, 2026) — but it's a one-time compliance event, not a recurring source of demand.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They don't need to — Wayflyer, Clearco/Uncapped, and Payability's "Instant Access" are already structured as receivables-purchase-plus-bank-debit, which is "on much safer ground legally" per multiple independent legal analyses, because the lender never touches Amazon's side of the payout relationship. Amazon's own in-house MCA product (via Parafin) is trivially compliant since Amazon deducts its own product's repayment directly.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet:** It already is on the informational side — amazonsellers.attorney, myamazonguy, feedvisor, novadata, and velocitysellers all published detailed compliance analysis within days of the change, and law firms are actively selling paid compliance-review engagements now.
- **What will change in next 2 years:** Non-compliant lenders and aggregator earnout deals either restructure to bank-debit or exit; the opportunity window (already narrow) closes to near zero as case law/enforcement precedent resolves the remaining ambiguity.
- **Competitors:** Wayflyer, Clearco/Uncapped, Payability (Instant Access), Parafin (Amazon-native MCA), plus law firms already selling compliance-review engagements.
- **Who pays:** Sellers or aggregators needing one-time legal restructuring — project-based legal/consulting work, not a recurring self-serve SaaS buyer.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / LTV:CAC / ARR ceiling:** Not modeled — this isn't a productizable recurring-revenue business; it's a one-time legal-advisory need already being met by law firms, and actual lending requires capital and lending-law licensing neither available to nor appropriate for this audience.
- **Time to first sellable version:** A "financing agreement compliance scanner" SaaS could ship in weeks, but the addressable pool (sellers with legacy pledge-structured facilities) is a few thousand at most and law firms already give the same check away free as a lead-gen hook into $2-5K engagements.
- **Operational load:** Not evaluated — moot.
- **Sources:** [amazonsellers.attorney](http://www.amazonsellers.attorney/blog/amazons-august-24-2026-bsa-change-what-the-new-transfer-and-pledging-rules-mean-for-account-sales-aggregator-deals-and-revenue-based-lending), [novadata.io](https://novadata.io/resources/news/amazon-bsa-account-transfer-ban-august-2026), [velocitysellers.com](https://www.velocitysellers.com/2026/08/03/amazon-bsa-update-august-24-2026-transfer-pledge/), [Nasdaq — Amazon/Parafin MCA launch](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/amazon-launches-new-merchant-cash-advance-program-provided-by-parafin-doubling-down), [debanked.com — Amazon exits lending](https://debanked.com/2024/03/amazon-exits-the-business-loan-business/), [Payability Instant Advance](https://help.payability.com/hc/en-us/articles/360010677254-Instant-Advance), [CT Acquisitions — FBA valuation multiples 2026](https://ctacquisitions.com/ecommerce-business-valuation/), [SmartScout — Amazon seller statistics](https://www.smartscout.com/blog/amazon-seller-statistics)
- **Kill reason:** The exposed deal structure (pledging Amazon payout rights directly) is already a minority practice among sophisticated financiers, who are structured around it via receivables-purchase-plus-bank-debit; the remaining gap is a one-time legal-advisory need law firms are already monetizing as a lead-gen hook, not a recurring self-serve product a solo founder could build and sell.

### 2. Creative-provenance/audit-trail tool for Google Ads AI-disclosure — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A tool letting DTC/performance advertisers log and export their Google Ads AI-content disclosure attestations (the self-attestation checkbox in the "How this ad was made" panel launched Jul 9, 2026) as a tamper-evident audit trail, protecting against future account-penalty liability.
- **Risks:** Third rejection of an identical shape this project has now hit on three different platforms/labels (Meta ad-rejection predictor, EU AI Act Article 50 widget, now Google Ads) — thin feature surface, no reported enforcement to sell urgency against, and a live broader-scope competitor already covers the substance for free.
- **Business pain (quantified):** Unquantified and currently theoretical — zero reported account suspensions tied to AI-disclosure non-compliance as of Aug 2026; Google's own help documentation states it "will not perform its own check" and disclaims guaranteeing regulatory compliance.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Google's Jul 9, 2026 rollout is genuinely fresh, but the compliance mechanic it triggers (logging an AI-content disclosure attestation) is not — it's the same EU AI Act Article 50 obligation (enforceable Aug 2, 2026, €15M/3% global turnover) already being served elsewhere.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They're building the underlying standard instead of a wrapper around it — Google, Meta, and OpenAI are all C2PA members, and Adobe already writes Content Credentials automatically across Firefly/Creative Cloud; the industry is converging on infrastructure-layer provenance, not point-solution audit logs.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet:** It isn't saturated on the specific keyword "Google Ads," but the substance is: DiscloAI already logs AI-content disclosures (one of four Article 50 categories it covers) platform-agnostically, with tamper-evident SHA-256-hashed, exportable audit logs, starting free — it doesn't care whether the ad ran on Google, Meta, or TikTok because the underlying obligation is identical across platforms.
- **What will change in next 2 years:** C2PA adoption widens (6,000+ members already) and Google/Meta likely start auto-ingesting Content Credentials metadata directly, closing the manual-attestation gap this idea would have filled entirely.
- **Competitors:** DiscloAI (live, free tier, platform-agnostic, four Article 50 disclosure types including AI content), Disclos, ActReady (adjacent EU Article 50 tools), auditsocials.com (the Meta-side precedent that killed the earlier version of this idea), Truepic and Adobe Content Credentials (C2PA infrastructure), plus generic ad-ops suites (Optmyzr, Revealbot, Smartly.io) as plausible free bolt-on shippers.
- **Who pays:** Would be DTC/performance advertisers using AI creative — but see kill reason; also no founder-market fit, since careerpicai.com runs Meta ads exclusively, never Google Ads.
- **Expected CAC / LTV / LTV:CAC / ARR ceiling:** Not modeled — a live, broader-scope, free-tier competitor (DiscloAI) already occupies the price point before a funnel is worth building.
- **Time to first sellable version:** Technically fast, moot given the competitive findings.
- **Operational load:** Not evaluated — moot.
- **Sources:** [TechCrunch — Google AI ad disclosure](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/09/google-will-now-disclose-which-ads-are-made-with-ai/), [Google Ads Help — AI content label settings](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/17140115), [My Ad Center Help — AI Transparency](https://support.google.com/My-Ad-Center-Help/answer/17196133), [DiscloAI](https://discloai.com/), [Truepic — content provenance](https://www.truepic.com/blog/a-more-transparent-internet-is-on-its-way), [PPC Land — Google AI labels ahead of EU deadline](https://ppc.land/google-adds-how-this-ad-was-made-ai-labels-24-days-before-eu-deadline/)
- **Kill reason:** DiscloAI already ships this exact mechanic (tamper-evident, exportable AI-content-disclosure audit logging) platform-agnostically and free-to-start, and the C2PA standards track is absorbing the underlying problem over the next 24 months — compounded by zero founder dogfooding since careerpicai.com never touches Google Ads.

### 3. Privacy-first co-parenting coordination app (vs. Life360/Cozi trust backlash) — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A no-dark-pattern shared-custody coordination app (calendar, expense-splitting, kid info) targeting recently-divorced/separating parents, positioned against the quantified Life360/Cozi trust erosion — easy cancellation, no nagging notifications, no data selling, transparent pricing.
- **Risks:** Fails B2C_RULES.md Gate 1 outright — see kill reason. Also shaky on Gates 3 and 9 even before that.
- **Consumer pain:** Real and quantified on the incumbent-trust side (Life360: 1.6/5 Trustpilot across 268 reviews, 68% 1-star on Sitejabber/508 reviews, over paywalled safety features and hard-to-cancel subscriptions; Cozi: 2.1/5 after paywalling previously-free calendar history) — but this is dissatisfaction with existing tools, not validated demand for a new co-parenting-specific one.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** No sharp trigger found beyond the ongoing trust erosion, which has been building for years, not a new-this-cycle event.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They do, extensively, and already on this exact "kinder alternative" positioning.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet:** It's already occupied. AppClose (2.4M+ parents served since 2016, 1M+ Google Play downloads, $5.44M raised) was free for co-parents until a Jan 1, 2026 shift to a paid $7.99-8.99/mo plan; Fayr ($9.99/mo since 2018), 2Houses, TalkingParents, Custody X Change, and BestInterest/PeaceParent (explicitly marketed as private, no-data-selling, usable even if only one parent signs up) all already compete on the "not predatory" positioning this idea proposes as its differentiator.
- **What will change in next 2 years:** New-divorces-per-year is a real, continuous inflow but a shrinking one — 2.3 divorces/1,000 population in 2024, down from 3.6/1,000 in 2010 (NCFMR/BGSU) — working against, not for, this candidate over time.
- **Competitors:** AppClose, Fayr, 2Houses, TalkingParents, Custody X Change, BestInterest/PeaceParent — several funded, one at 2.4M-user scale.
- **Who pays / Expected CAC / LTV / LTV:CAC / ARR ceiling / Time to first sellable version / Operational load:** Not modeled — killed on the targeting gate before unit economics are reachable.
- **Sources:** [checkthat.ai — Life360 reviews](https://checkthat.ai/brands/life360/reviews), [famiguard.com — why Life360 is bad](https://www.famiguard.com/tips/why-life360-is-bad/), [justuseapp — Cozi reviews](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/407108860/cozi-family-organizer/reviews), AppClose/Fayr/2Houses/Custody X Change public pricing and press pages, NCFMR/BGSU divorce-rate data, `B2C_RULES.md`.
- **Kill reason:** Fails Rule 1 (Meta-targetable via life-event) outright — "recently divorced" is not an actual Meta life-event category (unlike "new parent," which genuinely is one); the only available target is the static "Relationship Status = Divorced" demographic bucket the rule explicitly excludes. Even setting that aside, the category is already densely populated by funded, scaled "kinder alternative" incumbents (AppClose alone at 2.4M users) with no visible moat a new entrant could bring — "we're not predatory" is a cloneable feature, not a defensible one, and AppClose itself proves even the trustworthy-free incumbent eventually had to start charging.

**Bottom line:** Zero survivors today, and a useful pattern to name: two of three kills (Amazon BSA financing, Google Ads disclosure) trace to the same lesson the 2026-08-03 digest already flagged — check incumbent maturity and structural exposure before spending research budget on a fresh-looking regulatory trigger, since sophisticated existing players are frequently already structured to be unaffected by exactly the change that looks disruptive on the surface. The third (co-parenting) is a reminder to verify a rule's factual premise (does the targeting category actually exist on the platform) before scoring the rest of the gates.
