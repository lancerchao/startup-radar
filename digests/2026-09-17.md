## Ideas

Three candidates deep-dived this cycle, all rejected — the forty-fourth straight cycle with zero survivors.

### 1. NP-specific clinical documentation / EHR-companion tool — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** AI documentation tool with note templates, coding assist, and prior-auth workflows tuned to nurse-practitioner scope-of-practice and NP-specific billing (incident-to, split/shared, 85% Medicare fee schedule), sold to NP-owned independent clinics. Triggered by recurring r/NursePractitioner-style complaints that mainstream EHR templates and billing workflows are built around physician scope/billing codes.
- **Risks:** The named root cause (reimbursement/credentialing policy) isn't fixable by a documentation tool; general AI scribes already cover the note-burden layer "close enough" regardless of prescriber type.
- **Pain (quantified):** ~461,000 licensed NPs nationally (AANP, Nov 2025), ~307,390 in direct patient-care roles (BLS) — but no credible national count of NP-*owned* independent clinics exists; 2025-2026 peer-reviewed literature (JAANP, JAMA Health Forum) treats NP ownership as an emerging, small, undercounted phenomenon (one study profiles just 4 practices). No NP-vs-physician comparative documentation-time study found. Full-practice-authority states number ~27-30 + DC, but New York's FPA law lapsed in July 2026 — the trend is mixed, not uniformly expanding.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Weak. NP workforce growth (BLS: +40% 2024-2034) is a genuine multi-year tailwind, but the "trigger" is evergreen EHR-burnout discourse rehashed for NPs, not a new event, and FPA expansion is stalling/reversing in at least one state.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do. General-purpose AI scribes (Freed $99/mo, Heidi ~$110/mo, Nabla $119/mo, Abridge $250-500/mo, Suki $300-400/mo) are prescriber-agnostic and already generate notes for NPs; small-practice EHR/PM vendors (Tebra, PracticeQ, DocVilla, OmniMD) already run dedicated "solutions for nurse practitioners" marketing with built-in billing/PA modules.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet:** It effectively is — both the scribe layer and the EHR layer already have NP-facing offerings; there's no vacant lane, just an unbundled one. Peer-reviewed barrier research names reimbursement differentials and payer-contracting delays — not EHR template design — as the real obstacles to NP practice ownership.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** NP workforce keeps growing; incident-to/reimbursement rules may get federal/CMS attention, but that could shrink the pain (parity reform) or entrench it — neither outcome favors a new entrant now.
- **Competitors:** Freed, Abridge, Suki, Nabla, Heidi Health, DeepScribe, Ambience Healthcare, Corti (prescriber-agnostic scribes); Tebra, PracticeQ, DocVilla, OmniMD, Praxis EMR (small-practice EHRs already marketing to NPs).
- **Who pays / sales motion:** Founder-led B2B sales to independent NP-owned clinics, reachable in theory via AANP, state NP associations, AANP's national conference, NP-specific Facebook groups/subreddit — but the addressable pool is a fragmented, unquantified subset of ~461K mostly-W-2 NPs.
- **Expected CAC:** ~$1,500-3,000/clinic (conference presence, content, outbound) — typical solo-clinic vertical SaaS.
- **LTV:** ~$3,000-6,000 given high churn risk in solo/small practices, at ~$100-300/mo ARPA undifferentiated from existing $99-250/mo scribe pricing.
- **LTV:CAC:** ~2:1 at best — marginal.
- **ARR ceiling:** Likely sub-$1-2M given the unquantified, probably small buyer pool and thin willingness-to-pay above existing tools.
- **Time to first sellable version:** Weeks, if reduced to "another AI scribe with NP-flavored templates" — but that doesn't address the actual named pain (incident-to billing, prior-auth, payer credentialing), which needs deep EHR/payer integration: months to years, not weeks.
- **Operational load:** High support burden from a fragmented, non-technical solo-clinic buyer base.
- **Sources:** [AANP — NP profession grows to 461,000](https://www.aanp.org/news-feed/nurse-practitioner-profession-grows-to-385-000-strong), [Nurse.org — NP Full Practice Authority 2026](https://nurse.org/education/np-full-practice-authority/), [JAMA Health Forum — NP Entrepreneurs](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama-health-forum/fullarticle/2843822), [JAANP — Nurse practitioner owners](https://journals.lww.com/jaanp/fulltext/2025/06000/nurse_practitioner_owners__the_front_lines_of.6.aspx), [PubMed — Barriers of NP practice ownership](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42520531/), [Keragon — AI medical scribe pricing 2026](https://www.keragon.com/blog/best-ai-medical-scribe).
- **Kill reason:** The documented root cause is reimbursement/credentialing policy, not EHR UX, so a documentation tool doesn't fix it; general AI scribes and NP-marketed small-practice EHRs already cover both plausible product layers; and the actual buyer pool (NP-*owned* clinics, not the 461K-strong NP workforce) has no credible size estimate and appears small and fragmented.
- **Not scored against B2C_RULES** — B2B, buyer is an NP-owned clinic.

### 2. Breached-document fraud check for SMB identity verification — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A KYC/fraud-detection API for small lenders, marketplaces, and gig/rental platforms that flags when a submitted driver's-license image matches a known-breached corpus (e.g., the Nexus/IDScan leak), priced below Persona/Socure enterprise tiers.
- **Risks:** Building fraud-detection infra that actually works (liveness, injection detection, device fingerprinting, biometric matching) is a multi-year technical undertaking, not a weeks-to-ship wrapper; a bare "check against breach corpus" feature is trivially gamed by using documents not yet in a known corpus.
- **Pain (quantified):** Real at the macro level — US synthetic-identity losses projected >$3.1B in 2026 (up from $1.8B in 2020, ~16%/yr growth); 8.3% of digital account creations in H1 2025 were suspected fraudulent (industry/vendor reporting, not independently audited). Not specific to SMBs lacking a solution, though: the breach itself (153M+ licenses) is the largest of 88+ ID-verification breaches counted since 2024, not a new category of risk.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** Document-scan-as-trust-anchor has been visibly broken for two years (AU10TIX 2024, IDmerit ~1B records Nov 2025, Veriff Dec 2025, Sumsub Feb 2026, Persona Feb 2026 all breached) — Nexus is the biggest single incident in an existing trend, not a structurally new risk.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** They already do, and have for years. Post-IDScan reporting confirms vendors are "reducing reliance on document-only checks in favor of biometrics with active liveness signals" — because it doesn't matter whether the *document* is stolen if the *live biometric* doesn't match a deepfake/injected image. No major vendor markets "check against a known-breach corpus" as a standalone feature, partly because a database of breached PII documents is itself a liability magnet.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet:** It is, at both tiers that matter. Enterprise: Persona, Socure, Onfido, Jumio, Incode, Veriff, Alloy, Prove all ship liveness + injection detection. SMB self-serve: Stripe Identity ($1.50/verification, 50 free, no sales call) and Plaid IDV (~$1.10-2/verification, self-serve) already own the cheap, solo-founder-friendly segment.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** More breaches (trend is up), continued vendor consolidation around biometric-first verification, likely regulatory pressure forcing IDV vendors to tighten retention — a vendor-side compliance opportunity, not an SMB fraud-detection product one.
- **Competitors:** Persona, Socure, Onfido, Jumio, Incode, Veriff, Alloy, Prove (enterprise); Stripe Identity, Plaid IDV (SMB self-serve).
- **Who pays / sales motion:** Would require per-verification usage billing competing directly against Stripe's near-zero-friction API — a race to the bottom against a company with a payments distribution moat a new entrant can't match. No Meta-ads-native path to this buyer.
- **Expected CAC:** High for a no-name entrant selling trust infrastructure to SMBs who default to whatever their payment processor already bundles.
- **LTV:** Low-to-moderate at usage-based SMB pricing; churns hard once Stripe/Plaid ship an equivalent checkbox feature.
- **LTV:CAC:** Unfavorable — commodity-priced product against a subsidized incumbent.
- **ARR ceiling:** Low single-digit millions at best before hitting Stripe/Plaid price competition or enterprise vendors moving downmarket.
- **Time to first sellable version:** Weeks for an API wrapper checking a leaked corpus, but that alone doesn't solve fraud; a genuinely defensible product needs liveness/injection detection — months to years.
- **Operational load:** High — storing/matching against leaked driver's-license images is itself a compliance and breach-liability magnet for a solo founder.
- **Sources:** [TechCrunch — IDScan breach confirmed](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/10/id-verification-giant-idscan-confirms-data-breach-with-more-than-150-million-drivers-licenses-stolen/), [Krebs on Security — FBI probes Nexus](https://krebsonsecurity.com/2026/09/fbi-probes-service-selling-153m-drivers-licenses/), [Security Affairs — 88 ID verification breaches](https://securityaffairs.com/197855/reports/88-id-verification-breaches-show-the-cost-of-collecting-identity-data.html), [BIIA — Synthetic Identity Fraud Statistics 2026](https://www.biia.com/synthetic-identity-fraud-statistics-2026-hard-numbers-big-threats/), [Stripe Identity pricing](https://www.spotsaas.com/product/stripe-identity/pricing), [Plaid pricing](https://plaid.com/pricing/).
- **Kill reason:** Liveness/injection-detection, already deployed industry-wide, neutralizes stolen-document reuse regardless of the document's breach status, and Stripe Identity/Plaid already own the cheap, self-serve SMB tier at prices a new entrant can't beat without months-to-years of real biometric-fraud engineering.
- **Not scored against B2C_RULES** — B2B, buyer is a small lender/marketplace/platform.

### 3. Affordable AI diligence tool for boutique M&A shops and search funds — REJECTED (new)

- **Brief description:** A sub-$200/mo self-serve tool that reads CIMs/data rooms and drafts diligence memos and pitch decks for small M&A advisory shops, business brokers, and search funds — the tier OpenAI's new Morgan Stanley/Evercore-designed "ChatGPT for Financial Services" (launched Sept 10) explicitly ignores.
- **Risks:** "AI reads a CIM" is now a commodity feature already bundled into live competing products at this exact price point; the real bottleneck for this buyer segment is deal sourcing/broker relationships, not document-synthesis speed.
- **Pain (quantified):** Real tailwind independent of the OpenAI trigger — McKinsey estimates ~6M small/mid businesses face ownership transition by 2035 (~$5T enterprise value), SBA projects 10M boomer-owned businesses changing hands 2019-2029, peak transition volume projected 2027-2028 (Fed 2025 survey). But that's a tailwind for deal-sourcing/volume tools, not evidence diligence-drafting is the bottleneck.
- **Why now, not 2 years ago:** The boomer business-transition wave is a genuine multi-year trend, but OpenAI's launch targets enterprise banks, not this tier — it's context, not a fresh trigger for this specific wedge.
- **Why don't existing big players compete:** Correct that Rogo ($2B val., $300M+ raised, 35,000+ users at 250+ institutions), Hebbia ($161M raised, $13M ARR by mid-2024), and AlphaSense (6,000 customers) are enterprise-priced and genuinely ignore the sub-$10M-deal searcher/broker tier.
- **Why isn't it saturated yet:** It is — that gap is already filled by a different, cheaper, live layer. SearcherOS ($79-149/mo) and DealOrb (~$79-999/mo) already do AI CIM analysis, add-back/earnings-bridge auditing, risk scorecards, deal-pipeline CRM, and LOI generation specifically for search funds/independent sponsors, self-serve, no sales cycle. A trade blog is already cataloging "35 Due Diligence Agents for Search Funds and Independent Sponsors" as of 2026 — this niche is late and fragmenting, not early.
- **What will change in the next 2 years:** Rogo/Hebbia-class vendors trickle down to mid-market boutique banks; frontier-model access makes "read a CIM and draft a memo" near-zero-marginal-cost for any competitor to bolt on, compressing a standalone tool's value further.
- **Competitors:** Rogo, Hebbia, AlphaSense, Daloopa (enterprise); SearcherOS, DealOrb, Eilla AI (self-serve, search-fund-specific, live); DealCloud/Intapp (adjacent enterprise CRM, $100K+/yr, search funds already "run their whole business" on it).
- **Who pays / sales motion:** Self-serve subscription (matches Lance's preference), but SearcherOS/DealOrb already execute that exact motion into the same reachable trade-association channels (IBBA ~3,142 US brokers, ~862 tracked search funds per Stanford's 2026 study, ACG ~15,000 members).
- **Expected CAC:** Low-to-moderate if content-led, but two incumbents already own the SEO real estate for "AI CIM analysis" and "deal sourcing software for searchers."
- **LTV:** Modest — $79-999/mo comps suggest ~$1K-3K/yr per seat.
- **LTV:CAC:** Plausible on paper, but no differentiated wedge to win share from funded, live competitors already there.
- **ARR ceiling:** Low — total addressable pool (~800 active search funds + ~3,000 brokers + boutique IB shops) at $1-3K/yr blended ARPU caps a realistic outcome well under $5-10M ARR even at strong penetration.
- **Time to first sellable version:** 4-8 weeks for an MVP — but equally easy for SearcherOS/DealOrb to ship the same feature next quarter.
- **Operational load:** Moderate — financial-data-accuracy expectations from a professional-services buyer, ongoing CIM/broker-feed integration burden.
- **Sources:** [TechFundingNews — Rogo $160M](https://techfundingnews.com/rogo-160m-series-d-kleiner-perkins-investment-banking-ai/), [Axios — Rogo $750M valuation](https://www.axios.com/pro/fintech-deals/2026/01/28/sequoia-rogo-750m-valuation), [Stanford 2026 Search Fund Study](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/case-studies/2026-search-fund-study-selected-observations), [SearcherOS pricing](https://searcheros.ai/pricing), [DealOrb tool comparison](https://dealorb.ai/blog/best-deal-sourcing-software), [McKinsey boomer business transition, via Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/02/26/great-small-business-wealth-transfer-mckinsey-5-trillion-baby-boomer-businesses-sale/), [DealCloud pricing](https://www.softwareadvice.com/marketing/dealcloud-profile/).
- **Kill reason:** The underserved-tier thesis is factually wrong — SearcherOS and DealOrb are already live, funded, SMB-priced products doing AI CIM analysis and diligence specifically for search funds/ETA buyers, with a trade blog already cataloging 35+ competing tools in this niche, and the buyer pool is too small (~800 search funds, ~3,000 brokers) to clear a meaningful ARR ceiling even with zero competition.
- **Not scored against B2C_RULES** — B2B, buyer is a small M&A shop, broker, or search fund.

**Bottom line:** Forty-fourth straight cycle, zero survivors. All three candidates this cycle were triggered by genuinely fresh signals (a 153M-record ID breach three weeks old, OpenAI's Sept 10 enterprise financial-services launch, and an underexplored NP-documentation complaint pattern) rather than recycled regulatory-compliance angles — a deliberate attempt to route around the "calculator/checker for a regulatory shift" shape this ledger has flagged as over-mined. All three still died the same way as most recent cycles: not on "no competitor exists" but on "the competitor already exists at the exact price point and buyer tier this idea was aiming for" (Stripe Identity/Plaid for SMB IDV, SearcherOS/DealOrb for search-fund diligence) or "the layer that would actually need building already has adjacent free/cheap coverage and the real bottleneck is somewhere the product can't reach" (NP documentation's real blocker is reimbursement policy, not EHR UX). Worth carrying forward: for any regulated-professional-services vertical (M&A, healthcare), check the SMB/prosumer self-serve tier specifically before assuming enterprise-only coverage — that's where the live competition actually sits.
